03 / INSPECT A CONNECTION
A NOTE ON DIRECTION

Higher genus maps down.

A nonconstant separable map E → C with g(C) > 1 is ruled out by Riemann–Hurwitz. Over these perfect fields, no nonconstant inseparable map exists either. The covers here run C → E; pullback and norm connect their Jacobians in both directions.

Search scope & mathematical conventions

All curves mean smooth projective models. Nodes retain coefficient models rather than deduplicating isomorphism classes. Composite paths represent further maps; they are not enumerated as separate edges. “Transfer” here means a displayed isogeny, cover, pullback or norm—not every possible correspondence or cohomological transfer.

Cover genus: 2g = (m−1)(3n−1) − gcd(m,3n) + 1. Validity requires a nonsingular base cubic, f(c) ≠ 0, and p ∤ mn. The construction v⁴ = f(u+c) is a smooth nonhyperelliptic plane quartic of genus 3.

References: Vélu, “Isogénies entre courbes elliptiques” (1971); Riemann–Hurwitz; the Kummer-cover genus formula. Jacobian arrows describe divisor-class maps; no full Jacobian equations are computed.

What does this tool compute?

For E : y² = x³ + ax + b over 𝔽ₚ, we enumerate every rational 2-torsion kernel on each reached model, then use Vélu’s formula for the quotient. Search depth is bounded.

For covers, substitute x = uⁿ+c and y = vᵐᐟ², with m = 2 or 4. This produces explicit higher-genus curves and maps back to E. The m = 4, n = 1 example is a nonhyperelliptic genus-3 plane quartic.

Pullback π* and norm π_* connect E with J(C). Their composition is multiplication by deg π. These arrows are distinct from maps of curves.

The graph is an exact, bounded construction, not a classification of all transfers. Change fields and bounds to explore another portion of the picture.
