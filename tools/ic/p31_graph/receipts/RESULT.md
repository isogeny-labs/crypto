# Exhausted 25,000-point p31 full-graph boundary

The factor base is exactly the first 25,000 points of the verified 60,000-point triangle-bootstrap base on the same p=2,147,483,647 curve and selected prime-order subgroup. `setup.json` binds the source input, binary factor base, and exact prefix by SHA-256. The frozen three-phase unordered-pair space is **937,462,500 queries**, and the native emitter exhausted it completely. This pilot reuses an existing base prefix; it does not measure a cold 25k setup.

The run found **468,754,075** split lines, **107,552** one-large-prime lines, and **0** full lines. It retained **299,945,714** two-large-prime edges under the fixed `floor(4p/5)` x cutoff in 3,599,348,568 bytes. Emission took **185.55 seconds external wall**. `audit25k.py` independently recomputed 1,024 spread edge witnesses with integer-only line arithmetic and p31 curve-point checks, and hashed the whole file to `c38f3a50cbc6521782cde7679f318a77a9c7a271fb2a3dac3dbfced7dfca22f2`. The audit took **1.77 seconds external wall**.

The direct-index filter checked every retained edge ordinal and cutoff and found **371,767,656 large-prime vertices**, **71,859,387 connected components**, **37,445 graph cycle edges**, and a largest component of **51,316,150 vertices**. Exactly **three** connected components contain cycles. Therefore the PP rows, each with two nonzero large-prime coefficients over the selected subgroup field, have at least **37,442 independent large-prime-free formal combinations**. This bound exceeds the 25,000 factor-base columns; it does not establish their rank, explicit sparse rows, or a solve. The graph pass took **47.24 seconds external wall**, with **7,088,537,600 bytes maximum resident set size** and no swap.

The two successful compiles, emitter, audit, and graph pass sum to **236.52 seconds** measured external wall, excluding preparation commands and earlier p31 experiments. The collector and graph stages alone take 232.79 seconds, far above the 36.735421917-second total required for 50-fold gain over the fastest measured completed triangle-bootstrap arm. A 25k full-graph solve could still be a stronger *known* IC baseline after weighted relation extraction, factor-base rank, linear algebra, descent, and original-curve scalar verification are measured. Near the graph transition, cycle paths may be dense; the formal nullity surplus of 12,442 rows is not by itself a rank or cost certificate.

This exact exhaustion shows the old rooted-tree 40k rejection cannot rule out the published full-graph method even at 25k. It does not establish a novel algorithm or a 50-fold end-to-end improvement. The next gate is explicit weighted rows and their 25,000-column rank, with every attempted extraction and interruption charged.
