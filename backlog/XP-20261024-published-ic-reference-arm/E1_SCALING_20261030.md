## Verdict: undecided, with a flat result only when neither arm is swept

- With no base-size sweep on either side the ratio is flat over n = 9 to 13: 68 to 87 whole run, 16 to 20 solver-only, slope consistent with 0 and with
  x1.5 growth per 4 bits. The n = 15 cell cannot join this fit because the lab arm at l = 4 was not run there (a lab cost at l=5 of 2.3e10 shows a fixed small base is not a
  sensible lab setting at n = 15).
- The lab arm's own base-size sweep is the dominant term. Once the lab takes its best l, the ratio rises 89, 68, 223, 136, 634 (whole run) and the fitted slope is positive with the
  interval only just excluding 0 (p = 0.05). The lab's optimum moves with n (l = 4, 5, 5, 6, 7) and at n = 15 is on the edge of the sweep (l = 8 not run), so even this lower
  bound on the lab's advantage is not closed at the large end.
- The sweep is asymmetric the other way as well: the published side was swept only over (l, spec) in {(4,4), (5,5)} (and n=9 (5,7)); at n = 15 (5,5) was cut after 2 targets and no larger l or spec was tried.
  If the published arm's optimum also moves up with n the growth would be smaller. So the 3x per 4 bits is an upper-leaning estimate for "tuned vs tuned", and the flat result is a
  lower-leaning one. The published arm's whole-run cost also includes this harness's own Rust work (about half of it; section 9.1).
- Not decided by this data: a matched sweep (lab l=4..8, published l and spec jointly) at n = 15 and one cell at n >= 17 are needed. At 1.2e12 Ir per target for the published arm at n = 15 and
  roughly 4x per 2 bits beyond, n = 17 is already about 5e12 per target at best, 40 to 60 machine-minutes per 8 targets for l=4 alone.

Not done: no larger size than n = 15; n = 15 published l=5/spec=5 incomplete (2 targets, `runs/E1_k0n15_partial_l5s5`); no repeat of the published arm for A/A (the lab A/A holds to 0.5 %);
msolve 0.6.5 and WDSat as in section 9.6.
