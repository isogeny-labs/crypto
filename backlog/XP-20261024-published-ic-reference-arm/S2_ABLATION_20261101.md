## 1. Answer

1. **About half to two thirds of the lab's whole-run edge is the WDSat arm's own harness, not WDSat and not a lab method.** In the R2W WDSat arm at its best l, the Rust child (almost all of it the R5 emitter that rebuilds and renders the S4 system for every target) plus the WDSat process start-up cost 2.5e9, 3.5e9 and 6.8e9 Ir per target of 3.85e9, 5.77e9 and 13.4e9 (65 %, 61 %, 51 %) at K_1 n=11, K_0 n=13, K_0 n=15 (measured, section 3). The emitter alone costs 6.0e7, 7.1e7 and 1.3e8 Ir per system in process, which is 1.5 to 3 times the lab's entire cost per system (2e7 to 5e7). With the harness removed, WDSat's search costs 3.0, 2.1 and 3.6 times the lab's whole run.
2. **The rest (a genuine solver difference, about 2 to 4x) comes from the lab's variable-splitting order and its use of inherited bases, not from UNSAT detection.** Projected from paired per-system probes: switching the split rule from highest-free (the lab default under inherited F4) to lowest-free multiplies the lab's cost by 17, 33 and 52 at fixed l; switching inherited bases off (from-scratch F4 per node, split rule and linear elimination kept) by 2.1, 2.9 and 4.2. Template reuse across systems (1.01x), linear elimination (1.1x to 1.2x), chain variable order (1.3x to 1.7x) and support-local multipliers (1.3x to 1.5x) are small.
3. **The leading hypothesis (the lab is faster at proving UNSAT) is not supported at the configurations that matter.** At each arm's best l, 150 random systems per cell contain 0 to 22 UNSAT ones (0 at WDSat's best l = 8, 8, 10; 22 at K_1 n=11 l=5; 1 at K_0 n=13 l=6; 0 at K_0 n=15 l=7), so there is almost nothing to refute. Where there are UNSAT systems the lab's advantage on them (42x at K_1 n=11 l=5 against WDSat (a)) is about the same as on SAT systems (28x).
4. **Verdict on portability.** Not a portable method in the form measured: the harness part is an artefact of the R5 glue (an S4 emitter rebuilding the system per target and one process per system). The solver part is a real property of the lab's solver family, but this experiment does not show that it transfers to WDSat: the lab's split order acts on the S_3-chain formulation (3l + n unknowns, interleaved order) and on a degree-3 Macaulay matrix with linear elimination; WDSat is run on a different formulation (S_4 e-form) and branches on the 3l x-bits. No ablation in S2 changes the formulation (the S_4 e-form needs 9l - 3 > 64 unknowns for l >= 8 and does not fit the lab's `u64` monomials), so formulation and solver stay confounded. See section 6 for the exact wording.
5. **XG (coordinator amendment):** WDSat without XG and without `-b` (config (b)) is 1.2x to 6x cheaper per system than config (a) on the same systems (mean over 150, by l and cell; section 4). So part of the R2W edge is WDSat misconfiguration, as the CP 2020 note says. The size on the whole run could not be measured (no ecbench session for (b)); the lower bound of (b)'s whole run (child + start-up, which XG does not change) is 2.5e9, 3.5e9, 6.8e9, still 5.4x, 3.4x, 3.6x the lab.

- Caveats that cut both ways. (3) S and I are projected from per-system probes, not counted sessions.

## 6. Portable method or artefact?

- **Harness part: artefact.** WDSat is spawned once per system, its input is rendered from an S4 system the R5 emitter rebuilds each time, and the pipeline parses the output. That costs 51 to 65 % of the WDSat whole run and has nothing to do with WDSat's search nor with any lab idea. A fair published-method arm would build the S4 system once per base (the lab's template reuse, which is worth 1.01x to the lab but would be worth a factor 1.5 to 3 here) and call the solver in process or in batch.
- **Search part: partly a real lab advantage, with a portability claim that S2 does not support.** Under the pre-registered rule the split order (highest-free variable, together with the interleaved chain order and linear elimination that it relies on) explains the lab's edge in the pipeline; inherited bases explain it for solver-only cost. Both are choices inside the lab's Boolean F4 on the S_3-chain system, and both could be written up as a method (variable order for a Semaev chain over a subspace base, and basis inheritance). Whether the order helps WDSat is not tested: WDSat would have to be fed the chain system, and `-g` was not explored. Until that is done, the defensible statement is that the lab is a faster solver for its own formulation, by about 2 to 4x once the harness is removed (3x, 2x, 3.6x against WDSat (a); less against (b), unmeasured on the whole run), and that the published-arm comparison of R2W overstates the gap by a factor of 2 to 2.5 because of the harness and by an amount not measured because of the XG configuration.
- The "edge grows with n" question of R2W is not touched here.
