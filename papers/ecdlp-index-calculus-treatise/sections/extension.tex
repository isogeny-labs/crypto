\chapter{Extension fields: $k=4$ and the $D_4$ symmetry}\label{ch:extension}

Over $\FF_{q^k}$ with a factor base of points whose abscissa lies in
$\FF_q$, Gaudry's index calculus~\cite{gaudry2009} decomposes each target
into $k$ summands by Weil restriction of $\Sem{k+1}$. It costs
$\tilde O(q^{2-2/k})$, below rho's $q^{k/2}$ for $k\ge3$, but with a
Gröbner constant that grows very fast in $k$. Faugère, Gaudry, Huot and
Renault~\cite{fghr2014} show that on curves with rational 2-torsion the
system has the larger symmetry group $D_k$, the Weyl group of type D, in
a well-chosen coordinate. At $k=4$ this chapter reports how far that
symmetry moves the measured gap to rho. Chapter~\ref{sec:prime}
(\S\ref{sec:gaudry}) gives the odd-characteristic engineering in detail;
Chapter~\ref{ch:glvprogramme} gives the later $k=4$ programme and supersedes
the odd-characteristic distances below.

\section{The $D_4$ system}

On $E$ with rational 2-torsion point $T$, the coordinate
$Y=(x-x_0-c)/(x-x_0+c)$ satisfies $Y(P+T)=-Y(P)$. In $Y$, $\Sem5$ is
invariant under $D_4$, of order 192. Written in the invariants
$\sigma_k=e_k(Y_i^2)$ and $\pi=\prod Y_i$, the system shrinks
(Table~\ref{tab:extension-system}).

\section{Distance to rho}

\paragraph{Odd characteristic.} Over $\FF_{p^4}$ the best line (GLS twists
with full rational 2-torsion) measured $1.5\cdot10^3\times$ its strongest
matched rho at $n=2^{42.4}$ (E32, validated), falling as $n^{-0.24}$, with a
fitted crossover at $2^{85.5}$ $[2^{79.2},2^{94.3}]$ at $w=0$; that is a fit,
not a measured crossing (Chapter~\ref{ch:glvprogramme}). The earlier
single-point models of \S\ref{sec:gaudry} are superseded.

\paragraph{Characteristic two.} Over $\FF_{(2^m)^4}$ the gap closes with
size (Table~\ref{tab:pf-d4} and Figure~\ref{fig:pf-d4}, two targets per
size up to $m=12$). IC over rho's theoretical $S=0.886$ falls from
$5{,}080$ and $4{,}124$ at $m=6$ to $6.06$ at $m=14$, a single target. The
fitted slope over $m\ge8$ (seven points) is $-0.248$ in $\log_2 n$, which
is Gaudry's $n^{-1/4}$. The extrapolated crossover was $n\approx2^{68.1}$,
where rho takes about $2^{34}$ steps, so it could be tested directly. The
follow-up study ran $m=15$--$18$: $m=15$ completed in a clean session at
$3.61$ and $3.67\times$ rho's theoretical cost; the $m=16$ and $m=17$
records come from sessions killed by container restarts and were not
replayed, and $m=18$ produced no record
(\art{research/gaudry_k4_char2_crossover_20261015/README.md}). The ratio to
the same-session rho run scatters more than the ratio to theory, because
one rho run is a single draw: at $m=15$ it is $19.1$ on one target and
$3.8$ on the other.

Linear algebra is 0.4\% of the cost at $m=15$. Basic Gaudry linear
algebra grows as $q^2$, like rho, so the $n^{-1/4}$ trend lasts only while
the relation phase dominates. Beyond that point double large primes are
required.

The red team froze a plan to settle the question: $m=14$--$17$, eight fresh
targets per arm, a strong rho with batched inversion and charged
canonicalisation, counted and hardware units, about 30\,h of wall time.
It has not run. It is the cheapest experiment that could put the $k=4$
characteristic-two family below rho, as a measured size crossover; other
weak families already have measured below-rho rows, the validated
Joux--Vitse sieve row among them (Chapter~\ref{sec:transfer},
\S\ref{sec:jac-jv}). It would be a crossover in a weak family:
$\FF_{(2^m)^4}$ is not a field deployed curves use.

\paragraph{Three per-trial probes, 12 November.} Three leads of the
11 November survey targeted the cost of one characteristic-two $k=4$ trial
(\art{research/leads_20261111/README.md}; local, unverified). The
chain unknown of the Frobenius-reduced system is fixed by the weight
vector; it already gives 12 chains, not the 16 the lead assumed, and the
only alternative variable gives 28 chains at $2.34\times$ the cost per
trial, so the lead is closed (\art{research/leads_20261111/probes/CHAIN_VARIABLE.md}).
The 21 carrying monomials have $\FF_2$-rank of differences 4 on 300 of
300 instances, so they lie in no index-2 coset, and that lead is closed.
The quadratic part has rank 2, not 3, on 300 of 300 targets, so the
constant-quadratic elimination is falsified as written; its rank-2 variant
is untested (\art{research/leads_20261111/runs/B_d4bin_probes/RESULT.md}).
None of the three moves the distances of Table~\ref{tab:pf-d4}.

\section{The Joux--Vitse cover, 11--12 November}\label{sec:ext-jv-nov}

Four studies extend the Joux--Vitse account of \S\ref{sec:jac-jv}. Two
change the cost of a solver arm; two charge or bound the cost of reaching
the weak class at all. Every number below is an unverified assertion
(\texttt{[pcc:unverified]} in each source); none has a pcc claim. All are
local runs on a 4-vCPU container, on the weak class of curves over
$\FF_{p^6}$ with full rational 2-torsion. The validated sieve row they
refer to is the pooled table-line arm U of the 3 November study:
$0.0141$ $[0.0122,0.0167]$ of matched negation rho in U2 at $w=0$, and
$0.0857$ and $0.0900$ at $w=0.1$ and $1$ (\art{research/jv_sieve_line_vs_rho_20261103/VALIDATION.json},
validated 11 November; leaderboard component
\texttt{jv\_sieve\_line\_vs\_rho\_component}).

\paragraph{The Hilbert stop on the Nagao arm.} The Hilbert early stop of F4
(\texttt{--stop hilbert:16}) had lowered counted $S$ by $1.848$--$1.875$ at
$p=53$--$503$, and the scale runs at $p\ge1301$ never used it
(\art{research/leads_20261111/README.md}, lead~1). A preregistered test ran
it on the nine line-search instances of \S\ref{sec:jac-jv}
($p\in\{1301,1451,1601\}$, seeds 1--3), with a drift control that
reproduced the frozen record exactly
(\art{research/jv_nagao_hilbert_20261112/README.md}). Relations, residuals
and the relation digest are identical on 9 of 9 instances. Counted $S$
falls to $0.5276$--$0.5280$ of the line-search arm; F4 multiplications
fall to $0.3894$--$0.3895$; the emitted native F4 counter falls to
$0.983$; about $0.08\%$ of systems fall back. All four preregistered
falsifiers pass. Re-scored with the unchanged tool against the pooled
matched negation rho (126 runs), the arm moves below rho in U2 at
$p=1451$ and $1601$ at every weight of the band
(Table~\ref{tab:ext-jv-hilbert}). At $p=1301$ it is level with rho at
$w=0$ ($1.003$ $[0.916,1.104]$), so the order does not hold across the
band there. The cover's native count omits its $\FF_p$ additions, so a
``below'' could narrow under complete counts. The source classifies the
change as engineering: the counted cost falls while the native counter
stays flat.

\paragraph{The same stop in the sieve's descent: killed.} The sieve route's
descent built its F4 options without the Hilbert rule; an opt-in patch
(\texttt{JV\_SIEVE\_HILBERT=16}) wired it in and ran 16 paired seeds at
$p=1511$, the plain arm reproducing the committed records exactly
(\art{research/jv_sieve_hilbert_descent_20261112/README.md}). Digests,
relations and solutions agree on 16 of 16 seeds. Descent F4
multiplications fall to $0.407$ and counted $S$ to $0.856$ (paired
geometric mean; per seed $0.709$--$0.969$); $S^+$ falls to $0.961$; 11
of 20{,}405 systems fall back. The descent's native F4 operations rise to
$1.139\times$, above the preregistered kill limit of $1.10\times$. In the
band, Hilbert over plain is $0.709$--$0.832$ at $w=0$, $0.927$--$0.947$ at
$w=0.1$ and $1.095$--$1.101$ at $w=1$ on the four seeds tabulated, so the
order flips inside the band. The lead is killed by its own falsifier; the
plain sieve row is unchanged. The source's reading: at $p=1511$ the
descent's native F4 work (about $6.7\cdot10^7$ operations) is large next
to its counted work (about $1.0\cdot10^7$ group-addition equivalents), so a
change that trades multiplications for checks ranks only if it wins at
$w=1$.

\paragraph{Emitting the sieve's unemitted additions.} The validated row
priced the cover's F4, descent and linear-algebra additions by an upper-bound
proxy. A preregistered run on seeds 17--32 emitted them
(\art{research/leads_20261111/runs/C_sieve_emit_price/RESULT.md}, lead~3).
Emitted over proxy is $0.816$ as a ratio of per-run means on seeds
17--24 ($0.815$ on all 16), $0.44$--$0.93$ per seed, geometric mean
$0.715$. The layer-A cells move by less than 3\%; the worst emitted cell is
$0.1196$ (U2, $w=1$), and U1 at $w=1$ is $0.1185$. Neither falsifier
triggered; the verdict is ``partial'', because the ratio prediction
$0.92$ $[0.80,1.05]$ holds only as a ratio of means. The proxy priced a
linear-algebra operation at 16 additions; the emitted count is one, and the
skeptic's note records that at 16 the ratio is about $0.99$. This is
accounting: the proxy was an upper bound, so the validated row needs no
erratum.

\paragraph{The walk into the weak class, charged.} The validated row
starts on a weak curve. A frozen protocol charged the isogeny walk from a
non-weak start $C$, built by $M\in\{8,32,128,512\}$ random isogeny moves
away from the weak curve, on the validated instances ($p\in\{1009,1511,1823\}$,
seeds 17--24; 96 runs, 0 failures)
(\art{research/jv_walk_charge_20261111/README.md}). With the walk charged,
the pooled arm-U ratio stays below rho at every weight
(Table~\ref{tab:ext-jv-walk}); the preregistered verdict ``walk charged,
still below rho'' held. The walk cost is heavy-tailed: medians stay at
0.1--4\% of the route, while the worst instance rises from $0.18\times$
the route at $M=8$ to $1.48\times$ at $M=512$ ($p=1009$;
$1.9\cdot10^9$ $\FF_p$ multiplications). As an arithmetic mean, a
statistic declared after the run, the walk adds 21\%, 1.6\% and 6.2\% at
$p=1009$, $1511$ and $1823$, and the charged mean is $0.039$, $0.012$ and
$0.0086$ of rho. The walk's hash-set probes are not emitted.

\paragraph{How often a curve reaches the class.} The walk-charge starts were
built by the walk's own moves, so the walk can always return. A second
frozen study asked how often a curve over $\FF_{p^6}$ lands in a weak class,
and how often the implemented walk reaches a weak curve from a random
admitted start (\art{research/jv_weak_coverage_20261111/README.md}; the
cubic norm-one branch only, two disclosed amendments).
Table~\ref{tab:ext-jv-coverage} gives the shares. About a quarter of
uniform curves pass the proved necessary condition
$t\equiv\pm(p^6+1)\pmod{16}$ at every size; the prediction of $1/8$
failed. At $p=37$--$67$ exact censuses find a weak curve in 98.8--99.6\%
of admitted classes; at $p\ge1009$ that share is unresolved, apart from
three classes reached at $p=1009$. The implemented walk reaches a weak curve
from about 40\% of admitted starts at $p=37$ and 53, from 3 of 60 at
$p=1009$ and from none of 60 at $p=1511$ and $1823$ (prediction $W<0.1$ at
$p\ge1009$ held). The odd jumps exist only where an $\ell$-torsion point is
rational over $\FF_{q^3}$, so from a random start they reach a small part
of the class. The three successful walks at $p=1009$ took 19--153 isogeny
steps and $1.8\cdot10^9$--$3.0\cdot10^{10}$ $\FF_p$ multiplications,
$0.008$--$0.14$ of matched rho and up to about $30\times$ the route. The
validated sieve row therefore holds for curves in the weak class; the
walk charge of Table~\ref{tab:ext-jv-walk} holds for starts within the
walk's reach, not for an arbitrary curve of the class. Reaching the class
from a typical curve needs odd isogenies with irrational kernel points,
which are not implemented.

\section{Other extension-field routes}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Joux--Vitse $(n-1)$-decomposition.} The
  $(n-1)$-decomposition~\cite{jouxvitse2012} at $n=5$ over $E(\FF_{p^5})$
  completed verified logarithms at $4.5\cdot10^7$--$1.8\cdot10^8\times$ rho,
  with a crossover extrapolated to groups near $2^{291}$
  (\art{research/joux_vitse_n1_20261015/}).
\item \emph{EcGFp5 and EcMasFp5.} These curves over $\FF_{p^5}$, and a
  short remainder on $\FF_{p^4}$, are recorded with the named targets in
  \S\ref{sec:pt-gfp5}.
\item \emph{Joux--Vitse cover.} The cover of $E(\FF_{p^6})$ is summarised in
  Chapter~\ref{sec:transfer}, with the full account in \S\ref{sec:jac-jv}
  and the November follow-ups in \S\ref{sec:ext-jv-nov}.
\end{itemize}
