\chapter{The distance to rho, family by family}\label{ch:distance}

This chapter gives, for each curve family, the current distance between
index calculus and a matched rho. Each figure carries its reference, its
boundary and its validation status. Here a ratio is IC over rho, so
greater than 1 means rho is cheaper. Chapter~\ref{sec:frontier} gives the
frontier campaign, the improvement ladders of 14--24 October, and the
three frontiers (cold, online, stage). This chapter adds the work of
25 October to 3 November and states the position as it stands at the end
of the programme. Under the proof-carrying-claims rule, every leaderboard
row named here has a \texttt{pcc} measurement claim, and every such claim
has standing \emph{unverified} (\texttt{pcc.py standing}, 6 November);
every figure without a claim id is a producer-reported value (an
unverified assertion), whatever its validation-gate status.

\section{Koblitz curves}

The leaderboard's Table~A measures a Koblitz descent: aimed $m=3$
collection, then an $m=2$ or $m=3$ descent. It is measured cold on one
unseen public point per process, with 64 points per size, at six sizes
from $2^{36.6}$ to $2^{47.2}$. Figure~\ref{fig:koblitz} shows the rule at
three baselines, and Table~\ref{tab:distance-koblitz} gives its ratios.
From v3 to v7 the rho stayed the same and index calculus became
$1.52$--$1.89\times$ faster; the board carries the v7 figures, validated
(\S\ref{sec:gate}). In v8, rho's signed-Frobenius canonicalisation, batched
16 lanes at a time in the same binary, raised the gap by
$1.30$--$1.58\times$ at every size. All six v8 values lie inside ranges
declared before the rows were taken, and the 408 rows of each run all pass
the \texttt{vs\_rho} check
(\art{research/ic_tool_program/rule/v8/README.md}). The v3$\to$v7
improvement lowered the constant, not the growth.

\paragraph{The priced campaign.} The closest Koblitz arm, compact
Frobenius orbits, is $3.8$--$15\times$ signed-Frobenius rho at $w=0$. Fully
priced against fully priced rho, its best cell is $1.566$ $[0.992,2.347]$,
and the ratio grows as $r^{0.165\,[0.117,0.213]}$. A later fully priced
component on the same kind of cell, the lazy orbit-member index, is closer:
$1.27$ $[0.77,1.80]$ over signed-Frobenius rho at $n=29$, $r=2^{15.4}$
(\art{research/ecbench_lazy_orbit_members_20261018/}; a component, not a
board row, not validated). No point estimate is below 1; at $r=2^{15.4}$
the intervals include 1.

\paragraph{$n=83$.} The cold pipeline uses the base K1107/root11, which
confirmed a saving of $1.195$\,s $[0.957,1.433]$ on a $115$\,s process (16
public points, GitHub Tier~1 host). The last cold ratio is the K1120
holdout's $55.4$, from five points: cold IC over the same-point plain-rho
online wall (rho has no set-up, so its online wall is its cold cost;
Chapter~\ref{sec:koblitz} admits no cold ratio on a stricter reading). A matched precomputed-rho
reproduction on 28 October gave the median online IC over rho of
Table~\ref{tab:distance-n83}, for three ways of matching rho's
precomputation. Cold, IC took $155.5$\,s against $36.96$\,s for plain rho over all 16
targets. In operation counts the order flips across the pricing band, so
no ranking is made.

\section{Prime curves}

\paragraph{Generic track.} The prime-field programme's owner decisions of
29 October split prime-field index calculus into two counted tracks
(\art{research/prime_ic_program_20261029/DECISIONS.md}):
\begin{itemize}
\item a \emph{generic} track, with table, pair-table and root-finding
  oracles, labelled ``bounded by $\sqrt r$; cannot beat rho'';
\item an \emph{algebraic} track, whose prime baseline is empty.
\end{itemize}
Table~\ref{tab:distance-prime} gives the distances on the generic track.
The campaign pair table's exponent against rho's is the $r^{2/3}$ against
$r^{1/2}$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:model}. The $k$-sum oracle
(\art{research/prime_ksum_table_20261028/}) is a negation-folded table of
every signed 4-sum of a smaller base. It lowers mean $S$ at $w=0$ by
$1.9\times$ at 20 bits, rising to $7.2\times$ at 40 bits ($83.7\to11.6$),
and its fitted exponent falls from $0.620$ $[0.586,0.650]$ to the
tabulated value, against the theory value $k/(2k-1)=4/7$. 2{,}016 runs
were verified and replayed; the source notes that the method is generic,
so its limit is baby-step giant-step. The new 20-bit baseline
(\art{research/prime_base_oracle_tuning_20261029/}) raises the factor base
from 32 to 45, which gives $0.818$ $[0.773,0.866]$ of the incumbent at
$w=0$; a second host (L2) replayed all 1{,}008 records identically and gave
$0.836$ $[0.806,0.866]$. The same rule at 24 and 28 bits was a
relabelling, and at 32 bits null.

\paragraph{Algebraic track.} The Kummer tower is $29$--$159\times$ rho at
$w=0$ (29.4 at 24 bits, \art{research/pkm_tower_ecbench_20261013/README.md};

\section{Binary curves of prime degree}

Table~B's random-binary rows are $10.6$--$38.2\times$ matched rho at
$\FF_{2^{15}}$--$\FF_{2^{27}}$, and they are validated. A cofactor-projected
base cuts three of them by $1.29$--$3.36\times$, leaving $6.1$--$14.4\times$.
That base is not yet a board row. For Certicom ECC2-131, the $m=3$ floor
with a free oracle is already above rho~(Chapter~\ref{sec:binary}).

Only in these families (Figure~\ref{fig:weak},
Table~\ref{tab:distance-weak}) does index calculus approach rho or go
below it. The genus-3 hyperelliptic row and the Joux--Vitse sieve row are
the programme's preregistered decisions of IC below the strongest rho, and
the sieve row is the only one validated
(Chapter~\ref{sec:transfer}, \S\ref{sec:jac-jv}). It has no pcc claim, so
its statement is an unverified assertion under the proof-carrying-claims
rule. The plane-quartic row uses double large
primes, structured elimination and Lanczos; its 501 IC units and 2{,}442
rho runs are all verified, and it is a measurement of a known result
(Diem, Diem--Thomé).

On every prime-order curve of cryptographic shape that the programme
measured (at the toy and medium sizes measured, $\le2^{47}$), rho is
cheaper. Index calculus goes below rho only in weak
extension-field families, which is where theory said it would. There the
programme's contribution is matched, priced, verified measurement of
known algorithms.
