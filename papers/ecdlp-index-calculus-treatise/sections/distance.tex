\chapter{The distance to rho, family by family}\label{ch:distance}

The distance between index calculus and a matched rho is given here family
by family, each with its reference, its boundary and its validation status.
A ratio is IC over rho: greater than 1 means rho is cheaper.
Chapter~\ref{sec:frontier} gives the frontier campaign, the improvement
ladders of 14--24 October and the three frontiers (cold, online, stage);
this chapter adds the work of 25 October to 12 November. Under the
proof-carrying-claims rule every leaderboard row named here has a
\texttt{pcc} measurement claim, and every such claim has standing
\emph{unverified} (\texttt{pcc.py standing}, 6 November). A figure without
a claim id is a producer-reported value (an unverified assertion), whatever
its validation-gate status.

\section{Koblitz curves}

The leaderboard's Table~A measures a Koblitz descent: aimed $m=3$
collection, then an $m=2$ or $m=3$ descent. It is measured cold on one
unseen public point per process, with 64 points per size, at six sizes
from $2^{36.6}$ to $2^{47.2}$. Figure~\ref{fig:koblitz} shows the rule at
three baselines, and Table~\ref{tab:distance-koblitz} gives its ratios.
From v3 to v7 the rho stayed the same and index calculus became
$1.52$--$1.89\times$ faster; the board carries the v7 figures, validated
(\S\ref{sec:gate}). In v8, rho's signed-Frobenius canonicalisation, batched
16 lanes at a time in the same binary, raised the gap by
$1.30$--$1.58\times$ at every size. All six v8 values lie inside ranges
declared before the rows were taken, and the 408 rows of each run all pass
the \texttt{vs\_rho} check
(\art{research/ic_tool_program/rule/v8/README.md}). The v3$\to$v7
improvement lowered the constant, not the growth.

\section{Prime curves}

\paragraph{Generic track.} The prime-field programme's owner decisions of
29 October split prime-field index calculus into two counted tracks
(\art{research/prime_ic_program_20261029/DECISIONS.md}):
\begin{itemize}
\item a \emph{generic} track, with table, pair-table and root-finding
  oracles, labelled ``bounded by $\sqrt r$; cannot beat rho'';
\item an \emph{algebraic} track, whose prime baseline is empty.
\end{itemize}
Table~\ref{tab:distance-prime} gives the distances on the generic track.
The campaign pair table's exponent against rho's is the $r^{2/3}$ against
$r^{1/2}$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:model}. The $k$-sum oracle
(\art{research/prime_ksum_table_20261028/}) is a negation-folded table of
every signed 4-sum of a smaller base. It lowers mean $S$ at $w=0$ by
$1.9\times$ at 20 bits, rising to $7.2\times$ at 40 bits ($83.7\to11.6$),
and its fitted exponent falls from $0.620$ $[0.586,0.650]$ to the
tabulated value, against the theory value $k/(2k-1)=4/7$. 2{,}016 runs
were verified and replayed; the source notes that the method is generic,
so its limit is baby-step giant-step. The new 20-bit baseline
(\art{research/prime_base_oracle_tuning_20261029/}) raises the factor base
from 32 to 45, which gives $0.818$ $[0.773,0.866]$ of the incumbent at
$w=0$; a second host (L2) replayed all 1{,}008 records identically and gave
$0.836$ $[0.806,0.866]$. The same rule at 24 and 28 bits was a
relabelling, and at 32 bits null.

\paragraph{Walk arms on prime-field miniatures, 8--12 November.} The
generic track's closest arms are walk tables over seed abscissae on
Curve25519-family and prime-field miniatures (\S\ref{sec:c25519}).
Table~\ref{tab:distance-walk} gives their distances as of 12 November.
The F$=1$ target-in-walk arm \texttt{twcj} has one unknown base logarithm;
its source states that it does no decomposition work and is not
decomposition-based index calculus. The $C=2$ two-seed arms
(\texttt{tw2cj}) count as index calculus by the owner's ruling of 4
November. Three changes moved them.
\begin{itemize}
\item Dropping the per-collision recomputation (\texttt{cv=0}) costs
  $0.879$ $[0.876,0.881]$ of the \texttt{twcj} arm at $k=20$ and $0.950$
  $[0.948,0.951]$ at $k=24$ ($w=0$, after the validator's erratum E1 was
  fixed and re-measured on 16 fresh sessions); on the two-seed walk the
  same change is $0.997$ and $0.999$, a negligible constant
  (\art{research/curve25519_engine_items_20261110/README.md};
  \art{research/curve25519_autolab_20261104/siblings/stage1-eks/README.md}).
\item Sibling points (each walk step stores a second point) cut the
  two-seed walk's counted cost to $0.45$--$0.51$ of \texttt{tw2cj-lazy} at
  $w=0$ on all three prime-field shapes, flat in $r$ from $2^{30}$ to
  $2^{40}$; at $w=1$ the ratio is $0.93$--$1.06$, because the second points,
  lifts and table inserts are native operations
  (\art{research/curve25519_autolab_20261104/siblings/stage3-scaling/README.md}).
\item On $j=0$ curves, orbit-canonical keys under the order-3 automorphism
  (\texttt{aut=3}) cut both two-seed walks to $0.56$--$0.61$ of their cost
  at $w=0$ and $0.84$--$0.90$ at $w=1$; at $w=0$ this is close to
  rho-cubic's $0.544$--$0.558$ of rho-neg
  (\art{research/curve25519_autolab_20261104/siblings/stage4b-aut3/README.md}).
\end{itemize}
Two rho choices matter. A chained jump set-up for rho-neg saved its set-up
as modelled but lengthened the walk, to $1.532\times$ $[1.402,1.673]$ and
$2.738\times$ $[2.583,2.903]$ rho-neg at $k=20$ and $24$; it was rejected
as a reference, so distances stay against \texttt{rho.negation}. On $j=0$
curves the matched rho is rho-cubic ($A=6$), the walk on the orbits of
the order-3 automorphism and negation.

The aut=3 sibling arm is the one walk arm at or below its matched rho at
$w=0$: $0.875$--$0.992$ of rho-cubic at 34--40 bits. It is $1.200$--$1.356$
at $w=0.1$ and $2.663$--$3.000$ at $w=1$, so the order does not hold
across the band; per curve, 10 of 15 are unresolved and on 5 of 15 rho-cubic
is lower at every weight. Stage~4 had reported $1.8$--$1.9$ at $w=1$; its
validator found the aut=3 canonicalisations unpriced on the IC arm while
rho-cubic's identical rotations were priced (erratum E1), and the
corrected values were $2.7$--$2.8$. Stage~4b re-ran the design on fresh
curves with the canonicalisations priced, and an independent validator
passed its GAE comparison (\art{research/curve25519_autolab_20261104/siblings/stage4b-aut3/VALIDATION.json},
\texttt{order\_holds\_across\_band: false}). It is a validation-gate result
without a pcc claim, so its statement is an unverified assertion; it is not
a leaderboard row. Its bit-op ratios are not covered (erratum N1). At L0
the IC arm takes 5.2--5.3 times rho-cubic's wall time. A design note
models the remaining gap: the two-seed walk needs three independent
collisions where rho needs one, so it stores about $1.95\times$ rho's steps
before the cost per point is counted, and at $w=1$ no change that stores a
point per operation can close it
(\art{research/curve25519_autolab_20261104/siblings/stage5-design/README.md};
desk note, unverified).

On every prime-order curve of cryptographic shape that the programme
measured (at the toy and medium sizes measured, $\le2^{47}$), no
index-calculus arm is below its matched rho across the pricing band. At
$w=0$ alone the sibling walks are $0.78$--$0.927$ of rho-neg, and with
\texttt{aut=3} $0.875$--$0.992$ of rho-cubic on $j=0$ curves of 34--40
bits; at $w=1$ rho is cheaper in every case (Table~\ref{tab:distance-walk}). Index calculus goes below rho only in weak
extension-field families, which is where theory said it would. There the
programme's contribution is matched, priced, verified measurement of
known algorithms.
