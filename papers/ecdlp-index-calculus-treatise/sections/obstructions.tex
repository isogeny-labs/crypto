\chapter{Obstructions: what moving the problem cannot buy}\label{ch:obstructions}

The programme kept 153 leads against ECDLP in a lead atlas at its
30~October review
(\art{crypto-autoresearcher:knowledge/leads/ecdlp/REVIEW_STATE.json}).
Of these:
\begin{itemize}
\item 74 are closed with a named obstruction or exhausted at scale;
\item 53 are open with a decided next step;
\item 17 are blocked;
\item 9 are unexamined.
\end{itemize}
Later rounds, including the split of 4~November, added more
(Chapter~\ref{ch:unconvmap}).
Most obstructions are textbook results applied to the case at hand: the
Shoup--Nechaev generic bound, the Corrigan-Gibbs--Kogan preprocessing bound
$ST^2\gtrsim N$~\cite{cgk2018}, isogeny invariance of the group order, and
the Balasubramanian--Koblitz embedding-degree bound. This chapter records
the few that the programme \emph{proved} itself and that constrain index
calculus specifically. Each states a lever that cannot work, and why.

\section{Transport conservation}\label{sec:obs-transport}

A homomorphic transfer $\varphi:E\to E'$ with rational kernel $\kappa$
changes the number of ordered $m$-decompositions of a target by exactly
$\abs{\kappa}^{m-1}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:conservation} in
Chapter~\ref{sec:transfer}). So an isogeny buys only three things:
\begin{itemize}
\item the kernel symmetry, which is trivial on prime-order curves;
\item the factor-base geometry available on the destination curve;
\item the cost of arithmetic there.
\end{itemize}
Chains of isogenies compose to one homomorphism. A loop is an endomorphism
acting as a known scalar. Multi-curve factor bases glued by isogenies are
single-curve bases in disguise. Measured across sizes at fixed cofactor,
fibre-aware index calculus loses ground to rho as $\#E^{1/6}$; the full
measurements are in Chapter~\ref{sec:transfer}.

\section{The summation cover is the same everywhere}

Chapter~\ref{ch:cover} proved:
\begin{itemize}
\item the monodromy of the summation cover is $(\ZZ/2)^{m-2}$ for every
  curve;
\item its fibres split completely on the factor-base locus;
\item mean yield at fixed base size is a double count.
\end{itemize}
Together these close three families of leads: curve selection by
anomalous monodromy, fibre censuses as a relation-rate probe, and
factor-base geometries claimed to raise yield.

\section{Oracle-augmented index calculus: label-valued oracles stay generic}

Several leads proposed giving index calculus an extra oracle on the group.
Since the split of 4~November the programme separates two classes.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Label-valued oracles} are Shoup-simulable with overhead
  $\Theta(\log N)$ or $O(m)$: the endomorphism image (by an eigenvalue
  argument; the premise $\operatorname{End}_{\FF_p}(E)=\ZZ$ of the earlier
  derivation is false), label-valued halving, an incidence check over a
  label factor base, and a label-valued net. They stay closed at the
  $\sqrt r$ floor (\texttt{LEAD-EXO-augmented-oracle-ggm}; derivation
  tier, not yet ratified).
\item \emph{Coordinate-revealing oracles} ($x$, $y$, the full point,
  $x$-valued halving, field-valued jet and net, the threshold oracle
  $C_t$) are not simulable, and the generic group model gives no bound for
  them (\texttt{KN-FIND-194294}, \texttt{KN-FIND-982fdf};
  \texttt{LEAD-EXO-coordinate-revealing-oracles}, open;
  \S\ref{sec:um-oracles}). The earlier jet verdict is withdrawn.
\end{itemize}

Table-based decomposition obeys the
preprocessing bound $ST^2\gtrsim N$. Balanced generic index calculus costs
about $r^{2/3}/n^{4/3}$, consistent with the measured growth of the Koblitz
gap to rho as $r^{0.165\,[0.117,0.213]}$
(\texttt{LEAD-ICB-generic-table-oracle}). These are known instances of
generic-group reasoning~\cite{cgk2018}; they are recorded because each
retired or scoped a concrete proposal.

\section{Lifting sections does not produce relations}

Silverman's xedni calculus lifts points to a curve over a global field, in
the hope that rank forces relations. On a frozen family of rational
elliptic surfaces (with $\deg b=6$ and monic quadratic $x$), the programme
derived the exact lift probability
\[
  P_{\rm lift}(p)=\frac{1-2/p+p^{-3}}{2p^3},
\]
so the exponent is $\alpha=3$, far above the $\alpha<1/2$ that a relation
source would need. This was checked against $596{,}268{,}874$ enumerated
slots with no disagreement, and a parameter count reproduces $\alpha=3$
(\texttt{LEAD-ICP-xedni-function-field-sections}, \texttt{EV-XEDN-001}).
The scope is one family ($\deg b\le10$, $p\le31$). A dimension-count theorem
over all section shapes would be needed to close the route in general.

The classic obstruction to xedni is also conditional. A kernel-checked Lean
audit of Jacobson, Koblitz, Silverman, Stein and Teske's analysis
(\art{research/jksst_xedni_audit_20261101/}) does three things:
\begin{itemize}
\item it repairs their conditional theorem;
\item it refutes several overbroad readings of it;
\item it leaves open the premises it rests on: Lang's height conjecture, a
  uniform height--discriminant comparison, and the algorithm-specific
  bridge from a one-shot relation probability to the actual attack.
\end{itemize}
The audit calls the result a strong conditional asymptotic obstruction for
classic xedni, not a proof that the route is closed.

\section{Twist points carry no relations}

A curve and its quadratic twist share $x$-coordinates, and therefore share
summation polynomials. Twist points supply no relations for the curve:
Frobenius acts as $+1$ on $E(\FF_q)$ and as $-1$ on the
embedded twist, so the twist part $B$ of any sum satisfies $2B=0$.

The study \art{research/twist_obstruction_20261026/} checked this
exactly on toy binary curves in the ECC2K-130 model (odd $n$, twist
$a\mapsto a+1$):
\begin{itemize}
\item 0 of 356{,}530{,}036 mixed sums are rational;
\item 0 violations on 4{,}954 decomposition targets at $m=2,3$;
\item the off-parity solution sets equal the predicted ones on 1{,}154 of
  1{,}154 targets;
\item positive controls found all 18{,}042 and 95 planted solutions.
\end{itemize}
A heuristic that ignored the obstruction predicted that 74--89\% of
solutions would contain a twist-lifting coordinate; 0\% did at $m=2$.

A follow-up asked whether a factor-base subspace can \emph{avoid} the twist
altogether (\art{research/lift_dense_factor_base_20261027/}; 11 Lean
theorems, standard axioms only).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Composite degree $N=dn$, $n\ge3$: yes.} On suitable
  $\FF_{2^d}$-lines the lift condition collapses to the constant
  $\operatorname{Tr}(a)$. When $\operatorname{Tr}(a)=0$ every abscissa
  lifts, giving $2^n$ relations per Gaudry--Diem trial. This applies to
  c2pnb208w1 and c2pnb368w1, but the modelled gain is $+0.50$ bits, a
  model output, not a measurement.
\item \emph{Prime degree (ECC2K-130): this construction does not exist.}
  A prime-degree field has no proper intermediate subfield, so there are no
  $\FF_{2^d}$-lines to use. This does not rule out other twist-avoiding
  subspaces. At the small prime degrees tested, all-lift subspaces of
  dimension 4--5 exist, though fewer than for density-paired random sets
  (exact all-lift 4-subspace counts $0.921\pm0.018$ of the null at $N=11$,
  permutation $p=2\cdot10^{-5}$). This is toy evidence. Whether useful
  twist-avoiding subspaces exist at cryptographic prime degree is
  unresolved.
\end{itemize}

\section{A cover's relations are collisions on the curve}\label{sec:obs-pushforward}

For a cover $c:C\to E$ over $\FF_p$, $J_C\sim E\times P$ with $P$ the Prym and
$c_*c^*=[\deg c]$. Pulling back preserves the logarithm on the order-$r$
subgroup when $r\nmid\deg c$, but a shortcut must say how it computes
logarithms on the embedded elliptic subgroup. Two studies of 11--12
November made this a measurement for bielliptic covers
$C:y^2=F(x^2)$ with $\varphi_1(x,y)=(x^2,y)$ and the complementary map
$\varphi_2$; a third repeated it for a degree-3 cover, and a proof note
states the obstruction once, with its hypotheses. Every measured value is
\texttt{[pcc:unverified]} at the source.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Across to the complement.} The complement $E_2$ has a
  different order, so $\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_2)=0$ unless $\#E_2=\#E$
  (Tate), and the composite $\varphi_{2*}\varphi_1^*$ is identically zero,
  forced by the deck involution $\sigma:(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)$ with
  $\varphi_2\circ\sigma=-\varphi_2$. A sweep of 5{,}641 covers over
  $p\in\{1009,2003,4001,8009\}$ reached up to 141 distinct complement orders
  and 3{,}479 complements smoother than the target; a census of 300 covers
  per prime found $\varphi_{2*}\varphi_1^*=0$ in both directions on every
  sampled generic fibre (14{,}371--14{,}397 of 14{,}400 per prime), with the
  control $\varphi_{1*}\varphi_1^*=[2]$ holding on all
  (\art{research/chained_prime_transfer_20261111/ATLAS.md}). The counter of
  complements both reachable and easier is 0 by the theorem, not by
  observation.
\item \emph{Pushed forward to $E$.} The reduced class of $[\alpha]G+[\beta]T$
  in $J(C)$ is the fibre $P+\iota(P)$ of $Q=[\alpha]G_E+[\beta]T_E$, which
  splits into rational places exactly when $X(Q)$ is a nonzero square, and
  both places map to $Q$. A genus-2 relation therefore pushes forward to
  $[2]Q=Q+Q$, one factor-base element, not a two-term decomposition
  (\art{research/cover_pushforward_20261112/PUSHFORWARD.md}). At 7 primes
  from 211 to 12{,}841, all 37{,}866 relations of the primary run (189{,}330
  in a 15-seed run) were of this fibre type and valid on $E_1$ and $E_2$,
  none of the pushed $E_2$ rows involved the target, and the base
  $B=\varphi_1(C(\FF_p))$ held $0.4707$--$0.5367$ of $\#E_1$. The matrix is a
  collision table on $\pm Q$, with exactly twice the columns of a native
  $E_1$ arm that uses the same random stream; the two arms have identical
  group-operation counts at every seed.
\end{itemize}
Table~\ref{tab:obs-pushforward} gives the costs. Index calculus on $J(C)$,
the same search on $E_1$, and a generic two-term index calculus on $E_1$ over
$B\cap\langle G_E\rangle$ are above the matched rho on $E_1$ at every prime and
all three prices. The two-term arm was below rho on 1 of 15 seeds at
$p=821$ (472 against 475). Stopped at the first repeated $X(Q)$, the $J(C)$
search is a birthday collision on $\langle G_E\rangle$ that discards about
half its trials; a control on $E_1$ without the square-abscissa filter is
never worse. Both are below the median rho on 13 to 15 of 15 seeds at the
three smallest primes, where rho's fixed set-up dominates, and the cover arm
is above it on 13 of 15 seeds at $p=6421$ and 12 of 15 at $p=12{,}841$.
That ``below'' does not survive a stronger reference. Against a negation-map
rho run in the same process, the three first-collision arms, recorded below
the plain rho at $p=211$ to 3{,}203 (15 rows, ratios $0.17$--$0.85$), are
above it, or unresolved lower bounds where they emit no native counter (the
birthday control at $p=211$ to 1{,}619 and all three at $p=211$); the recorded
``below'' was the plain rho's start-up, not a property of the cover
(\art{research/negation_remeasure_20261113/RESULTS.md}; validated,
\S\ref{sec:jac-chain}). The full-stop $J(C)$ arm at $p=12{,}841$ moves from
394, 4{,}720 and $43{,}658\times$ to 2{,}473, 20{,}667 and $51{,}693\times$ at 0,
$0.1$ and 1~GAE. To help, a cover would have to supply a target-side
factor base well below $r$ (about $r^{1/2}$ times a polylogarithm) on which
relations with independent terms are found at a constant rate; here the
base is half the group and the two terms coincide. A smaller Prym does not
enter: $\varphi_{1*}$ kills it, and nothing computed on it sees the target.
The scope is degree-2 bielliptic covers, one curve per prime, weight-2
relations and $p\le12{,}841$.

\paragraph{Degree 3.} A frozen protocol repeated the measurement for the
genus-4 cover $C:y^2=c(h(x))$, $h(x)=x^3+h_1x+h_0$, of $E:Y^2=c(s)$ by
$(x,y)\mapsto(h(x),y)$, at the eight primes 53 to 251, three instances per
prime and 15 stream seeds per instance, with $r$ from 11 to 83
(\art{research/cover_pushforward_deg3_20261112/PUSHFORWARD_DEG3.md};
Table~\ref{tab:obs-deg3}). The pull-back homomorphism was checked for every
$k\in1..r$ in each instance (854 checks) before any relation was collected.
All nine frozen predictions held:
\begin{itemize}
\item 28{,}815 of 28{,}815 collected $J(C)$ relations are a single full fibre
  $c^*(Q)$, including 1{,}317 ramified fibres $2P+P'$;
\item in 159{,}508 of 159{,}508 trials the reduced class of $[\alpha]D+[\beta]T$,
  computed by Cantor arithmetic, equals the Mumford pair $(h(x)-s,\,Y)$ of
  $Q=[\alpha]G_E+[\beta]T_E$ computed on $E$ alone;
\item the index calculus on $J(C)$ and the collision search on $E$ accept
  identical trials, cost identical group operations and recover the same
  log in 360 of 360 seed-cells;
\item every pushed relation equals $[3]Q$, the degenerate $Q+Q+Q$.
\end{itemize}
A trial is accepted when the fibre $h(x)=s$ splits completely, pooled
$0.1861$ of non-identity trials against $1/6$ (the admission rule required
two subgroup abscissae in the split set, which raises it). The full-stop
$J(C)$ arm was below the matched plain rho on 0 of 360 seeds. Its first
collision is below the same seed's rho on 291 of 360, but a birthday control
with no cover and no filter on the same stream is below on 359 of 360 and at
or below the cover arm on every seed: the cover only discards about 5 of 6
trials. Against a negation-map rho (354 of 360 seed-cells recovered; 6 hit
the step cap and are excluded) the full-stop gap is $11.2$--$268.7$ per cell at
$w=0$. The run's recorded command exited 1 because of those 6 step-cap
failures; every arm under test certified in 360 of 360. The construction is
limited to $p<256$ and one cover shape, with a tiny subgroup.

\paragraph{The obstruction, stated once.} A proof note of 12~November
gives an argument or a citation for each step and ties each to the code or
measurement that checked it
(\art{research/chained_transfer_proof_20261112/PROOF.md}). For a degree-$d$
cover $c:C\to E$ over $\FF_p$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $c_*c^*=[d]$; $J_C\sim E\times P$ with $\#J_C(\FF_p)=\#E(\FF_p)\cdot\#P(\FF_p)$
  and $\#P(\FF_p)=p^{g-1}(1+O_g(p^{-1/2}))$; and $c^*$ is injective on $E[r]$
  for $r\nmid d$, so the order-$r$ logarithm transfers (T1--T4);
\item fibre rigidity (T5): if $C$ is hyperelliptic with a rational
  Weierstrass point at infinity, $d\le g$ and $c^*(O)\sim d\infty_C$, then for
  every $Q$ outside an explicit finite set the reduced representative of
  $c^*[Q-O]$ is the fibre $c^*Q$, so every relation of weight at most $g$ in
  that class is the fibre; T6 extends this to non-hyperelliptic $C$ with
  $g>d^2-d+1$ and $c$ indecomposable, by Castelnuovo--Severi;
\item every dependency among fibre relations is a collision $Q_i=\pm Q_j$ on
  $E$ (T7), and $\varphi_{2*}\varphi_1^*=0$ for the two quotients of a
  bielliptic curve (T8);
\item isogenies, pairings and Weil restriction keep the characteristic, and
  isogenies keep $\#E$ (T9, cited).
\end{itemize}
The note narrows two earlier statements. The atlas's ``a DLP-preserving
homomorphism $E\to E'$ exists iff $\#E=\#E'$'' holds only in the ``only if''
direction: Tate gives an isogeny when the orders agree, but not necessarily
one of degree prime to $r$ (\art{research/chained_prime_transfer_20261111/ATLAS.md},
corrected). ``Index calculus on the transferred subgroup is a collision
search'' is a statement about relations in $c^*(E)$, not about index
calculus on all of $J_C$. Three cases stay open: a cross-characteristic map
(O1), lifting to characteristic~0 beyond a conditional $C_0/p$ bound (O2),
and index calculus on the whole $J_C(\FF_p)$ (O3). O3 has since been measured
on the transferred instance and remains open under its frozen rule
(\S\ref{sec:jac-chain}).

\section{What the obstructions leave open}\label{sec:obs-open}

None of the obstructions above concerns the cost of \emph{solving} the
decomposition system, nor the linear algebra. The cover obstruction leaves
the three cases O1--O3 of \S\ref{sec:obs-pushforward} open, and a note of
15 November names three more.

\paragraph{O4--O6 and the O4 protocol.} The note labels as O4--O6 the
hypotheses under which T5--T7 are silent; the labels are the note's, not
the proof note's (\art{research/chained_transfer_next_20261115/NOTE.md}).
O4 is a non-hyperelliptic cover with $g\le d^2-d+1$, the smallest being
$g=3$, $d=2$. O5 is a hyperelliptic cover outside T5's hypotheses ($d>g$,
$c^*(O)\not\sim d\infty_C$, relations of weight above $g$, or $Q$ in the
excluded set); every cover measured so far has $d\le g$. O6 is the toy
size of the measurements ($p\le12{,}841$ at degree 2, $p\le251$ at
degree 3). The note chose O4 for the next measurement: the bielliptic
plane quartic $C:v^4=x^3+ax+b$ over $E:y^2=x^3+ax+b$ by
$(x,v)\mapsto(x,v^2)$, genus 3, degree 2, whose line sections have
weight 4 and class $K$ and are not fibres. Its existing code recovers
logarithms only up to $p=257$ and has no rho comparison. The frozen
protocol runs arm Q (line relations), a control D with the same columns
and rows found by elliptic additions, and the matched negation rho on
$E$, priced at 0, 0.1 and 1 GAE and at calibrated weights
(\art{research/chained_transfer_next_20261115/PROTOCOL.md}).

A red-team review found the frozen rule unreadable before any code was
written (\art{research/chained_transfer_next_20261115/REDTEAM.md}). Arm Q
is nearly free at 0 GAE, because its work is native, so its order against
rho flips inside the band, and the named classifier would class every cell
unresolved (R1). The factor base has $(r-1)/2$ columns at $p\equiv3\pmod
4$ and about $p/4$ at $p\equiv1\pmod 4$, and the frozen prime ladder mixed
both residues (R2). The copied section arithmetic overflows \texttt{u64}
at $p\ge41{,}874$ (R6). Amendment~1 adopted all 17 findings before any
code or data. Its ladder is $229$, 421, 821, 1621, 3209, 6421, 12{,}841
(stage 1) and 25{,}601, 51{,}217, 102{,}409 (stage 2), all $\equiv1\pmod
4$, with $n/p$ between $0.2347$ and $0.2874$ on stage 1. Its first
selection pass picked an anomalous curve at $p=229$ ($r=\#E=229$), which
is why the amendment adds $r\ne p$ and embedding degree above 6 as checks;
the source discloses this as a selection rule changed after seeing
selection output, with no arm cost seen. The counted code is written and
its 16 unit tests pass, including the $p=53$ known answer (45 points, 79
sections, 11 columns, scalar 17)
(\art{research/chained_transfer_next_20261115/PROGRESS.md}). The run is
not done: it waits on the checkpoint gate and an authorised route. The
protocol's own prior for the verdict ``O4 closed by data'' is $0.75$. O4
is frozen, not yet run; every number is \texttt{[pcc:unverified]}.

\paragraph{O1: torsion bridges across characteristics.} Two lines of
12--14 November built explicit cross-characteristic torsion maps and the
necessary bounds on them
(\art{research/lift_reduce_second_prime_20261112/CT38_CROSS_CHAR_TORSION.md},
\art{research/cross_characteristic_global_torsion_20261112/README.md},
sub-studies CT51--CT94). Every statement is \texttt{[pcc:unverified]}, and
the sources call them classical results applied to toy instances.
\begin{itemize}
\item Positive controls. The rational 5-torsion of Cremona 11a1 reduces
  isomorphically onto $E(\FF_3)$ and $E(\FF_2)$, both of order 5, which
  gives a logarithm-preserving map $E(\FF_3)\to E(\FF_2)$ by a five-entry
  table; CT38 does the same on $y^2-y=x^3-x^2$ between $\FF_{101}$ and
  $\FF_{103}$. Applying such a map to an arbitrary point needs its global
  preimage, which enumeration finds in $O(N)$, above rho's
  $\tilde O(\sqrt N)$. CT69 gives a degree-8 number field in which one
  3-torsion point of 11a1 reduces to $(18,5)\in E(\FF_{29})$ and to a point
  of $E(\FF_{2^8})$.
\item Local lifting without the logarithm. Hensel lifting carries an
  $\ell$-torsion point to $E(\ZZ/p^N\ZZ)$ from its coordinates alone, so
  $Q=[x]P$ survives without $x$ (CT74, at $p=29$, six precisions); lifting
  only the curve equation gives an integral point that is not 3-torsion
  for $N\ge2$. CT79 chains this to a binary residue for one fixed 3-torsion
  point, but its candidate list is equivalent to enumerating the
  logarithms, so it supports no complexity statement.
\item Necessary bounds. Good reduction of a global $N$-torsion point into
  a $g$-dimensional abelian variety over $\FF_{q^f}$ forces
  $N\le(q^{f/2}+1)^{2g}$ (Table~\ref{tab:obs-crosschar}). Serre's bound
  gives $\ell\le B(f)^g$; over $\mathbb{Q}$ a rational point of order
  $2^{127}-1$ on a variety with good reduction at 2 needs $g\ge55$, and of
  order $2^{255}-19$ needs $g\ge110$ (CT56). A full $\ell$-torsion basis
  needs a field of degree at least $\ell-1$, however the global variety is
  chosen (CT58). On the binary fibre of 11a1 no nonzero
  $\FF_{2^d}$-equivariant map of 7-torsion exists for $d<24$ (CT94).
\end{itemize}
The bounds are logarithmic in $N$ at fixed $g$ and are not complexity
lower bounds. No study constructs a transfer of arbitrary encoded points
with $N^{o(1)}$ lifting cost, which the sources name as the gate before
any solver is tried. O1 stays open, with no measured arm and no distance
to rho.
