\chapter{Obstructions: what moving the problem cannot buy}\label{ch:obstructions}

The programme kept 153 leads against ECDLP in a lead atlas at its
30~October review
(\art{crypto-autoresearcher:knowledge/leads/ecdlp/REVIEW_STATE.json}).
Of these:
\begin{itemize}
\item 74 are closed with a named obstruction or exhausted at scale;
\item 53 are open with a decided next step;
\item 17 are blocked;
\item 9 are unexamined.
\end{itemize}
Later rounds, including the split of 4~November, added more
(Chapter~\ref{ch:unconvmap}).
Most obstructions are textbook results applied to the case at hand: the
Shoup--Nechaev generic bound, the Corrigan-Gibbs--Kogan preprocessing bound
$ST^2\gtrsim N$~\cite{cgk2018}, isogeny invariance of the group order, and
the Balasubramanian--Koblitz embedding-degree bound. This chapter records
the few that the programme \emph{proved} itself and that constrain index
calculus specifically. Each states a lever that cannot work, and why.

\section{Transport conservation}\label{sec:obs-transport}

A homomorphic transfer $\varphi:E\to E'$ with rational kernel $\kappa$
changes the number of ordered $m$-decompositions of a target by exactly
$\abs{\kappa}^{m-1}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:conservation} in
Chapter~\ref{sec:transfer}). So an isogeny buys only three things:
\begin{itemize}
\item the kernel symmetry, which is trivial on prime-order curves;
\item the factor-base geometry available on the destination curve;
\item the cost of arithmetic there.
\end{itemize}
Chains of isogenies compose to one homomorphism. A loop is an endomorphism
acting as a known scalar. Multi-curve factor bases glued by isogenies are
single-curve bases in disguise. Measured across sizes at fixed cofactor,
fibre-aware index calculus loses ground to rho as $\#E^{1/6}$; the full
measurements are in Chapter~\ref{sec:transfer}.

\section{The summation cover is the same everywhere}

Chapter~\ref{ch:cover} proved:
\begin{itemize}
\item the monodromy of the summation cover is $(\ZZ/2)^{m-2}$ for every
  curve;
\item its fibres split completely on the factor-base locus;
\item mean yield at fixed base size is a double count.
\end{itemize}
Together these close three families of leads: curve selection by
anomalous monodromy, fibre censuses as a relation-rate probe, and
factor-base geometries claimed to raise yield.

\section{Oracle-augmented index calculus: label-valued oracles stay generic}

Several leads proposed giving index calculus an extra oracle on the group.
Since the split of 4~November the programme separates two classes.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Label-valued oracles} are Shoup-simulable with overhead
  $\Theta(\log N)$ or $O(m)$: the endomorphism image (by an eigenvalue
  argument; the premise $\operatorname{End}_{\FF_p}(E)=\ZZ$ of the earlier
  derivation is false), label-valued halving, an incidence check over a
  label factor base, and a label-valued net. They stay closed at the
  $\sqrt r$ floor (\texttt{LEAD-EXO-augmented-oracle-ggm}; derivation
  tier, not yet ratified).
\item \emph{Coordinate-revealing oracles} ($x$, $y$, the full point,
  $x$-valued halving, field-valued jet and net, the threshold oracle
  $C_t$) are not simulable, and the generic group model gives no bound for
  them (\texttt{KN-FIND-194294}, \texttt{KN-FIND-982fdf};
  \texttt{LEAD-EXO-coordinate-revealing-oracles}, open;
  \S\ref{sec:um-oracles}). The earlier jet verdict is withdrawn.
\end{itemize}

Table-based decomposition obeys the
preprocessing bound $ST^2\gtrsim N$. Balanced generic index calculus costs
about $r^{2/3}/n^{4/3}$, consistent with the measured growth of the Koblitz
gap to rho as $r^{0.165\,[0.117,0.213]}$
(\texttt{LEAD-ICB-generic-table-oracle}). These are known instances of
generic-group reasoning~\cite{cgk2018}; they are recorded because each
retired or scoped a concrete proposal.

\section{Lifting sections does not produce relations}

Silverman's xedni calculus lifts points to a curve over a global field, in
the hope that rank forces relations. On a frozen family of rational
elliptic surfaces (with $\deg b=6$ and monic quadratic $x$), the programme
derived the exact lift probability
\[
  P_{\rm lift}(p)=\frac{1-2/p+p^{-3}}{2p^3},
\]
so the exponent is $\alpha=3$, far above the $\alpha<1/2$ that a relation
source would need. This was checked against $596{,}268{,}874$ enumerated
slots with no disagreement, and a parameter count reproduces $\alpha=3$
(\texttt{LEAD-ICP-xedni-function-field-sections}, \texttt{EV-XEDN-001}).
The scope is one family ($\deg b\le10$, $p\le31$). A dimension-count theorem
over all section shapes would be needed to close the route in general.

The classic obstruction to xedni is also conditional. A kernel-checked Lean
audit of Jacobson, Koblitz, Silverman, Stein and Teske's analysis
(\art{research/jksst_xedni_audit_20261101/}) does three things:
\begin{itemize}
\item it repairs their conditional theorem;
\item it refutes several overbroad readings of it;
\item it leaves open the premises it rests on: Lang's height conjecture, a
  uniform height--discriminant comparison, and the algorithm-specific
  bridge from a one-shot relation probability to the actual attack.
\end{itemize}
The audit calls the result a strong conditional asymptotic obstruction for
classic xedni, not a proof that the route is closed.

\section{Twist points carry no relations}

A curve and its quadratic twist share $x$-coordinates, and therefore share
summation polynomials. Twist points supply no relations for the curve:
Frobenius acts as $+1$ on $E(\FF_q)$ and as $-1$ on the
embedded twist, so the twist part $B$ of any sum satisfies $2B=0$.

The study \art{research/twist_obstruction_20261026/} checked this
exactly on toy binary curves in the ECC2K-130 model (odd $n$, twist
$a\mapsto a+1$):
\begin{itemize}
\item 0 of 356{,}530{,}036 mixed sums are rational;
\item 0 violations on 4{,}954 decomposition targets at $m=2,3$;
\item the off-parity solution sets equal the predicted ones on 1{,}154 of
  1{,}154 targets;
\item positive controls found all 18{,}042 and 95 planted solutions.
\end{itemize}
A heuristic that ignored the obstruction predicted that 74--89\% of
solutions would contain a twist-lifting coordinate; 0\% did at $m=2$.

A follow-up asked whether a factor-base subspace can \emph{avoid} the twist
altogether (\art{research/lift_dense_factor_base_20261027/}; 11 Lean
theorems, standard axioms only).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Composite degree $N=dn$, $n\ge3$: yes.} On suitable
  $\FF_{2^d}$-lines the lift condition collapses to the constant
  $\operatorname{Tr}(a)$. When $\operatorname{Tr}(a)=0$ every abscissa
  lifts, giving $2^n$ relations per Gaudry--Diem trial. This applies to
  c2pnb208w1 and c2pnb368w1, but the modelled gain is $+0.50$ bits, a
  model output, not a measurement.
\item \emph{Prime degree (ECC2K-130): this construction does not exist.}
  A prime-degree field has no proper intermediate subfield, so there are no
  $\FF_{2^d}$-lines to use. This does not rule out other twist-avoiding
  subspaces. At the small prime degrees tested, all-lift subspaces of
  dimension 4--5 exist, though fewer than for density-paired random sets
  (exact all-lift 4-subspace counts $0.921\pm0.018$ of the null at $N=11$,
  permutation $p=2\cdot10^{-5}$). This is toy evidence. Whether useful
  twist-avoiding subspaces exist at cryptographic prime degree is
  unresolved.
\end{itemize}

\section{A cover's relations are collisions on the curve}\label{sec:obs-pushforward}

For a cover $c:C\to E$ over $\FF_p$, $J_C\sim E\times P$ with $P$ the Prym and
$c_*c^*=[\deg c]$. Pulling back preserves the logarithm on the order-$r$
subgroup when $r\nmid\deg c$, but a shortcut must say how it computes
logarithms on the embedded elliptic subgroup. Two studies of 11--12
November made this a measurement for bielliptic covers
$C:y^2=F(x^2)$ with $\varphi_1(x,y)=(x^2,y)$ and the complementary map
$\varphi_2$. Every value is \texttt{[pcc:unverified]} at the source.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Across to the complement.} The complement $E_2$ has a
  different order, so $\operatorname{Hom}(E,E_2)=0$ unless $\#E_2=\#E$
  (Tate), and the composite $\varphi_{2*}\varphi_1^*$ is identically zero,
  forced by the deck involution $\sigma:(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)$ with
  $\varphi_2\circ\sigma=-\varphi_2$. A sweep of 5{,}641 covers over
  $p\in\{1009,2003,4001,8009\}$ reached up to 141 distinct complement orders
  and 3{,}479 complements smoother than the target; a census of 300 covers
  per prime found $\varphi_{2*}\varphi_1^*=0$ in both directions on every
  sampled generic fibre (14{,}371--14{,}397 of 14{,}400 per prime), with the
  control $\varphi_{1*}\varphi_1^*=[2]$ holding on all
  (\art{research/chained_prime_transfer_20261111/ATLAS.md}). The counter of
  complements both reachable and easier is 0 by the theorem, not by
  observation.
\item \emph{Pushed forward to $E$.} The reduced class of $[\alpha]G+[\beta]T$
  in $J(C)$ is the fibre $P+\iota(P)$ of $Q=[\alpha]G_E+[\beta]T_E$, which
  splits into rational places exactly when $X(Q)$ is a nonzero square, and
  both places map to $Q$. A genus-2 relation therefore pushes forward to
  $[2]Q=Q+Q$, one factor-base element, not a two-term decomposition
  (\art{research/cover_pushforward_20261112/PUSHFORWARD.md}). At 7 primes
  from 211 to 12{,}841, all 37{,}866 relations of the primary run (189{,}330
  in a 15-seed run) were of this fibre type and valid on $E_1$ and $E_2$,
  none of the pushed $E_2$ rows involved the target, and the base
  $B=\varphi_1(C(\FF_p))$ held $0.4707$--$0.5367$ of $\#E_1$. The matrix is a
  collision table on $\pm Q$, with exactly twice the columns of a native
  $E_1$ arm that uses the same random stream; the two arms have identical
  group-operation counts at every seed.
\end{itemize}
Table~\ref{tab:obs-pushforward} gives the costs. Index calculus on $J(C)$,
the same search on $E_1$, and a generic two-term index calculus on $E_1$ over
$B\cap\langle G_E\rangle$ are above the matched rho on $E_1$ at every prime and
all three prices. The two-term arm was below rho on 1 of 15 seeds at
$p=821$ (472 against 475). Stopped at the first repeated $X(Q)$, the $J(C)$
search is a birthday collision on $\langle G_E\rangle$ that discards about
half its trials; a control on $E_1$ without the square-abscissa filter is
never worse. Both are below the median rho on 13 to 15 of 15 seeds at the
three smallest primes, where rho's fixed set-up dominates, and the cover arm
is above it on 13 of 15 seeds at $p=6421$ and 12 of 15 at $p=12{,}841$. To help, a cover would have to supply a target-side
factor base well below $r$ (about $r^{1/2}$ times a polylogarithm) on which
relations with independent terms are found at a constant rate; here the
base is half the group and the two terms coincide. A smaller Prym does not
enter: $\varphi_{1*}$ kills it, and nothing computed on it sees the target.
The scope is degree-2 bielliptic covers, one curve per prime, weight-2
relations and $p\le12{,}841$.

\section{What the obstructions leave open}

None of the obstructions above concerns the cost of \emph{solving} the
decomposition system, nor the linear algebra. Chapter~\ref{ch:falldegree}
shows that the solving cost is genuinely open. The algorithms of
Part~\ref{part:algorithms} attack the remaining free terms.
