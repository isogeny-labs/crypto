\chapter{Gr\"obner-basis engines}\label{sec:groebner}

Before root finding, the decomposition oracle was a Gr\"obner solve, and
for $m\ge3$ over binary fields it still is. The repository grew a family
of engines (\art{docs/ic/GROEBNER_F4F5F6_AUDIT_20261010.md}, \S1; file names below are under \art{src/cryptanalysis/}):

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=1.4em]
\item[Matrix F4 and F5 over $\FF_2$] (\art{koblitz_groebner.rs},
  \art{matrix_f5_f2.rs}): Macaulay matrices of the Weil-restricted Boolean
  system, reduced by a shared Four-Russians kernel (\art{gf2_elim.rs}, four
  8-bit tables, AVX-512 or AVX2).
\item[Inherited F4] (\art{inherited_f4.rs}, from 22 September, the
  default algebraic oracle): a DPLL-style tree over partial assignments in
  which each child \emph{inherits} its parent's echelon form and re-reduces
  only what the assignment changed, instead of building a fresh Macaulay
  matrix per node.
\item[F6-IC] (opt-in): inherited F4 plus a factor-base domain gate (a
  fully assigned summand whose abscissa is not a base point refutes;
  partial support pruning refutes earlier), and meet-in-the-middle leaves
  (with $m-1$ summands fixed the last is looked up; at $m=3$ and
  $\abs\Fb\le256$ every compatible second point is added and the third
  looked up).
\item[$\FF_p$ F4] (\art{f4_fp.rs}, \art{f4_fp_tower.rs},
  \art{sig_fp_tower.rs}): degree-bounded F4 with sparse-by-reducer then
  dense reduction, a Faug\`ere--Lachartre block for the Kummer-tower
  oracle, and a signature variant.
\item[Exhaustive search] (engine \texttt{fes-f2}): Gray-code enumeration of
  the Boolean system, charged two word XORs per point, so its counted cost
  is on the same basis as inherited F4's.
\item[M4GB] (\art{m4gb_oracle.rs}, engines \texttt{m4gb-f2} and, from 11
  November, \texttt{m4gb-f2-server}): the upstream M4GB at a pinned commit
  with repository patches (\path{third_party/m4gb-patches/}), run as an
  external process per call or as one persistent process per binary. It
  reports no counted unit, so its rows are priced by wall time.
\end{description}
Two variants of inherited F4 were added on 12 November: \texttt{inherited-f4-next}
(the same eliminations with SIMD row XOR, Four-Russians table reuse and
the existing exact matrix builders) and \texttt{inherited-f4-batched}
(kept rows rewritten only when read or at a split). Both leave every
decision and tree statistic unchanged (\S\ref{sec:bo-ladder}). Two size
rules route by unknowns: \texttt{fes-then-inherited} and
\texttt{fes-then-batched} run \texttt{fes-f2} on quadratic systems of at
most 14 unknowns and inherited F4, or its batched variant, otherwise. From
13 November \texttt{fes-f2-gpu} runs \texttt{fes-f2}'s search on a
persistent CUDA worker, charged as \texttt{fes-f2} and returning its
solutions in the same Gray order (\S\ref{sec:in-gpuic}).

\paragraph{Correctness.} The audit pinned every engine against every
other and against brute force (88+15+9+14+13+12+21+6 passing library
tests in the touched modules), and found one soundness bug
(\S\ref{sec:corrections}, item 7), two contract defects and several latent
holes, all fixed with regression tests.

Matrix and inherited F4 can return different decompositions, so their
splitting trees may differ; only matrix F4 and F5 share a tree by
construction. F6-IC's $S$ ratios, once its batched group additions are
charged, are an accounting loss, not a frontier move. Those ratios used
inherited F4 before its word XORs were priced (\#1774); priced, F6-IC is
still a loss at K13, while the R13 and $n=19$ cells remain lower bounds.

The $\FF_2$ engine campaigns (about 90 directories, 22 September--17
October; the programme record~\cite{icprogram2026}, clusters ``boolean'',
``gf2-elim'', ``f5-kernel'', ``f5-support-cut'', ``f4-f5-shared'') gated
each micro-change against the strongest reference, and most failed. Kept as
defaults: the branchless strip, the bitmap seen-set, batched pair inserts,
five-column tables, and direct packing in F5 (default since 17 October).
About thirty unpack, pivot, layout and packing variants were refuted, and
graded reuse in F5 fell short of the campaign's $2\times$ goal. These are
stage speed-ups of the decision engine; none changes an index-calculus
distance to rho. Every reduced basis of \texttt{f4\_fp}, after
Gebauer--M\"oller criteria, packed monomials, interned symbolic
preprocessing and a parallel symbolic pass, agrees with Singular or msolve.
The Boolean comparison is a difference of task (decide versus compute the
basis), not of engine quality.

\paragraph{What did not work.} Degree-2 early-abort probes after a
2025--26 proposal: word-XOR ratios $1.00083$--$1.00378$ (no change) and
$0.99714$--$0.99928$ (worse). Gate-first geometry: $1.57$--$1.78\times$
fewer word XORs but lane-batched F4 roots cost $2.6$--$4.0\times$ a
sequential control. Convolution splitting of the summation polynomial:
$1.21$--$2.07\times$ \emph{more} word operations despite lower degree;
Frobenius auxiliary variables: $57$--$701\times$ more
(\art{research/lit2026_w\{4,5,8\}_*_20261011/}). Symmetry-breaking clauses
in SAT did not help (\art{RESEARCH_KOBLITZ_INDEX_CALCULUS.md}). A
registered law for the $m=3$ refutation degree resolved all 68 units but
ended with the verdict ``neither'': one variant met its generic degree
window on 32/32 draws, the other fell below it on 8, and the heaviest
degree-10 Macaulay solves were $2.4\cdot10^5\times2.0\cdot10^5$ matrices at
42--51 minutes each (\art{research/ic_semiregular_law_20261005/}).

\section{Prime-field encodings at toy size, 15 November}\label{sec:gb-toy15}

A study opened on Stange's division polynomials
(\art{research/stange_torsion_division_20261113/}) ended with exploratory
measurements of how a Gröbner decomposition over $\FF_p$ grows with the
factor base. They are msolve~0.6.5 and Python runs on a shared container,
not \texttt{bench.record} sessions, with a second reviewer's reruns but no
validator report; every number is \texttt{[pcc:unverified]}.

\paragraph{Encodings at $m=4$.} On $y^2=x^3+5x+7$ with $p\approx n^3/3$ and
$n=8$--30, a target is written $R=P_1+P_2+P_3$ through $\Sem4$, with 8
targets per cell and every Gröbner answer checked against brute force
(\art{research/stange_torsion_division_20261113/sym_m4/README.md}).
Symmetrising $\Sem4$ cut the solve time about $2.3$--$2.8\times$ for a dense
small-$x$ base and for the base $\mu_n$ alike. The base $\mu_n$, which is
PKM's \S3.3 base~\cite{pkm2016}, gave 3--12 times fewer distinct
relations, because only about half of $\mu_n$ are abscissae of
$\FF_p$-points, and no advantage per relation. The split-variable encoding
of Amadori, Pintore and Sala~\cite{aps2018} (two $\Sem3$ joined by an
auxiliary variable) was $1.7$--$2.7\times$ slower than $\Sem4$, by a factor
that grows with $n$. Disjoint partitioned bases, one part per variable, were the cheapest
Gröbner encoding per relation, about $5$--$8\times$ below the symmetrised
one and $13$--$20\times$ below plain $\Sem4$, at about one sixth of the
relations. A hash join of $x(P_1\pm P_2)$ against $x(R\mp P_3)$ returned
exactly the brute-force relations and beat every Gröbner encoding by two
to four orders of magnitude.

\paragraph{Growth exponents.} Least-squares slopes of log time on $\log n$
(Table~\ref{tab:gb-growth}) put every msolve $\Sem4$ arm at a per-target
exponent $\alpha$ of about $3.9$--$4.7$
(\art{research/stange_torsion_division_20261113/growth_fit/README.md}). The
encodings change the constant, not the exponent. The only arm with
$\alpha\le1$ is the amortised hash join, whose counted cost is exactly
$2n$ additions per target after an $n^2+n$ pair table; it is generic, and
its one fitted interval below 1 (wall time over $n=40$--60, three sizes) is
an artefact of call overhead, since its counted additions are exactly
linear. Under the study's heuristic model, $k$ points per relation and a
per-target cost $n^\alpha$ give a total of $p^{2/(k+1-\alpha)}$ for
$\alpha\le1$ and no gain from a larger base once $\alpha\ge k-1$, which
every Gröbner arm here meets. The reviewer limited the verdict to the
encodings tested: each enters the base as a degree-$n$ univariate, so the
ideal degree grows with $n$ by construction, low-degree composed-map bases
were not tested, and $n$ is confounded with $p$.

\paragraph{End to end, in group operations.} The same study ran the hash
join as a complete toy index calculus against Pollard rho on prime-order
curves, three per prime, every logarithm asserted
(\art{research/stange_torsion_division_20261113/ic_e2e/README.md}). The
plain form was $7.9$, $10.0$ and $12.9\times$ Brent rho at $p=10{,}007$,
$100{,}003$ and $1{,}000{,}003$. Storing the $2n$ leftover points per
target as single large primes brought it to $2.15$--$2.40\times$ a
store-all negation rho, a constant: its best $n$ is 1--4, so it is a
two-collision birthday search with an almost empty base. Counted in field
operations, sharing one inversion between $x(R-F)$ and $x(R+F)$ cut that
arm by 32--33\%, but a rho given the same shared inversion and batching
stays ahead: best index calculus over best rho $1.96$ ($I=20M$) and $2.03$
($I=80M$) at $p=1{,}000{,}003$
(\art{research/stange_torsion_division_20261113/ic_e2e_pm/README.md}; the
reviewer notes that uncounted set-up exceeds the counted work at these
primes). An independent Python Weil-descent decomposer over $\FF_{p^2}$
(double large primes, $\log_2r=16$--36) was $14$--$27\times$ a store-all
negation rho with inversions at $3M$ and $11$--$15\times$ at
$1.5\log_2p\,M$; over $\FF_{p^3}$, with msolve and linear algebra
uncounted, its lower bound fell from $137$ to $95\times$ over $\log_2r=15$--24
(\art{research/stange_torsion_division_20261113/ext_field/README.md}). The
README sets the $\FF_{p^2}$ gap beside the repository's Rust study of
Gaudry's $k=2$ on $E(\FF_{p^2})$
(\art{research/blindspots_20261018/01-fp2-gaudry-k2/}), whose best arms
cost $50$ and $52\times$ the matched negation rho at 40 bits in GAE; the
units differ. None of these arms beats rho.
