\chapter*{Introduction}\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Introduction}\label{ch:intro}

For a subgroup $\langle G\rangle$ of prime order $r$ on an elliptic curve
$E/\FF_q$, Pollard rho~\cite{pollard1978,vanoorschot1999} computes discrete
logarithms in about $\sqrt{\pi r/(2A)}$ group operations, where $A$ is the
number of efficiently computable automorphisms~\cite{wiener1998,gallant2000}.
No algorithm is known to beat it on curves of cryptographic shape. Index
calculus, subexponential for finite-field logarithms, reached elliptic
curves through Semaev's summation polynomials~\cite{semaev2004} and the
extension-field analyses of Gaudry~\cite{gaudry2009} and
Diem~\cite{diem2011}. The question here is where it crosses rho: on which
curves, at which sizes, under which accounting.

This treatise records the answer one research programme reached between
July and 6 November 2026 across three repositories (\texttt{crypto},
\texttt{crypto-autoresearcher}, \texttt{cryptanalysis}).
Table~\ref{tab:fm-glance} and Figure~\ref{fig:fm-glance} give its volume
by month. A chronological programme record~\cite{icprogram2026} and an
improvement ledger~\cite{icledger2026} organise the same work by date and
by incumbent; this document organises it by theory, algorithms, measured
frontier and open problems. It runs no new experiment, re-verifies one
certificate (\S\ref{sec:fd-binary}) and cites a committed artefact for
every number except the session-reported Kummer-tower last fall degrees of
\S\ref{sec:fd-pkm}, which are labelled where used.

\section*{The argument in brief}

\paragraph{1. Where the cost lives (Part~\ref{part:foundations}).}
Relation collection costs (trials per relation) $\times$ (cost of one
decomposition). Chapter~\ref{ch:model} writes the cold cost as four terms,
maps each lever to the term it moves, and gives measured per-operation
costs. On the 19 validated leaderboard rows, set-up and relation
collection take $98.2$--$99.98\%$ of cold $S$ and linear algebra at most
$0.41\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:fm-phases}).

\paragraph{2. What is fixed for every curve (Chapter~\ref{ch:cover}).} The
summation cover has Galois group $(\ZZ/2)^{m-2}$ for every curve, field and
characteristic (a draft proof upheld by an integrity audit; the
characteristic-2 separability lemma awaits an expert check). Its fibres
over $\FF_q$ split completely on the factor-base locus, and the mean yield
at fixed base size is a double count. No curve has anomalous monodromy, no
fibre census can find an anomalous relation rate, and no factor-base
geometry can raise mean yield. Oracle cost, the difficulty of the
decomposition system, the linear algebra and the choice of field remain
free.

\paragraph{3. How hard the decomposition system is
(Chapter~\ref{ch:falldegree}).} Every subexponential claim for
prime-degree binary curves assumes that the solving degree stays near the
first fall degree. In three presentations the first fall degree is a
constant of the presentation (for binary Weil descent, whenever every
descended equation has degree 2, as on every instance computed). The last
fall degree, which equals the solving degree of an unsatisfiable system,
exceeds it: by proof for an explicit-membership chain ($(4,5)$ at $B=3$);
by independently checked closure certificates for binary Weil descent
($\ge5$ against 2 at $n=33$--$45$); by session report for prime-field
Kummer towers (6 against 4; raw data not committed). Whether it grows
without bound is open, as Huang, Kosters and Yeo~\cite{hky2015} and
Kousidis and Wiemers~\cite{kousidis2019} left it.

\paragraph{4. What hardness rests on (Chapter~\ref{ch:hardness}).} An
index-calculus complexity claim chains six obligations: solver growth,
yield, relation rank, geometry, a non-generic interface and end-to-end
composition. The literal Petit--Quisquater Assumption~2 fails on all 119
census instances; a kernel-checked left-kernel theorem shows that a single
target needs no rank condition; PKM's ``$\Delta=10$ suffices'' fails at toy
sizes; Nagao's Lemma~4 is refuted in three reading--placement cells and
proved in the fourth. Except where a Lean kernel checked them these are
unverified assertions, and none moves a row of Tables~A--C.

\paragraph{5. What engineering bought (Part~\ref{part:algorithms}).}
Root-finding oracles cut the cost of one decomposition $400$--$5\cdot10^4
\times$ against F4 where they apply (up to $10^5$ in wall time against the
PDP baseline). Gr\"obner engines improved one to two orders in counted
work. Factor bases closed under maps of known logarithm cut linear algebra
by constant factors. On the cold one-target frontier the stages these
changes leave alone (relation yield per probe, table set-up) now dominate.

\paragraph{6. Where index calculus wins (Chapter~\ref{ch:extension} and
Chapter~\ref{sec:transfer}).} Only in weak extension-field families does
the measured cost reach a fairly priced rho: $E_0/\FF_{q^3}$ through a
genus-3 GHS cover, by double-large-prime hyperelliptic or Diem's
plane-quartic index calculus; the Joux--Vitse cover of $E(\FF_{p^6})$,
whose sieve route is $0.0065\times$ a matched negation rho at $p=1511$
(validated as accounting, weak class only); and possibly $k=4$ over
$\FF_{(2^m)^4}$, at $5.2\times$ rho at $m=15$ with an extrapolated
crossover only. The plain Nagao arm of the Joux--Vitse cover is above
matched rho at $p=1301$ and $1451$ and unresolved at $1601$, so its
extrapolated crossing near $p\approx1330$ does not hold. These algorithms
are known; the contribution is matched, priced and replayed measurement,
validated in one case, on undeployed families.

\paragraph{7. The rho side (Part~\ref{part:rho}).} A collision floor,
derived in the generic group model and checked by Monte Carlo, puts a
single-target index calculus with $F$ base elements of unknown logarithm at
$1.5$, $1.875$ and $2.188\times$ rho or more for $F=1,2,3$; the measured
generic incumbent, near $1.8\times$, sits at the $F=2$ constant
(Proposition~\ref{prop:collisionfloor}). Chapters~\ref{ch:matchedrho}
and~\ref{ch:rhovariants} examine how rho itself was measured and varied.

\paragraph{8. What the measurements say (Part~\ref{part:frontier}).} At a
matched reference rho is cheaper on every prime and prime-degree binary
curve measured. The validated gaps are $3.4$--$11.5\times$ on Koblitz
curves of 37--47 bits (rule v7; $4.7$--$18\times$ against rule v8's
same-binary rho) and $5.5$--$13.5\times$ on prime curves of 10--24 bits.
The unvalidated two-summand pair table is $10.5$--$102\times$ at 20--40
bits, its gap widening as about $r^{1/6}$; the generic $k$-sum table
($5.3$--$13.1\times$, no validation) narrows that growth to about
$r^{0.02}$, with baby-step giant-step as its limit; the generic residual
collectors are $1.3$--$1.7\times$ at 36--42 bits ($w=0$). An equal-advice
audit against Bernstein--Lange rho~\cite{bl2012} retired every online win.
Matching the reference, pricing native work and replaying records moved
most recorded gaps upward: 65 benchmark rows left the admitted table, 56 of
them as incomplete (Table~\ref{tab:fm-standing}). Chapter~\ref{ch:audit}
lists the corrections that moved the leaderboard and
Appendix~\ref{ch:corrections} the rest.

\section*{Conventions}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Unit.} Costs are group-addition equivalents per $\sqrt r$
  ($S$) unless a family uses its own counted unit, such as $\FF_q$
  multiplications.
\item \emph{Ratios.} ``$X\times$'' is baseline over candidate for an
  improvement and IC over rho for a distance; each sentence says which.
\item \emph{Intervals.} Intervals are 95\%, as the source recorded them.
\item \emph{Standing.} No \texttt{pcc} claim covers a result here unless
  one is named. A measured figure without a named \texttt{VALIDATION.json}
  is its producer's reported value, an unverified assertion; ``validated''
  means only that such a file names it. A theorem is ``kernel-checked''
  only where a Lean kernel accepted it.
\item \emph{Change classes.} A change is an \cls{advance} (an exponent or
  crossover moves under complete accounting), \cls{engineering} (a constant
  moves), \cls{relabelling} or \cls{accounting}. Against rho the programme
  produced no \cls{advance} on any prime-order curve of cryptographic
  shape.
\item \emph{Paths.} Artefact paths are relative to the \texttt{crypto}
  repository unless prefixed \texttt{crypto-autoresearcher:} or
  \texttt{cryptanalysis:}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope.} No speed-up on a deployed curve, no key recovery, no
asymptotic sub-rho algorithm for prime fields, and no statement about the
security of ECC2K-130, P-256 or secp256k1. The programme's GPU Pollard-rho
work on ECC2K-130 is published separately (IACR ePrint 2026/2469).
