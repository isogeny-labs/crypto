\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

From July to 6 November 2026 the programme filed 1{,}402 ledger decisions
to 30 September, then 745 ecbench sessions with 129{,}945 sealed records,
145 admitted benchmark rows and 28 leaderboard rows, 19 of them validated
(Tables~\ref{tab:fm-glance} and~\ref{tab:fm-standing}). Its results fall
into four kinds.

\paragraph{Theory.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Fixed for every curve.} The summation cover's monodromy is
  $(\ZZ/2)^{m-2}$ everywhere, its fibres split completely where relation
  search operates, and the mean yield at a given base size is a double
  count. No choice of curve or base geometry raises the mean decomposition
  yield at matched base size. Coverage, oracle cost and the difficulty of
  the solve remain free; Part~\ref{part:algorithms} works on those.
\item \emph{Open.} In three presentations the first fall degree is a
  constant of the presentation and the last fall degree exceeds it: proved
  for the guarded chain, certified on named instances of binary Weil
  descent ($\ge5$ at $n=33$--$45$), session-reported for prime-field
  Kummer towers (raw data not committed). The subexponential claims for
  binary curves assume the step these results show is not automatic.
  Whether the last fall degree grows without bound is unproved.
\item \emph{Hardness} (Chapter~\ref{ch:hardness}). The literal
  Petit--Quisquater Assumption~2 fails on all 119 census instances, PKM's
  ``$\Delta=10$ suffices'' fails at toy sizes, and a kernel-checked
  left-kernel theorem removes the rank condition for a single target. The
  census figures are unverified assertions.
\item \emph{Generic floor} (Chapter~\ref{ch:genericfloor}). A
  single-target index calculus whose base logarithms are unknown costs at
  least $1.5\times$ rho, and $1.875\times$ with two base elements, the
  constant of the measured generic incumbent (generic group model, checked
  by Monte Carlo).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Engineering.} Root finding cut one decomposition
$400$--$5\cdot10^4\times$ against F4 where it applies; the Gr\"obner
engines improved one to two orders in counted work; known-logarithm-closed
bases, a $k$-sum table, set-up and linear algebra each cut a constant. The
cost moved to set-up and relation collection, which take
$98.2$--$99.98\%$ of cold $S$ on the validated rows
(Table~\ref{tab:fm-phases}). No exponent moved below rho's on a prime or
prime-degree binary field. The generic $k$-sum oracle moved its own
exponent from $0.62$ to $0.52$; its limit is baby-step giant-step.

\paragraph{Measurement.} At a matched reference, priced across a band and
replayed, rho is cheaper on every prime-order curve measured: by
$3.4$--$11.5\times$ on Koblitz curves of 37--47 bits (validated, rule v7;
$4.7$--$18\times$ against rule v8's same-binary rho), $5.5$--$13.5\times$
on prime curves of 10--24 bits (validated), $10.6$--$38\times$ on random
binary curves (validated), $5.3$--$13.1\times$ for the $k$-sum table and
$10.5$--$102\times$ for the campaign pair table at 20--40 bits, and
$1.3$--$1.7\times$ for the generic residual collectors at 36--42 bits. No
validated leaderboard row is below its reference. Off the board, one
validated measurement is: the Joux--Vitse sieve route on the weak class at
$p=1511$, $0.0065\times$ a matched negation rho in group additions and
$0.016\times$ in $\FF_p$ multiplications, with the order holding across
the band (\texttt{VAL-20261103-c31c76}; no \texttt{pcc} claim). The
validator classes it as accounting: a local run, a sampled replay, and the
F4, descent and linear-algebra additions bounded rather than emitted. An
equal-advice audit retired every online win, and 65 benchmark rows left
the admitted table, 56 as incomplete. Matching, pricing and replaying
lengthened the measured distance more often than any single improvement
shortened it.

\paragraph{Where index calculus wins.} In weak extension-field families
($E_0/\FF_{q^3}$ through genus-3 covers, Joux--Vitse covers of
$E(\FF_{p^6})$, perhaps $k=4$ in characteristic two) the measured cost
reaches rho, as theory predicts. The plane-quartic route measured exponent
$\approx1$ against rho's $1.5$, at $0.005\times$ negation rho near
$q=2^{21}$ (L0, no validation file). The plain Nagao arm of the
Joux--Vitse cover is above a matched negation rho at $p=1301$ and $1451$
and unresolved at $1601$, so its extrapolated crossing near $p\approx1330$
does not hold. These algorithms are known; the programme contributed fair
measurement.

\paragraph{What would change the picture.} In order of what they would
settle (Table~\ref{tab:fm-open}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item a proved, growing lower bound on the last fall degree for any
  presentation of the binary decomposition system (open problem~12; best
  certified value now 5). This is the only result that bears on the
  subexponential claims;
\item more certified instances at $n=33$--$51$, with the certificates
  published, to see whether the last fall degree reaches 6 (problem~13);
\item the frozen $k=4$ replication at $m=14$--$17$: the cheapest
  experiment that could put a measured size crossover on record, an
  \cls{advance} in a weak family (problem~6; now $5.2\times$ rho at
  $m=15$);
\item owner rulings on whether the Curve25519 BSGS-boundary rounds count
  as index calculus, and on admitting the Joux--Vitse cover result to the
  leaderboard. That result is validated as accounting only; emitting and
  pricing its F4, descent and linear-algebra work (problem~11) would turn
  it into a priced result.
\end{enumerate}
Nothing here concerns a deployed curve or key recovery.
