\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

Eighteen weeks of work on elliptic-curve index calculus, from July to
early November 2026, produced four kinds of result.

\paragraph{Theory.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{What is fixed for every curve.} The summation cover's monodromy
  is $(\ZZ/2)^{m-2}$ everywhere. Its fibres split completely where relation
  search operates. The mean yield at a given base size is a double count.
  So no choice of curve or base geometry can raise the \emph{mean}
  decomposition yield at matched base size. Coverage, the cost of the
  membership oracle and the difficulty of the solve remain free, and the
  engineering of Part~\ref{part:algorithms} works on those.
\item \emph{What is open.} For three presentations of the decomposition
  system the first fall degree is a constant of the presentation, and the
  last fall degree is not bounded by it:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item proved for the guarded chain;
  \item certified on named instances of binary Weil descent;
  \item session-reported (raw data not committed) for prime-field Kummer
    towers.
  \end{itemize}
  The subexponential claims for binary curves assume exactly the step
  these results show is not automatic. Whether the last fall degree grows
  without bound is unproved.
\item \emph{What hardness rests on} (Chapter~\ref{ch:hardness}). The
  literal Petit--Quisquater Assumption~2 fails on all 119 census
  instances, PKM's ``$\Delta=10$ suffices'' is contradicted at toy sizes,
  and a kernel-checked left-kernel theorem removes the rank condition for
  a single target. The census figures are unverified assertions.
\item \emph{The generic floor} (Chapter~\ref{ch:genericfloor}). A
  single-target index calculus whose base logarithms are unknown costs at
  least $1.5\times$ rho, and $1.875\times$ with two base elements, the
  constant at which the measured generic incumbent sits (a derivation in
  the generic group model, checked by Monte Carlo).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Engineering.} The decomposition oracle became about
$400$--$5\cdot10^4\times$ cheaper per decomposition against F4 where root
finding applies. The Gröbner
engines improved by one to two orders in counted work. Factor bases
closed under known-logarithm maps, a $k$-sum table, and set-up and linear
algebra engineering each cut a constant. None of this moved an exponent
below rho's on a prime or prime-degree binary field. The generic $k$-sum
oracle moved its own exponent from $0.62$ to $0.52$, but its limit is
baby-step giant-step, and the collision floor keeps any generic
single-target arm with unknown base logarithms at least $1.5\times$ rho.

\paragraph{Measurement.} At a matched reference, priced across a band and
replayed, rho is cheaper on every prime-order curve the programme measured:
\begin{itemize}
\item on Koblitz curves of 37--47 bits, $3.4$--$11.5\times$ on the
  validated board (rule v7) and $4.7$--$18\times$ against rule v8's
  same-binary rho;
\item on prime curves, $5.5$--$13.5\times$ on the validated rows of
  10--24 bits, $5.3$--$13.1\times$ for the $k$-sum table and
  $10.5$--$102\times$ for the campaign pair table at 20--40 bits, and
  $1.3$--$1.7\times$ for the generic residual collectors at 36--42 bits;
\item $10.6$--$38\times$ on random binary curves.
\end{itemize}
Nineteen leaderboard rows passed a validation gate that demands independent
replay, recomputation and a matched rho. None of them is below its
reference. Off the board, one validated measurement is below a matched
rho: the Joux--Vitse sieve route on the weak class at $p=1511$, at
$0.0065\times$ a matched negation rho in group additions and $0.016\times$
in $\FF_p$ multiplications, with the order holding across the band
(\texttt{VAL-20261103-c31c76}; no \texttt{pcc} claim). The validator
classes it as accounting: a local run, a sampled replay, and the F4,
descent and linear-algebra additions bounded rather than emitted. Every online win disappeared under an equal-advice audit, and
the corrections forced by matching, pricing and replaying lengthened the
measured distance more often than any single improvement shortened it.

\paragraph{Where index calculus wins.} In weak extension-field families
($E_0/\FF_{q^3}$ through genus-3 covers, Joux--Vitse covers of
$E(\FF_{p^6})$, and perhaps $k=4$ in characteristic two) the measured cost
crosses or approaches rho, as theory predicts. The plane-quartic route
measured exponent $\approx1$ against rho's $1.5$, at $0.005\times$ negation
rho near $q=2^{21}$ (L0, no validation file). On the Joux--Vitse cover the
sieve route is the validated row above, while the plain Nagao arm is above
a matched negation rho at $p=1301$ and $1451$ and unresolved at $1601$, so
its extrapolated crossing near $p\approx1330$ does not hold. These are
known algorithms; the programme's contribution there is fair measurement.

\paragraph{What would change the picture.} Three items, in order of what
they would settle:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A proved lower bound on the last fall degree that grows with
  $n$}, for any presentation of the binary decomposition system. It would
  answer the question left open by Huang, Kosters and Yeo and by
  Kousidis and Wiemers, and it is the only result that bears on the
  subexponential claims.
\item \emph{More certified instances} at $n=33$--$51$, with public
  certificates, to show whether the last fall degree reaches 6.
\item \emph{The frozen $k=4$ replication} at $m=14$--$17$, the cheapest
  experiment that could put a measured size crossover (an \cls{advance})
  on record. It would be in a weak family. The one validated below-rho
  measurement, the Joux--Vitse sieve at $p=1511$, is accounting and below
  rho at the one size matched; emitting and pricing its F4, descent and
  linear-algebra work (open problem~11) is the cheapest way to turn it into
  a priced result.
\end{enumerate}
Nothing here concerns a deployed curve. The open problems of
Chapter~\ref{sec:open} state each remaining question as the experiment or
proof that would settle it.
