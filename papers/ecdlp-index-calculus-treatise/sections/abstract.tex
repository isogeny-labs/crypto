Pollard rho remains the fastest known algorithm for the discrete logarithm
on elliptic curves of cryptographic shape. Index calculus could in
principle exploit more structure, but it has never beaten rho there. This
treatise collects everything a research programme of July to early
November 2026 learned about why. Every number is drawn from committed
evidence and carries its boundary: oracle stage, complete solve, cold
one-target cost, or online cost. No proof-carrying claim covers a result
here unless one is named: a measured figure is its producer's reported
value, ``validated'' means only that a validation file names it, and
``kernel-checked'' means that a Lean kernel accepted it.

\emph{Theory.} We give, in a draft proof upheld by an integrity audit
(the separability lemma in characteristic 2 still awaits an expert check),
the Galois group of Semaev's summation cover. It is
$(\ZZ/2)^{m-2}$ for every curve, field and characteristic, with arithmetic
monodromy equal to geometric. We also give an exact factorisation law for
its fibres over $\FF_q$. Two consequences follow:
\begin{itemize}
\item fibre statistics are constant on the factor-base locus;
\item at fixed factor-base size the mean decomposition yield is fixed.
\end{itemize}
So levers that rely on curve-dependent monodromy or on raising mean
yield at matched base size are closed without experiment. Coverage, oracle
cost and solving difficulty remain free.

On fall degrees, we prove for the binary Weil descent of $\Sem3$ that:
\begin{itemize}
\item the first fall degree (Caminata--Gorla) is 2 for every $n$
  whenever every descended equation has degree 2, as on every instance
  computed, including Semaev's chained systems, where 4 was stated;
\item the last fall degree is at most $k+2$, or $(t-1)k+t$ for chains.
\end{itemize}
We also certify that the last fall degree is at least 5 on named
instances at $n=33$--$45$ and in the $m=t=3$ chain, with certificates that
an independent program checks. Every F4 run on those systems therefore
exceeds degree 4, and Semaev's Assumption~1 fails as stated. For an
explicit-membership chain we prove $(d_{\rm ff},d_F)=(4,5)$ exactly at
$B=3$ for every $p>3$, and $\max(5,B+1)\le d_F\le3B-4$ in general. For PKM
Kummer towers we prove a constant first fall degree of 4 and show that the
last fall degree depends on the presentation, not the ideal. Throughout,
the first fall degree is a constant of the presentation and the last is
not. A hardness audit refutes the literal Petit--Quisquater Assumption~2 on
all 119 census instances, and a left-kernel theorem, kernel-checked in
the producer's compile, shows
that one target needs no rank heuristic; the census numbers are unverified
assertions. A generic collision floor, derived in the generic group model
and checked by Monte Carlo, shows that a single-target index calculus
whose base logarithms are unknown costs at least $1.5\times$ rho, and
$1.875\times$ with two base elements, the constant at which the measured
generic incumbent sits.

\emph{Algorithms.} The programme engineered:
\begin{itemize}
\item root-finding and meet-in-the-middle decomposition oracles, about
  $400$--$5\cdot10^4\times$ cheaper per decomposition than F4 where they
  apply;
\item inherited and gated F4 engines, and an $\FF_p$ F4 at msolve parity;
\item compact Frobenius-orbit and known-logarithm-closed factor bases;
\item FGHR's $D_4$ symmetry at $k=4$, cutting one decomposition
  $370$--$481\times$;
\item double-large-prime genus-3 index calculus after the cubic GHS
  transfer, from a fitted $q^{1.91}$ to $q^{1.37}$ (local exponent
  $1.356$ at $q=2^{22}$, still above $4/3$);
\item Diem's plane-quartic index calculus on the same family, at
  exponent $\approx1$.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Frontier.} A priced campaign of 4{,}848 executions found no index
calculus below rho on any prime or prime-degree binary curve at any
weight of a pricing band applied to every arm. On Koblitz curves of
37--47 bits the gap to a matched rho is $3.4$--$11.5\times$ on the
validated board (rule v7), and $4.7$--$18\times$ against the same-binary
rho of rule v8. On prime curves the validated rows are $5.5$--$13.5\times$
at 10--24 bits; the campaign's two-summand pair table is
$10.5$--$102\times$ at 20--40 bits; the closest prime arms, generic
collision searches at rho's exponent, are $1.3$--$1.7\times$ at 36--42
bits ($w=0$). Only in weak
extension-field families does index calculus reach or cross rho:
\begin{itemize}
\item genus 3 via GHS: below the strongest rho at $q=2^{16}$ under the
  registered unit price, above it at the protocol's sensitivity price,
  unresolved at the other sizes (no validation file);
\item plane quartics: $0.005\times$ a negation rho at $q\approx2^{21}$
  (no validation file);
\item Joux--Vitse covers: the sieve route at $0.0065\times$ a matched
  negation rho in group additions ($0.016\times$ in $\FF_p$
  multiplications, at most $0.12\times$ across the band) at $p=1511$, on
  the weak class only, validated as accounting
  (\texttt{VAL-20261103-c31c76}, no \texttt{pcc} claim); the plain Nagao
  arm is above a matched negation rho at $p=1301$ and $1451$ and
  unresolved at $1601$, so its extrapolated crossing near $p\approx1330$
  does not hold;
\item $k=4$ in characteristic two: $5.2\times$ rho at $m=15$ after
  corrections; a crossover near $m\approx17.6$ is an extrapolation.
\end{itemize}
Every online win was retired by an equal-advice audit against
Bernstein--Lange rho. We record the corrections that matching references,
pricing native work and replaying records forced. Nothing here concerns a
deployed curve or key recovery.
