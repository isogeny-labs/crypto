Pollard rho solves the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm in a group of
prime order $r$ with $A$ efficiently computable automorphisms in about $(\pi r/(2A))^{1/2}$
group operations, and no index calculus has beaten it
on a curve of cryptographic shape. This treatise collects what one
research programme proved and measured about index calculus on elliptic
curves between July and 12 November 2026. Its sibling ledger holds
1{,}402 decisions and 2{,}074 run directories to 30 September; on
6 November this repository held 1{,}402 study directories, 745 ecbench sessions with
129{,}945 sealed records, 145 admitted benchmark rows and 28 leaderboard
rows, 19 of them validated. Each number
states its boundary (oracle stage, complete solve, cold one-target cost or
online cost) and its standing. ``Validated'' means that a validation file
names the result, and ``kernel-checked'' that a Lean kernel accepted it.
None of the 166 proof-carrying (\texttt{pcc}) claim records has a verdict,
so a result without one of these labels is its producer's unverified
assertion.

\emph{Theory.} The Galois group of Semaev's summation cover is
$(\ZZ/2)^{m-2}$ for every curve, field and characteristic, with arithmetic
monodromy equal to geometric, and its fibres over $\FF_q$ split by an exact
law (a draft proof upheld by an integrity audit; the characteristic-2
separability lemma awaits an expert check). Fibre statistics are therefore
constant on the factor-base locus, and the mean decomposition yield at a
fixed base size is fixed. For the binary Weil descent of $\Sem3$ the first
fall degree is 2 for every $n$ whenever every descended equation has degree
2, as on every instance computed, Semaev's chained systems included, and
the last fall degree is at most $k+2$, or $(t-1)k+t$ for chains.
Independently checked certificates give a last fall degree of at least 5 on
named instances at $n=33$--$45$ and in the $m=t=3$ chain, so Semaev's
Assumption~1 fails as stated. An explicit-membership chain has
$(d_{\mathrm{ff}},d_F)=(4,5)$ at $B=3$ for every $p>3$ and
$\max(5,B+1)\le d_F\le3B-4$ in general; PKM Kummer towers have first fall
degree 4 and a last fall degree that depends on the presentation, not the
ideal. The literal Petit--Quisquater Assumption~2 fails on all 119
census instances (unverified census), and a kernel-checked left-kernel
theorem removes the rank condition for one target. In the generic group
model, checked by Monte Carlo, a single-target index calculus whose base
logarithms are unknown costs at least $1.5\times$ rho, and $1.875\times$
with two base elements.

\emph{Algorithms.} Root-finding and meet-in-the-middle oracles cost
$400$--$5\cdot10^4\times$ less per decomposition than F4 where they apply.
FGHR's $D_4$ symmetry at $k=4$ cuts one decomposition $370$--$481\times$.
Double-large-prime genus-3 index calculus after the cubic GHS transfer
moved from a fitted $q^{1.91}$ to $q^{1.37}$ (local exponent $1.356$ at
$q=2^{22}$, above $4/3$); Diem's plane-quartic route on the same family
runs at exponent $\approx1$.

\emph{Frontier.} A priced campaign of 4{,}848 executions found no index
calculus below rho on any prime or prime-degree binary curve at any weight
of a pricing band applied to every arm. On the validated board the gap to
a matched rho is $4.1$--$11.1\times$ on Koblitz curves of 37--47 bits
(rule v9, 11 November),
$5.5$--$13.5\times$ on prime curves of 10--24 bits and $10.6$--$38\times$
on random binary curves. The closest prime arms, generic collision
searches at rho's exponent, are $1.3$--$1.7\times$ at 36--42 bits ($w=0$).
Index calculus reaches or crosses rho only in weak extension-field
families: the Joux--Vitse sieve route at $0.0065\times$ a matched negation
rho at $p=1511$ (weak class only; validated as accounting,
\texttt{VAL-20261103-c31c76}, no \texttt{pcc} claim); plane quartics at
$0.005\times$ a negation rho near $q=2^{21}$ and genus 3 via GHS below the
strongest rho at $q=2^{16}$ under the registered unit price (neither has a
validation file); $k=4$ in characteristic two at $5.2\times$ rho at
$m=15$, with a crossover near $m\approx17.6$ only by extrapolation. The
plain Nagao arm of the Joux--Vitse cover is above a matched negation rho at
$p=1301$ and $1451$; with a Hilbert stop it reads $1.003$, $0.837$ and
$0.695$ of that rho at $p=1301$, 1451 and 1601 (local, unverified). An equal-advice audit against Bernstein--Lange rho retired every
online win. Nothing here concerns a deployed curve or key recovery.
