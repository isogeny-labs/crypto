Pollard rho remains the fastest known algorithm for the discrete logarithm
on elliptic curves of cryptographic shape. Index calculus could in
principle exploit more structure, but it has never beaten rho there. This
treatise collects everything a research programme of July to early
November 2026 learned about why. Every number is drawn from committed
evidence and carries its boundary: oracle stage, complete solve, cold
one-target cost, or online cost. No proof-carrying claim covers a result
here unless one is named: a measured figure is its producer's reported
value, "validated" means only that a validation file names it, and
"kernel-checked" means that a Lean kernel accepted it.

Theory. We give, in a draft proof upheld by an integrity audit (the
separability lemma in characteristic 2 still awaits an expert check), the
Galois group of Semaev's summation cover. It is (Z/2)^(m - 2) for every
curve, field and characteristic, with arithmetic monodromy equal to
geometric. We also give an exact factorisation law for its fibres over
F_q. Two consequences follow:

- fibre statistics are constant on the factor-base locus;

- at fixed factor-base size the mean decomposition yield is fixed.

So levers that rely on curve-dependent monodromy or on raising mean yield
at matched base size are closed without experiment. Coverage, oracle cost
and solving difficulty remain free.

On fall degrees, we prove for the binary Weil descent of S₃ that:

- the first fall degree (Caminata–Gorla) is 2 for every n whenever every
    descended equation has degree 2, as on every instance computed,
    including Semaev's chained systems, where 4 was stated;

- the last fall degree is at most k + 2, or (t-1)k + t for chains.

We also certify that the last fall degree is at least 5 on named instances
at n = 33–45 and in the m = t = 3 chain, with certificates that an
independent program checks. Every F4 run on those systems therefore
exceeds degree 4, and Semaev's Assumption 1 fails as stated. For an
explicit-membership chain we prove (d_ff, d_F) = (4,5) exactly at
B = 3 for every p > 3, and max (5,B+1) ≤ d_F ≤ 3B - 4 in general. For
PKM Kummer towers we prove a constant first fall degree of 4 and show that
the last fall degree depends on the presentation, not the ideal.
Throughout, the first fall degree is a constant of the presentation and
the last is not. A hardness audit refutes the literal Petit–Quisquater
Assumption 2 on all 119 census instances, and a left-kernel theorem,
kernel-checked in the producer's compile, shows that one target needs no
rank heuristic; the census numbers are unverified assertions. A generic
collision floor, derived in the generic group model and checked by Monte
Carlo, shows that a single-target index calculus whose base logarithms are
unknown costs at least 1.5× rho, and 1.875× with two base elements, the
constant at which the measured generic incumbent sits.

Algorithms. The programme engineered:

- root-finding and meet-in-the-middle decomposition oracles, about
    400–5 ⋅ 10⁴× cheaper per decomposition than F4 where they apply;

- inherited and gated F4 engines, and an F_p F4 at msolve parity;

- compact Frobenius-orbit and known-logarithm-closed factor bases;

- FGHR's D₄ symmetry at k = 4, cutting one decomposition 370–481×;

- double-large-prime genus-3 index calculus after the cubic GHS
    transfer, from a fitted q^(1.91) to q^(1.37) (local exponent 1.356 at
    q = 2²², still above 4/3);

- Diem's plane-quartic index calculus on the same family, at exponent
     ≈ 1.

Frontier. A priced campaign of 4,848 executions found no index calculus
below rho on any prime or prime-degree binary curve at any weight of a
pricing band applied to every arm. On Koblitz curves of 37–47 bits the gap
to a matched rho is 3.4–11.5× on the validated board (rule v7), and
4.7–18× against the same-binary rho of rule v8. On prime curves the
validated rows are 5.5–13.5× at 10–24 bits; the campaign's two-summand
pair table is 10.5–102× at 20–40 bits; the closest prime arms, generic
collision searches at rho's exponent, are 1.3–1.7× at 36–42 bits (w = 0).
Only in weak extension-field families does index calculus reach or cross
rho:

- genus 3 via GHS: below the strongest rho at q = 2¹⁶ under the
    registered unit price, above it at the protocol's sensitivity price,
    unresolved at the other sizes (no validation file);

- plane quartics: 0.005× a negation rho at q ≈ 2²¹ (no validation file);

- Joux–Vitse covers: the sieve route at 0.0065× a matched negation rho
    in group additions (0.016× in F_p multiplications, at most 0.12×
    across the band) at p = 1511, on the weak class only, validated as
    accounting (VAL-20261103-c31c76, no pcc claim); the plain Nagao arm is
    above a matched negation rho at p = 1301 and 1451 and unresolved at
    1601, so its extrapolated crossing near p ≈ 1330 does not hold;

- k = 4 in characteristic two: 5.2× rho at m = 15 after corrections; a
    crossover near m ≈ 17.6 is an extrapolation.

Every online win was retired by an equal-advice audit against
Bernstein–Lange rho. We record the corrections that matching references,
pricing native work and replaying records forced. Nothing here concerns a
deployed curve or key recovery.
