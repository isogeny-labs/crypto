Title: Strict Splitting Counts in Reconstructed Elliptic-Curve Candidate Streams: An Exact Reproduction and Its Limits
Authors: Anonymous draft; author information pending
Keywords: elliptic curves; Frobenius discriminant; Elkies primes; parameter generation; reproducibility; selection bias
Suggested area: elliptic-curve cryptography and parameter-generation analysis; final ePrint category to be chosen by authors

We reproduce a previously reported arithmetic separation between published elliptic curves and a particular set of unpublished candidates reconstructed from historical seed streams. For each curve over a prime field, we count odd primes at most 691 for which the Frobenius discriminant is a nonzero square. All eleven unpublished candidates have larger counts than every publication in their respective field, across four fields containing eight publications. Two further publications are retained without unpublished same-field controls. Separate GMP and PARI implementations agree on 6,342 odd-prime characters, 303 cutoff positions and all 4,480 label assignments preserving publication counts within streams. Exactly 23 assignments separate at some cutoff in the declared family. Including zero characters destroys the separation at 691 in the 192-bit field. The reported cutoff and outcomes were known before this replay. The result neither authenticates a historical selection rule nor establishes a discrete-logarithm advantage.
