Title: Pollard Rho Across a Large Prime Conductor Gap: A Large-Sample Measurement and a Formal Invariance

Keywords: elliptic curve discrete logarithm, isogeny class, endomorphism ring, conductor gap, Pollard rho, Jao-Miller-Venkatesan, generic group algorithms, Lean

Category: Attacks and cryptanalysis

Abstract: Jao, Miller and Venkatesan proved that elliptic curves in one isogeny class over a prime field have equally hard discrete logarithms, up to polynomial factors, whenever the conductors of their endomorphism rings differ only at small primes; a gap at a large prime ell, bridged only by isogenies of degree divisible by ell, is the one case their expander argument does not reach, and it has been open since 2005 whether the two sides can differ. We measure the question for Pollard rho, the best known attack over prime fields, at the largest sample size to date: 24 audited ecbench sessions with 87,000 verified solves on CM curves with discriminant -19 and prime order, the crater (End = O_K) against floors (End = Z + ell O_K), under a protocol frozen before data with isomorphic-model negative controls and a sqrt(2) positive control. Where the gap is hard to cross (kernel field degree 2736 to ell-1; ell = 8209, 131101 and about 2^20), the floor/crater ratio of charged group operations is 0.998 [0.985, 1.011] over 12,288 paired solves, 1.005 [0.960, 1.052] and 0.982 [0.940, 1.028], closing an earlier unexplained lead of 1.22. Where the gap is cheap to cross (ell about 2^25 at 53 bits) a persistent 8 % deficit reproduced under fresh targets but vanished between re-coordinatised endpoints (1.040 [0.965, 1.119]), so it belongs to the coordinate models under the walk's representation hash, not to the endomorphism ring. We complement the measurement with a machine-checked theorem (Lean 4, no Mathlib, axioms propext and Quot.sound): transporting an r-adding walk along any group isomorphism preserves its trajectory and rho-length and is a bijection of the space of partition and multiplier choices, so representation-hashing rho is conductor-blind in distribution and a fixed partition is the only channel left, which is what the measurement bounds. We state precisely what remains open: non-generic algorithms are bounded only by the cost of the connecting isogeny, and a proof of equal hardness is unavailable for any curve.
