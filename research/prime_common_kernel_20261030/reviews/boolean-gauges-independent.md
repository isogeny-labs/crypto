## 1. Counterexample verified exactly

For an n-by-n matrix W define its 2^n-by-2^n Boolean kernel by

    K(W)[Y,X] = product_j l_(Y_j)((W X)_j),
    l_0(z)=1-z, l_1(z)=z, X,Y in {0,1}^n.

Take

    W = [[1,1],[0,1]],  V = [[1,0],[1,1]].

Use bit order 00,01,10,11, with the first listed bit the first vector
coordinate. Since V(1,1)=(1,2), the column K(V)[-,11] is

    (0,0,-1,2)^T.

Further, K(W)[11,10]=0 and K(W)[11,11]=2. Therefore

    (K(W) K(V))[11,11] = 4.

But WV=[[2,1],[1,1]] and WV(1,1)=(3,2), so

    K(WV)[11,11] = 3*2 = 6.

The difference is already nonzero over the integers and modulo 4. It
vanishes modulo 2, so a mod-2-only check would miss this example.

The reason is exact: summing over an intermediate Boolean assignment
multilinearizes powers Z_j^k to Z_j for positive k. Matrix multiplication
WV does not perform that reduction. With s=VX, the difference between the
11-row second moment of the two expressions is

    sum_j W[1,j] W[2,j] s_j(1-s_j).

Thus K is not a representation of general linear transformations on the
physical port-coordinate vector. One cannot absorb W -> P W P^(-1) into
arbitrary Boolean entry tensors merely by writing K(P)^(-1) and assuming
K(P W P^(-1))=K(P)K(W)K(P)^(-1).

There are valid restricted cases. Permutations give exact Boolean
renaming, and diagonal physical maps give independent one-bit maps.
More generally, K(W)K(V)=K(WV) for every V when every column of W is
supported in at most one row, because every row polynomial of K(W) is
then multilinear in the intermediate coordinates. Over characteristic
zero, the converse holds if all V are allowed: the two-output moment
forces W[r,j]W[s,j]=0 for each r!=s. For invertible square W this is
precisely a monomial matrix. This converse is not asserted over arbitrary
rings with zero divisors.

## 2. The correct universal gauge identity

Let H(T) be the full Boolean-index kernel tensor, which may equal K(W(T))
or another specified common deformation. Group its indices into factors
E_v belonging to individual original vertices. An actual local gauge is
an invertible linear map G_v on E_v, not automatically a linear map on
the list of physical port variables. Put G=the tensor product of the G_v.

If the transformed kernel is H'=G H (with the chosen tensor/vector
orientation), transform each attached covector f_v to f_v G_v^(-1).
Contraction with H' equals contraction with H, by inserting and canceling
the inverse matrices. This is an exact identity for every collection of
entry tensors. With row and column transformations written as matrices,
the same statement is H'=L H R and corresponding inverse-transpose maps
on the attached vectors. No assumption K(LWR)=K(L)K(W)K(R) is involved.

Node-dependent gauges can also preserve a common deformation pointwise,
provided the complete transformed kernel and all attached tensor changes
are included. A new standard Cauchy matrix under a new physical slot
assignment is not thereby identified with that transformed kernel.

## 3. What such a gauge preserves exactly

Let R be any commutative ring, including Z/2^p or the source's commutative
truncated coefficient ring. For any index partition, suppose every G_v
acts wholly within one side of that partition. The corresponding
flattening matrices satisfy

    M' = L M R0,

where L and R0 are invertible over R. For every k, let I_k(M) denote the
ideal generated by the k-by-k minors. Cauchy-Binet writes every k-minor
of LMR0 as an R-linear combination of k-minors of M. Hence
I_k(M') is contained in I_k(M). Apply the same argument to
M=L^(-1)M'R0^(-1) for the reverse inclusion. Therefore

    I_k(M') = I_k(M) for every k.

Consequences include unchanged rank over a field, unchanged Smith
elementary divisors when a Smith form exists over the chosen coefficient
ring, and unchanged truncated 2-adic content of these ideals over Z/2^p.
A Smith form is not being assumed for a general ring containing a
nilpotent V variable. The determinantal-ideal statement remains valid.

This does not prove that a gauge cannot improve a particular explicit
mode enumeration. It says that a group-preserving invertible integral
gauge cannot lower intrinsic flattening rank or increase the divisibility
of all k-minors. The desired mode-density target has not been reduced to
such an invariant here. Gauges that mix different groups or use nonunits
are outside this scoped invariance argument and have additional costs.

## 4. Costs which remain chargeable

For a degree-d Boolean tensor, independent one-bit gauges can be applied
entrywise by a sum over 2^d old assignments. This is polynomial space and
O(2^d) old entry calls, with polynomial extra arithmetic per assignment.
If d<=Delta=O(s^0.9), one application fits the source's permitted
exp(O(s^0.9)) entry time, provided the gauges and their inverses have
uniform polynomial-size descriptions and polynomial-time scalar
evaluation at the required precision. This is an upper bound, not an
automatic efficient construction of a useful gauge.

A fully dense 2^d-by-2^d vertex gauge needs a compact entry oracle and an
inverse-entry oracle; its exponentially large table cannot be stored in
the required polynomial space. A chain of general block gauges must not
silently multiply the old entry cost at every recursive depth. Integral
matrices with integral inverses cause no precision loss. Even-denominator
changes need an explicit precision debit; an inverse over the fraction
field is insufficient for the ring-level claim.

Canonical lifting is another obligation. A gauge must be applied to the
same immutable lifted entries used to define the common parent
deformation, before reduction at the required node precision. Re-evaluating
an older tensor at a new creation precision can change the deformation.

## Result and next discriminating lemma

The proposed 4-versus-6 counterexample is correct and rejects unrestricted
physical-port functoriality. True finite-index gauges are available, and
their exact ring-level flattening invariants and one-step entry costs are
explicit above. No common sparse two-axis factorization follows yet.

The next constructive target is an explicitly described family G_(i,t)
acting within original vertex index spaces, with integral inverses and
uniform evaluation cost, for which the *actual* transformed Boolean
kernel (not K of a newly named port matrix) has the desired targeted
two-index modes. Its density, coefficient valuations, creation precision,
and recursive entry costs must then be proved. A transform which merely
redescribes a flattening without changing its invariants supplies no
mode-count theorem on its own.
