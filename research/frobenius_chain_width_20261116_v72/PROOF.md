# Sparse triangular structure does not bound root count

Consider x_(i+1)=x_i^2 for i=0,...,k-1, with x_k=1. Each equation involves two neighboring variables; the variable graph is a path, of treewidth one. Forward substitution uniquely determines the tuple from x_0, and elimination gives x_0^(2^k)-1. In odd characteristic with 2^k dividing q-1, the cyclic multiplicative group gives exactly 2^k distinct roots. Its derivative is nonzero at each root, so multiplicities do not explain the expansion. Listing every original tuple requires at least proportional output work.

In a binary finite field the k-fold Frobenius is a bijection, so there is exactly one distinct original tuple, consisting entirely of ones. The elimination polynomial may have repeated algebraic roots; this solver's contract is every distinct finite-field original tuple. Backward inverse Frobenius recovers the unique predecessor without a domain scan. Odd predecessors use exact square roots with both signs retained.

The independent small oracle enumerates every starting field coordinate, propagates forward, checks the final coordinate and compares complete original tuples to backward recovery. F257 and F256 give an explicit same-order-magnitude field contrast. This disproves graph sparsity alone as a generalized speed predictor; it does not disprove useful sparse block algorithms. Track remaining parameter cardinality, block-image/quotient degree and output size. The proposed next mechanism is contraction of genuinely linearized/Frobenius blocks in characteristic two, preserving exceptional fibers, with separate multiplicative/univariate treatment in odd characteristic. No novelty or GB-performance claim is made.
