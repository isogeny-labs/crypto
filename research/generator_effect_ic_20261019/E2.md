# E2: the cofactor ladder, where the abscissa base and a known-log base separate

PLAN §4 E2 (unchanged by Amendment 1): population P1, the same arms as E1,
at cofactor `h ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}`. **No security claim.** The known-log base
is a generic precomputation table in the Bernstein–Lange shape, and no
exponent moves. **P-192 and P-224 have `h = 1`, so E2 says nothing about
them.**

## PROTOCOL (frozen before any measured run)

Frozen in the commit that adds this section, together with
[`e2/curves.jsonl`](e2/curves.jsonl), [`e2/prereg.jsonl`](e2/prereg.jsonl)
and the 40 specs `e2/specs/h*-c*.json`. No E2 record existed when it was
written. Only the "Results" sections below are written after the run.

### Population P1

- 24-bit `p`, curves `y² = x³ − 3x + b` (`a = −3`, as NIST), `#E = h·r`
  with `r` prime and `r > h`, 8 curves per `h`, 40 in all.
- Found by `examples/a_minus_3_prime_order_curves.rs -- 24 8 20261019 H`
  (seed 20261019 for every `h`). At `h = 1` this is the E1 finder unchanged,
  and its first two curves are DRYRUN.md's c1 and c2. At `h > 1`, `#E` is the
  one multiple of a point's order in the Hasse interval, and
  `G = [h]P` for the smallest-abscissa `P` with `[h]P ≠ ∞`, so `G` has order
  `r`. Because `r` is prime and `r ∤ h`, `⟨G⟩` is the only subgroup of order
  `r`, and any other generator gives the same subgroup (§1B).
- `#E` of every curve is re-counted by `PrimeCurve::point_count` (`O(p)`
  Legendre symbols) in `generator_effect_e2_n0` before anything is
  predicted: 40 of 40 agree.

### Arms (per curve, all at the same `B`)

`B = ⌈#E^{1/3}⌉` (`family_optimum_base(#E, 1, 1)`, the family optimum; 205
to 256 here). Oracle `mitm:negation_folded=1,m=2`, walk targets, for every IC
arm.

| arm | method | role |
|:--|:--|:--|
| `rho-neg` | `rho.negation` | matched rho reference |
| `ic-abscissa` | `ic.pipeline`, `prime-abscissa:size=B` | baseline |
| `ic-knownlog-random` | `ic.pipeline`, `known-log:size=B,scalars=random,seed=20261019` | candidate |
| `ic-knownlog-random-aa` | the same with `seed=20261020` | A/A of the candidate: a second, independent random base of the same law |

The A/A uses another scalar seed because `ecbench` gives every arm the same
algorithm seed per (workload, round): a duplicate arm with identical
parameters would reproduce the candidate exactly and test nothing.

### Workloads and measurement

- One `ecbench` session per curve (`e2/specs/h{H}-c{i}.json`, 40 in all;
  `B` differs per curve and an arm's parameters are fixed per session):
  **768 `planted_shared` targets per curve**, target seed 20261019,
  1 measured round, no warm-up, order `alternate`, seed 20261019, isolation
  L0, timeout 600 s per run.
- **Sizing.** A known-log run stops at its first relation, so each `h` gives
  `8 × 768 = 6144` known-log relations. The relative standard error of their
  pooled hit rate is about `1/√6144 = 1.28%`, and the abscissa runs add little
  (each collects tens of relations). That gives a 90% half-width of about
  **±2.1%** on the yield ratio, inside the ±5% target. It also leaves room to
  resolve the 3.3% gap between the two predictions at `h = 16` (below).
- One host, shared (L0). Operation counts and GAE are exact and
  deterministic. Wall time is reported only as run time.

### Predictions (computed before the run, `e2/prereg.jsonl`)

`examples/generator_effect_e2_n0.rs` builds each arm's base exactly as the
pipeline does and records, per base:

- `N₀`, the class-cancelling count of `exact_decomposition_ceiling`
  (`ic_boundary.rs:451`, `m = 2`, over the signed points);
- `sumset`, the number of distinct nonzero pair sums `±P_i ± P_j` (`i ≤ j`)
  lying in `⟨G⟩`. This is exactly the set the negation-folded pair table can
  match, and the predicted relations per target tried is `sumset / r`.

`N₀` counts multisets; the table holds distinct sums. They differ by the
birthday collisions among sums. These are negligible for the abscissa base
(only `≈ 1/h` of its sums lie in `⟨G⟩`), but they grow with `h` for a
known-log base, whose `≈ 2B²` sums all lie in a subgroup of order `r ≈ #E/h`.
That subgroup is 12% to 14% full at `h = 16`.

Predicted online-yield ratio known-log / abscissa (per curve, then the mean
of the 8):

| h | N₀ ratio, range (mean) | sumset ratio, range (mean) | sumset / N₀ (mean) | A/A sumset ratio, range |
|--:|:--|:--|--:|:--|
| 1 | 1.0000 – 1.0000 (1.0000) | 0.9989 – 1.0011 (0.9999) | 0.9999 | 0.9984 – 1.0006 |
| 2 | 1.9737 – 1.9961 (1.9913) | 1.9678 – 1.9918 (1.9863) | 0.9975 | 0.9986 – 1.0010 |
| 4 | 3.8293 – 3.9898 (3.9324) | 3.8048 – 3.9625 (3.9076) | 0.9937 | 0.9982 – 1.0039 |
| 8 | 7.5971 – 7.9356 (7.8207) | 7.4748 – 7.8320 (7.7097) | 0.9858 | 0.9967 – 1.0016 |
| 16 | 14.824 – 15.528 (15.298) | 14.262 – 15.043 (14.795) | 0.9671 | 0.9952 – 1.0034 |

At `h = 1` every base has one class, so the `N₀` ratio is exactly 1.000 on
every curve: this is the control that ties E2 to E1. The ratio falls short of
`h` because the smallest abscissae do not spread evenly over the `h` classes.

### Statistics

`examples/generator_effect_e2_summary.rs`. For each arm, `O/E` is the
relations found over `Σ targets_tried × predicted rate`, pooled over the
curves of one `h`. The **measured / predicted yield ratio** is
`O/E(known-log) / O/E(abscissa)`; on one curve it is
`(y_kl / y_abs) / (pred_kl / pred_abs)`. Intervals are percentile 90% over
10 000 bootstrap resamples (seed 20261019) of targets within each curve.
A two-level interval (curves, then targets) is reported beside it as a
sensitivity check, with no decision weight. Total S is `(GAE + w · unpriced
ops) / √r` at `w ∈ {0, 0.1, 1}` (AGENTS.md §5a). Ratios are ratios of means
over the same targets.

### Decision rules

1. **Primary (PLAN E2):** at each `h`, the `N₀` prediction holds if the 90%
   interval of measured / predicted (`N₀`) contains 1. Expected from the
   collision count: it holds at `h ≤ 4`. At `h = 8` and `h = 16` it is
   expected to read about 0.986 and 0.967, and at `h = 16` the interval
   should exclude 1. That would be the predicted `N₀` over-count, not a
   failure of the class mechanism.
2. **Sumset prediction (exact):** at each `h`, the 90% interval of measured /
   predicted (sumset) contains 1. If this fails at any `h`, the mechanism is
   reported as incomplete there.
3. **Controls:** `h = 1`, both predictions contain 1. A/A, at every `h`,
   measured / predicted (sumset) of `aa / candidate` contains 1. A control
   failure voids the `h` it fails at.
4. **Width:** the 90% half-width of measured / predicted is at most 5% at
   every `h`. If it is wider, the `h` is reported as underpowered.
5. **Total S (§5a):** known-log over abscissa and both over rho, paired by
   target. An order is stated only where the interval is on one side of 1
   at all three weights. Expected direction, with no decision weight:
   known-log below abscissa at every `w` and `h`. Gap to rho: reported as
   distance remaining, not as a gate.
6. Every run that fails to verify is listed with its recorded reason.

### Change made before freezing (found in the smoke test)

A two-target smoke test on an `h = 16` curve showed `ic-knownlog-random`
finding its relation and then ending `exhausted`. The relation loop's
unknowns are the logs of `[h]P_v` (its rows read `Σ c·x − h·b·d = h·a`), but
the known-log solver was handed `s_v`, the log of `P_v`. The pipeline now
hands it `h·s_v mod r` (`ic_framework/mod.rs`). At `h = 1` that is the
identity, so E1's dry-run records are unaffected. A unit test recovers the
planted log on this E2 `h = 16` curve with three seeds.

The smoke runs (2, then 16 targets, on the first `h = 16` curve, after the
fix: 64 of 64 records verified) are scratch runs that check the tooling. They
are not part of E2's data and are not used in any number below.
