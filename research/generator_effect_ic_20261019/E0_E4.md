# E0 and E4: does the generator matter to index calculus?

PLAN §4, experiments E0 (exact checks) and E4 ("the standard's G" against
random generators). **No security claim.** Both experiments ask whether the
generator `G` that a standard fixes changes anything that index calculus can
use. The plan predicts no change: the abscissa base never reads `G`, and the
relation stream depends on `G` only through a change of coordinates (PLAN §1B).

## E4 PROTOCOL (frozen before any E4 run)

Frozen in the commit that adds this section. The commit hash is quoted in the
results. Nothing below changes after the first E4 record is written. Any
deviation is listed under "Deviations" in the results.

**Population.** Eight 24-bit prime-order curves `y² = x³ − 3x + b`, c1–c8,
the first eight printed by
`examples/a_minus_3_prime_order_curves.rs 24 8 20261019`. c1 and c2 are the
dry run's curves. Parameters are in [`e4/generators.jsonl`](e4/generators.jsonl).

**Cells (generators) and strata (curves).** Five cells per curve:

- `gstd`: the finder's point with the smallest abscissa, standing in for "the
  standard's G";
- `grand1`–`grand4`: `[c]gstd`, with `c` drawn by `random.Random(20261019 + i)`
  in `[2, r − 2]` ([`e4/make_specs.py`](e4/make_specs.py)). On a prime-order
  curve this is a uniform non-identity point.

**A/A cell.** `gstd` runs again in its own session with `target_seed`
20261020: same generator, independent targets, independent algorithm seeds.

**Arms.** Both run on every cell with `B = ceil(r^(1/3))` and
`mitm:negation_folded=1,m=2`, default (walk) targets:

- `ic-abscissa`: `prime-abscissa:size=B`;
- `ic-knownlog-random`: `known-log:size=B,scalars=random`.

**Size.** 2000 `planted_shared` targets per cell, one measured round, no
warm-up. That makes 8 × 5 × 2000 × 2 = 160 000 main records and
8 × 2000 × 2 = 32 000 A/A records. The binding metric is the known-log arm's
yield. It finds one relation per run, so a cell's yield rests on about 2000
relations. `gstd` pools 16 000 runs over the curves and `grand` 64 000. With a
geometric trial count (CV ≈ 1), the gstd/grand ratio has SE ≈ √(1/16 000 +
1/64 000) = 0.88%, so the 90% half-width is about ±1.45%. That is inside
±2%. A planted +3% shift is then 3.4 SE, and the detection rule below fires
with probability about 0.96. The abscissa arm finds about B relations per run,
so its yield is about 14 times tighter. Total S varies by about 2.5% per run,
so its interval is far inside ±2% in both arms.

**Metrics,** per run:

- yield `relations_found / targets_tried`, pooled as Σfound / Σtried;
- total S = (total GAE + w · unpriced ops) / √r at `w` ∈ {0, 0.1, 1}
  (AGENTS.md §5a), pooled as the mean.

**Primary test: equivalence of gstd and grand.** For each arm and metric,
`R = gstd / grand` is the equal-weight geometric mean over the eight curves
of the per-curve ratio. In each ratio, `grand` pools the four random cells. The
90% interval comes from 10 000 bootstrap resamples of runs within each
(curve, cell), percentile method, seed 20261019.

- **Equivalent:** the 90% interval lies inside [0.98, 1.02].
- **Different:** the interval excludes 1 and is not inside [0.98, 1.02].
- **Inconclusive:** anything else.

**Secondary tests: heterogeneity among generators.** For each arm and metric:

- the Cochran Q of cell means about their curve means (df = 8 × 4 = 32);
- its DerSimonian–Laird τ;
- the largest standardised cell deviation max|z|.

Each statistic gets a permutation null: runs are shuffled among the five cells
of each curve, 2000 permutations. We report the p of Q and of max|z|, and τ
beside the null's 95th percentile. These tests are descriptive. A
p < 0.05 is reported as heterogeneity to investigate. It does not override the
primary reading, and with 2 arms × 4 metrics, about 0.4 such flags are
expected by chance.

**Controls (run on the same records).**

- **A/A:** `gstd` against `gstd-aa`, the same ratio and interval. It must
  read "equivalent" on every arm and metric. Q, τ and max|z| are reported over
  the two-cell partition.
- **Planted +3%:** the analysis multiplies the gstd cell's yield numerator
  (Σfound) or S by 1.03 on every curve and recomputes `R`. The plant is
  **detected** when the interval's lower end is above 1. If the plant goes
  undetected for an arm and metric, E4 is **inconclusive** for that arm and
  metric, whatever the unplanted reading.

**Exact by-products (checked, not tested).** By E0a the abscissa base, and so
its pair table, is the same for every generator. The known-log base uses the
same scalars under every `G`, so its construction cost and its pair-table
count do not depend on `G`. Base construction and oracle set-up GAE must be
identical across a curve's five cells, record for record. Any difference is a
bug.

**Prediction.** Equivalent on every arm and metric. A/A equivalent. Plant
detected.

**Tooling.** `examples/generator_effect_e4_analysis.rs` computes every
number; Python only draws `e4/analysis.json`. Records are ecbench sessions,
one per spec in [`e4/specs/`](e4/specs/). The run tree lands as
`e4/runs.tar.xz` (AGENTS.md §13a). Host: L0, shared and loaded. Operation
counts and GAE are exact and deterministic. Wall time is reported as run time
only.
