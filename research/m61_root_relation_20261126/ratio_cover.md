# Geometry of a fixed multiplicative ratio fiber

For the selected curve let `f(x)=x³−3x+40`. Fix a nonidentity
`t∈μ_4950`. The natural correspondence behind a ratio fiber is the
normalization of

`C_t: y₁²=f(x), y₂²=f(tx)`.

It has two maps to `E`: `(x,y₁)` and `(tx,y₂)`. Thus each rational point
whose two x-coordinates are in the selected base gives a pair of elliptic
points at ratio `t`. This is a possible *organizing device* for relations;
it is not a point-addition homomorphism or a relation locator.

## Genus calculation

The certified group order is odd, so `f` has no root in `F_p`: a root
would give a rational point of order two. A cubic over a finite field with
no rational root is irreducible. If `f(x)` and `f(tx)` shared one root over
the algebraic closure, Frobenius would carry that root through all three
roots of the irreducible cubic. Therefore their root sets would coincide.
Comparing the constant terms of `f(tx)=c f(x)` gives `c=1`; comparing
the linear terms gives `t=1`, a contradiction. The two cubics have six
distinct finite roots for every nonidentity `t∈F_p*`.

The resulting biquadratic degree-four cover of the x-line has six finite
branch points plus infinity. At each branch point there are two points of
ramification index two, giving total Riemann–Hurwitz ramification `14`.
Consequently `2g(C_t)−2=4(−2)+14=6`, so **`g(C_t)=4`**. Simultaneous sign
change `(y₁,y₂)↦(−y₁,−y₂)` has quotient

`D_t: w²=f(x)f(tx)`, `w=y₁y₂`,

a squarefree degree-six hyperelliptic curve of genus two. The two other
involution quotients are copies of `E` (the second by `X=tx`). At the
Jacobian level, the standard idempotent decomposition of a biquadratic
cover suggests an isogeny from the three quotient Jacobians
`E×E×Jac(D_t)` to `Jac(C_t)`. This **does not** map the target ECDLP into
an easier genus-two DLP: the two elliptic factors still carry the original
points, and an explicit, cheap relation-producing map remains to be found.

The direct common-parameter extension grows rapidly. For `n` distinct
nonzero ratios `t₁,…,t_n`, normalize the cover
`C_{t₁,…,t_n}: y_i²=f(t_i x)` over the `x`-line. The scaled cubics
have pairwise disjoint roots by the same irreducibility argument above;
their squareclasses are independent, so the cover has degree `2^n`.
Its branch locus consists of `3n` distinct finite roots and infinity.
Each branch point has `2^(n−1)` points above it of ramification index
two. Riemann–Hurwitz therefore gives

`2g−2=−2·2^n+(3n+1)·2^(n−1)=3(n−1)·2^(n−1)`,

or `g=1+3(n−1)2^(n−2)` for `n≥2` (and `g=1` for `n=1`).
Thus `n=2,3,6,7` have genera `4,13,241,577`, respectively. In
particular, putting all six points of a three-pair relation above one
shared `x` produces a genus-241 direct cover.

There are lower-genus quotients, so genus 241 alone is not an
obstruction. For each nonempty subset `S` of the `n` ratios, the
character quotient is
`v_S²=∏_{i∈S}f(t_i x)`, of genus
`⌊(3|S|−1)/2⌋`. For `n=6`, the character dimensions add as
`6·1+15·2+20·4+15·5+6·7+1·8=241`.
The all-product quotient has genus eight, but stores only
`∏y_i`, losing the individual lift signs needed to replay six
elliptic points. A useful lower-genus route would have to recover
those signs and a target sum with an explicit cost bound. This genus
ledger does not exclude special correspondences or batching across
different parameter values; the generic seven-term locator remains
open.

The natural quotient for a **sum x-coordinate** is sharper than the
all-product quotient. Simultaneously changing every sign sends
`ΣP_i` to `−ΣP_i` and preserves `x(ΣP_i)`. Quotienting by this
diagonal sign involution gives degree `2^(n−1)` over the x-line.
It kills the common ramification at infinity, while each of the
`3n` finite branch points still contributes `2^(n−2)` simple
ramification points. Therefore

`2g_diag−2=−2·2^(n−1)+3n·2^(n−2)`,

so `g_diag=1+(3n−4)2^(n−3)` for `n≥2`. The genera for
`n=2,3,6,7` are `2,6,113,273`. Equivalently, its character
quotients are exactly those indexed by nonempty *even*-size
subsets; for `n=6`, their dimensions
`15·2+15·5+1·8=113` cross-check the formula.

For the selected M61 curve with six fixed ratios
`1,z,…,z⁵`, `z=636328216386749903∈μ_4950`, the
[native sign probe](native/examples/sum_sign_quotient.rs)
([frozen output](sum_sign_quotient.out)) scans all 4,950 common
parameters and checks this six-ratio set has no nontrivial
multiplicative stabilizer in `μ_4950`. Exactly 75 make all six
elliptic lifts; **all 75**
give 32 distinct values of `x(ΣP_i)` after fixing one sign,
with no sum at infinity. One such unramified fiber proves that no
sign automorphism beyond the diagonal one fixes `x(ΣP_i)` as a
function. By the Galois correspondence, the field generated by
`x` and this sum x-coordinate is the full diagonal-sign quotient
field, of genus **113** for this ratio tuple. Thus the genus-eight
all-product quotient genuinely discards sum-coordinate data here.
The same primitive-element conclusion holds for any distinct ratios
with independent squareclasses. If two sign-pattern sums had the
same x-coordinate as functions, the sums would agree up to global
negation. A genuinely different pattern would force twice a
nonempty partial sum of the `P_i` to be identically `O`.
Flipping one sign in that partial sum would then force
`[4]P_i=O`, impossible since `x(P_i)=t_i x` is nonconstant.
The finite M61 scan independently replays this sign separation.
Equivalently, the minimal polynomial of `X=x(ΣP_i)` over `F_p(x)`
has degree 32 for six lifts; this says nothing favorable about its
degree in `x` or the cost of solving `X=x(T)` for a target.
It can be written
`M(T)=∏_{ε∈{±1}⁶/±}(T−x(Σ ε_iP_i))∈F_p(x)[T]`.
Even a recovered rational common parameter contributes to the
chosen factor base only when every `t_i x` passes its membership
and lift tests.
The same native probe exercises a fixed-tuple descent on the M61
subgroup. It plants a target by summing six allowed points at the
first fully lifted parameter with sign mask 17, projects by `[7]`,
then scans all 4,950 common parameters independently of the planted
witness. Exactly one of 2,400 signed candidate sums recovers the
target; an unrelated fixed subgroup target has no hit. The 2,400
projected sum x-values are all distinct. Allowing global negation,
this ratio tuple covers exactly **4,800** nonzero prime-subgroup
targets, or `4800/r≈1.46×10^−14` of the selected subgroup.
The online scan charges 29,700 lift tests, 14,400 direct group-add
calls, and 2,400 cofactor multiplications; target construction is
outside that scan.
The same probe compares all 2,400 projected six-sum x-classes with
all 2,447 projected root-base column x-classes and finds **zero**
intersections. Thus this particular fixed ratio tuple supplies no
signed seven-point row through a six-sum plus one base column.
For independent uniform x-classes the expected intersection count is
`2·2400·2447/r≈3.57×10^−11`, so this null result is ordinary at
the M61 scale. It does not test other ratio tuples or a batch locator.
There is a useful symmetry experiment with the ratio set `μ₆` itself.
Multiplying the common parameter by a sixth root of unity permutes
the six inputs, and hence permutes the 32 sign-orbit sums. The native
probe finds 84 fully lifted parameters and 2,688 signed evaluations,
but exactly 448 distinct projected sum x-classes: each occurs in a
six-element parameter orbit. Scanning just 825 parameter-orbit
representatives performs 4,950 lift tests and 448 signed sum
evaluations and reproduces the complete image. This tuple covers
exactly 896 signed subgroup targets (`896/r≈2.72×10^−15`), and its
intersection with the 2,447 projected base columns is empty. The
symmetry divides both the parameter search and the target coverage
by six here. It may simplify a fixed-tuple equation, but supplies
neither an asymptotic cross-tuple selector nor a seven-point row.

Every six-element subset of `μ_4950` with a nontrivial
multiplicative stabilizer is a union of orbits under the unique
subgroup of order `h=2,3`, or `6`; hence it selects `6/h` liftable
root orbits. The exact M61 orbit census gives:

| Stabilizer order `h` | Liftable root orbits | Stable six-x sets | Upper bound on signed sum x-classes | Uniform expected base matches (upper bound) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 616 | 38,767,960 | 1,240,574,720 | `1.84×10^−5` |
| 3 | 194 | 18,721 | 599,072 | `8.90×10^−9` |
| 6 | 14 | 14 | 448 | `6.66×10^−12` |

The order-three family is fully enumerated: its 599,072 projected
sum x-classes are distinct and none matches a projected base column.
For the order-two family, each six-point sum is three antipodal-pair
sums. Hashing `A+B` for two distinct pair orbits requires 3,030,720
group additions and yields 1,515,360 distinct x-classes. Probing
`C+P` for each signed pair sum `C` and canonical projected base
point `P` requires 6,029,408 additions; **zero** x-classes match.
Because both signs of each pair sum are present, an x match would
cover the possible overall sign. The zero result therefore certifies
no seven-point row from any order-two-stable six-x set on this base.
The order-six family is included in both larger scans and was also
checked directly above. This quadratic meet-in-the-middle search is
a valid exact witness test for the entire symmetric family, but it
produces no row. In a growing family with `B≈p^(1/6)`, a uniform
model gives only `O(B⁴/p)=O(p^−1/3)` expected base matches for
order-two symmetry; algebraic concentration could violate that model.
The fixed-ratio edge census sharpens the asymmetric search budget.
Across all 4,949 nonidentity root ratios, directed base-pair counts
sum to `B(B−1)=5,985,362` and range from 1,157 to 1,257.
For any one ratio, the number of signed seven-point supports using
one of its pairs is at most `64·E_t·C(B−2,5)`. Uniform projected
group sums give at most 177.10 expected rows for the busiest ratio,
versus 2,446 dimensions needed for full rank. Asymptotically one
fixed ratio gives only `O(B⁶/p)=O(1)` expected rows when
`B=Θ(p^(1/6))`. A promising path would therefore need an
`Õ(B)` amortized witness extractor per ratio across `Θ(B)` ratios,
plus independent-rank and target-descent proofs. The present census
does not provide that extractor or rule out algebraic enrichment.
This is a genuine but planted fixed-tuple six-column decomposition;
it is not a descent for a general target. Assuming unrelated ratio
tuples cover independent uniform target sets, one would need roughly
`r/4800≈6.86×10^13` tuples for a constant target hit chance,
before their parameter scans. The independence estimate is not a
lower bound against an algebraically selected tuple family.
The standard seventh summation polynomial has degree 32 in each
of its six base abscissae. Substituting `x_i=t_i x` therefore gives
a fixed-ratio target equation of degree at most `6·32=192` in
the common `x` before cancellations. Constant-degree root finding
for **one** ratio tuple does not pay for searching the roughly
`m⁵` possible relative six-point ratio tuples. If only `O(B²)`
ratio tuples are tried, each over `O(B)` allowed common parameters,
the uniform prime-subgroup model gives only `O(B³/r)` expected
target hits at `B=Θ(r^(1/6))`. A structured batch or correlation
would have to change that count; polynomial degree alone does not.
This is a function-field identification, not a hardness theorem:
specialized inversion or batching may still exist. A ratio tuple
with a nontrivial multiplicative stabilizer can have an additional
symmetry moving `x`; the primitive-element claim here is explicitly
over `F_p(x)`.

## Exact pair-sum map and its limited gain

The genus-two quotient retains the x-coordinate of a signed elliptic pair
sum. For `P=(x,y₁)` and `Q=(tx,y₂)`, set `u=x(P+Q)` and `w=y₁y₂`.
The chord formula gives

`u=(f(x)+f(tx)−2w)/((t−1)²x²)−(t+1)x`.

Eliminating `w` using `w²=f(x)f(tx)` and removing the extraneous `x²`
factor yields the specialized third summation polynomial, divided by
`(t−1)²`:

`F_t(x,u)=t²x⁴−2t(t+1)ux³+((t−1)²u²+6t)x²`
`         +(t+1)(6u−160)x+9−160u=0`.

This is a **degree-four map** from `D_t` to the pair-sum x-line. For a
specified target x-coordinate `u`, the native locator scans the 4,949
nonidentity ratios, finds the roots of each quartic over `F_p`, retains
roots whose two x-coordinates are in the root base, and replays all signs
by group addition. Equal x-coordinates would require a separate diagonal
case; the locator intentionally asks for distinct base columns. This is
an exact distinct-column pair-decomposition procedure with roughly
`O(4950·polylog(p))` expected field work per query if constant-degree
finite-field factorization is used. It uses little precomputation, whereas
a full pair-sum hash table uses `Θ(B²)` preprocessing and storage. The
identity is a specialization of the known Semaev polynomial, not a new
ECDLP algorithm.

The [ratio-fiber probe](native/examples/ratio_fibers.rs) verifies one concrete
M61 witness: `x=1`, `t=1483046891616554412`, and
`u=961675082590561403` give `F_t(x,u)=0` and are obtained from actual
base points. It also checks the quartic on 128 signed adjacent base pairs.
The separate [Rust locator](native/examples/pair_locator.rs) implements
polynomial gcd with `X^p−X` to isolate rational roots, then recursively
splits the squarefree linear part using quadratic-character tests at
deterministically seeded shifts. It checks four-distinct-root, repeated-root,
and no-root controls, recovers planted `(+,+)` and `(+,-)` M61 pair
witnesses, replays every reported point sum, and matches exhaustive
signed pair search on a 16-column prefix. A release run took 102 ms for
the first planted target on this workspace; timings are illustrative,
not a cross-machine performance guarantee. The splitter gives an explicit
failure after 128 unsuccessful shifts rather than silently dropping a
factor. The roots are therefore complete except for that reported
probabilistic failure mode.

Even a complete pair locator does not yield the
required six-term relations cheaply: among the
`4·C(2447,2)=11,970,724` distinct-column signed unordered pairs, a
uniform prime-subgroup target would have only `3.63×10⁻¹¹` expected
decompositions after projection. Building enough target-dependent
four-term complements or homogeneous rows remains the expensive step.

## The exceptional ratio `t=−1`

Because `−1∈μ_4950`, one ratio has additional involutions. Its quotient is

`D_{−1}: w²=1600−(x³−3x)²`.

The involutions `x↦−x` and `(x,w)↦(−x,−w)` give genus-one quotients with
coordinates `X=x²,W=w` and `X=x²,V=xw`:

`W²=1600−X(X−3)²`, `V²=X[1600−X(X−3)²]`.

For the first quotient, `U=2−X` gives the short Weierstrass model
`W²=U³−3U+1598`. The pair-sum x-coordinate is
`u=(40−W)/(2X)`, and the ratio quartic simplifies to

`X²+(4u²−6)X+9−160u=0`.

The [native ratio scan](native/examples/ratio_fibers.rs) counts exactly
1,232 ordered lift-valid `(x,−x)` pairs, or 616 unordered base-column
pairs. It also replays one pair-sum x-coordinate through the genus-one
quotient formula. This accelerates one ratio's pair query, but it covers
only `616/C(2447,2)=0.0206%` of unordered column pairs. Under the
existing uniform tuple model, requiring **some** opposite-x pair leaves
about 0.178 six-term decompositions of a fixed target and about 86.8
seven-term homogeneous rows; these first-order expectations remain well
below the 2,446 rows needed for rank. The even-sextic splitting is a
standard geometric feature. The quotient records only the x-coordinate
of `P+Q`: simultaneous sign change makes the full sum `−(P+Q)`, so it
does not by itself supply an elliptic group homomorphism or DLP transfer.
The [exact point counts of both elliptic quotients](exceptional_quotient_order.md)
differ from that of the selected curve, ruling out an `F_p`-isogeny
through either factor on M61.

## Reproducible instance check and cost boundary

[`ratio_fibers.rs`](native/examples/ratio_fibers.rs) runs Euclidean gcds
over `F_p` for `f(x)` and `f(tx)` at all 4,949 nonidentity root ratios. It
finds zero shared-root ratios, consistent with the proof. The complete
output is frozen in [`ratio_fibers.out`](ratio_fibers.out). The same run
counts 1,157–1,257 lift-valid ordered pairs per ratio. Hence choosing one
ratio leaves `Θ(B)` pairs, and enumerating all ratios recovers `Θ(B²)`
pairs. The quotient yields the quartic pair-query formula above, but no
compression of the six-term witness search. Any proposed extension must
produce point-addition relations or target descents and charge its full
cost; genus alone is not an algorithm.
