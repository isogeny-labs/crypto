# Dickson trace base: exact rows, residual gap

This experiment uses the selected `E: y²=x³−3x+40` over
`p=2^61−1`, with `#E=7r` and `r=329406144237565159` prime. The
dependency-free [native example](native/examples/trace_dickson.rs) checks
every asserted group-law row; [frozen output](trace_dickson.out) records the
finite censuses. This is a curve-specific relation source, not a DLP solver.

## Construction and rank proof

Let `H=μ_m ⊂ F_p*`, where `3|m`, and use distinct x-coordinates
`x(t)=t+t^−1` for `t∈H` whenever they lift to E. The Dickson identity is

`x(t)^3−3x(t)=t³+t^−3`.

For a primitive cube root `ζ∈H`, the three x-values from `t,ζt,ζ²t`
have the same right-hand side and sum to zero. Choosing the same square
root `y` for all three, the horizontal-line chord law gives

`(x(t),y) + (x(ζt),y) + (x(ζ²t),y) = O`.

Inversion `t↦t^−1` identifies parameter pairs. Generic orbits of the
group generated by inversion and `t↦ζt` contain six parameters and three
distinct x-columns. Their supports are disjoint. When `m` is even, the
two candidate exceptional orbits each give a tangent row with two distinct
columns (one repeated); odd `m` has one candidate exceptional orbit.
Each lifted exceptional row is checked. Thus the displayed rows have **exact rank equal
to the number of liftable parameter orbits**, over any coefficient field
where their nonzero coefficients remain nonzero, including `F_r`. They
also remain valid after multiplying each point by the cofactor 7.

### Dihedral cover and its Jacobian

The parameter curve behind these rows has the smooth genus-three model
`C_b: v²=t⁷+b t⁴+t`, where `v=t²y` and `b²≠4`. It has maps to three
copies of `E_b: y²=x³−3x+b`:
`x_j=ζ^j t+ζ^−j t^−1`, `y=v/t²`, for `j=0,1,2`.
It also maps to `T_b: W²=u³+b u²+u` by `u=t³`, `W=t v`.
The three `E_b` image points add to `O`, which is the horizontal row
above. Thus their three maps have only two independent components.

There are exactly three independent components. A basis of regular
differentials on `C_b` is `dt/v, t dt/v, t² dt/v`. The pullbacks of
`dx_0/y` and `dx_1/y` are respectively
`(t²−1)dt/v` and `(ζt²−ζ^−1)dt/v`; these are independent because
`ζ≠ζ^−1`. The pullback of `du/W` is `3t dt/v`, independent of both.
The induced map from `Jac(C_b)` to `E_b²×T_b` is therefore a separable
isogeny over `F_p`. This is the standard dihedral-cover splitting,
derived here to delimit the construction, not a new ECDLP algorithm.
The [native small-field check](native/examples/quotient_order.rs) verifies
`#C_b=2#E_b+#T_b−2(p+1)` for `b=40` at `p=103` and `127`.

On the selected M61 `b=40` curve, the exact trace-quotient order is
`#T_40=2305843006991436568` (trace `2222257384`), while
`#E_40=2305843009662956113`; they are not `F_p`-isogenous. The
split Jacobian contains two copies of the original DLP group, but the
explicit maps account for the known horizontal triples and do not
create a cross-orbit row, a target decomposition, or a faster locator.

There is a nonzero comparable M61 curve where the two elliptic factors
*are* isomorphic. Their invariants are
`j(E_b)=−6912/(b²−4)` and
`j(T_b)=256(b²−3)³/(b²−4)`, so equality requires
`(b²−3)³=−27`. The root `b=0` has supersingular
`#E_0=p+1=2^61`, with no large prime-order subgroup. Of the two
other cube-root branches, exactly one has a square `b²` over M61.
Thus `b=0` and one pair `±b` exhaust the equal-`j` choices in this
one-parameter family over M61.
Choosing `b=1913898821915072477` gives
`b²=397061152296657049`, exact shared order
`2305843006682964424=104·22171567371951581`, and a prime
55-bit factor. The [native order probe](native/examples/quotient_order.rs)
checks its primality, a nonzero point of that order, and the explicit
isomorphism to the translated short model of `T_b`:
`(X,Y)=(λ²x,λ³y)` with `λ=899262398837438475`.
Thus its parameter Jacobian is isogenous to `E_b³`.
The other sign `b=391944187298621474` is its quadratic twist.
The same native Hasse-interval count certifies shared `E_b` and
`T_b` order `2305843011744423480=204120·11296507014229`;
the last factor is prime but only 44 bits. On `μ_7137`, this twist
has 1,767 liftable columns and 589 free generic orbits; its
exceptional `t³=1` orbit is unlifted. Its `B²=3122289` scale is
already close to `√r≈3361028`, before relation collection,
linear algebra, and target descent. The positive sign's 55-bit
subgroup remains the more informative hard-subgroup fixture.

An exhaustive `μ_7137` census on this equal-`j` curve finds 1,775
liftable columns and 592 disjoint free orbit rows. Its signed
distinct-column three-point scan gives exactly `(591,0)` total and
cross-orbit rows, both before and after `[104]` projection. All
`2·C(1775,2)=3,148,850` signed pair-sum x-values are distinct in
both systems. Thus the isomorphism adds no observed cross-orbit
three-point row, distinct-column four-point row, or pair-sum
compression on this fixture. The scan does not address longer rows.

The natural trace transfer still gives no new endomorphism. If
`R(t)=(t³,t³y)` on `T_b`, the two parameter points over the same
`E_b` point are `t` and `t^−1`, and
`R(t^−1)=(t^−3,t^−3y)`. The group law on
`W²=u³+bu²+u` gives `R(t)+R(t^−1)=(0,0)` for every liftable
`t`. Consequently `τ_*π^*:E_b→T_b` is zero on divisor classes,
even after identifying `T_b≅E_b`; the constant two-torsion sum
cancels in degree-zero divisors. The native test checks this identity
for multiple M61 points on both `b=40` and the equal-`j` curve.
Other correspondences or generic-ratio correlations remain open, but
the direct norm-through-the-cover route cannot supply a cross-orbit
relation or DLP transfer.

The isomorphism also gives a nonconstant mixed map on the same curve:
`R_E(t)=(λ^−2(t³+b/3), λ^−3 t³y)` and
`S_±(t)=P(t)±R_E(t)` with `P(t)=(t+t^−1,y)`. Its x-coordinate is a
rational function of `t`; for `S_+`, writing
`A=λ^−2(t³+b/3)` and `c=λ^−3t³`, it is
`x(S_+)=(t³+t^−3+b)(c−1)²/(A−t−t^−1)²−A−t−t^−1`
when the denominator is nonzero. A base-membership query is therefore
cheap per parameter, but useful relation supply still needs image
overlap. The native exhaustive `μ_7137` scan finds 3,549 lifted
parameters and **zero** hits of `x(R_E)` or either `x(S_±)` in the
1,775-column trace base. The 7,098 signed sum queries give 7,096
distinct x-values; their only two repeated values come from the
inversion pair `t=ζ,ζ²`, with `t³=1` and the same sign. Thus this
mixed map yields no new trace-base row on the fixture. The finite
result does not exclude a differently chosen base or useful
higher-arity intersections of these images.

Cofactor projection changes the apparent collision count, but only by
the known inversion symmetry. Since `R_E(t)+R_E(t^−1)` is rational
two-torsion, `[104]R_E(t^−1)=−[104]R_E(t)`, while
`P(t^−1)=P(t)`. The projected scan has 3,548 distinct x-classes
among 7,098 mixed-sum queries and 3,550 repeated entries; **none**
joins different inversion parameter orbits. The three parameters
with `t³=1` have `[104]R_E(t)=O`; their two signed queries each
return the existing projected base point, accounting for all six
projected base hits. Thus projection creates no new cross-orbit row
from this map on the fixture.

The same release example exhaustively sorts the x-coordinates of both

For `μ_7137`, its `2·C(1703,2)=2,898,506` signed pair-sum x-values

The example counts seven-column tuples using **at most one column from

`(1+3z)^696(1+2z)^2`, then multiplying by `2^7` for signs. For

uniform projected zero-row estimate is 6,559.01. Any actual nonzero
row with this support lies outside the span of the 698 free rows,
because a free row occupies two or three columns in one orbit. This
establishes a support-based independence test **relative to the free
rows only**; it neither finds such rows nor proves their mutual rank.
On a certified `p=131071` control curve of the same shape, the
[toy rank probe](toy_relation_rank.md) does find 44 seven-distinct-orbit
rows on a 21-column trace base; those rows alone attain rank 20.
This supports the rank possibility but uses a quartic-time collector
and does not transfer to M61.

## Two-parameter trace-identity template

The trace identities `x(tu)+x(t/u)=x(t)x(u)` and
`x(t²)=x(t)²−2` suggest a bounded family of seven-column supports
from the parameter exponents
`(i,j,i+j,i−j,2i,2j,2i+2j)`. There are `m²`
parameter pairs and at most 64 signed rows per distinct support after
fixing global sign. This is an actual `O(m²)` candidate generator,
but the identities concern field x-values, not elliptic point sums;
any row must still be verified.

The [native toy replay](native/examples/trace_template.rs)
([frozen output](trace_template.out)) uses the same-form curve over
`p=131071`, subgroup order `r=3541`, and `μ_771`. Its projected
base has 174 columns. Of 594,441 parameter pairs, 5,544 pass lift
and distinct-orbit filters, leaving 1,386 unique seven-orbit
supports and 88,704 sign-folded candidates. Exactly 25 verified
zero rows have rank 25. A uniform-group model predicts 25.05 rows;
16 deterministic equal-count random distinct-orbit support controls
average 25.88 (range 18–37). The template produces genuine
independent cross-orbit toy rows but shows no measured enrichment
beyond random support selection, and 25 rows are far below the
173 dimensions required for a full log system on that base.

For selected M61 `μ_8190`, even the impossible upper assumption that
all `m²=67,076,100` parameter pairs pass the filters gives only
`64m²/r≈1.30×10^−8` expected rows under the same uniform model,
versus 1,393 residual dimensions. A working variant would need a
very strong algebraic concentration or a fundamentally different
batch mechanism. The toy result is not an unconditional rejection
of every trace-identity template.

## Why this does not yet meet the target

The correspondence is the ordinary horizontal chord law. The two
nontrivial branches of the cube-root action add to `−P`; they do not
define a new single-valued endomorphism of E. The explicit rows connect
columns only within disjoint triples, reducing the unknown count by a
constant factor. They do not provide cross-orbit rows, a six-term target
locator, or an asymptotic family. Even on `m=8,190`, with `B²=4,376,464`,
the seven-row count in the table is only a uniform heuristic. A direct
3+4 meet-in-the-middle search still takes `Θ(B⁴)` time.

The next test is an actual projected relation collector that removes
zero-subsum consequences, measures sparse rank beyond these free rows,
and finds both generator and target decompositions within a fully charged
`Õ(B²)` budget. None is currently available. The existing
[locator contract](locator_contract.md) specifies the required checks.

The factor-base premise is not new: Petit et al., PKC 2016 §3.3 analyze
smooth multiplicative subgroup x-bases, and this repository already has
a Dickson trace-base implementation for P-256 in
`src/cryptanalysis/p256_dickson_factor_base.rs`. The special ternary rows
here follow immediately from the `a=−3` cubic and the chord law; their
finite rank and cost implications are the contribution of this probe.

## Fixed parameter-ratio quotient

For `x(t)=t+t^−1`, fix `q∈μ_7137`, put `u=t³` and `α=q³`, and write
`F(u)=u²+40u+1`, `Gα(u)=αu²+40u+α^−1`. The two lifts satisfy
`y₁²=F/u` and `y₂²=Gα/u`. For `α≠1`, the biquadratic cover of the
`u`-line is generically genus three: its two double covers share the
branch points `0,∞` and have four other distinct branch points, so
Riemann–Hurwitz gives `2g−2=−8+6·2=4`. Its simultaneous-sign quotient
has the **elliptic quartic**

`z²=F(u)Gα(u)`, with `z=u y₁y₂`.

This elliptic quotient varies genuinely with the ratio. Put
`s=α+α^−1`. The binary-quartic invariants of `FGα` are
`I=s²−1600s+2550412` and
`J=−2(s−3194)(s−800)(s+1594)`, with
`4I³−J²=68774832(s−2)²(s−1598)²`. Hence, away from the singular
parameters,

`j(Dα)=16I³/[159201(s−2)²(s−1598)²]`.

The [native ratio probe](native/examples/trace_ratio_quotient.rs)
([frozen output](trace_ratio_quotient.out)) verifies this
discriminant identity for every `α∈μ_2379` and finds **zero**
nonidentity ratios with `j(Dα)=j(E_40)`. More generally, since the
equation `j(Dα)=j₀` has degree at most six in `s`, a fixed cyclic
isogeny degree `d` can connect `Dα` to a fixed elliptic curve for at
most `12ψ(d)` values of `α` over the algebraic closure, where
`d` is prime to `p` and `ψ(d)=d∏_{ℓ|d}(1+1/ℓ)` is the
modular-polynomial degree. Each `α`
has at most three cube-root ratios `q`. This limits bounded-degree
isogeny transfer to `O(1)` generic ratio fibers for fixed `d`; it
does not exclude high-degree transfers or a batch relation locator.

The quadratics are separable and share a root only if
`α²−1598α+1=0`. The [native ratio probe](native/examples/trace_ratio_quotient.rs)
checks that none of the 2,378 nonidentity `α∈μ_2379` meets this
exception. More strongly, it verifies that **neither root is a cube in
`F_p*`**, so no rational parameter ratio `q` on the original `b=40`
curve can realize this degeneration. It also checks all 2,379 `u∈μ_2379` for the fixed
`q=556975957790817848`: 555 have two lifts, 664 have **both
radicands nonsquare** despite a rational quartic product, and 1,160
have mixed characters. Every valid pair's point-sum x-coordinate is
replayed from `z` and the chord formula. Thus a quartic point needs
a separate lift test.

Restoring `t³=u` ramifies at four points over `0,∞` and makes the full
pair cover genus 11 by Riemann–Hurwitz; the quotient
retaining `t` and `z` has genus five. The pair-sum x-coordinate depends
on the choice of the three cube roots of `u`: in the native check,
554 of 555 valid `u` have three distinct pair-sum x-values, with
one degeneracy. Summing those three pair points gives zero, but that
identity is just the sum of two already known free orbit rows. This is
a concrete lower-genus specialization of standard biquadratic
fiber-product geometry, compared with the [earlier x-ratio cover](ratio_cover.md).
It supplies a bounded pair-query representation, not cross-orbit
relations or the missing higher-arity locator.

An engineered comparison curve does realize the degeneration. Set
`h=556975957790817848∈μ_7137`, `s=h^42`,
`q=h^28`, and `b=s+s^−1=1361572249239761321`. Then
`α=q³=s²`, and

`F=(u+s)(u+s^−1)`,
`Gα=s²(u+s^−1)(u+s^−3)`.

The normalized product-sign quotient is the conic
`v²=(u+s)(u+s^−3)`, obtained by dividing `z` by
`s(u+s^−1)`. Equivalently `w=y₂/(s y₁)` satisfies
`w²=(u+s^−3)/(u+s)`, giving
`u=(s^−3−s w²)/(w²−1)`. The full pair cover in `t,y₁,y₂`
still has genus eight; the intermediate quotient retaining `t`
has genus two. The conic parameterization enforces equal quadratic
characters but does not force either elliptic lift or a point-sum
relation.

The single-lift trace cover has an elliptic quotient
`W²=u³+bu²+u`, via `u=t³`, `W=u·y₁`. Translating
`u=X−b/3` and running the same Hasse-interval order check gives
`#E_trace(F_p)=2305843006182255856` (trace `3031438096`) for the
engineered `b`. This differs from the engineered DLP curve's order
`2305843010179466656`, so the two elliptic curves are not isogenous
over `F_p`. The quotient describes the parameter cover, but does not
directly move DLP instances to the engineered curve. This comparison
does not exclude a higher-genus correspondence or a relation generator
using generic parameter ratios.

The [native order and ratio probe](native/examples/quotient_order.rs)
([frozen output](quotient_order.out))
certifies the nonsingular comparison curve's exact order
`#E_b(F_p)=2305843010179466656=32·72057594068108333`, with the
56-bit factor prime by deterministic `u64` Miller–Rabin and a checked
nonzero point of that prime order. The cubic has all three rational
two-torsion roots. Its trace base has `B=1775` liftable columns and
592 disjoint verified orbit rows. Of 888 liftable pairs at the
engineered ratio, none completes to a third base point, either before
or after `[32]` projection. An exhaustive signed three-point scan over
all base pairs finds exactly the 591 generic orbit triples and zero
cross-orbit triples, both before and after projection. All
`2·C(1775,2)=3,148,850` signed pair-sum x-values are distinct in both
systems, so this degeneration creates no pair collision or
distinct-column four-point relation on the fixture. Uniform projected tuple estimates on this
base are 9,667.49 signed seven-point zero rows and 38.25 six-point
target decompositions, but no such cross-orbit row or target witness
has been located efficiently. The genus reduction is a real algebraic
specialization with a concrete negative short-row result, not an
`O(p^(1/3))` attack.

All six parameter ratios with `q³=s²` or `s^−2` are present in
`μ_7137`. The Rust probe finds 888 liftable unordered base pairs for
each ratio, but their union contains only **2,661** pairs, or 5,322
signed pair-sum x-classes: 0.169% of the full pair-sum inventory.
Counting four signed point choices per pair gives at most 10,644
signed pair-sum options. Under a uniform prime-component model,
three ordered options from this restricted set have at most
`10644³/r≈1.67×10^−5` expected six-point target hits. Allowing one
additional signed base point and identifying global sign gives the
optimistic seven-point zero-row estimate
`10644³·1775/r≈0.030`. Index overlap and pair-order duplicates only
reduce distinct usable rows. These are conditional expectations, not
rigorous incidence bounds; a new algebraic correlation could change
them. They make the special conic fiber alone far too small under the
same model that supports the unrestricted trace-base estimates.

This has a family-level version. For general `b`, a shared root of
`u²+bu+1` and `αu²+bu+α^−1` requires
`α²−(b²−2)α+1=0`. There are at most two values of `α`, each with at
most three rational cube roots `q`: **at most six special parameter
ratios**, regardless of subgroup size. In a growing family with
`|H|=Θ(B)=Θ(r^(1/6))`, their union contains `O(B)` signed pair
options. Three such pairs give only `O(B³/r)=O(r^−1/2)` expected
decompositions of a fixed target under uniform prime components;
three pairs plus one base point give `O(B⁴/r)=O(r^−1/3)` expected
seven-point zero configurations. These expectations vanish and cannot
provide `Θ(B)` independent rows by this restricted route under that
model. The ratio-count bound is exact; the incidence estimates still
admit an unforeseen structured correlation, and strategies using
ordinary ratios as well are outside this exclusion.
