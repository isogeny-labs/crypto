# PROTOCOL: do the rails on the repository's ECC2K-130 S'4 encoding persist beyond d = 8?

Written 2026-11-16 before any measured run and committed alone before the first measured row.
Local pilot on a shared 4-core container (at most 2 of my processes at a time), no isolab host, no
replay certificate, no validator report, no matched rho arm, no benchmark record: every number is
`[pcc:unverified]`. Follow-up of `research/sat_smt_leads_20261115/B-dualrail-ecc2k130` ("pilot B").

## Instance

Semaev S'4 for K_0: y^2 + xy = x^3 + 1 over F_2[t]/(t^131 + t^8 + t^3 + t^2 + 1), factor base
V = {deg x < d} \ {0}, three summands, generated by
`research/nagao_relations/solver_16/weil.py` (`buildS4`, `System.cmsClauses`), imported unchanged.
A = number of admissible abscissae, pair count C(A, 2), scan count 2^d - 1.

- d = 7, 8, 9, 10. d = 11 only if every planned run finished with time left (not expected).
- Per d: 8 uniform (non-decomposable) targets, then 2 planted (decomposable) targets, drawn with the
  repository's `uniformTarget` / `plantedTarget` from `random.Random(2026111600 + d)`. New seeds:
  none of these targets was seen in pilot B.
- Every arm uses the same targets. Solvers are deterministic; one clause order per instance.

## Arms

| arm | instance | solve |
|---|---|---|
| a | `buildS4(..).cmsClauses()` as is | one free solve |
| b | a + pilot B's `railify` (for each AND gate t = a & rest and each input a: r = ~a & rest, XOR t + r + rest = 0) | one free solve |
| b2 | "quad-rail": b, and for each such (gate, input a) view also r2 = a & ~rest and r3 = ~a & ~rest with the XORs t + r2 + a = 0, r3 + r + a = 1, r3 + r2 + rest = 1 (each rail defined once) | one free solve |
| c-lsb | instance of b | outer loop x1 = 1 .. 2^d - 1, the d bits of x1 as assumptions, bit 0 first (pilot B's order), one incremental solver, stop at first model |
| c-msb | instance of b | same loop, assumptions bit d-1 first |
| c-gray | instance of b | x1 in reflected Gray-code order g(i) = i xor (i >> 1), i = 1 .. 2^d - 1, assumptions bit 0 first |
| L | pilot B's linear-leaf system with selector rails (x1 and u) | same loop as c-lsb |
| pair | plain pair enumeration: all unordered pairs of factor-base points and signs, test x(R -+ P_i -+ P_j) in V and admissible; standalone C (`pairenum.c`, batch inversion), and pilot B's Python `pairDecision` as cross-check | - |

Solver configurations:

- **cms-default**: pycryptosat 5.18.0, `threads=1`, default options. This is what pilot B ran
  (its `dr.CMS` passes no options), so "default" and "pilot B's options" are one configuration.
- **cms-gauss**: the same with `options={"autodisablegauss": "0", "sls": "0"}`, the options of the
  toy pilot that pilot B lists as not covered. An option the binding rejects is dropped and named.
  Arms a, b, b2 only.
- **cadical**: python-sat CaDiCaL 1.9.5 (`cadical195`; 1.5.3 if budgets fail) on the same formula
  with every XOR cut into chunks of at most 4 variables (fresh chaining variables) and expanded to
  clauses. Arms a, b, b2 only. Control on "the effect is CryptoMiniSat's Gauss engine".

Conflicts: CryptoMiniSat's own per-call count from its verbose line (pilot B's instrument);
CaDiCaL's `accum_stats()['conflicts']`.

## Coverage and caps (fixed now; time box about 90 minutes wall, each process <= 10 minutes)

Each process has a 9.5-minute deadline; a target not started by then is recorded `NOT_RUN`.
A censored row counts the conflicts spent as a lower bound and is named in every table.

| d | cms-default a | cms-default b, b2 | c-lsb, c-msb, c-gray | L | cms-gauss a, b, b2 | cadical a, b, b2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | all 10, 30 s | all 10, 30 s | all 10, full scan | all 10 | all 10, 30 s | all 10, 20 s |
| 8 | all 10, 40 s | all 10, 60 s | all 10, full scan, 100 s per target | uniform 0..3 + planted | uniform 0..3, 40 s | uniform 0..2, 30 s |
| 9 | all 10, 40 s | all 10, 150 s and 5 x pairs conflicts | uniform 0, 1: 90 s per target and variant (leaves reached) | uniform 0, 1 full scan | b only, uniform 0, 1, 150 s | not run |
| 10 | 8 uniform, 30 s | see staging | uniform 0: 90 s per variant | uniform 0: first 128 leaves | not run | not run |

Staging for d = 10 (decided now): if at d = 9 the median target of arm b or of arm b2 is decided
under its cap, that arm runs at d = 10 on 8 uniform + 2 planted targets with 150 s and 5 x pairs
conflicts per target; an arm whose d = 9 median is censored runs at d = 10 with a 45 s cap on the
8 uniform targets (lower bounds only). Scan leaves are capped at 20,000 conflicts each.

So the d >= 9 scan arms are leaf-rate measurements on 1 or 2 targets, not full decisions.

## Metrics

- Per (d, arm, configuration), non-decomposable targets: mean and median conflicts per target, as
  multiples of the pair count C(A, 2) and of A. With censored targets the mean is a lower bound;
  the median is a lower bound unless more than half of the targets are decided below it.
- Conflicts per leaf for c-lsb, c-msb, c-gray, L (total conflicts over leaves run); for b and b2
  the derived figure conflicts / (2^d - 1). Least-squares slope of log2(conflicts per leaf) on d,
  "bits per d", over the uncensored cells, with the censored cells' bounds stated next to it.
- Order sensitivity of c: ratios of conflicts per leaf between c-lsb, c-msb, c-gray on the same
  targets and leaves.
- Wall time per target for every arm against the C pair enumeration (decision loop time; build
  time and process start reported separately) and against the Python pair enumeration. Instance
  construction time of the SAT arms is reported separately. Shared machine: wall times are coarse.

## Pre-registered rule

- **rails persist**: arm b or arm b2 (cms-default) is uncensored at d = 9 and at d = 10, with
  median conflicts <= 4 x the pair count at both, and >= 10 x below arm a's lower bound (arm a's
  median, a lower bound if censored) at both.
- **rails do not persist**: at d = 10 the best rail arm's median (b, b2) exceeds 10 x the pair
  count, or is within 3 x of arm a.
- otherwise **inconclusive**.

"Uncensored" for an arm at a d: at least 5 of the 8 uniform targets decided, so the median is a
measured value. A censored median is a lower bound: it can establish "exceeds", never "<=" or
"within". A ratio against a censored arm a uses arm a's lower bound, so "within 3 x of arm a" can
be established only if arm a is decided. No threshold, cap or arm is changed after the first measured row.

## Prediction

Arm a censored at d >= 9 (pilot B: >= 40 x pairs in 45 s). Arm b at d = 9 near the cap: decided on
some targets at 2 to 6 x the pair count, so its median may be censored; b2 has about twice the
variables and XOR rows of b and is expected slower per conflict and censored earlier in time, with
conflicts per target not far from b. Scan arms: conflicts per leaf rise by about 3 to 4 bits per d
(31 to 390 from d = 8 to 9 in pilot B); c-msb and c-gray within a factor 3 of c-lsb, except that
the Gray order may be cheaper. L stays at 1.5 to 3 conflicts per leaf. d = 10 rail arms censored.
CaDiCaL: rails give little or no reduction. Expected verdict: inconclusive (censoring at d = 10),
with the evidence pointing to "do not persist".

## Correctness gates (a failed gate voids the cell)

1. Own arithmetic (`dr.FastField`) agrees with the repository's `field.Pb` on random products.
2. Ground truth for every target: C pair enumeration; equal to pilot B's Python `pairDecision`
   on every target at d <= 9 and on 2 targets at d = 10. Planted targets contain the planted
   triple; uniform targets have none.
3. Every SAT model lifts to curve points whose signed sum is exactly R (own arithmetic), passes
   the repository's `weil.verifyProjected`, and is one of the triples found by pair enumeration.
4. Every decided answer of every arm, configuration and engine equals the ground truth.
5. Rail soundness: for one uniform and one planted instance at d = 7, every XOR added by `railify`
   and by the quad-rail pass is checked implied (gate clauses + rail definitions + negated XOR is
   UNSAT, one SAT call each); a 500-XOR random sample at d = 9; the railed clause and XOR lists
   start with the original lists unchanged; every added clause contains a fresh variable.
6. XOR cutting for CaDiCaL: the cut formula's answers equal the ground truth (gate 4).
