The common scalar eliminates the finite mode proliferation caused by the
entry-dependent normalizer. At equal precision it has fewer admissible tuples
in every one of the 2,616 registered cells for each representation. This is
component evidence, not measured execution speed. All findings here have
\texttt{[pcc:unverified]} standing; no independent signature is supplied.

Let $L=2^a$, $S=\{-L,\ldots,L-1\}$, $C(T)=\prod_{s\in S}(T-s)$,
$B_d(T)=C(T)/((T-d)C'(0))$, interpreted as its punctured polynomial, and
$\rho_d=B_d(d)$. Compare $B_d$, $B_d^2/\rho_d$, and
\[R(T)B_d(T)^2,\qquad R(T)=\sum_{s\in S} B_s(T)/\rho_s^2.\]
At each source node, $R(s)=1/\rho_s$, so all original node values survive.
\texttt{CommonScalar.node\_preserved} proves this for the actual kernel.

\section*{What the origin constraint permits}
Zero is itself a source node. Preserving all node values therefore retains
the identity at zero. The actual matrix theorem is
\texttt{CommonScalar.matrix\_origin}. A value-preserving jet correction
$W_{\rm new}=W+CH$ satisfies $W_{\rm new}(0)=W(0)$ when $C(0)=0$.
Jet completion may change derivatives, but cannot replace this origin value.

\section*{Selected successor}
Replace the polynomial normalizer by one common rational denominator:
\[D(T)=\sum_s B_s(T)=\frac{C'(T)}{C'(0)},\qquad
 U_d(T)=\frac{B_d(T)^2}{D(T)}.\]
At a node, $D(s)=\rho_s$ is a nonzero two-adic unit and $U_d(s)=B_d(s)$.
The node statements have actual-kernel Lean proofs. For a finite homogeneous
contraction with exactly $M$ factors per term and $D\ne0$,
\[D^M\sum_j w_j\prod_k U_{j,k}
   =\sum_j w_j\prod_k B_{j,k}^2.\]
\texttt{RationalScalar.clear\_homogeneous\_sum} proves the general identity.
The cleared numerator has degree at most $M(4L-2)$, rather than $M(6L-3)$
for the polynomial scalar. The formal degree lemma is generic; its actual
puncture-polynomial bridge remains open. This rational candidate has not
been run as an experimental arm.

\section*{Next proof and construction costs}
At chart $t+\alpha z$, $\alpha=2L$, every $\alpha/(t-u)$, $u\ne t$,
is even and two-adically integral. The explicit formulas are
\begin{align*}
B_t(t+\alpha z)&=\rho_t\prod_{u\ne t}
 (1+\alpha z/(t-u)),\\
B_s(t+\alpha z)&=\rho_t\frac{\alpha z}{t-s}
 \prod_{u\ne t,s}(1+\alpha z/(t-u))\quad(s\ne t).
\end{align*}
Thus $D$ has unit constant coefficient and even higher coefficients;
its reciprocal formal series is integral. This is the written coefficientwise
argument; it is not yet a completed Lean series theorem. The pointwise
\texttt{kernel\_valuation} theorem explicitly assumes a unit denominator.

The proof programme is: formalize this actual local-ring bridge; connect
homogeneous clearing and the source all-level finite-precision recovery;
prove uniform all-level mode bounds; then charge the full recursive evaluator.
A point evaluation of $D$ by product/derivative accumulation costs $O(L)$ ring
operations, not a free scalar oracle. Truncated series, inverses, basis
construction, child entry queries, bit arithmetic and recovery all need charges.
A constant valuation shift alone does not establish a changed asymptotic exponent.
See \path{PLAN.md} for milestones and rejection tests.
