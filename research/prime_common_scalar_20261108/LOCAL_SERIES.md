## Denominator, inverse and rational kernel

Let P_m=sum_s localBase_(m,s). localDenominator_eval identifies it with the
actual D(m-L+Nz), where D(T)=sum_s B_s(T). Its constant coefficient is the
explicit unit rhoUnit, since only s=m contributes at z=0. Standard formal
power-series inversion over A constructs Q_m with

`P_m * Q_m = Q_m * P_m = 1`.

The central actual_integral_denominator_inverse theorem states existence of
this A-polynomial representative with the all-rational-z identity and this
A-series inverse. It does not ask the caller to supply a denominator-unit or
coefficient-integrality assumption. Every rational coefficient of Q_m therefore
has two-adic norm<=1. [pcc:unverified]

The local rational kernel series is W_(m,s)=localBase_(m,s)^2 * Q_m. Its defining
identity is `P_m * W_(m,s)=localBase_(m,s)^2`, and every coefficient is in A.
Together with localBase_eval and localDenominator_eval, these are the actual
formal expansion of B_s^2/D. This proves formal-series integrality, not
convergence of an infinite series at arbitrary rational z. [pcc:unverified]
