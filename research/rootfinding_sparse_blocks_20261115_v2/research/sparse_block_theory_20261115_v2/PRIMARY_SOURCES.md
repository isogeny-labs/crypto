# Primary-source overlap review

Reviewed 2026-11-15 through direct publisher pages and the original Tarjan PDF. This is a targeted overlap check, not an exhaustive novelty search. No external source code was executed.

- A. L. Dulmage and N. S. Mendelsohn, *Coverings of Bipartite Graphs*, Canadian Journal of Mathematics 10 (1958), 517-534, [DOI 10.4153/CJM-1958-052-0](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1958-052-0). The [publisher page](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/coverings-of-bipartite-graphs/413735C5888AB542B92D0C4F402800B1) verifies bibliographic details and describes decomposition into a core and irreducible parts. Graph matching/decomposition therefore cannot be presented as this project's new theorem. The full article was not downloaded/read in this cycle; no specific implementation detail is attributed to it.
- Robert Tarjan, *Depth-First Search and Linear Graph Algorithms*, SIAM Journal on Computing 1(2) (1972), 146-160, [DOI 10.1137/0201010](https://doi.org/10.1137/0201010). The [publisher abstract](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/0201010) and [original PDF hosted at CMU](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~cdm/resources/Tarjan1972-sccs.pdf) establish linear-in-vertices-and-edges strongly connected component algorithms. SCC computation is established machinery, not a rootfinding novelty claim.

Existing local prior work was inspected without execution: research/advance/fiber_solver.py and GENERAL_METHOD.md already use finite-domain specialization, exact coefficient-row-span caching, free-coordinate accounting and terminal univariate gcd. Their documented worst case is exponential. The new direction worth falsifying is whether guarded multiplicity elimination exposes smaller exact blocks or repeatable residual families on the actual input, with total costs charged. The all-roots block-composition proof in PROOF.md is a direct argument; it does not claim that the cited graph papers prove a finite-field rootfinding speedup.
