## Question and implementation boundary

Can the already fixed compact global model from round 01 lift an arbitrary
encoded source point to its unique r-torsion branch modulo p^N, without the
source scalar, an enumerated lift table, or expanding 2^r?

Implement the classical normalized division-polynomial recurrences using
memoized value/derivative pairs, Newton lifting of the x-root, and Newton
lifting of the source ordinate. Project compact coefficients directly modulo
p^N with GMP modular exponentiation. The evaluator receives the model,
coordinates and N; it has no scalar argument. The identity maps to identity.

Independent arithmetic path: Jacobian addition and doubling on the short
Weierstrass model over Z/p^N. Check r times each lift is the identity. For
small fixtures compare the lift of [k]P to [k]lift(P), with k confined to the
auditor. OpenSSL separately checks source points and group operations.

## Fixed inputs and observables

- Round-one source p=17, r=19, y²=x³+2x+2, P=(5,1).
- Silverman source p=257, r=83, y²=x³+23x+11, P=(7,1).
- Public secp256k1, all four points generated by SHA256 of the literal
  strings `prime-field-transfer/round-02/public-point/0` through `/3`,
  incrementing x modulo p until x³+7 is a square; choose the smaller root.
  No generating scalar is supplied or retained. OpenSSL checks membership
  and order. This is a component-size correctness control, not a measured
  ECDLP experiment or a standard-panel performance comparison.

Precisions N={1,2,4,8,16,32,64}. Test all 18 and 82 nonidentity points of
the two small subgroups, and all four public secp256k1 points. For each lift
record a digest of canonical coordinates, successful torsion and reduction
checks, recurrence-node count, and Newton rounds. At N=64 also check
negation and adding the fixed source generator on the secp256k1 inputs.
The finite small-subgroup enumeration belongs exclusively to validation;
the evaluator never queries it.

Controls: perturb x by p at N>=2 (must fail the division-root test); choose
the other y-root (must fail the encoded source-ordinate check). Check the
division recurrence against independently implemented source/Jacobian group
identities, including n=1,2,3,4 and r. Reject malformed/off-curve inputs.

Prediction: prime-to-p torsion lifting succeeds with memoized recurrence
size growing with the bit length of r, despite huge dense polynomial degree.
This would implement a useful local component and specifically refute using
dense field degree alone as a bound on local evaluation. It would not give
the missing cross-prime transport.
