## Sparse control: readable argument

Let ports be integers in [0,2^a). The sparse extension connects distinct x,y only when both their lower u bits and their quotient by 2^(u+1) agree. At a=u+1 the quotient condition is automatic; each residue class has exactly two ports, recovering the previous paired origin. [pcc:unverified]

When a>=u+2, use x=0 and y=2^(u+1). They have the same lower u bits but different block quotients, so the sparse origin entry is zero. Since x!=y, K=W+D(S-I) has Kxy=Wxy. ## Dense successor: readable identity

For all distinct ports sharing the lower u bits, define

    Uxy = product_{j<u} (2^j/(2^j-(y-x)))^(m_j)
    Sxy = -Uxy,

and set Sxy=0 otherwise. Keep the same original completed W, diagonal D=W00, and K=W+D(S-I). Arbitrary natural multiplicities are allowed. [pcc:unverified]

All retained mask bits agree, so the actual correction product equals its diagonal polynomial times the explicit scalar Uxy. Applying the original nodal/puncture law gives

    (T-(y-x))*Wxy(T) = T*D(T)*Uxy.

For these distinct matching ports Kxy=Wxy-D*Uxy. Subtracting gives the checked, denominator-free identity

    (T-(y-x))*Kxy(T) = (y-x)*D(T)*Uxy.

This statement holds at arbitrary a; it does not assume the minimal two-port residue-class size. The actual Uxy is OneModTwo by the existing anchor-ratio and product laws. No inverse is assumed or proved here. [pcc:unverified]
