# Prime-field factor-base index calculus: what the campaign established (evidence index, 2026-11-07)

**Objective (as set):** a reproducible improvement in prime-field factor-base index calculus over the strongest implemented baseline; fresh curve/target holdouts; construction, collection, elimination, extraction, lifting and verification costs accounted before claiming; independently checked evidence.

**Reading used here.** The repository's success criterion (AGENTS.md, owner 2026-10-15) counts every verified, measured improvement over the current index-calculus baseline as a success and treats beating Pollard rho as the long-term goal, reached iteratively; relabelling and accounting changes do not count. Everything below is local (unisolated host), at toy sizes (group order 2^14 to 2^25), unvalidated in the repository's gate sense (`pre-gate`: no pcc claim id, no `VALIDATION.json`, no isolab-eks run, no S3 checkpoint or ecdb entry because this container has no AWS identity or ecdb access, no leaderboard row).

## A. Improvements over the implemented IC baseline (same arithmetic, same binary)

Baselines: the shipped default of `ic solve-prime-file` (fb 8, 64 extra relations; 708.5 ms median solver time on the final panel), and, as the strongest implemented variant, the shipped solver retuned over factor-base size and with its per-call x-index hoisted (a control I added, `IC_HOIST_SWEEP=1`; the default path is unchanged). Cost units: solver `total_ms` (factor-base construction, relation collection including table build, elimination, extraction, verification; no lifting for prime-order groups) and process wall time.

Superseded intermediate conclusion (kept for the record; see the latest measurement below): after the post-merge validator replay (`results/walk_postmerge/VALIDATOR_REPORT.md`) the walk, whose own code still ran the slow point path, was not a supported improvement beyond a retuned table. What is supported, as measurements on the solver timer and on process wall time (local, unisolated host, group order up to 2^17, pre-gate):
- The pair-sum table (with main's new relation point) over the best retuned and hoisted sweep: 0.260x solver, 0.593x end to end at the frozen fb 48; a table retuned to fb 16 on the merged tree is about 2.3x lower again (post-hoc probe on the tune set, then on the evaluation instances; not a new frozen holdout).
- The walk over the best hoisted sweep still reproduces (0.152x [0.131, 0.173] solver, 0.537x [0.477, 0.613] end to end; 5.8x to 7.6x and 1.63x to 2.10x), but on the merged tree it comes from the new relation point and a smaller table, not from the walk: a retuned table beats the walk (walk / table at fb 16 = 1.45x solver [1.39, 1.58], about 1.01x end to end; the walk wins 19 of 120 pairs). (That code path was later moved to the fast point path, see below.)
- Versus the shipped default configuration the lowest-time variant is now about 76x lower in solver time and 11x end to end (seed 1 only, v5 evaluation instances); the earlier "about 200x" and "about 8x / 1.9x" figures were measured before the merge and are superseded.
- Before the merge, with the old arithmetic, the walk was an independently replayed improvement over the table (0.249x) and over the sweep (0.124x); that result (`results/walk/VALIDATOR_REPORT.md`) is a statement about the old binary.

Part of every gain above is baseline waste found and removed (hoisting, the relation surplus) or other sessions' arithmetic work; the table is the structural part. The lever named here was then applied (next paragraph).

**Latest measurement (merged baseline, fresh tune/eval with provenance; `RESULTS_merged_baseline.md`; independently replayed and validated as a measurement, `results/merged/VALIDATOR_REPORT.md`, with the qualifiers listed there):** with the table and walk moved to the repository's fast point path, 60 never-used instances x 2 seeds, 480/480 recovered, frozen settings interior to wide grids: table / best hoisted sweep = 0.066x [0.058, 0.072] solver and 0.546x end to end; walk / best hoisted sweep = 0.026x [0.022, 0.029] solver and 0.534x end to end; shipped default / walk about 500x in solver time and 12x end to end. Qualifiers from the validator: the sweep arm still runs the slow constant-time field arithmetic (a u64 sweep probe is 2.1x lower, so against it the table is about 7.6x and the walk about 20x lower in solver time and 1.5x end to end); most of the change from the previous panel is the new arms using the fast point path, not a new algorithm; the end-to-end 1.9x sits at the ceiling set by a fixed 23 ms process overhead; the table build now needs 2 additions per pair instead of 4, so the single-target distance to the matched rho is nearer 22x to 49x than 25x to 53x (the multi-target 1.40x to 1.53x against BSGS is unchanged). The strict-reading gaps to rho and BSGS remain.

**Latest measurement against the strongest implemented sweep (word-sized sweep, `IC_FAST_SWEEP=1`; `RESULTS_fast_baseline.md`; not yet independently replayed):** fresh instances (v7), tune/eval frozen and verified on the remote before the run, 600/600 recovered, provenance in every row. Table / word sweep = 0.162x [0.133, 0.179] solver and 0.747x end to end; walk / word sweep = 0.055x [0.047, 0.062] solver and 0.703x end to end; both 120/120 pairs; shipped default / walk about 600x solver and 13x end to end. Counted-operation gaps to rho and BSGS unchanged.

**Correction of the baseline (validator, `results/fast/VALIDATOR_REPORT.md`):** the repository contains a stronger certified IC solver, `prime_fast::ic_solve` (native Montgomery arithmetic, batched inversion, direct-subtraction sweep, pair table for 3-term decompositions), unreachable from `ic solve-prime-file` and not used in the panels above. All "strongest implemented sweep" and "strongest implemented baseline" statements in this file refer to the strongest solver reachable through the CLI's BigUint stack. In the validator's exploratory probe, `prime_fast::ic_solve` is 12.6x lower than the word sweep; against it the table was 1.89x higher and the walk 1.46x lower. The section above on the table and the walk therefore does not establish an improvement over the strongest implemented IC baseline. The comparison on `prime_fast` itself is in `RESULTS_prime_fast_table.md`: the table-lookup mode is 1.25x to 1.46x slower than `ic_solve` summands 3 on all 5 fresh instances (negative result).

## B. What is not established (and what was withdrawn)

- **No improvement over rho or other generic attacks.** Single target: the IC walk costs at least 25x to 53x the repository's matched negation-map rho in counted group operations at 20 to 24 bits, widening with size (`RESULTS_vs_rho.md`; validated as a correction, with the 22-bit S corrected to a measured 0.93 to 1.11). Many targets: against the strongest counted generic arm the multi-target IC costs 1.40x to 1.53x at price 0 and up to 2.19x at price 1, cheaper in 0 of 22 configurations in every cell (`results/bsgs_large_T/analysis.txt`, complete symmetric ledger; the earlier "unranked / wins at price 1" claim was a ledger artifact and is withdrawn).
- **Withdrawn claims** (kept in the files with correction notes): wall-time "parity with rho" against the in-binary Floyd rho (a weak, differently-implemented reference); "table 4 to 16x rho"; "IC wins 22/22 at price 1".
- **Scope limits:** toy sizes only; local unisolated host and the solver's own stage timer; the final panels use my own group-addition counters (checked against an instrumented library by the validator); the exponent of the IC (about p^(2/3) with the table; measured 0.72 to 0.73 in time vs rho's 0.5) means the gap to the matched rho widens with size, so nothing here suggests a crossover; the j = 0 symmetry lead was evaluated by a cost model only (it helps the generic attacks more than the IC).
- **Repository gates not met:** replay certificate, VALIDATION.json, matched-rho band at 0 / 0.1 / 1 GAE in the repository's own harness, pcc claim, bench record, isolab-eks run, ecdb and S3 checkpoint.
