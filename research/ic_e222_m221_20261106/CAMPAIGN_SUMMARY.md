## D. Handoff at the end of the 2026-11-07 to 2026-11-11 sessions (stop report required by AGENTS.md "Persistent scientific research loop")

**Objective (user `/goal`, 2026-11-07):** a reproducible improvement in prime-field factor-base index calculus over the strongest implemented baseline, with fresh holdouts, full cost accounting, and independently checked evidence; keep going until met or the user stops it.

**State.** Verified, replayed improvements exist only against IC baselines (A and A2 above): repeated `ic_solve` (78x to 122x measured at T = 200), the project's own earlier multi-target arms (sweep -> table collector 1.5x to 2.8x, batched per-target phase 1.8x at T = 10 000, batched setup collection, bucketed pair table 1.5x to 2.3x pooled), and, on the CLI stack, the table/walk/word-sweep results. Against the strongest generic competitors there is no improvement in any regime tested (25 to 35 bits, T = 1000 and 10 000): about 2x to 4x behind the tuned multi-target BSGS (the BSGS arm itself 1.1x to 1.3x slower than a compacted variant), no better than a tie with a lightly tuned multi-target rho (the small-size win over the untuned rho was withdrawn by the validator), 15x to 528x behind single-target rho. The single-target table-lookup mode is 1.25x to 1.46x slower than `ic_solve`.

**Why more constant-tuning will not close it (counted-operation reasoning, not a measurement; `RESULTS_prime_fast_multi_size.md`).** Setup about p^(2/3), per target about p^(1/3)/c^2; BSGS about sqrt(T n); the exponents meet at T ~ p^(1/3) so the IC is at best a constant factor from BSGS; k-term or smaller-factor-base variants do not lower the exponent. This is the multi-target form of the Gaudry-Diem barrier.

**Alternatives investigated (all recorded in the RESULTS files):** factor-base size/constant per T and size; 2-term, 3-term and table collectors; pair-table layouts (sorted, bucketed, x-only additions: null); batched setup and per-target phases; walk restarts; multi-target amortisation; comparison against repeated `ic_solve`, tuned BSGS and a multi-target DP rho; scaling to 35 bits.

**Not tried / open (each would change the object, not a constant):** (1) the order-3 automorphism on j = 0 curves (p = 1 mod 3): an orbit factor base of size fb/3 and x-only relations modulo the orbit; it speeds a rho by about sqrt(6) too, so it cannot close a gap that is growing with the size; (2) a different decomposition (Semaev S4 with a larger summand count) or a non-prime-field structure, outside the stated prime-field scope; (3) a negation map plus the validator's tuning on the rho arm (would only widen the gap); (4) research-kb prior-art check (the tools were not available in these sessions).

**Blocker preventing independently checked evidence in this container:** no AWS identity (isolab-eks, S3 checkpoints, bench records), no pcc signing key (claim ids, standing, `VALIDATION.json`), no ecdb access, no research-kb tools; the validators ran on the same host. The repository's validation gate (replay certificate, validator report, matched rho reference with the §5a band, named by `VALIDATION.json`) therefore cannot be completed here. Every result in this file carries `[pcc:unverified]`.

## E. The j=0 symmetry line, 2026-11-11 to 2026-11-14 (all [pcc:unverified]; same host, 1 thread, toy sizes 26-30 bits)
Evidence: `RESULTS_prime_fast_j0_sym.md` (all sections), `results/pf_j0/` (raw rows, frozen holdout protocol, `VALIDATOR_REPORT.md`), examples `prime_fast_j0_{sym,multi,rho,rho_multi,bsgs_multi}.rs`.

**The one verified improvement over the implemented baseline.** On j=0 prime-field curves, a factor base of order-3-automorphism orbit representatives with 3-summand relations and an orbit-keyed pair table (the "symmetric 3-summand IC") is about **2.0x-2.4x faster than the strongest implemented single-target IC, 3-summand `ic_solve`** at 26-30 bits (frozen protocol before the holdout; independent validator re-ran it: 490/490 independent certificates pass, 540/540 solves correct; fresh primes 2.1x-2.6x). Total time counts factor base, pair table, collection, elimination and verification. Withdrawn from the first draft: the 1.87x-2.93x range (mixed run pairings) and the 2-summand symmetric variant (15x-20x slower than the 3-summand baseline).
Limits the validator found: the freeze commit did not pin the source; the baseline c grid missed its optimum (~0.7; ratio vs c=0.7 about 2.1x); holdout curves at one size share p and n (one twist group); no isolation, no A/A interval; not leaderboard-eligible.

**Everything else on this line is negative or unranked:**
- Pair-table x-shift fix, rotated keys, fingerprinted linear-probe table, batched collector, B retune: no gain; the symmetric multi-target setup stays at about 0.75x of the batched baseline's setup (collection-dominated, per-candidate field arithmetic).
- Native x6-folded rho (with the short-cycle escape: steps reach sqrt(pi n/12)) ties `rho_parallel` at 26 bits; the symmetric single-target IC is 3.5x-5x behind the matched rho at 26 bits and about 21x behind at 30 bits.
- Multi-target: the symmetric IC beats an *unbatched* folded rho (a mismatch; withdrawn), loses to the batched folded rho for T <= 1000 and is 2.0x-2.5x ahead of it only at T=3000 (28-30 bits). The decisive comparator, a x6-folded multi-target BSGS with batched giant steps, is **3.4x-6.5x faster than the symmetric multi-target IC and 1.6x-15x faster than the batched folded rho in every cell** (26-30 bits, T=20-3000). No regime in the campaign has an IC arm ahead of the strongest implemented comparator.

**Why, and what a successor must do.** The setup costs about p^(2/3), the per-target cost grows with the pair-table work, and BSGS costs about sqrt(T n); symmetry gives the IC a constant factor (and gives rho and BSGS the same sqrt(6)). Constants do not close an exponent gap, in line with the Gaudry-Diem barrier. A further lead has to change the exponent or the relation yield per scan (single-large-prime relations; decomposition into more summands with a cheaper per-relation test), not the layout.
