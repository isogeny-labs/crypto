Multi-target distinguished-point rho on the native engine (examples/prime_fast_rho_multi.rs): r-adding walks (32 steps, shared table of multiples of G), start points u*G + Q_t, DP collisions between walks of different targets link the logs (k1 = +-k2 + d); an anchor target Q_0 = G (log 1) fixes a component; signed union-find; stop when all T targets are determined; all logs verified k G = Q.
Prediction written before the build/run: at 27 bits and T = 1000 the multi-target rho takes between 1x and 3x of the tuned multi-target BSGS time (no negation map; one thread). If it is slower than 3x or fails to resolve all targets, the rho reference is weak and I say so.

First run result (recorded after the fact): prediction FAILED. 27 bits, T = 1000: 872/1000 (res 3) and 877/1000 (res 1) targets resolved after 147 million steps (400 x sqrt(n T)), 9.3 to 9.5 s, against about 0.2 s for the tuned BSGS. Every resolved log verified (872/872, 877/877): the collision logic is sound; the walkers never restart, so merged trails repeat the same distinguished points and produce no new links.
Second version prediction (written before the rerun): with a restart at a fresh random start (Q_t + A_i + B_j from two tables of 4096 random multiples) after every distinguished point, all 1000 targets resolve and the run takes between 1x and 5x of the tuned multi-target BSGS time at 27 bits, T = 1000.
