# Guarded solving degree and regularity

This follow-up to merged [PR #2859](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/pull/2859)
separates three invariants of the actual guarded three-abscissa systems.
It adds Lean proofs and a design for the queued exhaustive prime/binary study.
**No exhaustive campaign has run in this follow-up.**

| Presentation | Last fall | Closed-Macaulay solving degree | BFS degree of regularity |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Original prime-field system, original UNSAT/curve hypotheses | Exactly 5 (published proof) | Exactly 5 | No finite value |
| Same prime system plus all five explicit field equations | Exactly 5 | Exactly 5 | At most `5(p-1)+1` |
| Ordinary-binary six-input system | Not proved in this follow-up | Not proved in this follow-up | No finite value |
| Binary system plus all five explicit field equations | Not proved in this follow-up | Not proved in this follow-up | At most `5(q-1)+1` |

Here the polynomial ring is `k[u,x,y,z,v]`. The six raw inputs are
`g(x), g(y), g(z), S3(x,y,u), S3(u,z,r), v(x-y)-1`, where
`g(T)=(T-a)(T-b)(T-c)`. The binary formula is
`S3(s,t,w)=(st+sw+tw)^2+stw+a6`; in characteristic two the guard is
`v(x+y)+1`. Ordinary total degrees are used, with no implicit Boolean
reduction or inserted field equations.

The regularity column uses the Bardet–Faugère–Salvy definition: the least
`d` for which every degree-`d` homogeneous polynomial belongs to the ideal
generated by the inputs' highest homogeneous components. The raw systems
fail the finiteness hypothesis of that definition. This is **not** a claim
that Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity is infinite.

[DegreeInvariants.lean](../guarded_fall_degree_20261028/formal/DegreeInvariants.lean)
proves the graded obstructions, the field-equation controls, and sharp
closed-Macaulay solving/last-fall statements. The solving predicate explicitly
requires a Gröbner basis under the leading-monomial divisibility criterion
inside the actual bounded-degree closure. It does not formalize a particular
F4/F5 implementation or its observed degree schedule. The classical solving
degree corollary for degree-compatible orders follows from Caminata–Gorla,
Theorem 3.1, and the published exact last fall; that literature bridge is
explained in [REPORT.md](REPORT.md), not claimed as a new Lean formalization
of a specific matrix algorithm.
