Let F=X^3+rX^2+sX+t and G=hX+k on Y^2+XY=X^3+AX^2+B. Choose t=z^3+rz^2+sz+(hz+k)y at a target (z,y) on the curve. Its norm N=F^2+XFG+(X^3+AX^2+B)G^2 is divisible by X+z. At fixed h,k, changing r by a changes F by a(X+z)^2; changing s by b changes F by b(X+z). In characteristic two, deltaN=(deltaF)^2+XG deltaF, with no cross terms between these changes. Dividing by X+z gives deltaH_r=a^2(X+z)^3+aXG(X+z), deltaH_s=b^2(X+z)+bXG. Expanding yields precisely the six direct coefficient columns in compiler.py. Squaring is F2-linear, so these columns plus one base norm reconstruct every r/s norm, not merely each sampled point.

The field, parameter order, coefficient packing, row order and Gaussian elimination are unchanged. Identical base/columns imply byte-identical affine particular/basis/rank and native scanner input, including inconsistent fibers. Degenerate h/k/z, even extension degrees and trace-one contexts require no divisions in the direct columns. The generic base-norm routine retains its existing preconditions. Complexity: one generic base norm plus O(n) exact field operations for2n columns, and the existing packing/linear solve cost; O(n) field elements for columns. No root/support/factorization/original-chain logic is changed. This binary implementation does not establish an odd-characteristic analogue or a performance claim.
