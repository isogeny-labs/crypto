# Protocol: bitop/v1 exponents on a 16–40-bit prime ladder, index calculus with a per-size base

Frozen 2026-10-19, before any train or hold-out session of this study ran.
Changes after the first session are listed under "Deviations" with their
reason. A costing pilot (one 24-, 32- and 36-bit curve, two targets, seed 99,
discarded and not committed) ran before this file was frozen; it chose nothing
below except dropping `mitm-prime-batch:m=2`, which priced identically to
`mitm:negation_folded=1` on every pilot run, and raising `max_trials`, because
the default of 10⁸ exhausted the 36-bit pair-table run.

## Question

The bitop/v1 study (`research/bitop_unit_20261016/`, PR #1970) fitted
exponents with intervals of about ±0.1 from 88 runs per arm on 16–26-bit
curves. All its index-calculus arms ran at a fixed base (32 or 128 abscissae).
This study asks two questions:

1. **Step 1, precision.** With about 2,000 runs per arm on 25 curves from 16
   to 40 bits, what are the exponents of rho, BSGS, kangaroo and index
   calculus in bitop/v1, and how narrow are their intervals?
2. **Step 2, the fair test.** When index calculus chooses its base size per
   curve size on a separate train panel, what is its exponent? Does it bend
   toward ½, or stay where the fixed base left it (MITM b32: 0.766
   [0.600, 0.909] in additions)?

It makes no security claim about any deployed curve. Public synthetic curves
and hash-to-subgroup targets only.

## Curves

[`curves.jsonl`](curves.jsonl): the deterministic `a = −3` prime-order curve
of `prime_fast::find_a3_curve` for every bit size from 16 to 40 (25 curves),
printed by `examples/ic_frontier_prime_curves.rs --bits 16,…,40`. The 20-,
24-, 28-, 32-, 36- and 40-bit curves are those of
`research/ic_frontier_campaign_20261015/prime_curves.jsonl`, byte for byte.
(`prime_search`, used by the bitop/v1 ladder, does not finish at 28 bits.)

## Arms

| arm | method | sizes | role |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| `rho-neg` | `rho.negation` | 16–40 | reference |
| `rho-neg-aa` | `rho.negation` | 16–40 | A/A control |
| `bsgs-neg` | `bsgs.negation` | 16–40 | generic (positive control) |
| `kangaroo` | `kangaroo.vow` | 16–40 | generic (positive control) |
| `ic-pair-rule` | `ic.pipeline`, `prime-abscissa:size=N(bits)` + `mitm:negation_folded=1` | 16–40 | candidate, base per size from the train panel |
| `ic-pair-b32` | `ic.pipeline`, `prime-abscissa:size=32` + `mitm:negation_folded=1` | 16–28 | continuity with the bitop/v1 ladder's fixed base |
| `ic-rootfind-m3-rule` | `ic.pipeline`, `prime-abscissa:size=N(bits)` + `rootfind-subtract:m=3` | 16–28 | candidate, base per size from the train panel |

Every `ic.pipeline` arm sets `max_trials` to `1000000000000`; the run ends by
solving, by the timeout, or by exhausting that cap, and each end is its own
status. The root-finding arm stops at 28 bits because the pilot measured it at
about 3,000× the floor and 7 s a run at 32 bits; the fixed-b32 arm stops at
28 bits because a 32-abscissa base is far from any size's optimum above that.

## Phase A: train panel (base-size selection)

- Target seed `20261019100`, `target_kind: public`. 8 targets a curve to 34
  bits and 4 targets a curve from 35 to 40 bits. One round, no warm-up,
  order `alternate`, measurement seed `20261019101`, `isolation_required: L0`,
  timeout 1,800 s an execution.
- **Pair-table grid.** Centre `G(b) = 32 · 2^(0.33 (b − 20))`, the
  interpolation of the campaign's own selection (20 bits → 32, 40 bits →
  3,251). Candidates `round(G(b) · 2^(k/2))` for `k = −4 … 2`, with duplicates
  and values below 8 removed.
- **Root-finding grid.** `{8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}` at every size 16–28.
- **Selection rule** (`select.py`, judgment-free): per curve and arm, the
  candidate with the smallest mean `bitops` (bitop/v1, `ecbench work`) among
  candidates whose train runs all verified and are all completely priced.
  If the winner is at either end of its grid, one more candidate one step
  beyond it (×2^(1/2) or ×2^(−1/2), not below 8) runs on the same targets, and
  the rule is applied again; at most three extensions a curve and arm. If no
  candidate qualifies, the arm is not run at that size, and that is reported.
- The selected sizes are written to `params.json`, committed, and only then
  are the hold-out specs generated from it.

## Phase B: hold-out ladder

- Target seed `20261019200`, `target_kind: public`, **80 targets a curve**,
  one round, no warm-up, order `alternate`, measurement seed `20261019201`,
  `isolation_required: L0`, `--cpus none`, timeout 3,600 s an execution.
  This is 2,000 runs a 16–40 arm and 1,040 runs a 16–28 arm.
- One spec a curve, `specs/holdout-P<b>.json`, generated by `make_specs.py`
  from `curves.jsonl` and `params.json`.
- `ecbench verify --replay 2` on every session.
- Bit-operation counts do not depend on the host, so sessions run at L0 and
  up to four run at once, each with its own lock. Wall time is not used.

## Measures (all from `ecbench`, none computed outside it)

- **Primary.** For each arm, `ecbench bound fit --unit bitop/v1` over all its
  hold-out sessions (`--param-rule` for the two rule arms, naming this
  protocol and `params.json`): the exponent `α` of `bitops = C·r^α` and the
  exponent in additions, each with its two-stage cluster bootstrap 95%
  interval (2,000 resamples, seed 20261005); the constant as the ratio to the
  floor. The same fit restricted to 16–26 bits for the arms the bitop/v1
  ladder had, so the interval widths compare like for like.
- **Gap to rho.** Per curve, `ecbench compare` of each IC arm against
  `rho-neg` (paired ratio of totals, cluster bootstrap 95% interval), and
  `ecbench table`.
- **§5a band.** `ecbench band` at 0, 0.1 and 1 GAE a native operation. Every
  arm is expected to price completely in bitop/v1, so the band should have
  no unpriced work to move; the band output is committed either way, and a
  ranking is stated only where the order holds across it.
- **Secondary.** `ecbench amortize` of `ic-pair-rule` against `rho-neg` on
  every curve.

## Decision rules, fixed now

- An exponent is reported only with its interval, and to the digits the
  interval's width allows.
- **Positive control.** If any generic arm's 95% interval in addition
  equivalents excludes ½ by more than 0.05, the instrument is suspect and that
  is reported before any index-calculus figure.
- **A/A control.** `rho-neg-aa` against `rho-neg` must give a paired ratio
  whose interval contains 1 on at least 23 of the 25 curves.
- **Step 2 verdict.** `ic-pair-rule` *bends toward ½* if its exponent in
  additions has an upper 95% bound below the bitop/v1 ladder's fixed-b32
  point estimate 0.766, *does not bend* if its lower bound is above 0.6, and
  is *unresolved* otherwise.
- **Success criterion** (CLAUDE.md): `ic-pair-rule` improves the
  index-calculus baseline if its exponent interval lies below `ic-pair-b32`'s
  from this same study, or if its constant is below the fixed arm's at every
  shared size with separated intervals. Every IC row reports its gap to
  `rho-neg` as distance remaining.
- An arm with an incomplete run, or a size with an unverified run, is reported
  and not admitted as a bound (ecbench's rule).

## Deviations

1. **Train panel split across cards (2026-10-19).** The first card
   (`cards/CARD-bitop_ladder-20261019-1.yaml`, all 25 train specs) was
   stopped after 30 minutes by the agent harness's limit on a background
   command, which is independent of the card's own 6-hour timeout. By then
   `train-P36` and `train-P37` were complete and verified; `train-P35`,
   `train-P38`, `train-P39` and `train-P40` were interrupted mid-session, and
   no other spec had started. The interrupted sessions were set aside
   uncommitted and are re-run unchanged from their frozen specs (same targets,
   same seeds) by a second card. The remaining specs run in a third card.
   Each card is sized to finish under the harness's 2-hour maximum, and the
   executor is dispatched with that maximum as its background timeout. No
   spec, seed, grid or rule changed.
