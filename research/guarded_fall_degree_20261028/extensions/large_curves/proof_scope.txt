Larger curves: uniform degree bounds and public UNSAT fixtures
Date: 2026-10-28

Status and purpose
------------------
This is a mathematical applicability audit and a proof of a public,
known-answer fixture construction. It is not a new field-size experiment,
timing result, independent closure computation, or Lean formalization.
Numerical fixture-generation and replay receipts, when supplied alongside
this note, certify their own explicitly stated execution scope.

Increasing a field modulus beyond a machine word requires a larger exact
arithmetic implementation. It does not change the hypotheses or degree
accounting of the already stated ordinary-ring theorems.

1. What already holds at larger field sizes
-------------------------------------------
For every prime p>3, let E/F_p be a nonsingular short Weierstrass curve
  Y^2=X^3+a*X+b.
Let S contain B distinct abscissas of affine rational points, with both
point signs admitted, and let R be an affine rational target, r=x(R).
For the explicit six-input presentation
  g(x), g(y), g(z), S3(x,y,u), S3(u,z,r), v*(x-y)-1,
  g(T)=product_(s in S)(T-s),
the established bounds have no upper restriction on the bit length of p:

* B=3, UNSAT: exact ordinary first/last pair (4,5).
* B>=3, UNSAT: ordinary first fall B+1 and
    max(5,B+1) <= d_F <= 3B-4.

Here first fall means homogeneous-top syzygies modulo Koszul syzygies, and
last fall uses ordinary total degree with the displayed explicit inputs.
The proof files are ../../proofs/universal_five_symmetric_audit.txt and
../growing_b/proof.txt. Those are mathematical derivations, not complete
Lean formalizations.

The standard curves secp256k1, secp256r1, secp384r1 and secp521r1 all have
this model over prime fields of the stated sizes. Thus a larger-curve
fixture satisfying the hypotheses is a direct application of the theorem;
its existence does not require an extrapolation from the characteristic-101
test case. The ordinary-ring variable count remains five when B=3, although
coefficient bit lengths and arithmetic costs increase.

The ordinary binary model is
  E/GF(2^n): Y^2+X*Y=X^3+a2*X^2+a6, a6!=0,
with
  S3(X,Y,Z)=(X*Y+X*Z+Y*Z)^2+X*Y*Z+a6.
The established characteristic-two theorem applies for arbitrary n. For
B=3, every UNSAT instance has ordinary first fall four and last fall
four exactly for split cube-orbit membership g(T)=T^3+c, and five otherwise.
If n is odd, a split cube orbit with three distinct roots is impossible:
3 does not divide 2^n-1, so GF(2^n) contains no nontrivial cube root of one.
Every UNSAT B=3 instance then has exact pair (4,5).

The SEC 2 binary field sizes 163,233,239,283,409,571 are all odd, and the
listed binary curves have exactly this ordinary model. They are therefore
covered by the exact (4,5) statement whenever the rational-lift and UNSAT
hypotheses hold. This extension does not claim a numerical certificate or
new closure computation on those binary standard curves. For B>=4 the
binary ordinary-ring bounds remain
  d_ff^ord=B+1,  B+1 <= d_F <= 3B-4.
See ../characteristic_two/extension_proof.txt and ../growing_b/binary_bridge.txt.

2. A nonvacuous public three-abscissa construction
-------------------------------------------------
Let E/k be either of the nonsingular curve models above. Choose a rational
point G satisfying
  [j]G != O for every integer 1<=j<=28.                 (A)
Since E(k) is finite, (A) is equivalent to ord(G)>28. The construction only
needs (A); it does not need the full group order, a primality proof of a
claimed subgroup order, or knowledge of the ambient curve's cofactor.

Define public points and the target by
  P1=G, P2=[2]G, P4=[4]G,
  S={x(P1),x(P2),x(P4)}, R=[16]G, r=x(R).
All scalars here are public construction data. This is not an unknown-
scalar target and no discrete logarithm is being computed.

The points P1,P2,P4 and R are affine by (A). Two affine points in either
model share an abscissa exactly when they are equal up to point negation.
In the prime-field model -(x,y)=(x,-y); in the ordinary binary model
-(x,y)=(x,x+y). If i,j are different members of {1,2,4}, equality of the
abscissas of [i]G and [j]G would imply
  [i-j]G=O or [i+j]G=O,
where the relevant nonzero absolute integer is at most eight. Condition
(A) excludes this. It also excludes a self-negative point among these
lifts, since [2*i]G would then be O. Thus S has three distinct abscissas,
each with the two rational point lifts +/-[i]G.

Similarly r cannot belong to S: an abscissa collision with [i]G would
give [16-i]G=O or [16+i]G=O. These are nonzero integers between 12 and 20,
again forbidden by (A).

Every sum of three signed factor-base point lifts, including repetitions,
equals [s]G for an integer s with |s|<=12. If such a sum equalled R or -R,
then
  [s-16]G=O or [s+16]G=O.
The integer concerned is nonzero and its absolute value lies between four
and 28. Negative multiples vanish exactly when their positive counterparts
do, so (A) excludes both equalities. There is therefore no signed three-
point decomposition of either target sign, even before imposing the guard.

To connect this fact to the displayed polynomial system, membership puts
x,y,z in S and the inverse guard forces x!=y. Hence the first summation
quadratic has nonzero leading coefficient and its roots are the rational
abscissas of sums/differences of the first two rational point lifts. Those
points are affine because their abscissas differ. Since z!=r, the second
summation quadratic, viewed as a polynomial in u, likewise has roots given
by the rational abscissas of sums/differences of a third factor-base point
and R. A common u would identify those points up to sign and give a signed
three-point relation to R or -R, already excluded above.

This also excludes zeros over an algebraic closure: the split membership
polynomials fix x,y,z to S, the guard fixes v rationally, and the first
quadratic already has all its roots rational. Thus no additional zero can
appear by extending the coefficient field. The ideal is the unit ideal,
and the applicable ordinary-ring theorem gives the claimed exact (4,5)
pair for prime fields p>3 and for the listed odd-degree binary fields.

3. What a fixture verifier must actually check
----------------------------------------------
A faithful numerical known-answer fixture can verify (A) without counting
the group or trusting a source-declared subgroup order:

* Bind the field and curve parameters to the named standard curve. Check
  that the supplied point G is affine, lies on that curve, and is nonzero.
  For the prime model check 4*a^3+27*b^2 !=0; for the binary model check
  a6!=0. The field interpretation still requires a valid prime field or a
  correctly represented binary extension field.
* Compute the sequential point chain [j]G, 1<=j<=28, using the actual curve
  law, and check that none is O. Checking only [28]G!=O is insufficient:
  a point of smaller order not dividing 28 could pass that weaker check.
* Bind S to the abscissas of entries 1,2,4 and R to entry 16. Check curve
  membership, distinct base abscissas, and r outside S explicitly as useful
  conformance checks, although the mathematical proof implies them.
* Reconstruct g and both S3 inputs from those exact parameters. Bind the
  reconstructed six-input polynomial system to the canonical problem
  digest used by any sparse proof-DAG replay.

The low-order point-chain check establishes a small-order exclusion. It
must not be reported as a verification of the full standardized subgroup
order or curve point count. The same construction works on a binary curve
with ambient cofactor two or four, because (A) concerns the selected point
G, not the full order #E(k).

4. Optional growing-base family on the larger curves
----------------------------------------------------
For B>=3, choose rational G satisfying
  [j]G != O for every 1<=j<=6B+1,
equivalently ord(G)>6B+1. Define
  S_B={x([i]G):1<=i<=B}, R=[3B+1]G.

Abscissa collisions between two different base indices would yield a
nonzero vanishing multiple of absolute value at most 2B, which is excluded.
The target is affine and outside S_B: its possible collision coefficients
are 3B+1-i and 3B+1+i, between 2B+1 and 4B+1. Every three signed base lift
sum has integer coefficient |s|<=3B. Equality to +/-R would require a
nonzero vanishing multiple with absolute coefficient between one and 6B+1.
Thus the same argument proves UNSAT of the guarded polynomial presentation.
The applicable growing-B degree sandwich then follows from the prior
theorem, rather than from empirical closure ranks.

For example the contiguous B=3 variant is S={x(G),x(2G),x(3G)} with
R=10G and ord(G)>19. The earlier {1,2,4}/16 construction uses a slightly
larger low-order exclusion and is convenient when doubling generates the
public target. This section proves a family, not a claim that certificates
for every B have been generated or replayed.

5. Ordinary-ring and Boolean presentations remain different
------------------------------------------------------------
The known-generator fixtures above use three explicit abscissas or a
contiguous list of generator multiples. Such a set is not thereby a linear
ell-bit subspace of GF(2^n). In particular cardinality three is not the
cardinality of an injective linear F2^ell embedding. An ordinary-field
degree-five certificate must not be presented as a certificate of a
Boolean Weil-descent instance merely by writing its coordinates as bits.

The separate direct Boolean theorem in
../characteristic_two/boolean_upper_bound.txt already allows arbitrary n:
for the specified binary two-S3 chain with one shared u, linear ell-bit
base maps, and explicit Boolean equations for all bits,
  d_F <= 3*ell+1 for ell>=1,
with or without the inverse guard. Its proof needs no rational-lift
assumption, but may have exponential proof size. It establishes no exact
Boolean first-fall value, growing Boolean lower bound, or universal ceiling
five. Applying it to an existing E12 record still needs an encoding audit.

Increasing field size while keeping B=3 does not enlarge the factor base.
Neither the field-size applicability nor the public UNSAT construction is
a timing result, an index-calculus speed improvement, or an ECDLP security
claim. No performance comparison or leaderboard promotion follows here.

6. Source assumptions, exact replay, and formalization boundary
---------------------------------------------------------------
A sparse polynomial-DAG replay checks its stated ideal identity and degree
cap with exact coefficient arithmetic and input binding. Over a modulus p
that guarantee is an identity over Z/pZ; it does not by itself certify that
p is prime, that the curve is nonsingular, that supplied coordinates lie
on the named curve, that a field representation is irreducible, or that
point-group semantics match the polynomial inputs. Those are separate
source assumptions or fixture checks. A probable-prime test, if used by
an implementation, is not a primality certificate.

The standardized prime-field parameters and binary reduction polynomials
are supplied by the primary source cited below. SEC 2 declares the order
of each standard generator prime and lists the cofactor. This note does
not independently certify those large group-order declarations; the
small-order exclusion (A) is sufficient for the fixture proof and can be
checked independently by ordinary public point arithmetic.

The complete filtered-closure theorems, point/sign interpretation and this
fixture-unsatisfiability proof are not Lean formalized in this packet.
The existing checked Lean identity lemmas remain partial formalization.
Any native development tests or future experiment receipts must retain
their separate labels and exact workload scope.
