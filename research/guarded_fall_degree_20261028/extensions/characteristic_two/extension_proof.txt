Characteristic-two extension of the guarded three-abscissa upper bound
Date: 2026-10-28

Status and literature boundary
------------------------------
This note gives an explicit algebraic derivation extending the session's
ordinary-degree upper bound to the ordinary binary Weierstrass model. It
does not claim novelty or establish a bound for growing Boolean Weil-descent
systems. The derivation below is separate from numerical tests and has not
been formalized in Lean.

Primary sources checked before this extension:
1. Igor Semaev, New algorithm for the discrete logarithm problem on elliptic
   curves, arXiv:1504.01175, Section 2, displayed binary S3 formula:
   https://arxiv.org/html/1504.01175
2. Michiel Kosters and Sze Ling Yeo, Notes on summation polynomials,
   arXiv:1503.08001, Section 2 (general S3 and Proposition 2.1), Section 4.2
   (binary Weil descent, trace relation, and first-fall limitations):
   https://arxiv.org/html/1503.08001
The first source's first-fall heuristic is not used as a theorem here.
The second source's trace/Weil-descent phenomenon concerns a different
presentation from the ordinary coefficient-field system treated below.

Theorem
-------
Let k be a finite field of characteristic two, E/k the ordinary nonsingular
curve
  Y^2+X*Y=X^3+a2*X^2+a6,  a6 != 0,
and S a set of exactly three distinct abscissas of k-rational affine points.
Both rational point signs are allowed in the factor base. Let R be an affine
k-rational target and r=x(R). Put
  g(T)=product_(s in S)(T+s),
  f3(X,Y,Z)=(X*Y+X*Z+Y*Z)^2+X*Y*Z+a6,
  F={g(x),g(y),g(z),f3(x,y,u),f3(u,z,r),v*(x+y)+1}.
If F is unsatisfiable over k, then its ordinary-polynomial-ring last fall
degree satisfies 4 <= d_F <= 5. No implicit Boolean reduction or field equation
is used. The ordinary top-syzygy/Koszul first-fall degree is four.
The complete classification is
  d_F=4 if sigma1=sigma2=0 (the cube-orbit family g(T)=T^3+c),
  d_F=5 otherwise.
In particular, over GF(2^n) with n odd, every such unsatisfiable B=3
instance has the exact pair (d_ff,d_F)=(4,5). The cube-orbit family can
split into three distinct roots only when n is even; there its pair is
(4,4). These statements include the repeated-root cases relevant below.

As usual V_D is the smallest k-linear space containing eligible inputs and
closed under multiplication whenever the ordinary product degree is <=D.
For a unit ideal, the least D deriving 1 equals last fall degree. We derive
1 in V_5 constructively.

Step 1: missing-root reduction within degree four
------------------------------------------------
Write g(T)=T^3+s1*T^2+s2*T+s3 and define
  Q=(g(x)+g(y))/(x+y),  w=s1+x+y,  h=v*(x+y)+1.
The exact identities
  v*(g(x)+g(y))+Q*h=Q,
  g(w)=g(y)+(w+y)*Q
put Q and g(w) in V_4. Set
  L=s1+w=x+y,  M=w^2+s1*w+s2.
Then x*y=M+Q. Expanding the binary S3 formula gives
  f3(x,y,u)=L^2*u^2+M*u+M^2+a6+Q*(u+Q).
Every term in the Q correction has degree at most four. Subtract it and
reduce coefficient powers of w modulo the monic cubic g(w), preserving
total degree. This yields q1(w,u) in V_4, where
  q1=alpha(w)*u^2+beta(w)*u+gamma(w),
  deg alpha,deg beta,deg gamma <=2, alpha=(s1+w)^2.
For w=c in S, the other two roots a,b satisfy a+b=s1+c !=0, so
alpha(c)=(a+b)^2 !=0. The first quadratic's roots are the rational
abscissas x(P_a+P_b),x(P_a-P_b), including multiplicity.

The second input is already
  q2(z,u)=(z+r)^2*u^2+z*r*u+(z*r)^2+a6,
whose coefficient degrees in z are at most two. An unsatisfiable target
has r outside S: if R lies above S, choose a different abscissa point P;
the decomposition R+P-P=R has distinct first abscissas and supplies a
solution. Hence the second leading coefficient is nonzero at every z in S.

Step 2: monic normalization within degree five, valid in every characteristic
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
For any q(t,u)=alpha(t)u^2+beta(t)u+gamma(t) with coefficient degrees <=2,
available in V_4, reduction modulo the monic cubic g(t) leaves t-degree <=2
and u-degree <=2, hence total degree <=4. Multiplication by t costs <=5
and reduction returns to <=4. Repeating produces
  q, NF_g(t*q), NF_g(t^2*q)
inside V_5. Let I(t)=I0+I1*t+I2*t^2 be alpha inverse modulo g. Their
scalar linear combination with coefficients I0,I1,I2 is the monic
normalization, with u and constant coefficients of degree <=2.

We thus have, inside V_5,
  H1=u^2+A(w)*u+B(w), H2=u^2+C(z)*u+D(z),
where A,B,C,D are quadratic or lower.

Step 3: the characteristic-two resultant identity
-------------------------------------------------
Take coefficient normal forms modulo g(w),g(z). Put
  E=A+C, J=B+D, L0=H1+H2=E*u+J,
  P=NF(E*L0)=K*u+G,
  K=NF(E^2)=NF(A^2+C^2), G=NF(E*J).
The product E*L0 costs degree at most five. Frobenius makes K a sum of
univariate quadratics after cubic reduction. G has bidegree at most (2,2).
Thus P has total degree at most four.

The reduced polynomial w*P still has total degree at most four: multiplying
the separable quadratic K by w gives coefficient degree <=3, and the scalar
part reduces back to bidegree <=(2,2). Multiplying once more by w therefore
costs at most five. As A depends only on w with degree <=2, these staged
products yield NF(A*P) within degree five, despite the formal product's
possible degree six.

Each of K*H1, u*P, and J*L0 also costs at most five. In characteristic two,
their sum with A*P has exact cancellation of all u terms:
  NF(K*H1+u*P+J*L0+A*P)
    = NF(J^2+A*E*J+B*E^2)
    = NF(Res_u(H1,H2)).
This is the replacement for the odd-characteristic identity that divided
by -2. No division by two is used. Repeated roots cause no exception to
the monic quadratic resultant criterion.

Step 4: invert on the nine-point state grid
------------------------------------------
Call this scalar resultant T(w,z). It has degree at most four. A zero at
any (w,z) in S^2 would give a common u root of the two quadratics. The first
quadratic splits over k by the rational-point sum/difference description,
so that common root lies in k and supplies a solution of the original
guarded polynomial system. Unsatisfiability implies T is nowhere zero.

The scalar quotient k[w,z]/(g(w),g(z)) is k^9. Every element has bidegree
<= (2,2), hence total degree <=4. Its normal forms already in V_5 are
closed under multiplication by w or z: multiply at degree <=5, then reduce.
They form an ideal containing the unit T, and therefore contain 1. This
proves d_F<=5. Substituting the linear w=s1+x+y never increases any degree.

Why the old lower argument fails, and its partial replacement
------------------------------------------------------------
The odd-characteristic lower proof compares quadratic functions of pair
differences against affine functions in x,y,v. That argument genuinely
fails in characteristic two. The three nonzero distinct pair sums d=x+y
have sum zero; their cubic is
  g_d(d)=d^3+tau2*d+tau3, tau3 !=0.
Since v*d=1, the base coordinate ring satisfies
  d^2=tau2+tau3*v.
Thus the u^2 coefficient of f3(x,y,u) is already affine in the inverse
guard variable. This relation can be obtained within degree four and can
reduce the first quartic summation input to a cubic. Thus the former lower
argument must not be transported verbatim.

A new independently audited argument supplies the generic matching lower
bound. Put kappa=sigma1*sigma2+sigma3!=0. The three unordered pair states
have affine coordinate basis 1,w,v and the degree-preserving quadratic
relations
 w^2+kappa*v+sigma2,
 w*v+sigma1*v+1,
 v^2+((sigma1^2+sigma2)/kappa)*v+(w+sigma1)/kappa.
Together with the orientation quadratic, the third membership cubic, and
the monic first S3 quadratic, these give a degree-preserving Groebner
basis. Its normal form of the second S3 has quartic top
 z^2*u*(A_w*w+A_v*v).
The first monic linear coefficient A is constant exactly when
sigma1=sigma2=0. Otherwise this nonzero quartic yields a closed separating
superspace at degree four, proving d_F>=5. See the complete proof and all
derivation-degree details in generic_lower_five_independent_audit.txt.

If sigma1=sigma2=0, three distinct roots of g require a nontrivial cube
root of unity. Such roots are absent in GF(2^n) when n is odd. Hence the
generic lower bound covers every B=3 instance over odd extension degrees.
The exceptional even-degree cube-orbit family is settled in the next step.

The lower bound four is immediate for the raw input presentation. At any
degree below four neither summation input is eligible. The membership and
inverse-guard inputs have a common zero (choose distinct x,y in S, any z
in S, v=(x+y)^(-1), and leave u arbitrary), so their ideal is proper and
cannot derive 1. Hence an unsatisfiable full system has d_F>=4.

Step 5: the cube-orbit family has exact last fall four
-----------------------------------------------------
Suppose sigma1=sigma2=0, so g(T)=T^3+c with c!=0. The state relations
  w^2+c*v, w*v+1, v^2+w/c
are available in V_4 and form a degree-preserving Groebner basis for
the three unordered pair states. The first S3 reduces to a cubic; multiply
it by w/c and reduce the state relations to obtain, within degree four,
  H1=u^2+u+b, b=c*v+(a6/c)*w.
Thus b is affine in the pair-state coordinates. Any squared state function,
including b^2, also has an affine normal form.

Let q=z^2+r*z+r^2, t=z^2+r^2, s=r^2*z^2+a6. Adding t*H1 to the second
S3 input costs degree four and gives the cubic
  P=u*q+b*t+s.
Unsatisfiability forces r outside S, so c+r^3 is nonzero. The identity
  q*(z+r)=z^3+r^3
shows that the inverse of q modulo g(z) is LINEAR:
  I(z)=(z+r)/(c+r^3).
Multiply P by I and reduce the cubic membership polynomial at degree four.
The result is
  L=u+R, R=b*f(z)+j(z), deg f,deg j<=2.
It has total degree at most three.

The product b*L costs at most degree four. After pair reduction b^2 is
affine, so NF(b*L) returns to total degree at most three. Multiplying this
by z and reducing also returns to degree at most three. A second z
multiplication therefore costs at most four, giving NF(z^2*b*L).
Consequently NF(f(z)*b*L) is available within degree four. The same staged
argument applied to L yields NF(j(z)*L) within degree four. Together these
give NF(R*L), despite the possible formal product degree six.

The characteristic-two identity
  H1+u*L+R*L+L=R^2+R+b
now supplies a scalar consequence within degree four. After state and z
reduction its degree is at most three: Frobenius removes cross terms,
b^2 reduces affine, and z powers reduce below three. A zero on any pair
state and third-abscissa state would give u=R and hence a common zero of
both S3 equations, contradicting unsatisfiability. This scalar is therefore
a unit on the nine states. Scalar representatives have maximum degree
three (affine pair state times a quadratic in z), so multiplication and
reduction stay within degree four. Unit inversion derives 1 in V_4.
The raw-input lower bound four proves d_F=4 exactly.

Full independent degree accounting is in cube_orbit_exact_four_audit.txt.

Growing factor bases and Boolean Weil descent
--------------------------------------------
The older unordered-pair interpolation proof uses only quartic symmetry,
quadratic degree in u, rational splitting, and degree-preserving membership
division. It also works with the binary S3 formula: unsatisfiable guarded
systems with B>=3 distinct rational abscissas satisfy d_F<=3B-3. The B=3
theorem above improves this to five. The B=2 special proof works unchanged
and gives exact d_F=4 on unsatisfiable raw-S3 inverse-guarded systems.

B here is CARDINALITY of the abscissa set. A binary factor base of
F_2-dimension ell usually has B=2^ell. The bound growing with B does not
prove a constant ceiling for those systems. Moreover Weil descent to F_2
changes the polynomial presentation: Frobenius terms become linear after
coordinate expansion/Boolean reduction, and trace-derived first falls are
known. The degree and first-fall statements here must not be identified
with that descended system without an explicit transfer theorem.

Verification status
-------------------
The normalized characteristic-two cancellation identity has been expanded
and independently audited algebraically. This proof does not depend on
the incomplete finite-field experiment campaign. True GF(2^m) arithmetic
