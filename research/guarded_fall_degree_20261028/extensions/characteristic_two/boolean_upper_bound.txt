Boolean upper bound for the two-S3, three-coordinate-block presentation
Date: 2026-10-28

Status and scope
----------------
This note proves a syntactic ordinary-degree upper bound for one explicit
Boolean presentation. It contains no new experiment, implementation, timing
claim, novelty claim, or Lean formalization. The argument has been audited
algebraically, including the staged multiplication degrees.

Primary context: Semaev, arXiv:1504.01175, Section 2, supplies the binary
summation polynomial; Kosters--Yeo, arXiv:1503.08001, Sections 2 and 4.2,
discuss summation polynomials and binary Weil descent; Huang--Kosters--Yeo,
arXiv:1505.02532, supplies last-fall-degree/Weil-descent context. No result
from those sources is being claimed as this note's proof. In particular,
the proof below does not use a first-fall heuristic or the ordinary-field
three-abscissa degree-five theorem.

1. Exact presentation and theorem
---------------------------------
Let k=GF(2^n), choose an F2-basis beta_1,...,beta_n of k, and let ell>=1.
Choose three F2-linear embeddings
  L_X:F2^ell -> k; L_Y:F2^ell -> k; L_Z:F2^ell -> k.
Their images may be different subspaces. Write the images of the standard
basis as alpha_j, gamma_j, eta_j, respectively. In the polynomial ring
over F2 introduce independent Boolean variables
  X_1,...,X_ell, Y_1,...,Y_ell, Z_1,...,Z_ell,
  U_1,...,U_n, V_1,...,V_n,
and define the k-valued linear forms
  x=sum_j alpha_j*X_j, y=sum_j gamma_j*Y_j,
  z=sum_j eta_j*Z_j, u=sum_j beta_j*U_j,
  v=sum_j beta_j*V_j.
When the guard is omitted, the V variables may also be omitted.

Fix r,a6 in k and put
  f3(A,B,C)=(A*B+A*C+B*C)^2+A*B*C+a6,
  f1=f3(x,y,u), f2=f3(u,z,r).
Optionally include the inverse guard
  h=v*(x+y)+1.
Take the n coefficient polynomials over F2 of each included k-valued
equation f1,f2,(h), together with an explicit Boolean equation
  W^2+W
for EVERY variable W. Call this generating set F. The coefficient inputs
may be supplied either raw or already in multilinear normal form. The
raw coefficient polynomials have degree at most four.

Let V(F,D) be the smallest F2-linear space containing every input of degree
at most D and closed under multiplication by any polynomial whenever the
ordinary product degree is at most D. Degree is ordinary total degree in
the Boolean-coordinate variables; all reductions used below are derived
from the explicitly included Boolean equations.

THEOREM. If F is unsatisfiable over F2, then
  1 belongs to V(F,3*ell+1), and hence d_F(F)<=3*ell+1.
The theorem holds with or without the guard and for the three possibly
different linear embeddings above. The proof even allows noninjective
linear maps, although embeddings give the intended factor-base model.

For the elliptic-curve interpretation one may assume
  E:Y^2+X*Y=X^3+a2*X^2+a6, a6!=0,
that each image consists of rational point abscissas, and that r=x(R) for
an affine rational target. Those hypotheses give the intended relation
semantics. They are NOT needed for this syntactic upper bound: the proof
uses only the displayed polynomial shapes and explicit Boolean equations.

2. Boolean reductions preserve the required degree cap
------------------------------------------------------
For any polynomial P of ordinary degree at most D, its multilinear normal
form NF(P) is obtained by replacing W^a with W for positive exponents.
The difference P-NF(P) is a sum of multiples of W^2+W whose products have
degree at most D. This follows by reducing one repeated variable at a
time: to replace a monomial M*W^a, a>=2, subtract
  M*W^(a-2)*(W^2+W).
Each new term has no larger degree, and the process terminates. Therefore
  P-NF(P) belongs to V(F,D).
In particular, if P already belongs to V(F,D), so does NF(P).

Raw input normalization costs at most degree four. For ell>=1 this is
within the final cap 3*ell+1. This statement explicitly covers raw quartic
coefficient inputs; it does not silently replace the input presentation.

3. The reduced inputs are affine in all auxiliary bits
-------------------------------------------------------
Let b denote the 3*ell base bits X,Y,Z and t the auxiliary bits U,V.
For a k-valued linear form q=sum c_j*B_j define
  q^[2]=sum c_j^2*B_j.
It is the multilinear normal form of q^2, because the characteristic is
two. In particular u^[2] is LINEAR in the U bits.

The reduced k-valued equations are
  NF(f1)=x^[2]*y^[2]+(x^[2]+y^[2])*u^[2]+x*y*u+a6,
  NF(f2)=(z^[2]+r^2)*u^[2]+r*z*u+r^2*z^[2]+a6,
  NF(h)=v*(x+y)+1, if the guard is included.
Consequently every F2 coefficient polynomial p_i has the form
  p_i(b,t)=c_i0(b)+sum_j c_ij(b)*t_j.
Thus it is affine-linear in ALL auxiliary bits, with no products of two
auxiliary bits. The first input has total degree at most three, the second
and the guard at most two. Their base supports satisfy
  f1 coefficients: only X,Y bits, at most 2*ell;
  f2 coefficients: only Z bits, at most ell;
  guard coefficients: only X,Y bits, at most 2*ell.
Extracting components in the fixed F2-basis changes neither these degree
bounds nor the affine-in-auxiliaries property.

4. A support-aware Boolean localization lemma
---------------------------------------------
Here is the general elementary fact used by the proof. Suppose a system
over F2 has s Boolean base bits b, arbitrary Boolean auxiliary bits t,
and multilinear equations
  p_i(b,t)=c_i0(b)+sum_j c_ij(b)*t_j.
Let A_i be the base support of p_i, with s_i=|A_i|. Suppose the Boolean
equations are explicit and the p_i have already been derived within cap D.
Assume also that the Boolean solutions of the p_i are exactly the solutions
of the original system; in particular this holds when they are normal
forms of all the original non-Boolean equations, as in this application.
If
  D>=s+1 and D>=s_i+2 for every i,
then unsatisfiability implies 1 belongs to V(F,D).

Proof. Fix a full base assignment a in F2^s. For A a subset of base bits
write its assignment indicator as
  delta_(a,A)=product_(B_j in A)(B_j+1+a_j).
This is one at assignment a on A and zero at other Boolean assignments.

Starting with p_i, multiply successively by the indicator factors for bits
in A_i, applying NF after EVERY multiplication. Each current normal form
is multilinear in at most s_i base bits and affine in the auxiliary bits.
It therefore has degree at most s_i+1. The next linear-factor product has
degree at most s_i+2, and its Boolean reduction is available within that
same cap by Section 2. All staged rows thus belong to V(F,D).

After all support factors have been used, the exact normal form is
  delta_(a,A_i)*p_i(a,t).
To justify this as a polynomial identity, both sides are multilinear in
the A_i bits and affine in t. On every Boolean assignment of A_i they
agree coefficientwise as polynomials in t, hence are identical by Boolean
interpolation. No assignment to auxiliary variables is used in this step.

Multiply by the complementary indicator delta_(a,base\A_i). The resulting
row is
  delta_a*p_i(a,t), delta_a=delta_(a,base).
Its ordinary degree is at most s+1, so this multiplication is also allowed.

For the fixed a, the affine system p_i(a,t)=0 is inconsistent over F2:
otherwise its solution, together with a, would satisfy the whole Boolean
system. An inconsistent affine system over a field has 1 in the constant-
coefficient linear span of its equations. Equivalently, elementary linear
algebra gives lambda_i in F2 with
  sum_i lambda_i*p_i(a,t)=1
as a polynomial identity. Taking the same combination of localized rows
derives delta_a. Finally the EXACT polynomial identity
  sum_(a in F2^s) delta_a
    =product_j((1+B_j)+B_j)=1
derives 1. This proves the lemma.

5. Applying the lemma: the cap is 3*ell+1
-----------------------------------------
Here s=3*ell and every s_i<=2*ell. Raw inputs normalize within cap four.
For ell>=1,
  max(4, s+1, max_i(s_i+2))
    <= max(4,3*ell+1,2*ell+2)=3*ell+1.
Section 4 therefore derives 1 inside V(F,3*ell+1).

Every algebraic-closure zero of the full system has Boolean coordinates,
because each W satisfies W^2+W=0. Thus unsatisfiability over F2 is also
algebraic-closure unsatisfiability, and the generated ideal is the unit
ideal. For a unit ideal, the least cap deriving 1 equals last fall degree:
the last-fall definition applied to 1 gives one inequality, and once 1 is
available at cap D, multiplying it by each monomial of any polynomial f
derives f at cap max(D,deg f). Hence the refutation bound proves the stated
last-fall bound.

The theorem is stated for ell>=1. With the guard and zero-dimensional linear
images, x=y=0 makes the guard the constant one. If the guard is omitted,
that particular argument does not apply; zero-dimensional systems can be
analyzed directly and are not part of the stated theorem.

6. Exact limitations and relation to the other notes
----------------------------------------------------
* This bound is for the specified fully Boolean, coefficientwise descended
  system with EXPLICIT Boolean equations for all bits. It does not assert
  the same degree bound for a presentation which omits those equations,
  uses implicit reductions, or uses different auxiliary constraints.
* Linearity of the three coordinate maps ensures the support and degree
  bounds. Arbitrary nonlinear enumerations require a new degree audit.
* The single shared auxiliary u is important: after fixing x,y,z, both
  displayed S3 equations are affine in its Boolean coordinates. Longer
  chains can have products of distinct auxiliary blocks; the affine-fibre
  lemma then needs additional justification and is not asserted here.
* The optional inverse guard is also affine after fixing the base bits.
  Omitting it preserves this upper bound, but changes which relations and
  extraneous summation-polynomial solutions the system represents.
* The proof may use all 2^(3*ell) base assignments. It bounds degree, not
  proof size, arithmetic work, memory, practical running time, or ECDLP
  complexity, and makes no sub-rho claim.
* No first-fall equality or growing lower bound follows. The ordinary-field
  d_ff^ord=B+1 and cardinality-scale lower bounds concern another input
  presentation. This note does not transfer those lower bounds.
* This is not a certification of the earlier E12 instances, their closure
  computations, or any first/last-fall labels. Applying it to that ledger
  requires checking that the actual generators, guard convention, field
  equations, coordinate maps, and auxiliary blocks match this theorem.
* There is no conflict with finite d_F>=5 examples: an upper bound growing
  as 3*ell+1 neither proves nor disproves a constant ceiling in a restricted
  experimental family. No exact growing-ell last-fall value is claimed.

Under the additional rational-lift hypotheses of the local ordinary-field
theorem, its triple-projector transfer also gives the weaker 3*ell+5 ceiling:
its k-valued rows can be simulated coefficientwise using the multiplication
structure constants of k/F2. The direct affine-fibre proof above avoids
that dependency and gives the sharper bound with simpler degree accounting.
