Independent audit: characteristic-two generic degree-four obstruction
Date: 2026-10-28

Conclusion
----------
For the ordinary characteristic-two inverse-guarded B=3 model in
extension_proof.txt, assume sigma1 and sigma2 are not both zero. Every
unsatisfiable instance has last fall degree exactly five. The separate
uniform upper-five proof supplies the upper bound; the argument below
supplies the lower bound. The first fall under the explicit ordinary
top-syzygy/Koszul convention is four, as in the earlier proof.

When sigma1=sigma2=0 this lower argument does not apply. It proves no
exact degree for that exceptional family. In GF(2^n) with n odd, the
exceptional family cannot occur, so the exact pair (4,5) holds for every
unsatisfiable B=3 instance over those coefficient fields.

Notation and state equations
----------------------------
Let g(T)=T^3+sigma1*T^2+sigma2*T+sigma3 have three distinct roots S.
Set w=sigma1+x+y, d=w+sigma1=x+y, v=d^(-1), and
 kappa=sigma1*sigma2+sigma3=g(sigma1).
The scalar sigma1 cannot be in S: that would force the other two roots
to be equal in characteristic two. Hence kappa is nonzero.

The three unordered pair states are the points
 (w,v)=(s,1/(s+sigma1)), s in S.
They are noncollinear. Indeed an affine relation a*w+b*v+c=0 at all
three points, multiplied by w+sigma1, is a polynomial of degree at most
two vanishing at three distinct w values. Its coefficients force
a=b=c=0. Thus 1,w,v form a basis of their three-dimensional function
algebra; every function on these states has an affine representative.

Let h=wv+sigma1*v+1. The following equations are available within V_4:
 Rw=w^2+kappa*v+sigma2,
 h=wv+sigma1*v+1,
 Rv=v^2+((sigma1^2+sigma2)/kappa)*v+(w+sigma1)/kappa.
For explicit derivations,
 Rw=v*g(w)+(w^2+sigma2)*h,
 kappa*Rv=v*Rw+w*h+sigma1*h.
The first identity uses products of degree at most four. The second uses
products of degree at most three, after Rw has become available in V_4.
The previously established degree-four derivation of g(w) is used here.

In graded lexicographic order w>v their leading monomials are w^2,wv,v^2,
and their tails are affine. They form a Groebner basis. One proof avoids
any computation: those leading monomials leave at most the three standard
monomials 1,w,v, while evaluation on the three noncollinear state points
shows that these three remain independent. Therefore no further initial
relation is possible, and the displayed generating set is a Groebner basis.
Its division preserves ordinary total degree.

Orientation, original base ideal, and the monic first quadratic
--------------------------------------------------------------
Keep x as orientation coordinate; y=w+sigma1+x is a linear substitution.
The divided difference Q, together with Rw, gives the available quadratic
 Tx=x^2+(w+sigma1)*x+kappa*v+sigma1*w.
Its leading monomial is x^2 in graded lexicographic order x>w>v. Its
leading monomial is coprime to each state leading monomial, so adjoining
Tx preserves the Groebner-basis property. Adjoin g(z), with leading z^3.
The full order may be taken as graded lexicographic x>w>v>z.

These equations generate exactly
 I0=(g(x),g(y),g(z),v*(x+y)+1).
For precision, every displayed equation was derived from I0, giving one
ideal inclusion. The state quotient has dimension three. At each state,
Tx is the quadratic whose two distinct roots are S excluding w, so the
orientation quotient gives exactly six ordered distinct pairs. Adjoining
g(z) gives 18 reduced points. The original I0 also has exactly those 18
reduced points: its split, squarefree membership algebra is localized by
the inverse guard. Equal finite colength and the ideal inclusion prove
equality. The free u variable does not affect this reasoning.

Reduce the first binary S3 input using this degree-preserving basis. Its
normal form is q1=alpha(w,v)*u^2+beta(w,v)*u+gamma(w,v), with each
coefficient affine in w,v. Hence q1 has total degree at most three and
is available in V_4. Alpha=d^2 is nonzero at all three pair states.
Its inverse therefore has an affine representative. Multiplying q1 by
that affine inverse costs at most four; reduction stays within degree
four and yields
 H1=u^2+A(w,v)*u+B(w,v),
with A and B affine, of total degree at most two. In particular H1 is
available in V_4. Conversely the original first S3 input lies in
G=I0+(H1), since normalization multiplies by a unit modulo I0.

A degree-preserving basis for G
-------------------------------
Adjoin H1 and use graded lexicographic order u>x>w>v>z. Its leading
monomial u^2 is coprime to the previous leading monomials, so the result
is a Groebner basis of G. All quadratic tails have total degree at most
two, and the cubic g(z) has lower-degree tails. Division therefore never
increases total degree. The standard monomials show the quotient has
dimension 36 and is nonzero; no radicality assumption about H1 is needed.

Normal form of the second S3 and its top term
--------------------------------------------
The second binary S3 input is
 f2=(z+r)^2*u^2+zr*u+(zr)^2+a6.
Replacing u^2 by A*u+B gives its G-normal form P. It uses no orientation
x. Since A and B are affine state functions, the only possible terms of
total degree four are
 z^2*u*(A_w*w+A_v*v).
These are standard monomials. Consequently P has nonzero degree-four
part exactly when A is nonconstant on the three pair states.

The constant-A criterion is exact. On a state w,
 A=M/d^2,
 M=w^2+sigma1*w+sigma2,
 d^2=(w+sigma1)^2.
If A=lambda is constant, then M+lambda*d^2 is a degree-at-most-two
polynomial vanishing at all three roots of g, hence is identically zero.
Its quadratic, linear, and constant coefficients give lambda=1,
sigma1=0, sigma2=0. Conversely these conditions make A=1.

The closed separating space
---------------------------
Assume now that A is nonconstant, equivalently (sigma1,sigma2)!=(0,0).
Define
 W=(G intersect R_(<=4))+k*P.
Every original input belongs to W: the base inputs and f1 lie in G,
and f2-P lies in G. Suppose t in W has degree at most three. Its normal
form has degree at most three because G-division preserves degree.
But NF_G(t)=c*P for a scalar c, and P has a nonzero standard degree-four
part. Therefore c=0 and t lies in G.

It follows that W is closed under every multiplication permitted at
degree four: a nonconstant multiplier forces the multiplicand degree to
be at most three, hence to lie in G. The product then belongs to
G intersect R_(<=4). Constant multipliers are covered by linearity.
Also 1 is not in W: its normal form is 1, which cannot equal a scalar
multiple of a degree-four P, and G is proper. Thus V_4 is contained in
W and excludes 1. An unsatisfiable system consequently has d_F>=5.
Combined with the separately established upper bound, d_F=5.

Odd extension degree excludes the exceptional family
---------------------------------------------------
If sigma1=sigma2=0, the three roots are the roots of T^3+sigma3, all
nonzero. Distinct roots require a nontrivial cube root of unity in k.
For k=GF(2^n), such a root exists if and only if 3 divides 2^n-1,
equivalently n is even. For odd n the exception is impossible.

Scope and verification status
-----------------------------
This audit is mathematical degree accounting, not a Lean formalization
or a numerical experiment. It concerns three coefficient-field abscissas
and the raw inverse-guarded binary S3 presentation, not a Boolean Weil
descent or a growing factor base. The exceptional even-degree cube-orbit
family remains outside the lower-bound conclusion.
