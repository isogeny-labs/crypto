Characteristic-two degree-five quadratic-fibre lemma
Date: 2026-10-28

Setting
-------
Let k have characteristic two. Let g(w),g(z) be monic cubics and suppose
their equations are available in V_5. Suppose also that
 H1=u^2+A(w)u+B(w), H2=u^2+C(z)u+D(z)
are available in V_5, with A,B,C,D of degree at most two in their one
state variable. All degrees here are ordinary polynomial total degrees.
NF means reduction modulo g(w),g(z), without changing u powers.

Then the scalar normal form of Res_u(H1,H2) belongs to V_5. If g splits
with three distinct roots and the two quadratics have no common root on
any scalar grid branch, then 1 belongs to V_5.

Proof
-----
Set E=A+C, J=B+D, L=H1+H2=E*u+J. The product E*L has degree at most
five. Its normal form is
 P=NF(E*L)=K*u+G,
 K=NF(E^2)=NF(A^2+C^2), G=NF(E*J).

Frobenius is the essential replacement for division by two: K is a SUM
of a quadratic in w and a quadratic in z. There is no mixed A*C term.
G has degree at most two in each state variable, so its total degree is
at most four. Therefore P has total degree at most four.

Multiply P by w, then reduce. The u coefficient w*K has reduced total
degree at most three; after multiplying by u this contributes at most
four. The scalar coefficient w*G reduces to bidegree at most (2,2), also
degree at most four. Thus NF(w*P) has degree at most four. Multiplying it
by w a second time costs at most five and gives NF(w^2*P). Hence
NF(A(w)*P) is available by a scalar linear combination of P, NF(w*P),
NF(w^2*P), even though a direct unreduced product could have degree six.

The three other products
 K*H1, u*P, J*L
all cost at most five: K has degree at most two, H1 at most three,
P at most four, and J at most two while L is at most three.

Their sum with the staged A*P product gives, modulo the scalar cubics,

 K*H1 + u*P + J*L + A*P
   = E^2*(u^2+A*u+B)
       + u*(E^2*u+E*J)
       + J*(E*u+J)
       + A*(E^2*u+E*J)
   = J^2+A*E*J+B*E^2.

All u^2 and u terms cancel in characteristic two. The last expression
is exactly the monic quadratic resultant. Its scalar normal form has
bidegree at most (2,2), and has therefore been derived inside V_5.

If g has three distinct roots, the scalar quotient is k^9. A resultant
nonzero at each grid point is a unit. Every scalar normal form has total
degree at most four, so successive multiplication by w or z, followed by
reduction, costs at most five and returns to that scalar normal-form
space. Thus the available scalar rows form an ideal containing a unit,
and contain 1.

Degree-five normalization is characteristic-independent
------------------------------------------------------
For q(t,u)=alpha(t)u^2+beta(t)u+gamma(t), with coefficient degrees at
most two and alpha invertible modulo the cubic g(t), start from q in V_4.
Multiplication by t followed by cubic reduction costs five and returns
to degree at most four. Repeat once. If alpha^{-1} mod g=I0+I1*t+I2*t^2,
the linear combination of q, NF(t*q), NF(t^2*q) yields a monic H in V_5.
No division by two or perfect-field assumption is used.

Scope
-----
This is a purely algebraic lemma. Applying it to an elliptic-curve
presentation still requires the correct characteristic-two Semaev
polynomial, the missing-root reduction, and the rational/common-root
interpretation. It does not prove a claim about a Boolean Weil descent
or growing factor-base dimension by itself.

Checks
------
The unreduced displayed cancellation identity was independently expanded
over F_2 with symbolic coefficients A,B,C,D,u and reduced to zero. That
finite symbolic check supplements the written identity; it is not needed
as an assumption of the proof.
