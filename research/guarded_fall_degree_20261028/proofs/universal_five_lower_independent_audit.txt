Independent audit of the uniform degree-four obstruction, B=3
Date: 2026-10-28

Conclusion
----------
The proposed lower argument is sound. For every p>3 and every three-element
set S in F_p, the ordinary degree-four closure of the explicit inverse-
guarded chain excludes 1. In particular, every unsatisfiable instance in
the rational-curve class has d_F>=5. Combined with the independently audited
upper bound, every such unsatisfiable instance has exactly d_F=5.
The existing leading-syzygy argument gives d_ff=4 as well.

Set-up
------
Write x=x1, y=x2, z=x3, d=x-y, and let u,v be the auxiliary variables.
Let g(T)=product_(s in S)(T-s), h=v*(x-y)-1, and define
  I0=(g(x),g(y),g(z),h),
  f1=S3(x,y,u), f2=S3(u,z,r).
Every input has total degree at most four. The coefficients of u^2 in
f1 and f2 are respectively d^2 and (z-r)^2. No other feature of the
curve coefficients is needed for this lower bound.

Define the finite-dimensional subspace of polynomials of degree at most 4
  W=(I0 intersect R_(<=4)) + F_p*f1 + F_p*f2.
It contains every input. We show that W is closed under every multiplication
permitted at degree four and does not contain 1.

Lemma: the degree-at-most-three part of W lies in I0
--------------------------------------------------
Let w=i+c1*f1+c2*f2 belong to W, with degree(w)<=3 and i in I0.
The coefficient of u^2 in w is an affine polynomial
  ell=L0+Lx*x+Ly*y+Lz*z+Lv*v.
Evaluate only the base coordinates, setting x=a,y=b,z=c,v=1/(a-b)
for arbitrary distinct a,b in S and arbitrary c in S. Keep u as a FORMAL
indeterminate. Every generator of I0 then vanishes, so the entire evaluated
i is identically zero as a polynomial in u. Comparing the u^2 coefficients
therefore gives
  ell(a,b,c,1/(a-b)) = c1*(a-b)^2+c2*(c-r)^2.
This coefficient comparison does not rely on evaluating a polynomial
function at finitely many values of u.

For a fixed ordered pair a,b, varying c over the three distinct elements
of S gives a degree-at-most-two polynomial in c with three distinct roots.
Its quadratic coefficient is -c2, so c2=0. Its linear coefficient then
gives Lz=0.

The remaining right-hand side is symmetric in a,b. Comparing the two
ordered pairs (a,b) and (b,a), with v changing to -v, gives
  (Lx-Ly)*(a-b)^2+2*Lv=0.
The three squared unordered-pair differences cannot all be equal when
char(F_p)>3. Indeed, after translation write two differences as t and q.
Equality t^2=q^2 for distinct roots forces q=-t. The remaining difference
is 2t, so equality of its square with t^2 would imply 3*t^2=0, impossible.
Thus at least two squared differences differ, yielding Lx=Ly and Lv=0.

Write sigma1=sum(S), sigma2=sum_(a<b in S)(a*b), and let s be the unique
third root of S other than a,b. Then a+b=sigma1-s and
  (a-b)^2=-3*s^2+2*sigma1*s+sigma1^2-4*sigma2.
Consequently, for each of the three distinct s in S,
  c1*(-3*s^2+2*sigma1*s+sigma1^2-4*sigma2)
      = L0+Lx*(sigma1-s).
This quadratic has three distinct roots, so its leading coefficient
-3*c1 is zero. Since p>3, c1=0. We already had c2=0; hence w=i belongs
to I0, proving the lemma.

Closure of W
------------
Let w in W and let a polynomial m satisfy degree(m*w)<=4. If m is
constant, closure follows from linearity. If w=0 the product is zero.
Otherwise, if m is nonconstant, ordinary polynomial degree is additive
in this integral domain, so degree(w)<=3. The lemma gives w in I0.
Therefore m*w belongs to I0 and has degree at most four, hence belongs
to W. Thus W satisfies the full closure condition, including arbitrary
polynomial multipliers, not only variable multiplication.

Separation from 1
-----------------
The ideal I0 is proper: choose any distinct a,b in S, any c in S, put
v=1/(a-b), and leave u arbitrary. This supplies a rational common zero
of its generators. If 1 belonged to W, its degree is at most three, so
the lemma would imply 1 in I0, a contradiction. Hence 1 is not in W.

Since W contains all inputs and is closed at degree four, the least such
closed space V(F,4) is contained in W. Therefore 1 is not in V(F,4).
For unsatisfiable rational guarded systems, the ideal is the unit ideal;
last fall equals the least degree deriving 1, so d_F>=5.

Combined exact result
---------------------
The separate normalized-quadratic/resultant proof puts 1 in V(F,5) for
every unsatisfiable instance in the original rational-curve class. Thus
  d_F=5.
For the first fall, the degree-at-most-three top map on the three cubic
memberships and quadratic guard has no syzygy. The identity
  v*(g(x)-g(y))-Q*h=Q,
where Q=(g(x)-g(y))/(x-y) has degree two, supplies a non-Koszul top syzygy
in degree four; the first possible Koszul degree is five. Its consequence
Q is genuinely new below degree three. Hence the stated syzygy-defined
first fall is exactly four, giving the uniform pair
  (d_ff,d_F)=(4,5)
for every unsatisfiable inverse-guarded B=3 instance under the explicit
ordinary-ring convention and original hypotheses.

This is a three-abscissa fixed-cardinality theorem, not a bound for an
arbitrary increasing factor base or an unguarded presentation.
