## The actual cross-characteristic construction

Over Q take E : y² − y = x³ − x², with discriminant −11 and P=(0,0). The following integer-coordinate points are exact rational torsion:

| k | [k]P |
|---:|:---|
| 0 | O |
| 1 | (0,0) |
| 2 | (1,1) |
| 3 | (1,0) |
| 4 | (0,1) |

Thus [5]P=O. At good primes p different from 5 and 11, reduction is injective on these five points. Reduce the **same five global rational points** at 101 and 103 to identify the cyclic subgroups generated by P in E(F₁₀₁) and E(F₁₀₃). It is a group isomorphism on the *specified 5-torsion*, not an F₁₀₁-to-F₁₀₃ field homomorphism or a curve morphism between the fibers. The correspondence exists here because all five lifts are already listed.

Two independently coded standard-library arithmetic controls, Python and Go, enumerate the full groups and agree:

| p | #E(Fp) | all 25 ordered torsion-pair additions |
|---:|---:|:---|
| 7 | 10 | pass |
| 13 | 10 | pass |
| 17 | 20 | pass |
| 101 | 100 | pass |
| 103 | 120 | pass |

Python tests 75 further pairs of scalar multiplications across three different-prime comparisons. The deliberate naive-coordinate substitution of (2,29) from F₁₀₁ fails in F₁₀₃ because the same coordinate integers are off-curve there. No unital ring homomorphism F₁₀₁→F₁₀₃ exists: 101·1 vanishes in the source but not the target. This does **not** rule out a characteristic-zero bridge.

The initial Python implementation omitted the `−a₃y` contribution to the tangent intercept, which caused an on-curve assertion failure; the corrected numerator is `−x³+y`. Both ordinary and optimized Python modes retain explicit checks (not optimizer-stripped `assert`), and an independent Go implementation counts and checks the same groups. This history is a development error, not a successful test.

## Hard boundary, no generalized impossibility claim

For a dimension-g abelian variety A/Fq with an r-torsion rational point, the Hasse–Weil bound implies `r ≤ #A(Fq) ≤ (sqrt(q)+1)^(2g)`. For g=1 and growing prime r, q cannot be asymptotically tiny; for g≥2 the bound alone leaves smaller q possible. **A polynomial lower bound for the minimal number field of definition of an arbitrary r-torsion point for varying g-dimensional abelian varieties is not established here.** Do not apply the fixed-A literature or Gao–Gu's conditional result to arbitrary varying A unconditionally.

In this toy, one can map the given five inputs by table lookup; preparing and locating arbitrary large-order global lifts is precisely what remains unimplemented. This is not a transfer on arbitrary points of E109/E113 over F101, and the toy has no nontrivial solving-cost advantage.
