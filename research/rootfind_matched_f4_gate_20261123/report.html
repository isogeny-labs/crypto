Verdict

  • By the frozen scoring rule, release 2× and target 8× both FAIL. The scientific reading is inconclusive, because two failed conditions could not have been met by this design: - *Fastest baseline ≥ 1 s* was unreachable: the frozen calibration ladder stopped at M = 128, where the fastest F4 arm takes 0.10 s. - *A/A ≤ 10 %* for program B was unreachable: its processes last about 20 ms, so process start-up noise dominates (15 %), and the frozen native's 5-cases-per-process rule forbids batching more work per process. - Isolation (L0) and field/curve coverage (prime only) were unmet by construction. - The result is therefore not evidence against program B, and not evidence for it either (next point).
  • The matched panel is in an easy regime (no hard instances). At p = 4294967291, every M in Panel B (128) and in the post-hoc ladder (≤ 1365) lies below p^{1/3} ≈ 1625. There, fixing S = x+y+z makes the residues e2, e3 equal to the true integers (3M² < p and M³ < p), so the roots are the integer roots of one cubic t³ − S t² + e2 t − e3 per S: an exact O(M) scan of 3M+1 cubics. scripts/cubic_check.py implements that scan in pure Python with exact integer arithmetic. It reproduced the root sets of all 20 panel charts, a fresh brute force on 3 charts, and F4-visit's stored root hashes on 15 ladder chart-M pairs up to M = 1365, including the two M values where program B returned nothing [pcc:unverified]. No arm in this study was run in the hard regime M > p^{1/3}, where Panel A's NIST charts live.
  • "F4-visit" is a 2D enumeration tree, not Gröbner work. On every run its f4_runs equals 1 + (M+1) + (M+1)² exactly (16,771 at M = 128), at about 9.0 F_p multiplications per F4 call on matrices of at most 14 × 34. It branches over every (x, y) in [0,M]² and calls a trivial F4 at each leaf. Its roughly M² growth is the enumeration's. F4-ideal is the only real Gröbner arm.
  • Ratios on the M = 128 panel (component numbers, easy regime): - program B against F4-visit (the enumeration tree): geometric mean 21.0×, instance-bootstrap 95 % CI [19.8, 22.3], worst chart 15.5×, best 25.7× [pcc:unverified]; - program B against F4-ideal (Gröbner): 1,147× [pcc:unverified]. F4-ideal had no A/A duplicate (by protocol; it is never the baseline); - root sets are identical across all three arms and an independent brute force on 20/20 charts × 3 repetitions.
  • The gate is still unscoreable as written. Its "20 preregistered hard original-all-roots instances" never existed. The 5 NIST charts in the checkout are planted controls; the 20 metric training cases cannot be rebuilt (inputs absent). At the real chart scale (M = 2^40…2^106, p of 192–521 bits) no matched F4 arm can be posed: it would need domain polynomials of degree M+1 ≥ 2^40, and f4_fp requires p < 2^32.
  • Post-hoc ladder (exploratory, 3 charts, easy regime). Program B was complete up to M = 768 (about 3·p^{1/4}) and 9.8–114× faster than F4-visit on the 12 chart-M rows at M = 448–768 where F4-visit took ≥ 1 s [pcc:unverified]. At M = 1024 and 1365 it hit its frozen 100,000-node cap on 6/6 chart-M rows and returned no roots, flagged incomplete. The measured node growth is about M^5.6–M^5.9 (below). An M^8/p² box-volume argument and a p^{1/4} threshold are a model, not a finding; only one p was tested.

Do the program-B claims survive? No 2×/8× release claim survives, and the panel cannot support a positive characterisation either:

  • Program B is complete and quicker than both F4 arms at p ≈ 2^32 for M ≤ 768 [pcc:unverified], but that whole range is solvable exactly by an O(M) integer-cubic scan, so it is not a hard regime and the ratios do not transfer to M > p^{1/3}.
  • Program B becomes incomplete under its frozen cap by M = 1024 at this p.
  • Nothing here changes relation yield or the counted S of any ECDLP pipeline (survey: no study claims IC below a matched rho).

The easy regime M < p^{1/3} (scripts/cubic_check.py)

For (x,y,z) ∈ [0,M]³, e2 ≤ 3M² and e3 ≤ M³. When both are below p, the chart equations e2 ≡ t0 S² + t1 S + t2 and e3 ≡ u0 S² + u1 S + u2 (mod p) fix e2 and e3 as integers for each S ∈ [0, 3M]. Then {x, y, z} is the multiset of integer roots of t³ − S t² + e2 t − e3, so 3M+1 integer cubics settle the chart exactly. At p = 4294967291, M³ < p holds for M ≤ 1625, which covers Panel B (M = 128, 385 cubics) and the whole post-hoc ladder (M ≤ 1365, 4,096 cubics at M = 1365). The reviewer's argument checks out.

The script finds integer roots exactly (integer critical points, integer bisection on monotone pieces, exact deflation), with no floating point deciding a root. data/cubic_check.txt [pcc:unverified]:

Relabelled throughout as an (M+1)² domain enumeration with a trivial F4 call at each leaf; F4-ideal is named as the only real Gröbner arm. 2. Easy regime and missing O(M) baseline. Accepted and verified: the argument is correct, and scripts/cubic_check.py reproduces every checked root set. Panel B and the ladder are now described as an easy regime; no hard-regime evidence is claimed. The O(M) scan was not added as a timed arm (no reruns in this revision); doing so is an open thread. 3. Scaling claim unsupported. Accepted. M^8/p² and p^{1/4} are relabelled as a model; the measured exponent (5.5–6.2) and the single-p limitation are reported. The reviewer's "about 5.6–5.9" matches the fits above. 4. Gate outcome framing. Accepted. FAIL is kept as the frozen-rule result; the reading is inconclusive because the ≥ 1 s and A/A conditions were unreachable (ladder cap at M = 128; about 20 ms processes under the 5-case rule). The reviewer's post-hoc figure (9.8–114× at M = 448–768) is confirmed, with the caveat that those rows are also in the easy regime.
