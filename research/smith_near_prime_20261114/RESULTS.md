# Smith's conditions on near-prime genus-3 Jacobians: results (2026-11-14)

Status: `[pcc:unverified]` (one session, shared 4-vCPU container, no independent replay).
Protocol: [`PROTOCOL.md`](PROTOCOL.md), frozen before data (`frozen.sha256`); binary
`binary.sha256`; data `near_prime.jsonl` (`data.sha256`); tables and the scorecard are
regenerated by `python3 render.py near_prime.jsonl` ([`tables.md`](tables.md)).

## Question

The first genus-ladder run saw only 1 of 28 admitted near-prime genus-3 curves pass
Smith's Propositions 2 and 4, against 0.169 in a census of random curves. Is the
near-prime admission lowering Smith's pass rate, and if so, is the mechanism the
rational 2-torsion (the number `k` of irreducible factors of `f`)?

## Run

7,702 squarefree curves `y² = f(x)`, `deg f = 7`, over `p = 67, 131, 257, 521, 1031`
(seeds 1, 2, ... per prime until 150 admitted); exact `#J` for 7,701 of them (one
order search at p = 67 was ambiguous and is recorded as undetermined); Smith's
analysis on every curve. Wall clock about 2 minutes on 3 threads.

## Result: the lead is real, and `k` explains it

| population | curves | Smith `full_success` | Wilson 95% |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| all curves | 7,702 | 0.173 | [0.165, 0.182] |
| admitted, `h <= 8` | 761 | 0.108 | [0.088, 0.132] |
| admitted, `h <= 2` | 234 | 0.051 | [0.030, 0.087] |

By the number `k` of irreducible factors of `f` (rational 2-rank `k − 1`):

| k | curves | pass rate | admitted (`h <= 8`) | pass rate, admitted |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 1 | 1,110 | 0.000 | 209 | 0.000 |
| 2 | 2,663 | 0.128 | 320 | 0.103 |
| 3 | 2,421 | 0.198 | 189 | 0.185 |
| 4 | 1,200 | 0.287 | 43 | 0.326 |
| 5 | 281 | 0.505 | 0 | - |
| 6, 7 | 27 | 1.000 | 0 | - |

- `k = 1` (f irreducible, Frobenius cycle type (7, 1) on the Weierstrass points) never
  passes: no Frobenius-stable pairing exists, so there is no tractable subgroup. These
  curves are 14% of all curves but 27% of the admitted ones (the 2-rank 0 is what a
  near-prime order prefers), and `k >= 5` (2-rank 4, so `16 | #J`) is never admitted.
- The mixture `Σ_k w_k · rate_k`, with `w_k` the admitted k-distribution and `rate_k`
  the pass rate of all curves at that `k`, predicts 0.119 for the admitted population;
  measured 0.108 [0.088, 0.132]. Within each `k` the admitted and non-admitted rates
  have overlapping intervals (k = 2: 0.103 [0.074, 0.141] against 0.131 [0.118, 0.146];
  k = 3: 0.185 against 0.199; k = 4: 0.326 against 0.285). Admission acts through `k`
  within what this sample can resolve; a residual effect inside `k = 2` of the size of
  the gap seen there (about 0.03) is not excluded.
- `v_2(#J) >= k − 1` held on all 7,701 determined orders (the 2-rank identity used by
  the mechanism; a check of the order computation as much as of the theory).
- The first run's 1/28 sits inside the central 95% binomial range [0, 7] for n = 28 at
  the admitted rate 0.108: consistent, not an anomaly.

Scorecard: S1 to S6 all held (`tables.md`, section "Scorecard").

## Reading for the Smith route

Smith's 18.57% (and the 16.9% of these odd-degree models) is the share of random
curves. For curves of the kind one would attack, prime or near-prime Jacobians, the
share with an explicit Smith isogeny is 10.8% at `h <= 8` and 5.1% at `h <= 2` on
this sample, and for a prime-order Jacobian (2-rank 0, `k = 1`) it is zero: an
odd-degree genus-3 curve with prime `#J` has no tractable subgroup at all, because
every tractable subgroup is a rational subgroup of order 8 of `J[2]`, and prime order
leaves no rational 2-torsion. That last sentence is a theorem, not a measurement; the
census confirms the implementation agrees with it (0 of 1,110).

Consequences: the Smith route to a plane quartic is closed for prime-order genus-3
Jacobians in this model, open for cofactors divisible by 2 (`k >= 2`) with the rates
above, and the published rate overstates it for cryptographic instances by a factor
of 2 to 3. Even-degree models (`deg f = 8`, no rational Weierstrass point) have a
different 2-torsion structure (`J[2](F_p)` rank `k − 2` for `k` factors) and were not
measured here.

## Limits

Odd-degree models only; five primes below 2^10; `full_success` is Smith's condition
for his explicit construction, not the existence of any isogeny to a quartic (the
isogeny class may still contain Smith-reachable curves, `smith_kernel_coverage.rs`).
No map is built here.
