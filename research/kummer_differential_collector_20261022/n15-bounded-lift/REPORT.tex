\noindent For a generic degree-two divisor with
$u=x^2+a_1x+a_0$ and $v=b_1x+b_0$, the Kummer inverse supplies $a_0,a_1$
and $b_1^2$. Writing $f\bmod u=R_0+R_1x$, the candidate equations are
\[
 b_0^2-a_0b_1^2=R_0,\qquad 2b_0b_1-a_1b_1^2=R_1.
\]
For $b_1\ne0$, the second equation fixes $b_0$ after a modular square
root. For $b_1=0$, require $R_1=0$ and solve $b_0^2=R_0$. Each generic
branch has at most two candidates. Identity is explicit, and a degree-one
class becomes degree two after a suitable rational branch-point
translation. Projective checks against both supplied Kummer inputs select
the sign for odd-order $G$. This gives at most six conversions, twelve square
roots, and thirteen candidate checks, conditional on the frozen action and
projection identities. Those identities have finite source checks but no
all-state symbolic certificate in this packet.

\noindent A later exact 21-model basis audit confirms that the direct
degree-two matrix $C_2$ and the matrix $C_1$ inverted by \texttt{general()}

$C_2(1,-a_1,-a_0,q_3)^T=C_1(1,-a_1,a_0,q_3+\sigma a_0)^T$.
The third entry recovered by the source is therefore $a_0$ as expected.
An interim inversion-mismatch concern is withdrawn; the finite rows and
