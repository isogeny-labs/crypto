\noindent On the exceptional locus $u(r)=0$, validity gives $v(r)^2=f(r)=0$. Write $u=(x-r)(x-s)$ and $v=b_1(x-r)$. A repeated root $s=r$ cannot represent a valid reduced divisor: $v^2$ would have a double zero at $r$ while $f'(r)\ne0$ is verified for every recorded branch point. Thus $s\ne r$, and validity at $s$ gives $(s-r)^2b_1^2=f(s)$. Translation by $T_r=(r,0)-\infty$ sends $(r,0)+(s,y)-2\infty$ to $(s,y)-\infty$, since $2T_r=\operatorname{div}(x-r)$ as a divisor class.
