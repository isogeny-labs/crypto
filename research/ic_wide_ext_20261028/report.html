Prime IC relation phase: why wide batching stops paying, and the joint tuning (2026-10-28)

Extension of lead ICP-prime-ic-constant-engineering (PR 2783): a diagnosis of the lane-round cost, a check that the code changes move no counted quantity, the walk-prime-fast fix, and a joint tuning grid over base size, lane count and oracle.

Findings

  • Where a relation-phase trial goes (28-bit, ns/trial, 16 lanes): shared-inversion additions 45.9, touch 32.9, lane preparation 18.0, queue 4.9, and 157.1 in the loop body (target draw 19.9, guard insert 55.1, oracle probe 82.0; cycles at 2.1 GHz) of 330.0 in all. The plain walk is 370.4.
  • The shared inversion is amortised by about 32 lanes. The additions cost 77.7 ns/trial at 4 lanes, 45.9 at 16, 39.6 at 32 and stay at 43.6-46.6 from 256 lanes up (32-bit: 81.3, 51.9, 40.5, 47.9): more lanes buy nothing once the one inversion per round is spread thin.
  • Two costs grow with the width. (1) Lane start-up: each lane begins with two scalar multiplications and an addition, about 12 microseconds a lane, paid once. 1024 lanes cost about 12 ms, a quarter of a 28-bit relation phase and under 5% of a 32-bit one (lane preparation 109.7 ns/trial at 28 bits, 35.1 at 32). This is the same cost the counted S charges, and it is why wide batching loses on small curves and why the knee moves up with the size. (2) Cache reach: the round's touched lines (guard slot and table slot, 2 x 64 bytes a lane) must stay in L1 until their trial; at 1024 lanes they do not, and the guard insert and the oracle probe slow down (49.2 + 90.6 ns at 32 lanes, 62.7 + 126.3 at 1024 lanes, 32-bit). Neither can beat the knee, because neither is present at 32 lanes. A smaller lane layout was therefore not built: it could only flatten the tail.
  • The floor is hash-table work. The guard insert and the oracle probe are about 137.2 ns/trial at 28 bits (139.8 at 32 bits) at the knee, a third to a half of the trial, and do not depend on the width; lanes cannot touch them.
  • Intermediate widths: relation ns/trial at 28 bits: 16 lanes 330.0, 24 319.3, 32 318.1, 48 327.0, 64 364.1: a flat optimum over 16-48. In the grid, 32 lanes against 16 at 32 bits is 0.991 of the solve time (interval spans 1).
  • walk-prime-fast was slower because of its inverse, and is fixed. It ran at 1.658x the plain walk's relation time (interval 1.58-1.74) because Fp64::inv used the binary extended Euclid on 64-bit words; the plain walk's step uses 32-bit word Euclid for moduli below 2^32. A 32-bit word-Euclid path for moduli below 2^32 brings it to 1.042 (interval 0.996-1.090) of the walk, a relation-phase new/old of 0.625 (interval 0.594-0.659). 240 of 240 records are identical outside timing, so no counted quantity moved; the walk and the lane arms are unchanged within noise (new/old 0.995, 0.985, 0.978).
  • Joint tuning: the optimal base does not move. Whole-solve wall time is lowest at base 512 at 28 bits and 1024 at 32 bits for every method; the build of the B^2/4-entry table grows faster than the relation phase shrinks. Best configurations against the incumbent walk at the same base: 28 bits 51.4 ms (16 lanes) against 62.5 ms; 32 bits 332 ms (32 lanes) against 533 ms. The cache-blocked table (mitm-prime-blocked) halves the build at 2048 and above but its probe is slower, so it only wins at base 4096 (32-bit: 1,092 ms against 2,908 ms).
  • Not done. The harness's prime_search curves stop at 32 bits, so no 34- or 36-bit ladder; chunked inversion and software prefetch have no handle (the table is a std hash map whose slot addresses are not exposed, and the touch loop is already the overlapped-load form); an adaptive width has no room above a flat 16-48 optimum.
