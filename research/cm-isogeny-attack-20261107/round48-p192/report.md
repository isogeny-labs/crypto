# P-192 label-descent extraction and invariant construction target

Derived reduction. Let G have prime order r and Q=xG. Let H(A,B) be an explicitly represented nonconstant polynomial over F_r of total degree d<r. Suppose H(a,b)=H(c,e) whenever aG+bQ=cG+eQ, for every scalar pair. Then H(A,B)=h(A+xB) as a polynomial identity. Its coefficients yield x by one field division after scanning the representation. This is a reduction with an invariant-construction premise, not an algorithm for constructing that invariant. [pcc:unverified]

Proof of descent. Set U=A+xB and V=B, and F(U,V)=H(U-xV,V). All representations (u-xv,v) correspond to uG, hence F(u,v)=H(u,0) for all u,v in F_r. The difference has total degree at most d<r. A polynomial of degree below r in each variable that vanishes on the full r-by-r grid is the zero polynomial, by applying the univariate root bound twice. Therefore F(U,V)=h(U), where h(U)=H(U,0), and H(A,B)=h(A+xB). Since H is nonconstant, h has some degree d>=1 and leading coefficient c!=0. [pcc:unverified]

Explicit extractor. Write C_d for the coefficient of A^d and C_{d-1,1} for that of A^(d-1)B. Expansion gives C_d=c and C_{d-1,1}=cdx. Since d<r, d is invertible, so x=C_{d-1,1}/(d C_d) modulo r. For an arbitrary supplied polynomial, verify the whole identity H_B=x H_A and finally check [x]G=Q. Under total degree below r, the derivative identity also implies descent: after the same substitution F_V=0, so F is independent of V. Dense input scanning costs O(d^2) coefficient operations; sparse scanning is linear in the supplied term count. Coefficient size, representation and construction costs are not free. [pcc:unverified]

Order-five condition. Suppose additionally H(lambda A,lambda B)=H(A,B) for the nontrivial fifth root lambda used in the P-192 construction. The same full-grid root bound makes this a polynomial identity. Since h(lambda U)=h(U), every nonzero coefficient of h has degree divisible by five. Thus a nonconstant descending invariant has degree at least five. At degree five it is c0+c5(A+xB)^5, with x recovered from the A^4B and A^5 coefficients. The six homogeneous coefficients lie on a binomially scaled degree-five Veronese curve; guessing an arbitrary homogeneous invariant does not enforce the needed descent. [pcc:unverified]

Counterexample and controls. H=A^5 is invariant under simultaneous multiplication of both labels by lambda. For the public scalar x=17, labels (1,0) and (-16,1) both represent G, but their H values differ. Consequently order-five invariance by itself cannot define a partition of elliptic-curve points. The checker constructs 20 public descending invariants with degrees 5, 10, 15 and mixed support, for x in {0,1,2,17,20261107}. A separate checker extracts x from the top-degree coefficients and expands H(U-xV,V), checking cancellation of every positive V coefficient. Constant, B^5, and A^5+B^5 inputs exercise rejection paths. These are exact fixtures, with no unknown scalar in their construction. [pcc:unverified]

Separate coordinate-degree bound. For P-192, r<p. Suppose f(T) in F_p[T] interpolates x([k]G) for every integer 1<=k<r. Write G=(u,v) and E:y^2=x^3+a_E x+b_E. For k=2,...,r-2 the addition law gives (f(k+1)+f(k-1))(f(k)-u)^2=2[u f(k)^2+(a_E+u^2)f(k)+a_E u+2b_E]. There are r-3 distinct arguments in F_p, and no denominator vanishes there. If f has positive degree d, the difference polynomial has degree exactly 3d: its leading coefficient is twice the cube of the leading coefficient of f, whereas the right side has degree at most 2d. It cannot vanish at r-3 points unless 3d>=r-3. A constant f cannot represent all nonzero multiples of G, since a fixed x-coordinate has at most two curve points. Therefore d>=ceil((r-3)/3), which is 2092367245128893587945263141058686337922398257727614094693 for P-192. The numerator identity is checked symbolically. [pcc:unverified]

Field boundary. The label-descent reduction is over F_r. The coordinate interpolation bound is over F_p with integer indices before wraparound. There is no field embedding identifying these two fields. The latter bound does not rule out an arbitrary F_r encoding, an oracle, or a succinct high-degree function; it only excludes the specified low-degree F_p coordinate interpolation proposal. The written proofs are algebraic derivations, not machine-checked universal theorems. [pcc:unverified]
