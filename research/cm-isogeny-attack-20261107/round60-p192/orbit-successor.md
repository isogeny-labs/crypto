# Successor construction: labeled Mobius orbits and changing-target powers

Proposed successor with derived algebra, 2026-11-08. This file is a concrete interface for the next native qualification, not a completed orbit-walk experiment or novelty claim. The current cross-norm implementation and its first-variation cost remain those in report.md. [pcc:unverified]

Mobius action. For a point W=[z]G, the norm primitive computes T(W)=[1/(1-z)]G by M_W(G), outside its public exceptional inputs. The inexpensive complement S(W)=G-W encodes1-z. On the projective scalar line the transformations S(z)=1-z and T(z)=1/(1-z) satisfy S^2=T^3=1 and S*T*S=T^-1. The six transforms are z,1-z,1/z,1/(1-z),(z-1)/z,z/(z-1). A generic orbit can therefore be constructed with two norm calls, giving z,T(z),T^2(z), and three complements. Exceptional values0,1,infinity and-1,2,1/2 have public scalar labels; the possible roots of z^2-z+1 have their own stabilizers and require explicit handling. [pcc:unverified]

Transporter matrices. Represent a tracked scalar by f_M(k)=(a*k+b)/(c*k+d), M=[[a,b],[c,d]] in PGL2(F_r). Use S=[[-1,1],[0,1]] and T=[[0,1],[-1,1]]. Translation by a known scalar and multiplication by a known nonzero scalar also have2x2 matrices. All walk moves and canonicalizing transforms therefore update M by left multiplication; the tracked rational degree stays1. Canonicalization must return both the chosen encoded point and its matrix, not merely an orbit identity. A projective pole is a separate infinity tag and supplies the linear equation c*k+d=0. [pcc:unverified]

Collision equation. Equal finite encoded points for matrices M1,M2 give A*k^2+B*k+C=0, where A=a1*c2-a2*c1, B=a1*d2+b1*c2-a2*d1-b2*c1, C=b1*d2-b2*d1. Reject projectively proportional matrices, whose collision is tautological. Otherwise solve the nonzero polynomial of degree at most2 in F_r and verify every candidate against Q=[k]G. This supplies the scalar equation missing from an unmarked curve-identity collision. The next qualification must test matrix normalization, all exceptional orbits, denominator-zero cases, genuine collisions, tautology rejection, and replayed scalar verification. [pcc:unverified]

Cost boundary. A generic canonicalization invokes M_W(G) and M_T(W)(G). Both source targets change with the walk state. Charge both target-dependent constructors and first-variation evaluations at every invocation; the original Q-only preprocessing is not reusable by assumption. The direct norm backend is linear in r and does not make this an efficient walk. The purpose of the successor is to specify and check the transporter mechanism while a genuinely compressed first-variation circuit remains a separate dependency. No mixing or birthday-speedup conclusion is drawn here. [pcc:unverified]

Auxiliary-power alternative. The ordinary-action bridge also gives a changing-target multiplication operation on encoded scalars. For d with bit length l and Hamming weight w, left-to-right binary powering builds[k^d]G using l-1 squarings of the current encoded scalar and w-1 multiplications by the original k. Each squaring requires a constructor for its current point target; the original-k multiplications can reuse its constructor. Thus the call count is l+w-2 rather than d-1, but the former introduces changing-target setup. This is standard repeated squaring, not a new Cheon theorem. A cost ledger must compare the sums of the actual constructors and action calls, not only the oracle-query count. [pcc:unverified]
