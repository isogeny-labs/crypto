# Cross-norm, transverse coefficients and marked action

Derived construction, 2026-11-08. This written proof specifies the zero-order identity, deformation and scalar-action bridge. Native checks are recorded separately. Standard function-field norms, divisor calculations and isogeny facts are background; no novelty claim for them or a trusted scientific verdict is made. [pcc:unverified]

Assumptions. Let E/F_p be a nonsingular short Weierstrass curve y^2=x^3+ax+b, p>3, with E(F_p)=<G> of odd prime order r!=p. Let Q=[k]G be nonzero and Q!=+/-G. Set phi=1-pi, with pi the p-power Frobenius. Then phi is separable of degree r, has kernel E(F_p), and has differential1. In particular x(phi(P)) has leading term x(P) at the origin. All rational identities below are on E x E, interpreted first at generic points. [pcc:unverified]

Zero-order identity. Define N_Q(P,R)=product_(j=0..r-1)(x(P+jG)-x(R+jQ)). Then

N_Q(P,R)=c_Q*(x(phi(P))-x(phi(R))),

where c_Q=product_(j=1..r-1)(x(jG)-x(jQ)) is nonzero and lies in F_p. To prove the identity, inspect divisors. A factor vanishes when P-R=j(Q-G) or P+R=-j(Q+G). Since G-Q and G+Q generate the full rational group, the zero divisors of the product are exactly the two families P-R in E(F_p) and P+R in E(F_p), each with its generic multiplicity1. Its poles are the vertical and horizontal rational translates of the origins, each with multiplicity2. These are precisely the zero and pole divisors of x(phi(P))-x(phi(R)). Their ratio is constant on the projective integral surface. Let P approachO and then R approachO to obtain the stated c_Q; nonzero j gives x(jG)!=x(jQ). [pcc:unverified]

Compact expression. On a denominator-safe affine chart, x(phi(P))=((Y^p+Y)/(X^p-X))^2-X^p-X. This is the same short Frobenius normalizer constructed in Round56. It supplies a compact expression for N_Q/c_Q. The direct constructor for c_Q enumerates r-1 factors and is charged; it is not assumed free. The identities c_(-Q)=c_Q and c_(k^-1 G)=c_Q follow respectively from x(-T)=x(T) and reindexing the nonzero factors. The latter introduces r-1 minus signs, an even number. The native panels observe a single constant on each declared target panel, but no stronger universal k-independence is asserted. [pcc:unverified]

Square-zero deformation. Put d_j=x(P+jG)-x(R+jQ) and a_j=x(P+jG). In K[s,t]/(s^2,st,t^2), where K contains P,R, define F_Q(s,t)=product_j(d_j+s+t*a_j)=A+s*B+t*C. Coefficients are A=product_j d_j, B=sum_j product_(l!=j)d_l, and C=sum_j a_j*product_(l!=j)d_l. These are norm coefficients of the coupled graph, not a substitution into an uncancelled quotient. A streaming multiplication uses the update(A,B,C)<-(A*d,B*d+A,C*d+A*a), with right sides from the previous state. It does not invert any vanishing factor. [pcc:unverified]

Marked specialization. Choose P in E(F_(p^2)) with2P nonrational and let U be any rational subgroup point, includingO. Set R=P+U. Every translated input is finite. The plus equality P+jG=R+jQ holds for the unique j satisfying[j](G-Q)=U. A minus equality would make2P rational and is impossible. Thus exactly one d_j vanishes. At this specialization B=product_(l!=j)d_l is nonzero and C/B=x(P+jG). Since jG=[(1-k)^-1]U, lift the two y choices of C/B, subtractP and retain the rational point. Exactly one is rational: if both were rational, their sum would force2P rational. This defines M_Q(U)=[(1-k)^-1]U, including M_Q(O)=O. No scalar k or singular index is an input to the complete norm evaluator. [pcc:unverified]

A differential relation, derived. Keeping R fixed, the derivative of the unique vanishing d_j with respect to x(P) is y(P+jG)/y(P), by translation invariance of dx/(2y). Differentiating the zero-order identity and using d(phi)=1 gives B/c_Q=y(phi(P))/y(P+jG) at the marked input. This relates one transverse coefficient to the hidden translated y-coordinate. The native point-action implementation uses C/B and the two-choice lift instead; no claim that this differential relation alone gives a complete decoder is made. [pcc:unverified]

Why the short base expression does not determine the deformation. N_Q(P,R)=N_(-Q)(P,R) as rational functions, by the zero-order identity and c_(-Q)=c_Q. Consequently all derivatives in the original P,R variables are identical for that pair. For nonzero U, however, M_Q(U) and M_(-Q)(U) are distinct, because(1-k)^-1!=(1+k)^-1 modulo r for nonzero k. The transverse coefficient pair therefore cannot be recovered from the common base function and its P,R derivatives alone. An evaluator must retain the graph-dependent deformation. This is an explicit non-identifiability statement for that interface, not a general circuit-complexity lower bound. [pcc:unverified]

Ordinary marked action. To obtain[k]U, first handle public point cases Q=O,Q=G,Q=-G and[2]Q=G by returningO,U,-U and[(r+1)/2]U, respectively. Otherwise compute bG=M_Q(G), form Q2=G-bG, and return U-M_Q2(U). Indeed b=(1-k)^-1, Q2=[1-b]G, and(1-(1-b))^-1=b^-1=1-k. Thus the last subtraction gives[k]U. The second target is nonzero and not+/-G; its exceptional value-G would mean2k=1, already handled. The first oracle call and the Q2 constructor are reusable across later inputsU. The executable panels cover all declared nonzero Q targets; Q=O is the separate trivial branch of this written algorithm. [pcc:unverified]

Paired-resultant interface, derived. For S=(S1,S2),T=(T1,T2), write x(Si+Ti)=Hi-Ki and x(Si-Ti)=Hi+Ki, with the Round57 signed addition quantities. Put A0=H1-H2,B0=K1-K2. The paired cross-norm factor and its two first coefficients are A0^2-B0^2,2*A0 and2*(H1*A0-K1*B0). This follows by multiplying the two square-zero factors. K1*K2 retains the relative sign y(T1)y(T2). At the chosen nonrational shifts, the ordinary addition denominators against rational babies are nonzero even when a cross-difference vanishes. These formulas define a coupled first-variation norm contract for a later compressed evaluator. They are written algebra here; the native program directly multiplies unpaired factors. [pcc:unverified]

Zero-factor cancellation and subset scope. Once a block contains the unique zero factor, the common product of all other blocks cancels in C/B. An arbitrary subset does not promise this containment. The P-192 control uses sixteen indices and deliberately sets U=[j](G-Q) for one of its eight declared j values. It verifies the local coefficient interface on public points; it does not locate the singular index for an arbitrary P-192 input or evaluate the full norm. [pcc:unverified]

Costs and independent public transfer. The implemented complete graph norm uses O(r) point work and coefficient contraction, with explicit graph storage. Its product recurrence and the independent prefix/suffix cofactor method use O(r) field operations after inputs. A stream can reduce working storage, but the stored native graphs and reference arrays are charged. A separate public baby/giant method hashes[b](G-Q) together with[b]G, then scans U-[im](G-Q) while retaining[im]G, for m=ceil(sqrt(r)). A collision returns[j]G. This uses O(sqrt(r)) point work and memory with a reusable table. It checks the norm outputs without a scalar label. The target-dependent coefficient evaluator must improve its charged graph/encoding/contraction cost to make the new norm interface useful computationally; the compact zero-order formula does not establish that improvement.
