## Ranked leads

### Tier 1: could replace the per-trial loop

1. **Line sieve through each walk position.**
   - *Sources:* Sarkar–Singh, DCC 2017, ePrint 2014/815 (abstract only). Vitse–Wallet, LATINCRYPT
     2015 (abstract only), which adds large-prime variants and reports ≈ 3× on Diem/Kochinke
     relation search.
   - *Mechanism*, checked by hand here:
     - For reduced `D = (u, v)` with `deg u = g`, let `a = v + c·u`. Then `u | a² − f`, the cofactor
       `E_c = (a² − f)/u` has degree `g + 1`, and `div(y − a) = D + E_c − (2g+1)∞`, so
       `[D] = −[E_c]`.
     - A base point `(x₀, ±y₀)` lies on `E_c` exactly when `c = (±y₀ − v(x₀))/u(x₀)`.
     - So one pass over the base, with the `u(x₀)` inverses batched, buckets the `c` values. A `c`
       hit `g + 1` times at distinct `x` gives a relation with no smoothness test.
   - *Count (inference):*
     - One line costs O(m) multiplications and yields about `p/(g+1)!` relations.
     - Full base needs about `m ≈ p/2` relations, so about `(g+1)!/2` lines: 3, 12 and 60 at genus 2,
       3 and 4.
     - The walk instead needs about `g!·m` trials, each a Jacobian operation plus an oracle call.
   - *Rows:* rows carry only `(α, β)` plus `g + 1` base entries. The ≈ 64 dense step columns go away,
     and the dense block of S2b with them.
   - *Risks:*
     - Rows from one line share `(α, β)` and come from one pencil, so rank and independence must be
       measured.
     - GTTD's analysis assumes independent `(α, β)`, so any exponent claim becomes heuristic.
     - Skip `u(x₀) = 0`.
     - Handle `y₀ = 0`.
     - Require `g + 1` distinct `x`.
   - *Gate:* sequence. Every relation is checked in the Jacobian, and every `k` is verified.
   - *First step:* a feasibility probe outside protocol. Measure relations per line, rank growth and
     multiplications per relation on round eight's rows, then freeze a protocol.
2. **Sieve with large primes.** In the same line, `g` hits leave a degree-1 cofactor: a large prime,
   with no Legendre symbol needed. `g − 1` hits leave a quadratic: one Legendre symbol and one square
   root. This is Vitse–Wallet's extension, and it is the route to the genus-3/4 exponent targets
   with sieve-speed relations. It depends on lead 16. Gate: sequence. [pcc:unverified]

### Tier 2: exact per-trial levers

These matter if the sieve fails, and some survive it: a sieve still needs one Jacobian operation
per line, and a large-prime finish needs a Legendre symbol and a square root.

3. **Fixed-degree oracle with root reuse.**
   - *Change:* compute `w = x^((p−1)/2) mod u` with fixed-degree squaring and shift. Test
     `x·w² ≡ x`. Skip the gcd when `disc ≠ 0`. Take roots from `gcd(u, w ± 1)` instead of a second
     exponentiation. Skip the multiplicity loop when `disc ≠ 0`.
   - *Prior art:* R1 `split_fast`/`roots_fast` in `examples/ic_improve4_01_genus3_rel.rs`
     (1294, 1683, 1725). There it measured split test 258 → 163 units, roots 92 → 34, and whole
     trial 689 → 325 at genus 3 (`research/ic_improve3_20261015/01-genus3-relations/README.md`).
   - *Estimate:* about −300 REDC per trial at genus 3 and at genus 4 (code hunt, counted).
   - Exact.
4. **Closed-form discriminant.**
   - *Change:* the cubic in about 9 multiplications; the quartic as `27Δ = 4I³ − J²`. This replaces
     the generic resultant.
   - *Estimate:* 56 → ≈ 18 REDC and −2 inversions at genus 3; 86 → ≈ 24 REDC and −3 inversions at
     genus 4. The second figure accounts for E9's genus-4 miss.
   - Exact.
5. **One-inversion Cantor for `+D` steps.**
   - *Change:* a coprime fast path that skips the second xgcd and the `s₃` term, with a half-xgcd
     and top-part exact division in `reduce`.
   - *Prior art:* R1 `cadd_fast` (`examples/ic_improve4_01_genus3_rel.rs:1451`), 206M + 6I →
     105M + 1I at genus 3.
   - *Estimate:* −40 / −70 / −110 REDC and −0.5 inversions per trial at genus 2 / 3 / 4, before the
     `reduce` rewrite, which takes another −50–60% of `reduce`.
   - Exact.
6. **Genus-2 explicit formulas.**
   - *Change:* Lange-style addition `I + 22M + 3S`, plus the mixed degree-2 + degree-1 case, with a
     Cantor fallback on degenerate inputs.
   - *Prior art:* `examples/n2_routes.rs:626` (`jadd_fast`): 113M + 4I → 41M + 1I add, 48M + 1I
     double (`research/weil_descent_angles_20261018/REPORT.md:270–279`).
   - Exact.
7. **Genus-2 square root and Legendre symbol.**
   - *Problem:* `Fp::sqrt` searches for a non-residue on every call, then runs a separate Legendre
     exponentiation.
   - *Change:* cache the non-residue and the Tonelli–Shanks constants. For `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, use one
     exponentiation plus an `r² = a` check. Use a binary Jacobi symbol (Hamburg ePrint 2021/1271;
     Pornin 2020/972) instead of Euler's exponentiation.
   - *Estimate:* −200 of 362 REDC at p = 1009, −60 of 266 at p = 4001. This explains why the
     genus-2 p = 1009 and 4001 rows have the slowest oracles.
   - Exact. Roots are sorted, so the choice of root sign is immaterial.
8. **p-sized tables at these sizes:** inverse, quadratic residue and square-root tables for
   p ≤ ≈ 2^20. `ic.rs` already allocates a p-sized `by_x`. They remove the remaining 3.0 / 7 / 9.5
   inversions per trial, and nearly all of the genus-2 oracle. They belong on a context struct,
   since `Fp` is `Copy`. At these p they supersede batched inversion. Exact.
9. **Small-p field core.** For `p < 2^32`, `a·b` fits in `u64`, so use Barrett (three multiply
   instructions) instead of two REDCs. Accumulate convolutions in `u64` and delay reduction in
   `divrem`. Expect 2–3× fewer multiply instructions throughout. Keep REDC for `p ≥ 2^32`. Exact.
10. **Cubic and quartic splitting tests.**
    - *Genus 3:* the C1 Lucas cubic-character test plus `roots_c1`
      (`examples/ic_improve4_01_genus3_rel.rs:1893, 1944, 1991`). Measured: test plus roots per trial
      0.438×, whole solve 0.756× at 2^22. It falls back to R1 when `9 | n`.
    - *Genus 4:* the quartic test through the resolvent cubic (Kappe–Warren 1989, recalled), which is
      plan T2(c).
    - Exact decisions.
11. **`divrem` micro-fixes.** Skip the cancelling top product, and zero only the touched range.
    About 10 / 60 / 90 REDC per trial. Exact.
12. **Const-generic `Poly<N>` per genus.** `CAP = 24` makes a `Div` 400 bytes, zero-filled and
    copied by value. This cannot be counted; it needs clean timing. Exact.

### Tier 3: sequence-changing walk levers

13. **Fewer or sparser D-steps.**
    - *Options:*
      - raise `d_step_in`: at genus 3, `d_step_in = 16` cuts walk REDC by ≈ 45%;
      - a Teske r-adding walk over `αD₁ + βD₂` combinations (GTTD's "random linear combinations");
      - the 20-adding multiplier walk with restarts (repo prior art, `ic_improve4_01_genus3_rel.rs`
        ≈ 3449/3655), which keeps rows at weight ≤ g + 1 and needs 0.993–1.004× the trials of
        independent sampling.
    - *Risk:* correlated rows, and more solve rounds.
    - Gate: sequence.
14. **Batched inversion over lockstep walks (T2a).** Repo C2 measured walk addition 0.848× and whole
    0.959× at genus 3, 2^22, after R1. At small p, tables (lead 8) supersede it.
15. **Threads (T2e).** Order-preserving parallel relation collection exists for j = 0 curves
    (`benchmarks/20261016-j0-parallel-relations`). This is a wall-clock lever, reported per core.

### Tier 4: exponent and accounting (T1, T3, T4)

16. **Double-large-prime graph with online substitution (T1).** Repo
    `ic_improve4_01_genus3_rel.rs` `ic_solve`/`propagate`/`lpcol_rows` (≈ 3504–4141). It moved the
    genus-3 exponent from q^1.650 to q^1.300 over 2^8–2^15 (`benchmarks/README.md` row
    `20261014-ic-next10-07`). Port it generic in g. Genus 4 first, per PLAN.md §5.
17. **Genus-2 first-cycle 2LP (T1, genus 2).** Repo `n2_routes.rs:1621` (Arm J), 0.27–0.37 trials
    per p at h = 4–8 (VAL-20261024-wd3).
18. **External pins from GTTD**, full text at
    `/Volumes/SSD990-2/research/extracted_text/2004-153.txt`:
    - genus 3, q ≈ 2^15, #B = 512: 815,473 trials (0.78·q^{4/3});
    - q ≈ 2^27: 4·10^10 trials and 9 days of relations, 5.3× faster than rho;
    - 20 µs per step on a 1.7 GHz Pentium-M. [pcc:unverified]

    T1 must reach q ≈ 2^15 at genus 3 before any external comparison means anything.
19. **Linear algebra (T3).** Port `lpsgebw2` (SGE + block Wiedemann (2,1); 0.653× whole at 2^20).
    Track recombined row weight: GTTD's cycle length is ≈ log q, and Laine–Lauter (J. Math. Cryptol.,
    ePrint 2014/346, full text local) give `w ≤ 18 − 8 ln λ + ln q`. Prefer shortest cycles.
20. **κ and memory (T4).**
    - `g3ghs-v1` pins C4 for cost and `tuned:lpsgewd` at κ = 1.41 for the exponent
      (`research/ic_tool_program/baselines.json`).
    - R2's rule: `ln κ = 0.2669 − 0.0941(b − 20)`.
    - Diem's tree-only 2LP stores `Õ(q^{1−1/g+1/g²})` (arXiv math/0606607). Laine–Lauter measured
      ≈ 370 B per graph vertex.

## Do not chase again (measured negatives in this repo, and literature)

- **Single large prime with a reduced base, p ≤ 4001, genus 2–4:** about 1%, no gain. Half and
  quarter bases at genus 2 cost 1.23× and 1.60×. The jump count (8, 16 or 32) makes no difference
  (`RESEARCH_HYPERELLIPTIC_IC_RHO.md:1245–1354`; `research/four_followups_20261021/REPORT.md`).
- **A falling κ raises the fitted exponent** (rounds 2–4).
- **C3, oversampling plus clique removal:** 0.979× at 2^22, but the gain shrinks with q and the LA
  exponent rises by +0.088.
- **Block Wiedemann (4,4) and block Lanczos, single-threaded:** 1.18× and 1.29× worse.
- **Native difference-rows:** the solve was 4× slower (comment in `ic.rs`).
- **Memory-capped IC reverts to rho.** A large cofactor gives no IC win.
- **Counted cuts understate wall clock.** A 60% counted cut gave 1.5–2.2× on the clock, so report
  both.
- **Kummer-surface arithmetic does not apply:** the walk needs full addition.
- **Thériault's single-large-prime exponent `q^{2−4/(2g+1)}` is never below q at genus 2.** It is
  only a T1 rung at g ≥ 3.
- **Extension-field sieves do not apply over F_p:** Joux–Vitse's 960× and the lab's ≈ 10³× rely on
  an F_{p²} identity.
