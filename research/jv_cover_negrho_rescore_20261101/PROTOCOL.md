# Protocol: frozen re-scoring of the Joux-Vitse cover against the matched negation-map rho (frozen 2026-11-01, before any new run)

Labels everywhere: **measured** (read from a run record), **derived** (computed from measured values by `fit/`, Rust), **model** (extrapolation).

## Question
`research/jv_cover_crossover_20261101/` compared the cover against `rhodp` (no negation map). Its independent review (`review/README.md`, red team C1/C2/R5-R7, validator 2.3) concluded that the negation-map rho is the matched reference and that the per-prime rhostrong n = 6 was too thin; the red team's numbers were post-hoc. This study repeats the comparison as a frozen measurement: the matched rho on the **same instances**, enough runs per prime, in two units, with the verdict rules fixed in advance. Per the repository's success criterion each row is reported as a gap to the matched rho (the distance remaining), not as a pass/fail gate; a measured improvement over the index-calculus baseline is still a success even when the gap is above 1.

Scope: the `jv_cover` genus-3 cover weak class only (never prime order). Lower-bound comparison. No statement about prime-order or deployed curves. Nothing enters the leaderboard, `benchmarks/` or any other study.

## Arms
* **Cover arm (no new runs).** The 9 existing records `research/jv_cover_crossover_20261101/results/dlp_p{1301,1451,1601}_s{1,2,3}.json` (copied unchanged into `results/inputs/cover/`, sha256 in `results/inputs/MANIFEST.sha256`). All must be `solved`, `correct`, `mismatches == 0`. Cover S = `total_muls` / (`c_add_e` sqrt(l)) (measured). These were measured with the crossover study's binary (sha256 `4a1284e9...9752`, linear stage `JV_LIN` default "fast"); the review showed the predecessor's "sparse" stage counts 1/0.949 as many linear multiplications; this is a property of the cover records and is not changed here.
* **Primary reference: the negation-map rho**, `run_strong_rho` via `--exp rhostrong --neg 1 --batch 16 --r 256 --dp-bits 16` (the 2-cycle-detecting negation walk, 256 adding functions, 16 walkers sharing a Montgomery batched inversion, distinguished points of 16 bits), on the **same 9 instances** (p in {1301, 1451, 1601}, instance seeds 1-3). The tool asserts that `l` and `c_add_e` of every rho record equal those of the cover record of the same (p, seed).
  * **Reused:** the 18 existing runs (run0, run1 on each instance), copied into `results/inputs/old_rhostrong/`.
  * **New:** run indices 2..K+1 on every instance (indices that do not collide with run0/run1; the run index fixes the walk's seed, independently of the instance seed, `strong_rho_core`), K = 12 (below).
  * **Red-team controls (reference estimator (c) only):** the 64 runs at p = 251 (seeds 1-2, run0..31, `--dp-bits 12`, the study's binary) in `review/red_team/controls/rhostrong_neg_p251_run*.json`, copied into `results/inputs/redteam_p251/`.
* The settings are identical for new and old runs. The new binary differs from the old (see Replay).

## Units, both primary (verified in `src/cryptanalysis/jv_rho_strong.rs` lines 145-171, 777-797)
The binary counts per run the `F_{q^3}` products (`mul`), squarings (`sq`), inversions (`inv`) of the walk and prices them: `muls_harness = 55 mul + 55 sq + 166 inv` (the harness's own `Fq3` prices, one affine addition = 331 `F_p` multiplications = `c_add_e`), `muls_tuned = 24 mul + 18 sq + 62 inv` (Karatsuba 24, Chung-Hasan 18, adjugate inversion 62: a textbook count crediting rho). Then `s_harness = muls_harness / (c_add_e sqrt l)` and `s_tuned = muls_tuned / (c_add_e sqrt l)`. The batched-inversion products (3(k-1) per round) are inside `mul`.
* **U1, group additions.** Ratio = cover S / rho `s_harness`. The cover S is in group-addition equivalents (`total_muls / c_add_e`) per sqrt(l); `s_harness` is the rho's counted harness-priced multiplications in the same equivalents, i.e. group additions per sqrt(l). Confirmation from the source: every walk step is exactly one group addition (`walk_adds == steps`), and `s_harness` equals `steps/sqrt(l)` up to the price of doublings, setup and batch products; the tool prints the difference on all runs (the 18 existing runs: -0.02% on average). `s_harness` is the frozen U1 reference; `steps/sqrt(l)` is used only for the theory comparison and the red-team consistency controls.
* **U2, textbook F_p multiplications.** Ratio = (cover `total_muls` / sqrt(l)) / (rho `muls_tuned` / sqrt(l)). Because `s_tuned` is normalised by the same `c_add_e` as the cover S, this equals cover S / `s_tuned` (c_add_e cancels; it is 331 for every record). The cover's `total_muls` are atomic `F_p` multiplications and carry no extension-field arithmetic that could be tuned; the rho's tuned count is a textbook count, not a calibration. Neither side prices its `F_p` additions in U2 (section 5a below covers them).

## Reference estimators (all reported; the primary is chosen now)
* **(a)** per-prime mean over that prime's runs: 3 instances x (2 old + K new) = 3(2+K) = 42 runs per prime at K = 12 (at least 15).
* **(b)** pooled mean over the three new primes: 9(2+K) = 126 runs.
* **(c)** (b) plus the red team's 64 p = 251 runs (190 runs).
A reference mean is the plain mean of the unit's per-run values over the estimator's runs; the same mean is used for all three primes under (b) and (c).

**Homogeneity test across p (frozen).** On steps/sqrt(l) of the runs at the three new primes (3 groups): one-way ANOVA F and Kruskal-Wallis H, each with a **permutation** p-value (20000 permutations of the pooled values over the groups, group sizes kept). **Homogeneity holds if both permutation p-values are >= 0.05.** (The Kruskal-Wallis chi-square p, the same tests on s_harness and s_tuned, with the p = 251 group added, and across the 9 instances are printed for information.) With low power, "holds" means "not rejected".
**Frozen choice of primary: (b) if homogeneity holds, otherwise (a).** The verdicts under all three estimators are reported; only the primary decides.

**Comparison to theory and positive control (frozen).** Theory for the negation walk with r = 256 adding functions: `sqrt(pi/4) / sqrt(1 - 1/r)` = 0.88796 steps/sqrt(l). z = (mean - theory) / (sd/sqrt(n)) with n the number of runs. **The pooled references (b) and (c) must lie within 3 se of theory (|z| <= 3), or the reference is flagged** in the README and every verdict carries the flag. Per-prime z are reported for (a).

## Statistics (frozen)
For prime p and estimator e: **S/rho = geometric mean over the 3 cover seeds of S, divided by the reference mean** (unit U1: `s_harness`; unit U2: `s_tuned`). Interval: 95% percentile bootstrap, **20000 resamples**, resampling jointly (i) the 3 cover seeds with replacement (independently per prime) and (ii) the reference runs with replacement within each instance (so the group sizes are kept); under (b) and (c) one resample of the instance groups is shared by the three primes (and by the units, w values and estimators). RNG: splitmix64, fixed seed (`fit/src/main.rs`). Verdict on [lo, hi]: **below** if hi < 1; **above** if lo > 1; otherwise **not resolved**. With 3 seeds the seed resampling has 10 distinct multisets (stated, not corrected). **Distance remaining** (the repository's success criterion) = the factor by which the cover must improve to reach the matched rho: S/rho when it is above 1 (with its interval); if S/rho < 1 the cover is ahead by 1/(S/rho) and the distance is none.

## Section 5a (frozen)
* rhostrong emits native counts (`canon_cmp + hash + dp_check + scalar_ops + table_ops + cycle_cmp`) and also `fp_adds`. The cover emits `f4_native_ops` (a **lower bound**: its `F_p` additions, pair selection, row-assembly and memory traffic are not emitted); the cover's F_p additions are not emitted at all. Byte counters are not operations and are not charged (5a, 2026-11-01).
* **Band:** native operations are priced at **w = 0, 0.1 and 1 group-addition equivalents each, on both arms**; S_w = S + w (native / sqrt l). In U2 the same w is converted at c_add_e = 331 `F_p` multiplications per equivalent (numerator and denominator), so U2_w = geometric mean (S + w nat_cover) / (s_tuned + w nat_rho). The band is evaluated with the primary estimator and the same bootstrap draws.
* **Order holds across the band** for a (unit, p) cell only if the verdict (below / above / not resolved) is the same at w = 0, 0.1 and 1. The cover count is a lower bound, so an "above" verdict is robust to the missing counts (a complete count can only raise the cover) while a "below" or "not resolved" verdict is not. The rho `fp_adds` per sqrt(l) is listed as an unpriced counter; since the cover does not emit its F_p additions it cannot be priced on both sides, so it is not part of the band and is reported as a listed gap.

## Controls (numeric rules, stated before data)
1. **Replay (before the new runs).** `run_strong_rho` p=1301 seed 1 run0 (and p=1451 s1 run0, p=1601 s1 run0, p=1601 s3 run1) is rerun with the new binary and compared with the existing record field by field (every field except `wall_ms`); the result is in `results/replay/REPLAY.txt`. All must be bit-identical; any difference is a finding that the instrument changed and the old 18 runs are then excluded from the references.
2. **Positive control:** above (pooled references within 3 se of theory).
3. **Consistency control (negative control for tool or definition errors).** The tool recomputes (i) the crossover study's own S/rhodp and **S/rhostrong (n = 6 per prime)** with that study's bootstrap procedure and RNG stream: point estimates and interval ends must match the published values (README of the crossover study) **to 3 decimals**; and (ii) the red team's R4, R5 (pooled 82 runs, steps/sqrt l), R6 (theory) and R7 (pooled 82 runs, s_tuned) numbers: point estimates must match to 3 decimals; interval ends are informational (a different RNG stream; tolerance 0.01). A failure means the tool or a definition is wrong and no new verdict is reported until it is fixed.
4. Every run must be `correct` and not `capped`, with `negation`, batch 16, r 256; an excluded run is listed (never silently replaced).

## Budget, timing and the cut (frozen)
Host: 4 vCPU Intel Xeon, 15 GB, nothing else running. The old runs used one process of 3 seeds (`--seeds 3`, `rayon` over the seeds, i.e. 3 single-thread runs at a time, 3 cores); the new runs use **one process per (p, seed, run)**, single-threaded, **4 concurrently** (`xargs -P4`), so all four cores are used. Timing (measured): the 18 old runs took 171-754 s of single-thread wall (mean 357 s: 257 s at 1301, 374 s at 1451, 439 s at 1601); the four replays run concurrently on 4 cores took 431 / 298 / 350 / 768 s against 423 / 288 / 339 / 754 s in the old study (+2-3%). Projected per round (9 runs, one run index per instance): 9 x 368 s = 3300 core-s = 830 s wall on 4 cores (model). **K = 12 new runs per instance (108 runs, 36 per prime, 12 rounds): about 9960 s = 2.8 h wall (model), sd about 0.15 h from the heavy-tailed run length.** Budget: total compute for the study at most 5 h wall; spent before the freeze about 0.5 h (build 6 min, replay 8 min, tool), analysis and report reserved 0.6 h.
**Cut rule (declared in advance): runs are launched in round order (run index 2, 3, ..., 13, each round = all 9 instances, longest prime first). No job launches after T0 + 3 h 36 min. Whatever is not launched by then is dropped as whole rounds: the analysis uses only run indices that completed on all 9 instances, so runs are cut per instance and equally on every instance, never a prime.** An incomplete trailing round is kept in `results/new/` but excluded from the analysis and listed. A job exceeding its timeout (5400 s) is recorded in `walls.txt` and its run index is then incomplete. At least 3 complete rounds (27 runs) are required; fewer means the study fails its protocol and says so.

## Crossover extrapolation (model, frozen)
For each unit, S/rho(p) = G (p/p_i)^-2.10 through the three measured points: slope **-2.10** from the crossover study (not re-fitted here; sensitivity at -1.99 and -2.21, that study's 95% t interval), G fitted as the mean of ln(S/rho_i) + 2.10 ln p_i over the three primes. p* = where S/rho = 1. Interval: 2.5/97.5 percentiles of p* over the same bootstrap draws (excludes slope uncertainty). The u64 limit of the implementation is p <= 2039; a p* above it is stated as out of reach of this implementation.
