### LEAD-EXO-hashed-factor-base-tensor-ecdl — hashed factor bases + Boolean tensor contraction (Akshima et al., Bochum v0.2)
Verdict: CLOSE as an experimental lead (obstruction named below); keep the preprint as a watch item only. Not UNVERIFIABLE: both primary sources were retrieved as full text. I read the Bochum paper end to end and the OpenAI preprint's front matter, Theorem 1.1, Appendix C.2 and Appendix D.1–D.4.2 (not its 60 pages of proof).

Novelty: Atlas neighbours are LEAD-ICB-decomposition-encoding-and-sat (closed; same PDP-as-#SAT idea, different encoding), LEAD-ICB-generic-table-oracle (closed by theorem) and LEAD-EXO-four-list-ktree-join (hashed bases make relation finding an unstructured k-list problem). The 19-lead study has nothing on hashed bases, tensors or ETH (grep). The prior art is the Bochum paper itself.

Hypothesis check (measured, primary text). Akshima, Bacho, Huang, Kiltz, Nishaburi, Nooripoor, Tittelbach, Wiedijk, v0.2, 2026-10-29 (the atlas pin 1ee44e56 is the head commit, "Various improvements"): prime-field curves of prime order (fixed cofactor via [c]S), expected time 2^{O(b/(log b)^{1/4})} with m=⌈(log b)^{1/4}⌉; relation finding is a tensor network with O(mb) edges and degree O(b^{3/4}). It is **conditional on Theorem 2 = simplified Theorem D.1 of OpenAI's "The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False"** (17 Oct 2026, rev. 25 Oct); the authors state they "did not attempt to independently verify" it. Status of D.1: **claimed, unreviewed, not retracted**. Secondary reports say Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams are organising a review ("not a certification"); none reported an error. The Lean theorem checked by Comparator is `exact_count_and_sat`: fixed-width k-CNF, constants existentially quantified. The general-degree theorem Bochum needs (vertices of degree s^0.9, poly-time predicates) is not that statement (I read the statement, not the Lean library). The Anthropic parallel paper is k-CNF only.

Decisive fact (both papers agree): the saving needs ≥ 2^{2^17} edges. Bochum fn. 4 says below that the contraction "does not recurse, instead falling back to brute force"; OpenAI D.4.2 sets ε = 1/(128·1024) = 2^-17 and u_0 = ελ with λ = ⌈log2 s⌉, so u_0 < 1 unless s ≥ 2^131072 ≈ 10^39457.

Audit against the ICB obstruction (paper, derived). Same inequality as the atlas, with edges in place of CNF variables. The bound s/√log s beats exhaustive search over the ~b unknown bits only if c·m < √(log s), where c is edges per bit per slot. The CNF route fails by the log b factor (the atlas argument, which Bochum also states). The tensor encoding removes that factor but keeps the constant c·m. With c = 100 and m = 4 it needs log s > 1.6·10^5. So it escapes the obstruction's proof only asymptotically and conditionally. At every measurable size it sits on the obstruction's side. The ICB `revisit_when` (UNSAT cost < m/2−1 bits per unit l) is met only in the limit.

Generic bounds. The algorithm is not generic: it computes on coordinates, as the paper says, so Shoup/Nechaev and Corrigan-Gibbs–Kogan do not apply. Dinur–Keller–Marmor is a non-adaptive-advice bound and is also not binding. The structured generic-group model (abstract only): Ω(min(√q,1/δ)). A hashed base has δ = r^{1/m−1}, so min = √r. The base structure buys nothing, and any gain must come from white-box contraction on δ≈1 structure, which the model does not exclude.

Prediction (derived, not measured). No gain at measurable size. With leaves brute-forced, the cheapest generic relation search (table of ⌈(m+1)/2⌉ slots) costs about 5m·r^{1/2+1/(2m)} (up to rounding of the split), against rho at 0.89·r^{1/2}. Explicit counts (appendix) give ~2×10² at b=30 (m=5) and ~1.5×10² at b=40 (m=6). Hazard: the counted group ops alone, about 5(N+1)·3b with N≈m·max(r^{1/m},10m), are ~3–4×10^5 GAE at b=40 against rho ~9×10^5. That is a spurious "beats rho" at 0 GAE per native op, an accounting artifact (§5a band; contraction priced at 0.1 and 1).

Decisive experiment: none worth running. The 84-case panel cannot discriminate: if D.1 is true, the panel is still below 2^{2^17}; if false, there is no theorem. Update the atlas to closed_obstructed. `revisit_when`: an explicit-constants D.1 whose saving starts below ~10^6 edges, or a published correction. If someone insists on a panel, price all native work at 1 GAE and kill if the slope in log r stays ≥ 0.5.

Risks and contradictions: the YAML omits that the bound is 2^{o(b)} but not 2^{O(b^{1−ε})}. The theorem is prime-field, so the YAML's ECC2K-130 attempts need a binary encoding that is not in the paper. The PR #2801 gave no URL for either source. Every `evidence:` path is absent locally (crypto problems/ and research/hashed_ecdl_speed_20261029, cryptanalysis docs, KN-LIT-caa51d): not verifiable, and I do not guess their contents.

Confidence: high on the vacuity below 2^{2^17} (two independent statements); low–medium on the exact break-even (hidden constants unknown, c unknown, D.1's own constant taken as 1); none on whether D.1 is true.

Sources: github.com/bochum-crypto/subexponential-ecdl (PDF, read); github.com/openai/math/…/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf (read: Thm 1.1, App. C.2, D.1–D.4.2); openai/math lean/formalization.yaml and ComparatorChallenges/SubexponentialSAT.json (read); github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/eth (README); kingy.ai tracker and lilting.ch (secondary, review status); PR isogeny-labs/crypto-autoresearcher#2801; Corrigan-Gibbs–Henzinger–Wu, ePrint 2026/384 (abstract and project pages only; PDF 403); Dinur–Keller–Marmor, ePrint 2025/783 = arXiv 2505.00894 (abstract); Corrigan-Gibbs–Kogan, Eurocrypt 2018 (abstract-level only, not binding here).

### LEAD-STR-class-representative-ic-selection — choose the isogenous curve or isomorphic model that minimises charged IC cost
Verdict: REVISE. Do not run the three-arm study as specified. First run a cheap discriminator on the committed exports: the projected-base rerun below.

Novelty: Overlaps LEAD-ICE-curve-presentation-invariance (mean yield is a class invariant; within-class variance rejected at scope), the closed LEAD-STR-isogeny-transport-preprocessing and LEAD-EXO-curve-model-representation (model changes constants only), and the pair46 03-floor-pullback null. Not in the 19-lead study. It interacts with LEAD-19. Literature: Tate (same #E ⇔ isogenous over F_q; checked via an arXiv statement); Faugère–Gaudry–Huot–Renault, J. Cryptol. 27 (2014) (symmetries for extension-field curves, so not applicable to prime fields).

Evidence check (local): v2 README matches the YAML (yield 0.942, S 1.045, 5.8%, τ 7.2%); census REPORT matches (42.4x, 61.5x). The census found 33 equivalent and 0 different, but at a ±10% IC margin, so it can neither see nor contradict a 5.8% effect. LEADERBOARD diagnostics match (0.975 [0.603,1.451] at l=8209; 1.127 [0.922,1.342] at l=131101). pair46 is an exact null for prime #E, where φ is a group isomorphism, so it is a tautology. The crypto-autoresearcher paths are stale: not verifiable.

Cofactor invariance (measured). #E, r and p are constant within all 28 volcano classes (7,760 curve-models in spec.json), so h = #E/r cannot move. LEAD-19's [h]P gain is class-independent and does not interact through h. The abstract instance (G,P,Q) is identical, so rho and BSGS are unchanged (H3, ±5%). What changes is only which subset of G the coordinates call "small".

What CAN change charged cost. (1) The factor-base set: its expected yield is fixed (KN-FIND-007); only realised luck varies; this moves counted ops. (2) The raw-base coset-class structure, which varies along the volcano (floor cyclic, e.g. "Z/n × Z/3 above a cyclic floor"). (3) Field-op cost of the model (a = −3 or 0, Edwards or Montgomery): native time per group op only, i.e. §5a priced term, not counted GAE. (4) Automorphisms: |Aut| 4 or 6 only for j = 1728 or 0 (special classes), and matched rho folds the same way. (5) 2-torsion symmetrisation: FGHR is for extension fields, and LEAD-ICB-cofactor-torsion-quotients is closed. Only (1)–(2) move counted operations.

Confound found. All 14 ℓ=2 classes have h ∈ {4, 12}. The IC arm is `prime-abscissa:size=32`, a raw unprojected base (l2-s1001 records show cofactor 12; the session mean is 11.5 relations from 85,707 targets and 88.6k GAE). That is LEAD-19's dead-weight regime (S ×1.29–3.36 at h=2–6). `prime-projected-abscissa` is a registered plug-in. The 4.5% S effect may be mediated by raw-base class luck, and by the ratchet rule the baseline is the projected one. I did not derive the sign of any such mediation.

Prediction (derived). Arm (b): at most 4.5% S [2.3,6.7], ℓ=2 floors only, 24-bit, class "engineering", and it needs an F_p-rational 2-isogeny, so #E even; it is inapplicable to cofactor-1 targets. Arm (c): ΔlnS ≈ 0.74·Δln(yield) (from H1/H5). Best-of-16 with perfect calibration is z≈1.77 × τ 0.072 = +12.7% yield, so ≤ 9.4% S; at reliability ≈ 0.8 (null p95 4.7% implies a noise floor of ≈3%, an assumption) that is ≤ 7.5%. The cheapest calibration is a [r]P class test per base point per model, ≈ 32×30 = 960 GAE × 16 = 15k GAE ≈ 17% of one solve (mean ic-mitm total_gae ≈ 88.6k), so net loss. LEAD-19's [h]P costs ≈ 4 GAE per point (~0.15% of a solve) and is deterministic. IC/rho stays 17–170x.

Decisive experiment. Re-run the committed v2 ℓ=2 classes with an IC arm `prime-projected-abscissa:size=32` plus the same oracle, same curves, models and targets: 3,904 curve-models × 8 = 31,232 measured IC runs (62,464 with warm-ups), about 2.4–7.7 CPU-hours from the committed per-run wall times (mean solve 0.14 s, mean process 0.45 s), on a runner (not this container). Keep the ℓ=3 classes as the internal null. Kill: if projected H1 floor/crater 90% CI ⊂ [0.97,1.03] and model τ ≤ null p95, CLOSE as "mediated by cofactor dead weight, absorbed by LEAD-19". If H1 persists (CI excludes 1, ratio < 0.97), run arms (b) and (c) on the projected baseline with transport and calibration charged and the §5a 0/0.1/1 band.

Risks: 24-bit only; mechanism of the ℓ=2 effect unknown (README); char-2 transport not checked.

Confidence: high on invariance and the ratchet point (committed data); medium on predictions.

## Appendix: derivations behind the numbers (all derived, no measurement)
- Bochum cost: relation finding 2^{O(mb/√log b)}, N = Σ|F_i| ≈ m·max(r^{1/m},10m), attempts ≤ 5(N+1), each fresh target [u]P+[v]Q ≈ 3b GAE.
- b=40, m=6: N≈610, attempts ≈3050, ×120 GAE ≈ 3.7×10^5, rho ≈ 0.886·2^20 ≈ 9.3×10^5.
- Generic hashed IC (leaf = table of j slots plus enumeration of m−j slots): cost ≈ L^j + 5(N+1)L^{m−j}, L=|F_i|. b=30, m=5, j=3: 2^18 + 1605·4096 ≈ 6.9×10^6 against rho ≈ 2.9×10^4, a ratio of ~240. b=40, m=6, j=4: ≈ 1.4×10^8 against 9.3×10^5, a ratio of ~150.
- Break-even needs c·m·b/√log2 b < b(1/2−1/m), i.e. √log2 b > 2c·m²/(m−2), minimised at m=4: log2 b > 256c². c=100 gives 2.6×10^6; c=10^4 gives 2.6×10^10. Bochum's 1100(d+1) is an upper bound for one multiplication; per-slot c was not counted.
- Cofactor 12: 15,810,996 = 12 × 1,317,583 (l2-s1001).

## Summary
- Best candidate: LEAD-STR-class-representative-ic-selection, after the projected-base discriminator (31,232 IC runs on committed exports). It is the only one with a measurable path, though the ceiling is single-digit % S and it may be absorbed by LEAD-19.
- Worst: LEAD-EXO-hashed-factor-base-tensor-ecdl. The conditional theorem has no content below 2^{2^17} edges, so no panel can test it, and a 0-GAE accounting reading would give a spurious "beats rho" near b≈40.
- Could not verify: whether OpenAI's Theorem D.1 is correct (unreviewed); the native implementation, the 84-case panel and every crypto-autoresearcher or post-2026-10-24 evidence path (absent locally); the bodies of ePrint 2026/384, 2025/783 and CGK 2018 (abstract level only; PDF 403 or TLS failure, not bypassed).
