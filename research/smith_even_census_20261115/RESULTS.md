# Smith's conditions on even-degree genus-3 models: results (2026-11-15)

Status: `[pcc:unverified]` (one session, shared container, no independent replay).
Protocol: [`PROTOCOL.md`](PROTOCOL.md), frozen before data (`frozen.sha256`); binary and
source `binary.sha256`; data `even_census.jsonl`, order verification `verify.json`,
tables `tables.md` (`data.sha256`), regenerated by `python3 -I render.py even_census.jsonl`.

## Question

The odd-degree census (`research/smith_near_prime_20261114`) found Smith's route closed
for prime-order genus-3 Jacobians, because an irreducible septic gives cycle type
`(7, 1)` and no Frobenius-stable pairing. In the even-degree model (`deg f = 8`) an
irreducible octic gives type `(8)`, which does admit a pairing. What share of near-prime
even-degree Jacobians passes Smith's Propositions 2 and 4, and does the prime-order case
pass through type `(8)`?

## Answer

No. Type `(8)` passes at 0.249, but **type (8) never has prime order: its rational
2-rank is 1, not 0**, so `#J` is even for every irreducible octic. A prime-order
Jacobian in the even-degree model has cycle type `(1, 7)` or `(3, 5)`, neither of which
has a stable pairing, and none of the 168 prime-order curves passes. The Smith route
stays closed for prime-order genus-3 Jacobians in both models.

This turned on the 2-rank formula. The brief gave `k − 1` for `f` with only even-degree
factors; working it out before data gave `k` (PROTOCOL.md §1a: over a finite field,
Frobenius always has an even subset `v` with `σv = Ω \ v` when every orbit is even, and
that adds one rational 2-torsion class; genus-1 check: an irreducible quartic gives an
elliptic curve with a rational 2-torsion point). The correction was frozen before the
run, with counter-predictions E1b and E3'. The data agree: 0 of 747 type-(8) curves have
odd `#J`; all 1,275 odd orders are of type `(1, 7)` or `(3, 5)`; `v_2(#J) >= r2` holds
on all 5,852 curves.

## Run

5,952 draws of `f`, degree 8, random nonzero leading coefficient (not forced monic), over
`p = 31, 47, 67, 97`; 100 not squarefree, 5,852 analysed. Seeds `1..64·blocks` per prime
(800, 1,600, 1,472, 2,048 draws) until at least 150 admitted (`h <= 8`) and 40
prime-order curves. Exact `#J` for all 5,852 (no Newton or Weil failure). Wall clock 507 s
on 3 threads.

## Reading for the Smith route

- Prime-order genus-3 Jacobians: closed in both models. The cause is a theorem, and the
  census confirms the implementation follows it: `#J` odd ⇒ `J[2](F_p) = 0` ⇒ the Weierstrass
  cycle type has 2-rank 0 ⇒ (odd model) `(7,1)`; (even model) `(1,7)` or `(3,5)`. None of
  these has a stable pairing. Nothing changes for odd cofactors `h` either. Any
  near-prime `#J = h r` with `h` odd has the same three types, so it fails too.
- Even cofactors: in the even-degree model, `h = 2` (2-rank 1) includes type `(8)` and
  the paired types `(1,1,6)` and `(2,3,3)`. The pooled admitted rate is 0.140, a little
  above the odd model's 0.108 [0.088, 0.132] (intervals overlap). This is a comparison
  across two runs at different primes, not a controlled contrast.
- The unconditioned even-degree rate (0.174) matches the odd-degree census (0.173) and
  sits slightly below Smith's 18.57%.

## Deviations from the brief

1. 2-rank formula: `r2 = k` (not `k − 1`) when every factor of `f` has even degree. This
   was corrected before data in the frozen protocol (§1a), and E1b/E3' were added. The
   brief's E1–E5 were scored as written. Both `r2` and `r2_brief` are on every record.
2. No monic normalisation: `smith_prop24::analyze_curve` accepts a non-monic `f` (it uses
   the monic associate for the Weierstrass points and `f` itself in Proposition 4), so
   `full_success(false)` is the outcome for the curve as drawn
   (`monic_normalised = false`); `full_success(true)` (twist) is recorded alongside.
3. Order verification (ii), "`#J` kills random divisors", was replaced for degree 8 by an
   exact cross-check against Cantor arithmetic on the isomorphic odd-degree model for
   octics with a rational root, plus the twist identity `L(−1)`.
4. Per prime, the stopping rule required both quotas, so the admitted counts overshoot
   150 (162, 257, 238, 289). Prime-order counts: 40, 42, 42, 44.

## Limits

Four primes below 100, chosen so that `F_{p^3}` can be enumerated. Type frequencies and
pass rates at cryptographic sizes are extrapolated, but the prime-order result rests on
the 2-rank argument, which does not depend on `p`. `full_success` is Smith's condition
for his explicit construction, not the existence of any isogeny to a non-hyperelliptic
curve. Curves isogenous to a Smith-reachable one are not covered
(`smith_kernel_coverage.rs`). One run, no replay. The degree-8 order cross-check against
Cantor arithmetic covers only octics with a rational root (60 curves). Irreducible octics
are checked indirectly, through the Weil bounds, Newton integrality and the 2-rank identity.
