\textbf{Status.} Producer interpretive clarification, PCC-20261117-15ef76.
The original report/PDF remain byte-pinned historical records.
No coefficient-search experiment or changed exponent is claimed.

\textbf{1. A sufficient budget target, not a lower bound.}
For the proposed per-seed query upper bound, charging every attempted
section \(\widetilde O(\sqrt k)\) work gives the sufficient testing budget
\[
k=B^\theta,\qquad C_{\rm seed}=B^\chi,\qquad \chi+\theta/2<3.
\]
The report/derivation/problem record use ``needs'' or ``requires'' too
strongly if read as a necessary complexity condition. Early refusals,
amortized scalar work or a genuinely faster/batched operator could yield
a tighter full cost. Missing this conservative product-of-upper-bounds
budget alone does not disprove a route. Every alternative still faces the
unchanged full cold, useful-yield, memory, private-selection, kernel and
recovery criteria.

\textbf{2. The sampler actually rejected.}
For a supplied target-bearing basis, draw a normalized coefficient vector
uniformly from its \(p^5\) chart, and explicitly generate/test each draw,
charging at least one ordinary operation per trial. The divisor count gives
\[
\Pr[\text{hit}]\le\binom{M+5}{6}/p^5=O(p^{-4})
\quad\text{at }M=O(p^{1/6}).
\]
Thus this explicit-trial model needs \(\Omega(p^4)\) trials; the rejection
uses that trial-count lower bound, not a query upper bound.
An algorithm processing a compact seed family with bulk zero search and
restriction is outside this model, even if some marginal distributions
are uniform. It must supply its own construction/extraction/cost proof.
Biased target-conditioned coefficient generation remains open.
