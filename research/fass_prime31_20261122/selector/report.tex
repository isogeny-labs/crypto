\heading{1. Equations and extraction path}
Let $E:y^2=x^3+ax+b$ be nonsingular over an odd prime field, let $T=(t,u)$ be a finite public point, and let $d\ge1$ divide $p-1$. Solve
\[
S_3(X,Y,t)=0,\qquad X^d=1,\qquad Y^d=1.
\]
Write $q(Y)=AY^2+BY+C$ with coefficients in $\F[X]$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=(X-t)^2,& B&=-2\bigl((X+t)(Xt+a)+2b\bigr),\\
C&=(Xt-a)^2-4b(X+t).&&
\end{aligned}
\]
In $K=\F[X]/(X^d-1)$ compute $N_d=A^d+C^d-T_d$, where
\[
T_0=2,\qquad T_1=-B,\qquad T_{n+2}=-BT_{n+1}-ACT_n.
\]
The quotient $K$ is a commutative ring, generally \emph{not} a field. No inverse of $A$ is used. The support polynomial is $G=\gcd(X^d-1,N_d)$.

\heading{2. Universal identity, including degree drops}
\mini{Proposition.} For every commutative ring $R$, every $A,B,C\in R$ and integer $d\ge1$,
\[
\boxed{\R_Y(Y^d-1,AY^2+BY+C)=A^d+C^d-T_d.}
\]
The monic selector is the \emph{first} argument: this is the determinant of multiplication by $q$ on $R[Y]/(Y^d-1)$. It continues correctly when the quadratic loses degree. No invertibility or characteristic restriction is needed for the identity; root-existence consequences require a field separately. \status

\mini{Universal proof.} Work first in $\mathbb Z[A,B,C]$, embed into $\mathbb Q(A,B,C)$, and let $\alpha,\beta$ be the generic quadratic's roots in a splitting field. Their sum and product are $-B/A$ and $C/A$. The sequence $A^n(\alpha^n+\beta^n)$ satisfies the initial values and recurrence, so equals $T_n$. Since the selector is monic,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\R(Y^d-1,q)
 &=\prod_{\zeta^d=1}A(\zeta-\alpha)(\zeta-\beta)\\
 &=A^d\bigl[(-1)^d(\alpha^d-1)\bigr]
          \bigl[(-1)^d(\beta^d-1)\bigr]\\
 &=A^d\bigl((\alpha\beta)^d-\alpha^d-\beta^d+1\bigr)\\
 &=C^d-T_d+A^d.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both sides are integer coefficient polynomials. Injection into the fraction field proves their equality before specialization; the coefficient homomorphism then gives the statement in any $R$, including rings with zero divisors. Division belongs only to this generic proof, never to the resulting algorithm.

\mini{Odd $d$ and the linear case.} If $A=0$, the expression is $C^d-(-B)^d$: $C^d+B^d$ for odd $d$ and $C^d-B^d$ for even $d$. If $A=B=0$, it is $C^d$. Silently replacing $(-B)^d$ by $B^d$, or reversing resultant arguments after a degree drop, is incorrect.

\mini{S3 diagonal.} At $X=t$, $A=0$ and $B=-4(t^3+at+b)$. A nonzero target ordinate gives a linear tangent fiber. If $t^3+at+b=0$, then $C=(3t^2+a)^2\ne0$ by nonsingularity, so no finite second summand exists there. Infinity targets need a separate inverse-pair path.

\heading{3. Ring-safe binary evaluation}
Set $s=-B$, $v=AC$ and $T_n=D_n(s,v)$, the first-kind Dickson evaluation. The identities
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{2n}&=T_n^2-2v^n,\\
T_{2n+1}&=T_nT_{n+1}-sv^n=T_nT_{n+1}+Bv^n,\\
T_{2n+2}&=T_{n+1}^2-2v^{n+1}
\end{aligned}
\]
follow by expanding powers of formal roots with sum $s$ and product $v$, then specializing universal polynomials. Maintain $(T_n,T_{n+1},v^n)$ from $(2,s,1)$; each exponent bit selects the even or odd update. This requires $O(\log d)$ ring products. Binary powering computes $A^d,C^d$; reduction after each step follows the quotient homomorphism.

\mini{Valid Prime31 selector.} The written integer equality
\[
2147483646=1302\cdot1649373,\qquad1302=2\cdot3\cdot7\cdot31
\]
gives a split, squarefree selector. Since $p-1=2\cdot1073741823$, a pure $2^k$ selector has its full base-field domain only for $k\le1$.

\heading{4. Coordinate support and signed relations}
For prime $p$ and $d\mid p-1$, $Y^d-1$ splits into distinct linear factors. For a selector root $x$,
\[
N_d(x)=\prod_{y^d=1}q(x,y).
\]
A product in the field is zero exactly when a factor is zero. Therefore
\[
G(x)=0\iff x^d=1\ \text{and}\ \exists y\in\F:\ y^d=1,\ q(x,y)=0.
\]
Reducing $N_d$ modulo $X^d-1$ preserves this support. Extract every root of $G$, solve its quadratic fiber, and recheck both selectors and S3. A general zero-polynomial fiber returns every selector root; a nonsingular S3 fiber cannot be identically zero by the diagonal argument. \status

\mini{Point stage.} Recover every rational ordinate, enumerate all signs, and replay $P+Q=T$. Optional order checks require $[r]P=[r]Q=[r]T=O$. An annihilator test alone does not identify a particular cyclic subgroup in an arbitrary group: a stronger subgroup claim needs its own order/decomposition certificate.

\mini{Negative lift fixture.} On $E/\mathbf F_7:y^2=x^3+x$, take $T=(0,0)$ and $\mu_3=\{1,2,4\}$. The coordinates $(2,4)$ satisfy the selectors and $S_3(2,4,0)=(8-1)^2=0\pmod7$. Yet $f(2)=3$ and $f(4)=5$ are nonsquares. The qualified fixture retained the coordinate candidates and rejected both ordered nonliftable forms. This finite check does not prove the general lifting theorem.
