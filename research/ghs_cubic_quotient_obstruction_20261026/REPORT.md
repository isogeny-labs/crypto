# A degree-two quotient obstruction for four cubic catalog curves

26 October 2026. Conductor T-5393. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md),
[Sage replay](audit.py), [certificate](certificate.json),
[LMFDB curve snapshot](lmfdb_curves_3974.txt), and
[Lean degree checks](DegreeGate.lean) test the
four hyperelliptic genus-three curves in the class matched to the order-3974
elliptic source in the [catalog audit](../ghs_cubic_catalog_q16_20261026/REPORT.md).

## Result

Each of the four hyperelliptic curves listed by
[LMFDB class 3.16.a_a_aet](https://www.lmfdb.org/Variety/Abelian/Fq/3/16/a_a_aet)
over `k=GF(16)` has **no separable degree-two morphism to an elliptic
curve**, even after extending constants to an algebraic closure. All four
exact equations are frozen in the [curve snapshot](lmfdb_curves_3974.txt).
The first is

```text
C: y^2 + (a^2*x^4+a*x)*y
     = x^8+x^3+a*x^2+(a^2+a+1)*x+a^3+a+1,
a^4+a+1=0
```

Each geometric automorphism group consists only of the identity and
hyperelliptic involution. This eliminates the explicit elliptic-quotient
tactic supplied by a Klein-four group of involutions for every
*catalog-listed hyperelliptic model* in this isogeny class. The isogeny
`Res_{GF(4096)/GF(16)}(E) -> Jac(C)` established by the Weil polynomial
still exists; a higher-degree map or correspondence may evaluate it.

These curve equations are already in LMFDB.
The relevant characteristic-two elliptic-quotient families and their
maps were classified by
[Nart--Ritzenthaler, Proposition 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.0546).
The broader genus-three Jacobian existence literature was checked against
[Borodin--May](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16885): their characteristic-two
parity screen lists three candidate exclusion residues, of which the first
two are proved in their cited follow-up. The cubic Weil polynomial here
has coefficient residues `(s,t,u)=(0,0,1)` modulo two and triggers none
of them. That screen neither constructs the Jacobian nor supplies a map.
This audit proves a route-specific obstruction for the four equations,
not publication novelty of a curve or a vulnerability.

## Family criterion

Let `F` be a perfect field of characteristic two and let `C/F` have a
smooth genus-three hyperelliptic model

```text
y^2+h(x)y=f(x),
h(x)=alpha*x^4+beta*x,
f(x)=kappa*x^8+gamma*x^3+delta*x^2+epsilon*x+phi,
alpha*beta*gamma != 0.
```

Assume that the four distinct roots of `h` are precisely the branch
points of the hyperelliptic map after extending to an algebraic closure.
Then `Aut(C_bar)` is the order-two group generated by the hyperelliptic
involution. Consequently `C_bar` has no separable degree-two elliptic
quotient. This is a structural exclusion family, not a bound on every
isogeny or discrete-log transfer.

**Proof.** Choose `lambda` with `lambda^3=beta/alpha`. The four branch
points are `lambda*F_4` inside the algebraic closure. Their projective
linear stabilizer is the 12-element affine group
`x -> c*x+d`, with `c^3=1` and `d` one of the four branch points.
Indeed these 12 affine maps preserve the set. The cross-ratio is a
primitive cube root of unity, whose anharmonic orbit has size two in
characteristic two, so no additional permutation of the four points is
induced by a projective linear map. Every automorphism of a genus-three
hyperelliptic curve descends to a member of this stabilizer.

For such a map `T(x)=c*x+d`, `h(T(x))=c*h(x)`. A lift must act as
`y -> c*y+u(x)` and satisfy

```text
u(x)^2+c*h(x)*u(x) = f(c*x+d)+c^2*f(x).        (*)
```

The right side has degree at most three. A rational `u` satisfying (*)
has no finite pole: at a pole of order `m>0`, `u^2` has order `-2m`,
strictly lower than the order of `h*u`, so cancellation is impossible.
Thus `u` is a polynomial. If `0<=deg(u)<4` and `u!=0`, the term `c*h*u`
has degree `deg(u)+4`, strictly greater than `2*deg(u)` and three. If
`deg(u)>4`, `u^2` has strictly greater degree than `c*h*u`. When
`deg(u)=4`, cancellation of leading terms forces its leading
coefficient to be `c*alpha`; replacing `u` by `u+c*h` preserves (*)
and lowers its degree below four. Hence (*) has a solution only when
its right side is zero.

If `c!=1`, the coefficient of `x^3` on the right is
`gamma*(1+c^2) != 0`. If `c=1,d!=0`, its coefficient of `x^2` is
`gamma*d != 0`. Only `c=1,d=0` remains; then (*) has the two
solutions `u=0` and `u=h`, yielding the identity and hyperelliptic
involution. A separable degree-two elliptic quotient would produce
a different involution, so none exists. ∎

## Finite replay and limitation

Each of the four curves has genus three and independently recounted
point counts `(17,257,3728)` over `k`, `k^2`, and `k^3`; these give the
class Weil polynomial `X^6-123X^3+4096`. The branch roots of the first
model over the Conway `GF(4096)` basis encode as `0,1609,2915,3370`.
For all four models, the simple-pole coefficients after Artin--Schreier
reduction are nonzero at all four roots. For each model, the replay solved
the projective-map equations for all 24 branch-point permutations.
Exactly 12 maps preserve the fourth point, all affine. Among their
nonidentity members, three give a nonzero quadratic right side in (*)
and eight give a nonzero cubic right side. The certificate records all
coefficients, each map, and source hashes.

`lean DegreeGate.lean` checks the natural-number inequalities separating
the three degree cases and the finite count identities. It does not
formalize the geometric automorphism classification or polynomial
identity (*); those are proved above and replayed in Sage.

This obstruction applies to degree-two quotients of the four **listed
hyperelliptic models** and to the stated coefficient family. LMFDB says
the number of nonhyperelliptic Jacobians in this class is unknown. The
obstruction does not rule out an unlisted model in the isogeny class, a
degree-three or higher cover, an isogeny of higher degree, or a
Jacobian correspondence. It supplies no attack timing or same-target
rho comparison.
