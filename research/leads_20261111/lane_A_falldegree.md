## 1. Structural facts the leads rest on

These are my own derivations from the formulas in `NOTE.md` (sections 4 and 6) and the treatise (Thm. `reform`). They are short but unchecked by machine. Each would need a Lean proof before the program relies on it (obligations are named in the leads).

Notation as in `NOTE.md`: `K = GF(2^n)`, `V` a `k`-dimensional `F2`-subspace, `A = X1`, `C = X1 + X2`, `c in F2^k` the coordinates of `C`, `M(k,e) = sum_{j<=e} C(k,j)`, `ell_0(C) = Tr(C) + Tr(a6/z^2)` (affine in `c`), `zeta = z^{-2}`.

**F1 (factorisation; PROVED by hand, ring identity).** Put `psi_C(A) = A^2 + C A` and `P(y) = y^2 + z y` (both `F2`-linear). Then
`Phi*_C(A) = A^4 + (C^2+z)A^2 + zCA = P(psi_C(A))`, so `S(A,C) = P(psi_C(A)) + h_C`.
Consequences: `psi_C(C) = 0`, hence `Phi*_C(C) = 0`. Hence `sum_i c_i * Tr(lambda(C) Phi*_C(v_i)) = 0` identically in `c` (a Koszul-type relation among the selector constraints of Prop. S (I)). Also `dim Phi*_C(V) <= k-1` for `C != 0`, since `C` lies in the kernel. The system is solvable at `C` only if `Tr(h_C/z^2) = 0`, i.e. `ell_0(C) = 0`; then it is solvable iff `psi_C(V)` contains `u_C` or `u_C + z`, where `P(u_C) = h_C` and `u_C` is `F2`-affine in `C`.

**F2 (the first-fall form is a selector of degree 0 on half the cube; PROVED by hand).** The adjoint of `P` is `P^dagger(lambda) = lambda^{1/2} + z lambda`, with kernel `{0, zeta}`. For all `C, v in V`: `Tr(zeta * Phi*_C(v)) = 0` and `Tr(zeta * h_C) = ell_0(C)`. (Check: `Tr(v^4/z^2) = Tr(v^2/z)` and `Tr(C^2v^2/z^2) = Tr(Cv/z)` cancel the other two terms.) So `lambda = zeta` satisfies Prop. S (I)-(II) exactly at the points with `ell_0 = 1`.

**F3 (trivial kernel of the homogeneous selector system; PROVED by hand).** For `q` a Boolean polynomial in `c` of degree `<= e-1`, `lambda_q(c) = q(c)(1 + ell_0(c)) zeta` has coordinates of degree `<= e` and satisfies the homogeneous system (I)=0, (II)=0: (I) holds by F2, and (II) reads `q (1+ell_0) ell_0 = 0` in the Boolean ring. The family has dimension `M(k-1, e-1)` when `V` is not inside `ker Tr` (the image of `q -> q(1+ell_0)` is the set of degree-`<=e` functions vanishing on `{ell_0 = 1}`). When `V` lies in `ker Tr` and `Tr(a6/z^2) = 0` (so `ell_0 == 0`), every `q` of degree `<= e` works and the family has dimension `M(k,e)`.

**F4 (hyperplane sandwich; PROVED by hand).** Let `H_0 = {c : ell_0(c) = 0}` and let `e'_min` be the least degree of a map `H_0 -> K` satisfying (I)-(II) at the points of `H_0` only. Then `e'_min <= e_min <= e'_min + 1`.
- Lower inequality: restrict a selector to `H_0`.
- Upper inequality: take `lambda = ell_0 zeta + (1+ell_0) lambda~`, where `lambda~` is a degree-`e'_min` extension of the `H_0` solution (eliminate one `c_j` with `Tr(v_j) = 1`); on `{ell_0 = 1}` it equals `zeta`, which is valid by F2.

So the selector problem has `k-1` effective `c`-variables when `ell_0` is non-constant, and `k` when `V` is in `ker Tr`. In `eta = P^dagger(lambda)` coordinates it has `n-1` unknown coordinates.

**F5 (measured: the logs show the trivial kernel is the whole kernel; derived from on-disk numbers).** In every `INCONSISTENT` round of `out/c/cert/*/log.txt`, `cols - rank` equals the nullity of the sampled homogeneous matrix (the `+1` column is the right-hand side, which is a pivot). That sample nullity is an upper bound on the full nullity; F3 is a proved lower bound. Whenever they coincide, the full nullity is exactly `M(k-1, e-1)`. They coincide in every row I checked (about 27 rows):

## 3. Ranking

1. **LEAD-A-selector-eta-kernel (A1).** It contains four structural facts proved by hand (F1-F4) that explain the rank deficits already in the logs, one cheap test that runs entirely on existing C code in minutes, and a sharp falsifier (a non-trivial c-only syzygy). Its outcome feeds the others: F4 gives the effective `c`-dimension that A2's Model B and A3's T-versus-R offset both depend on.
2. **LEAD-A-fall-effective-variable-law (A3).** The only lead that makes a testable statement about `d_F` itself (the 33 -> 35 step), linked to A1 through the same trace-form elimination. Its cost is a dependency (the native closure is not in this container), not a doubt about the test.
3. **LEAD-A-selector-counting-law (A2).** Cheap and preregistered, but it only scores two fitted heuristics; its value is the growth constant of `e_min`.
4. **LEAD-A-closure-dimension-laws (A4).** Fits from on-disk numbers, strongest where it reads as a free module `(N+1)*3n`; cheap at D=3, expensive at D=4.
5. **LEAD-A-bilinear-lift-size-degree (A5).** The size-degree datum the note asks for, but a single informative cell at the size limit.

## 4. What none of these establishes

- No lead gives a lower bound on `d_F` that grows with `n` or `k`. The selector degree is an upper-bound proof system and `d_SEL - d_F` already grows: from the tables in `NOTE.md` section 6 and the treatise, `d_SEL = e_min + 2` is 5 at n=15, 6 at n=19-23, 7 at n=27-29, while `d_F = 4` for random V at every n from 15 to 33; the gap is 1, 2 and 3 at those sizes.
- No lead supports or refutes the Petit-Quisquater or Semaev heuristics, nor any ECDLP cost or sub-sqrt(p) claim. A3 could point towards a slowly growing `d_F` (against problem 12's hoped-for lower bound); it is reported as such.
- All numbers are `[pcc:unverified]`; the hand-computed binomial sums must be recomputed by the executing agent before use.
