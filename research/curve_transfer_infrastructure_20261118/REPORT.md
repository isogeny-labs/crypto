# Outcome

This implementation slice adds two executable cover-transfer paths with
different source representations:

1. the existing ordinary-binary degree-three family now emits a certificate
   for the subgroup generated by a named target point and can export those
   witnesses across the live binary catalog; and
2. the direct odd-prime degree-two family now has pullback, arbitrary balanced
   pushforward, deck action, both induced-map composition checks, and the same
   named-generator recovery test.

The subgroup conclusion is deliberately narrow.  If a supplied positive
integer `r` kills a nonidentity point `G`, is coprime to the map degree `d`,
and the checked composition gives `push(pull(G)) = [d]G`, multiplication by
`d^{-1} mod r` after pushforward recovers every element of `<G>`.  Pullback is
therefore injective on that generated subgroup.  This argument does **not**
need `r` to be the exact order of `G`; exact cardinality and primality remain a
separate evidence tier.

![The solid arrows are implemented maps. Blue/green boxes record executable
composition witnesses; orange records evidence that is intentionally kept
separate.](transfer-evidence.svg){width=100%}

The implementation does not provide a destination relation solver, target
decomposition, or a matched cost comparison.  Consequently this record makes
no claim that either representation makes an ECDLP easier.  The full Jacobian
index-calculus solver remains a separate deliverable.

# Exact supported paths

| Family | Target and source | Implemented maps and identities | Current boundary |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Ordinary binary, degree 3 | An ordinary binary elliptic model over the declared binary field; the family-specific genus-three, one-infinity source | Point-class pullback, arbitrary reduced-class norm, `phi_* phi^*(G) = [3]G`, image endomorphism checks, and named-generator recovery | The checked `binary_cubic_pullback_v1` family only; a catalog row without a named EC1 generator has no subgroup record |
| Direct odd prime, degree 2 | `E: y^2=x^3+Ax+B`; `C: v^2=F(u^2+c)` with balanced split-sextic classes | Point-class pullback, arbitrary balanced-class pushforward, deck action, `pi_* pi^*=[2]`, `pi^* pi_*=1+deck_*`, and named-generator recovery | Prime characteristic greater than 3, direct short-Weierstrass target, nonzero `F(c)`, and a deterministic prime proof from either the complete `u64` test or the first sixteen prime bases under the strict exclusive `psi_13` bound |

The odd implementation treats the two rational infinities explicitly through
the balanced `(u,v,n_plus)` representation, and its deck involution swaps the
two infinity branches.  The pushforward formula is expressed in base-field
symmetric data; the small-field tests compare it with a separate pointwise
calculation over a quadratic extension.

The proof-range widening is deliberately a two-row live pilot. It admits the
two catalog rows over `p=5902977611310272157103`; it is not a claim of general
wide-prime support, and values outside the strict proof range remain
unsupported without a separately verified primality certificate.

These are one-map elementary families.  They do not constitute an executable
implementation of every case of Xarles's theorem about two independent maps
from a hyperelliptic curve of genus at most five.  The theorem statement and
its characteristic, field-of-definition, separability, and constructivity
limits are audited in the earlier
[hyperelliptic infrastructure report](../hyperelliptic_cover_infrastructure_20261006/README.md).

# Subgroup certificate logic

For either degree `d=3` or `d=2`, let `Psi` denote pullback and `R` denote
pushforward.  The executable certificate checks the following data on a named
point:

- `G` is a nonidentity point on the target;
- `r > 0`, `rG = 0`, and `gcd(r,d)=1`;
- `Psi(G)` is nonidentity and is killed by `r`;
- `R(Psi(G)) = [d]G`; and
- `[d^{-1} mod r] R(Psi(G)) = G`.

Every point of `<G>` is killed by `r`.  The degree inverse therefore gives a
left inverse to `Psi` on `<G>`, so equality after pullback implies equality
before pullback.  The generic Bezout argument is also present as the Lean
theorem
[`ChainedTransfer.injective_with_recovery`](../chained_jacobian_transfers_20261110/lean/ChainTransfer.lean).
That theorem takes the actual source subgroup, its exponent relation, a Bezout
witness, and the norm identity as explicit premises; it does not construct the
geometric maps.

The machine record keeps these layers distinct:

| Evidence layer | What this slice records | What it does not infer |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Model and map binding | Exact ICV1/EC1 identity, target-model digest, cover UID, map UID, map degree | Completeness over all possible covers or correspondences |
| Named generated subgroup | Generator, annihilator, pullback divisor, typed pushforward, degree multiple, inverse recovery, and individual Boolean checks | Exact order or primality from annihilation alone |
| Exact-order evidence | Separate `proved`, `probable`, or `unknown` entries with evidence references | A stronger status than the referenced source supports |
| Solver evidence | Explicitly `not_provided` | Relation generation, linear algebra, target decomposition, or key recovery |
| Advantage evidence | Explicitly `not_assessed` | Any complexity or runtime improvement |

# Catalog coverage

The `transfer-catalog-export` command joins
[`registry.json`](../../docs/curves/registry.json),
[`cover-links.yaml`](../../docs/curves/cover-links.yaml), and
[`traits.json`](../../docs/curves/traits.json).  It rebuilds the exact cover
graph from the registry and rejects any changed or missing row even if the
supplied graph retains its old registry digest.  It reconstructs each
supported binary cover, exercises the named-generator certificate, and
retains failures on both the affected subgroup and map records.  The traits
file is recorded as advisory provenance only and cannot promote exact-order
evidence.
Its output contract is
[`binary-subgroup-transfer-certificates/v1`](../../docs/curves/binary-subgroup-transfer-certificates.schema.json).

The current catalog test asserts the following bounded population:

| Population | Count | Meaning |
|:--|--:|:--|
| Binary map records | 174 | Every live row in the declared binary cubic family |
| Maps with at least one named subgroup binding | 147 | A map may have more than one EC1 representation |
| Maps without a named subgroup binding | 27 | Map evidence is retained; subgroup transport is `not_applicable` |
| Named subgroup records | 150 | Each is replayed independently |
| Named records accepted by the current test fixture | 150 | Generated-subgroup checks returned no per-record failure in that fixture |
| Exact named-generator orders proved locally | 102 | Deterministic Miller--Rabin below the `psi_13` bound, plus nonidentity and `[r]G=0` |
| Annihilation-only records | 48 | Traits records a probable-prime result, but the local exporter deliberately leaves exact order unknown without a proof or replayable primality certificate |

This is exhaustive only for the current declared binary family and catalog
snapshot.  It is not a parameter-space proof and an empty search result would
not establish nonexistence.

# Structured unsupported cases and next work

The standalone command returns structured unsupported results for odd
extension-field targets, direct odd primes without a deterministic proof,
normalized Montgomery/Edwards target transfers, and source infinity
configurations outside the balanced split-sextic implementation. An audit
found that normalized geometry had previously allowed the direct transfer
dispatcher to interpret supplied points as short-Weierstrass coordinates. For
the Montgomery curve `y^2=x^3+3x^2+x` over `F_11`, `(0,1)` lies on its
normalized short curve `Y^2=X^3+9X+1` but not on the original target. The
standalone transfer path now returns
`unsupported_normalized_target_transfer_backend` before parsing a point or
divisor under the wrong coordinate model. Verified cover geometry and source
balanced arithmetic remain separate capabilities.

The live odd-map census contains 20,539 direct short-Weierstrass maps with 675
linked EC1 records inside the current deterministic proof boundary, including
exactly the two 73-bit pilot rows over `p=5902977611310272157103`. Outside it
are 269 wide-direct maps/234 EC1 records, 30 normalized maps/22 EC1 records,
and 1,405 extension-field maps/9 EC1 records. The two-row widening is therefore
not general support for wide primes, normalized targets, or extensions.
The next bounded engineering steps are:

1. commit the atomically rebound successor corpus, then replay it from an
   extracted current-head release archive;
2. refresh the cryptanalysis mirror against the exact strict-primality and
   73-bit-pilot sources, then close required CI and review;
3. extend independent source-law evidence to the full 486-class `GF(8)`
   fixture and the generic `F_5` and shifted `F_7` even-model fixtures with
   mutation controls;
4. implement explicit normalized-coordinate adapters, including exceptional
   projective points, before promoting Montgomery or Edwards transfer; and
5. treat broader wide-prime and extension-field support as separate bounded
   work, and publish a package only after its exact-head release gates pass.

None of those steps is a substitute for the separate full Jacobian
index-calculus acceptance requirements: actual Jacobian relations, checked
linear algebra, target decomposition, independently replayed outputs, explicit
supported parameters, and complete accounting for preprocessing, failed
searches, duplicates, memory, and total runtime.
