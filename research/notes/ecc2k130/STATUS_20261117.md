# ECC2K-130 index-calculus status assessment

**Snapshot:** `origin/main` at `833a35d21ec2368ab081b2083d7762ed0bfd2bbc`
(2026-11-17).  This is an evidence inventory, not a new attack result.  It
separates work on the exact `a = 0`, degree-131 challenge from work on smaller
curves and the easier `a = 1`, degree-83 control.

## Bottom line

The repository has built most of the *experimental machinery* needed to study
ECC2K-130 index calculus: exact parameters and basis conversion, persistent
campaign state, bounded pair and SAT relation queries, several algebraic
solvers, wide sparse linear-algebra types, a resumable TPE sweeper, and
end-to-end validation on small curves.  It has **not** completed the first
scientific gate on the exact challenge: finding enough useful natural
prime-subgroup relations to form a matrix.

Consequently, on the exact curve:

- useful relations found: **0**;
- factor-base logarithms solved: **0**;
- public target logarithms recovered: **0**;
- complete DLPs: **0**;
- measured cold end-to-end IC/rho ratio: **none**.

The work is therefore substantial as infrastructure and negative evidence,
but it is not close to an ECC2K-130 solution in the end-to-end sense.  No
claim that an idea has “never been done before” can be certified from a code
repository or a bounded literature search.  Novelty should remain a hypothesis
until a current literature review and an independently reproducible result
support it.

## Exact natural-relation evidence

The independent brute-force relation study gives the same scientific answer
from larger searches:

- Six complete three-point sweeps enumerated **45,252,570** pairs.  Five found
  no relation.  The single reported curve-valid identity uses abscissae
  `0, 1, 1` and repeats `(1, 0)` twice; it is the degenerate torsion identity
  `(0,1) + 2(1,0) = O`, not a useful independent prime-subgroup row.
- The four-point collision sweep completed three supports totaling
  **37,507,774** signed pairs and reports no relation.  Its summary, three
  standalone records and three JSON records embedded in `four_point.log` now
  have a committed three-way custody verifier.  The earlier apparent conflict
  came from a stale README restored with files that sparse-branch merges had
  deleted, not from an incomplete run.
- The replacement orbit-grouped four-point study completed all eight normal
  bases: **1,020,114** pair-orbit classes and **490** characteristic-equation
  hits, with **0 certified relations**.

## Degree of regularity and hybrid guessing

The regularity work is the strongest algorithmic evidence, but it remains a
solver-stage result:

- MutantXL closure lowered plain-XL refutation degrees from 5–7 to 3–4 on 92
  unsatisfiable miniature systems while preserving 345 satisfiable controls.
- Degree 4 stops being sufficient by `ell = 7`; the optimistic constant-degree
  extrapolation is contradicted before the priced full-scale `m = 4, ell = 33`
  cell.
- At `m = 3, ell = 8`, the invariant guess on `x_1 + x_2` succeeded on 5 of
  8 fresh draws while the corresponding raw guess succeeded on 0 of 8
  (one-sided exact sign test `p = 0.031`).  Across 12 draws the comparison is
  7/12 versus 1/12.
- At the projected full-scale `m = 4` cell, the guess-then-degree-4 hybrid must
  need at most six guesses to beat degree 5.  The measured small-cell trend is
  far above that threshold, and the November 6 `m = 4` placement studies moved
  no end-to-end row.

This is a clever and measured choice of *which variables to guess*.  It has not
reduced exact-curve relation cost, because the exact wide system has not been
run through the same solver.  Full evidence and qualifications are in
[`RESEARCH_ECC2K130_DESCENT_DEGREE.md`](RESEARCH_ECC2K130_DESCENT_DEGREE.md).

## Hyperparameter search: implemented, but not an exact-curve optimiser

[`ic sweep`](../../../docs/ic/SWEEPER.md) is an in-tree, Optuna-style Rust
searcher with typed conditional spaces, TPE sampling, median pruning,
append-only resumable JSONL stores, count-based objectives, and child-process
isolation.  The committed studies include a 24-trial Koblitz algebraic run and
a 24-trial generic root-finding run.  On the algebraic run, every completed
candidate remained above the frozen baseline at the measured rungs and under
the projected objective; it found no regularity configuration that beat the
incumbent.

The current search cannot answer the ECC2K-130 question directly:

- the shared binary framework stops at degree 63;
- algebraic descent exposes only `m = 2, 3`;
- exact fixed-parameter campaigns are not a sweeper backend;
- solver operation scopes are not interchangeable, so F4/F5/F6 cannot be
  ranked merely by whichever reports the smallest counter;
- the objective projects from miniature rungs and does not include an exact
  relation-yield observation.

The sweeper is therefore a real implementation and a useful hypothesis
generator, not evidence that hyperparameter optimisation improved the exact
challenge.

## Required next experiment

The next credible effort is an **exact-compatible, stage-gated sweep**, not a
degree-only search:

1. Add the arbitrary-width fixed campaign as a sweeper backend, preserving its
   exact parameter, persistence, and certificate checks.
2. Freeze a matched panel with at least a small correctness rung, the `a = 0`
   degree-83 transfer gate, and bounded degree-131 attempts.  A candidate that
   cannot represent a rung is refused, never scored.
3. Search relation arity/base recipe, orbit handling, invariant-guess placement,
   closure degree, solver, and sparse-LA policy.  Keep solver scopes separate
   until they share one audited operation unit.
4. Record every phase for every arm: parameter/base setup, relation attempts
   including failures, relation verification, matrix build, rank/solve, target
   descent/root finding, final scalar verification, wall time, operation counts,
   peak memory, and durable artifact bytes.
5. Measure the matched rho baseline end to end on the same host and public
   targets.  Report both cold one-target cost and any explicitly amortised
   multi-target cost; never substitute online-only time for the cold baseline.
6. Rank lexicographically: correctness first, then non-degenerate verified
   relation yield, then cold total cost across the full accounting band, then
   regularity as a diagnostic.  A lower degree without better verified
   end-to-end cost is not a winner.

Until a candidate passes the relation gate, downstream stages should be
reported as **not reached**, not timed as zero.  This keeps future F4/F5/F6,
root-finding, relation solving, and linear-algebra comparisons tied to the
same end-to-end baseline the challenge actually requires.
