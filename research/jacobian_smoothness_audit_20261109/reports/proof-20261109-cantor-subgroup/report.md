# A finite Cantor subgroup has a different smoothness fraction

On the complete reduced-Mumford polynomial-pair model for
$y^2=x^5-x$ over $\mathbf F_3$, Lean proves all 64 Cantor additions and
unique reduced output. The proper cyclic subgroup generated by
$(u,v)=(X,0)$ has order two. It is entirely smooth, while seven of the
eight ambient representations are smooth. [pcc:unverified]

|:--|--:|--:|
| Smooth, including identity | $7/8$ | $2/2=1$ |
| Nonzero smooth numerator, whole-group denominator | $6/8=3/4$ | $1/2$ |

Lean disproves the assertion that every cyclic subgroup has the ambient
smoothness fraction in this finite arithmetic model. Formal identification with the geometric Picard/Jacobian
group remains open. Fixed order two over an odd field does not refute a
qualified binary large-prime, typical-subgroup or asymptotic heuristic.

# Proof, sources and verification

The base model ranges over every actual pair with $u$ monic,
$\deg u\le2$, $\deg v<\deg u$ and $u\mid v^2-f$. Its complete
coefficient classification gives eight pairs; all have $v=0$.
The curve polynomial is proved monic, degree five and squarefree.
[pcc:unverified]

Lean proves monicity and pairwise coprimality of
$X,X-1,X+1,X^2+1$, using explicit Bezout certificates. For every input,
the actual normalized gcd $d$ and monic composition polynomial $w$ satisfy
$d^2w=u_1u_2$ and $w\mid f$. The full gcd with $v_1+v_2$ is $d$,
and explicit standard-composition witnesses give zero for the $v$
numerator. Reduction keeps $w$ when its degree is at most two, and otherwise
proves $w\,u=f$. Exact monic-product degrees and a kernel Boolean
certificate select all 64 branches. [pcc:unverified]

Output uniqueness and an explicit bijection with $(\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z)^3$
establish equivalence between this Cantor relation and group addition.
The cyclic subgroup is proved proper, with exact coordinates, order,
cardinality and splitting counts. Transporting an arbitrary group onto
eight labels alone would not establish these statements. [pcc:unverified]
