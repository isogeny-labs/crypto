# Nonhyperelliptic genus-five carrier lead: exact class filter

An independent enumeration of the two known hyperelliptic carriers (masks 9735 and 17371) gives identical point counts over F3^r for r=1,...,5: **[4,10,28,82,314]**. Newton identities and the genus-five functional equation yield the L-polynomial `1+14T⁵+243T¹⁰`. The source-bound [`REPLAY.json`](REPLAY.json) has both raw count vectors and the coefficients; the Sage process exited zero with empty stderr. `CarrierPointCountArithmetic.lean` compiles and checks only the resulting integer arithmetic.

The LMFDB read-only `av_fq_isog` row for `q=3`, `g=5`, `poly=[1,0,0,0,0,14,0,0,0,0,243]` is labelled `5.3.a_a_a_a_o`; it records `has_jacobian=1` and five geometric elliptic factors after extension degree five. It does not list a nonhyperelliptic carrier equation. This isogeny-class match is a filter, not an existence proof for a new model.

A [published plane-sextic search by Kudo and Harashita](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.07270) constructs non-special genus-five curves over F3 and, in its reported Section 4.2 run, imposes at least 32 F9-points. This class has exactly 10 F9-points, so those reported high-point results do not settle this class. The paper's parametrization may still be applicable after changing the point filter, but that would require a new search and independent smoothness and L-polynomial checks. Trigonal models are a separate carrier type; canonical intersections of three quadrics give another representation of nontrigonal genus-five curves.

**Falsifier for this lead:** an exhaustive classification showing every F3-genus-five Jacobian in class `5.3.a_a_a_a_o` comes from the already counted hyperelliptic carriers, or a complete search of the chosen nonhyperelliptic model class with no matching L-polynomial. If a new nonhyperelliptic carrier is found, it remains a potential transfer candidate until an explicit cover to E_b over F3^5, a nonzero selected-subgroup norm, full decomposition/descent, and same-target rho comparison are verified. The current point-count result neither identifies a new vulnerable family nor changes the [8,33] hyperelliptic cover-degree window.
