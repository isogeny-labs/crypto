# Independent branch-point norm valuation derivation

Status: hand derivation and existing primary-source inspection; no compiler, trial or new producer candidate read. The prior nonbranch setting was known. Root subsequently disclosed the same proposed ramification formula; this is not a mutually blind design claim.

Let K be a field of characteristic not2, f∈K[x] a separable degree-five polynomial, a∈K with f(a)=0, and P=(a,0) on C:y²=f(x). Let R=O(C,P) be the actual affine curve local ring, with normalized DVR valuation vP. For a nonzero polynomial A, let va(A) be its multiplicity at x=a. Keep these two valuations distinct. Let b∈K×, n∈K[x], h=b*y−n(x), and N=n²−b²f.

## Local parameter and ramification

Write f=(x−a)g, g(a)=f′(a)≠0. The local image of g is a unit. The curve equation gives x−a=y²/g. Thus the maximal ideal (x−a,y) is generated by y. The actual local curve ring is a one-dimensional regular local domain, hence DVR, with vP(y)=1 and vP(x−a)=2. One can establish the domain globally because odd-degree f cannot be a square in K(x); char not2 gives the usual quadratic function-field extension. Smoothness at P also follows from the nonzero partial derivative −f′(a). For A=(x−a)^m A0 with A0(a)≠0, its germ satisfies vP(A(x))=2m. This is the ramification factor, not a doubling of base-polynomial multiplicities.

## Selected numerator order

If n(a)≠0, h(P)=−n(a)≠0, so h is a unit and vP(h)=0. If n(a)=0, write n=(x−a)k. Then n=y²k/g and

h=y*(b−y*k/g).

The parenthesized factor has nonzero residue b, hence is a unit. Therefore vP(h)=1. This argument includes n=0 and does not need a finite multiplicity for the zero polynomial. The conjugate −b*y−n=y*(−b−y*k/g) likewise has order1. Both conjugate factors vanish at the selected branch; neither is the complementary unit used in the nonbranch proof.

## Norm identity and the two order formulas

The hyperelliptic conjugation fixes P and sends y to−y. Its norm is N=h*conjugate(h)=n²−b²f. If n(a)=0, n² has base order≥2 (or is zero), whereas b²f has base order1; hence va(N)=1. If n(a)≠0, N(a)=n(a)²≠0 and va(N)=0. In particular N and h are nonzero. Thus

vP(h)=va(N)∈{0,1},
vP(N)=2*va(N)=2*vP(h).

Suppose the exact source norm identity is N=c*u1*u2*U for a nonzero scalar c∈K×. All three factor polynomials must be nonzero since N≠0. Nonzero scalars have order0. Consequently

va(u1)+va(u2)+va(U)=vP(h),
vP(u1(x))+vP(u2(x))+vP(U(x))=2*vP(h).

The source convention n−b*y differs from h only by−1, a unit, so orders agree. At a selected branch n(a)=0, the two sums are respectively1 and2. Exactly one of u1,u2,U has a simple root at a; the others do not vanish. If two factors vanish, the norm identity, b≠0 and simple-root assumptions cannot all hold. When source assumptions give u1(a)u2(a)U(a)=0 rather than n(a)=0, evaluate N=c*u1*u2*U at a: n(a)²=0 implies n(a)=0 in K.

## Explicit counterexample to transplanting the nonbranch stalk formula

Over Q take f=x⁵+x, a=0, b=1, n=x. The polynomial is separable: a root α is either0, where f′=1, or α⁴=−1, where f′=5α⁴+1=−4. At P=(0,0), g=1+x⁴ is a unit, x=y²/g, and h=y−x=y*(1−y/g) has order1. Its norm is

N=x²−x⁵−x=−x*(1−x+x⁴).

Therefore va(N)=1 but vP(N)=2. Claiming vP(h)=vP(N) gives1=2 and is false. Claiming vP(h)=2*va(N) also gives1=2 and is false. Ramification doubles the valuation of pulled-back base functions, not the final base-factor multiplicity sum for h. This example concerns general polynomial factors; it is not represented as a particular coefficientData fixture.

## Assumptions and attacks

If b=0, the selected order need not be1: n=x−a gives h=−(x−a) of order2. This attacks the0/1 conclusion, not the more general norm/ramification equality. If f has a repeated root, the indicated local ring can be singular and y need not be a uniformizer. In characteristic2, the usual distinct hyperelliptic conjugation and separable quadratic-cover interpretation fail; the elementary local formula may still hold under some premises, so this is not a claimed characteristic2 counterexample. Nonrational branch points require residue-field and closed-point definitions instead of silently reusing rational evaluation. Infinity requires a separate chart. No global divisor-class or execution statement follows from this local derivation.
