# Derived property of the prime-field presentation

This is an algebraic observation made after the frozen sweep started, not a solver measurement and not a claim about every index-calculus encoding. It does not change any recorded result or the frozen analysis.

For the prime-field cells with m >= 3, the ideal generated by the highest homogeneous parts has dimension exactly ceil((m-2)/2). Consequently the protocol's finite top-form degree of regularity is not defined for those cells. Increasing the solver time cannot make that particular quotient zero-dimensional.

Write b for all m*ell Boolean selector variables and u_1,...,u_(m-2) for the scalar intermediate coordinates. Each factor-base coordinate x_i is a nonzero linear form in its own selector block. Let B=(b_1,...,b_(m*ell)) in R=F_p[b,u], and let T be the top-form ideal of the exact polynomial list emitted by src/main.rs.

Each selector equation b_i^2-b_i contributes b_i^2 to T, so B is contained in radical(T). It therefore suffices to compute the radical after setting all selectors to zero.

For a chain link with three nonconstant arguments x,y,z, the degree-four part is (x-y)^2*z^2 - 2*(x+y)*x*y*z + x^2*y^2. The first link has x and y in B; every interior link has its fresh factor-base coordinate in B. At b=0 the first link is zero, while an interior link becomes the product of the squares of its two intermediate coordinates. The final link has a constant target coordinate, so its highest part is u_(m-2)^2*x_(m-1)^2, which vanishes at b=0.

Thus radical(T) is the ideal generated by all selectors together with u_j*u_(j+1) for j=1,...,m-3. The latter squarefree monomial ideal has coordinate components corresponding to maximal independent vertex sets in the path on m-2 intermediates. The dimension is ceil((m-2)/2), the maximum size of such a set. One can attain it by choosing alternate coordinates freely and setting the rest to zero. One cannot choose more because each disjoint adjacent pair contributes at most one freely nonzero coordinate. This establishes positive dimension for m=3..9 directly from the frozen formulas, independently of target coordinates.

The dimensions for m=3..9 are 1,1,2,2,3,3,4. These are derived dimensions, not completed Singular outputs. The top-form degree for m=2 is not established here. Binary encodings have field equations for every intermediate bit, so this argument does not apply to them. Nothing here bounds the maximum intermediate degree of an affine Groebner-basis computation, and nothing here proves that IC decomposition is impossible.
