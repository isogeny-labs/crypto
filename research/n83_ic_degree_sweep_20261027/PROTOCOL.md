# N83 index-calculus degree sweep

Requested scope: m = 2 through 9, all of Koblitz, prime-field and general binary elliptic curves at field size n = 83. Here m is the decomposition arity. This is a decomposition-system study, not a complete discrete-log solver or a speed comparison.

## Frozen design

Use ell = floor(83/m): 41, 27, 20, 16, 13, 11, 10, 9. The candidate abscissa domain has 2^ell elements; the actual factor base consists of the points that lift on the chosen curve. Binary domains are span(1,z,...,z^(ell-1)); the prime-field domain is the integer interval [0,2^ell). These domain constructions differ and the rows do not establish a causal comparison between fields. S3 chains are algebraic candidate systems: their roots still require curve-lift and sign verification before becoming IC relations. No subgroup membership claim is made for the new synthetic prime/binary fixtures.

Binary field: GF(2)[z]/(z^83+z^45+z^2+z+1). Koblitz: y^2+xy=x^3+1, the repository's known_n83_k0 fixture (prime subgroup order 2417851639230796216685689, cofactor 4). General binary: y^2+xy=x^3+x^2+b, b=2^82+0x51b. Prime: y^2=x^3+2x+3; p is the first Proth-certified p=c*2^42+1 in the frozen ascending odd-c range beginning at 2^40+1, tested with witnesses 2..19 after the small-divisor filter in src/main.rs. Its primality certificate is emitted. All exact model parameters, targets, public planted points, and system hashes are saved.

For each of 24 cells, construct one planted target by summing m actual base points, excluding zero partial sums. Construct one independent natural point by rejection-lifting a deterministic pseudorandom x. SplitMix64 starts with 20261027 xor (m << 32) xor family tag (1,2,3); no reroll after solver observations. Both targets are public synthetic inputs. The generator checks the group law and every planted binary equation; Singular checks every planted input and returned basis against the witness. Natural targets are not asserted nondecomposable. No unknown private key is requested or recovered.

## Measurements and interpretation

Run two separate computations per system, for 96 attempts:

1. **Dreg_top**: form the ideal generated by each input polynomial's highest homogeneous component, including selector field equations. Run a homogeneous standard basis. If the quotient is zero-dimensional, compute its exact Hilbert series from the leading monomial ideal and report one plus its last nonzero graded degree. A positive-dimensional top ideal has no finite Dreg under this definition and is reported separately. Coefficients of the monomial ideal are transferred to Q for Hilbert computation, since the monomial Hilbert function is characteristic independent. This is not Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of the homogenized/saturated affine ideal.
2. **Affine basis**: run Singular std with degree-reverse-lexicographic order and retain its protocol trace, basis, maximum final basis degree, input-reduction check, and planted-point check. Final basis degree is not the maximum intermediate solving degree. No numerical solving_degree is inferred from ambiguous textual progress or a final basis. Raw traces are retained for future instrumented analysis.

An incomplete basis never supplies a final degree. Timeout is an operational limit, not a mathematical lower bound. There are no heuristic Dreg estimates in the measured table. Separate top/affine attempts use the same inputs, but their degrees are distinct quantities. All clocks are diagnostic only and wall_valid=false; no performance comparison or whole-system IC/rho ratio is licensed.

## Controls and limits

Native tests cover field arithmetic, the Proth certificate, curve lifting/addition, and symbolic S3 evaluation. The exact same Singular driver checks two-variable positive and inconsistent fixtures over GF(2) and the certified 83-bit prime: top degrees 3 and 2 respectively; the inconsistent affine ideal must produce {1}. The first development control expected top-degree zero for a contradictory affine system and failed; the corrected fixture explicitly distinguishes the top ideal from affine inconsistency. Preserve both control logs. No target data had been taken when this correction was made.

Each Singular process receives 40 seconds, TERM then KILL after 5 seconds; each worker inherits a 3 GiB virtual-memory cap. Four independent shards run on one EKS Spot job with four CPU requests, 16 GiB memory and a 1,800-second job deadline. No automatic retries, no skipped high-m cells, and no adaptive extension after seeing results. The ordinary arbitrary-command route does not support qualified CRIU restoration. Inputs and completed target directories are uploaded to the job's private results prefix. A lost process remains interrupted, never a completed measurement. The resume plan and frozen source are checkpointed before submission.

Controls took about 0.006 seconds each in local development; this is not a pilot cost for n83. The target upper budget is 96*(40+5)/4 = 1,080 seconds plus construction/upload. This bounded first sweep is one observation per target kind, not a distributional or statistical claim. A/A timing and rho controls are inapplicable because no clock, speed or full attack claim is made. No first-fall-degree substitution, post-hoc selection, or extrapolation is allowed.
