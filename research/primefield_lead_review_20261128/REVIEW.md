## Research boundary and literature

The primary target is an ordinary curve over F_p, prime-order subgroup, arbitrary public target coordinates, complete cold single-target cost and verified recovered scalar. Compare to the strongest same-target IC baseline and an equally engineered negation-map rho. A cheap equation evaluator, a coordinate identity, or a rank-preserving transform is a component result until its exact signed relations, rank contribution, target pin, setup, solve, and recovery cost are charged.

Semaev's original proposal is conditional on finding bounded solutions of the summation-polynomial system cheaply; its paper explicitly leaves that solver question open. Diem's index-calculus proof is for extension fields with a base-field P1 factor base and does not transfer directly to ordinary E/F_p. The 2018 prime-field paper proposes fewer relation searches, a relevant comparator for any “new” base/single-relation proposal. The new Di Muzio–Semaev solving-degree bound is an upper bound for a specified Gröbner algorithm, not an EC relation-output or favorable cost bound. Sources: [Semaev 2004](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/031.pdf), [Diem preprint](https://www.math.uni-leipzig.de/~diem/preprints/ind-cal-ell-curves.pdf), [2018 prime-field proposal](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3Ac99fdb39-f5b1-428d-999c-770b63363e25), [Di Muzio–Semaev 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32456).

The repository's [boundary ledger](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/blob/main/research/notes/index-calculus/RESEARCH_IC_BOUNDARY_LEDGER.md) gives the older common unit; its [closure note](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/blob/main/research/notes/index-calculus/PRIME_FIELD_IC_CLOSURE_20261112.md) marks restricted XL conclusions and unproved hypotheses. Neither is a general impossibility theorem. The [active goal](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/blob/main/docs/ACTIVE.md) on 25 November was chained S3 on at least three eligible M31 curves with complete cost and independent validation; it changed on 28 November, as noted below.

## New bounded follow-up: fixed B677 architecture cost gate

I independently recomputed arithmetic from the phase medians in [#5184's report](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/blob/claude/b677-relation-cost-20261125/research/b677_relation_cost_20261125/REPORT.md). Its sampled-13 run reports fixture 1.2 ms, pair table 72.8 ms, relation source 178.7 ms, graph/replay/elimination 31.0 ms, cold total 287.6 ms, and equal-engineering rho 4.6 ms. The listed phases sum to 283.7 ms, leaving 3.9 ms unallocated by that breakdown. **Conditional on the fixed architecture and these producer medians**, setting relation-source scan time to zero still leaves 105.0 ms, or 22.83 times rho. The maximum scan-only gain on the phase sum is 2.70 times. The scan itself is 62.1% of reported cold total. This is a prioritization bound, not a lower bound for index calculus or for a redesigned collector. [Recomputation script](cost_gate.py) prints these values.

The same report's 915,304 table entries plus 1,906,054 probes are 1.13 times its optimistic 2,503,653 entry/probe floor, so further generic table tuning is primarily constants. The reference itself remains 62.6 times equal-engineering rho in wall time; rho lacks negation-map tuning. The [#5188 report](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/blob/claude/semaev-fast-path-rank-rate-20261125/research/semaev_fast_path_rank_20261125/REPORT.md) confirms that query latency alone is a poor objective: 0.64 ms default oracle versus 5.0 ms reference oracle, but about half the useful rank rate. These are one-host, one-workload observations and require admitted replication.

## Broad idea-screen conclusions

The three [100-item matrices](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/pull/5165) label 3 original-mechanism bounded executions, 32 partial controls, 16 literal-premise screens, and 49 ideas without a runnable EC construction or matching input. The 100 labels do not mean 100 full IC experiments. The concrete useful spillovers are #24 exact S4 relation/recovery at tiny size, #75/#76 natural partial-row closure, and safe coefficient-aware rank selection; none shows scalable cost gain. The graph matrix makes a critical correction: equal support can have different modular rank, so support-only or real-valued spectral heuristics cannot replace exact field arithmetic. The #96 adaptive base and #97 interval controllers did not beat their best fixed controls on the frozen small workloads.

The [G33 twelve-lead assessment](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto-autoresearcher/pull/3459) leaves several disciplined investigations, especially target-affine banded LA, tensor rank as an F4 representation, and higher-order norm ranking. It refutes broad shortcut claims for AG decoding, encoding-only SAT freezing, and universal monodromy or Lean runtime barriers. Its local controls are not guarded complete-cost trials. The exact 2018 prime-field single-relation proposal should be used as a comparator when revisiting those bases.

## Follow-up design added after review

The [RR5 B677 pilot](RR5_B677_PILOT.md) gives a public-input, five-sector execution protocol and an [exact tuple-count budget](rr5_sector_budget.py). Sectors with three or four Dickson points account for 74.17% of distinct four-point tuples under the fixed 509/168 base. For uniform public R, the expected raw signed matches are 27.35 and 20.59 respectively, out of 64.64 across all sectors. This supports a frozen *pilot order* only; sector-specific F4 cost, duplicate rows, marginal rank, target projection and excluded charts decide actual value. The immediate code gate is call-owned cold accounting in the #5120 streaming adapter, which currently states `phase_accounting_complete=false`.

A newly opened [lfd-summation-systems #68](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/lfd-summation-systems/pull/68) corrects an invalid inference from high ANF degree to exhaustive search and proposes a quadratic root-lift presentation. It concerns binary-curve relations; no solver timing or solving-degree observation is reported, so it does not change the generic prime-field ranking.
