For a fixed source order the first equation gives all u and the last, by symmetry, gives all w. The two middle equations are univariate quadratics in v: A v^2+L v+C and D v^2+M v+E. Their combination D*first+A*second is (DL+AM)v+(DC+AE). If the linear coefficient is nonzero, every common root is uniquely its constant divided by its linear coefficient, and evaluation of both original equations is necessary and sufficient. If the coefficient is zero and the constant nonzero, no common root exists. Otherwise enumerate every root of the first nonzero S3 polynomial and retain those satisfying the second. S3(a,b,V) is never the zero polynomial for B!=0. This includes both linear and inseparable quadratic exceptional cases. Thus all finite original roots are preserved without an inversion at an unguarded zero denominator.

The proof of linear-combination elimination itself holds over every field with subtraction signs restored, though this implementation is binary only. Applying it to odd-characteristic curve polynomials requires separate coefficient and quadratic-solver proofs. This is an exact sparse chain join, a standard elimination mechanism; novelty has not been established. Predict lower original reconstruction cost by constraining both boundary equations before middle branching. The bounded experiment may refute that cost prediction.
