# Algebraic oracle vs generic baseline at larger k (scale-up S2) - protocol

Written 2026-11-05 before any data of this directory. Amendments, if any, are
appended with their reason and never rewrite this text.

## Question

Lead A ([`../curve25519_algebraic_ecbench_20261102`](../curve25519_algebraic_ecbench_20261102/README.md))
measured the algebraic oracle (`pkm-semaev`, isogeny tower t = 6, G = 4)
against the generic baseline at k = 20 in ecbench's counted unit, priced at
the AGENTS.md section 5a band w = 0, 0.1, 1 GAE per native operation: 2.6x,
1.4e5x and 8.2e5x behind `proj-a`. How does that distance move at k = 24 and
28 with the best algebraic setting measured so far, and how does the generic
gap (`proj-a` against `rho-neg`) move up to k = 32? A distance measurement,
not an improvement claim and not a security statement.

## Statistic

S_X(w) = mean over verified solves of (counted GAE + w x unpriced native
operations) / sqrt(r). Distance to `proj-a` = S_arm / S_proj-a, distance to
`rho-neg` = S_arm / S_rho-neg, both at w = 0, 0.1, 1, on the chunks where the
arm finished. For the arm ratios the `proj-a` and `rho-neg` means are taken on
the same chunks as the arm (paired by target), and also on all chunks of the
k, both reported. Context columns (not measured here): k = 20 from lead A, and
the baseline's own 48-record rho/proj-a values at k = 20..32.

## Extrapolation rule (fixed now, used only for an arm that cannot finish)

1. An arm with at least one verified solve at that k is reported as the mean
   over its verified solves with the number of failures beside it. Because
   the failures are the expensive solves, this mean is a **lower bound**
   (right-censored) and is labelled so.
2. An arm with zero verified solves at that k has no measured S. A labelled
   **estimate of the F4 term only** is given (a lower bound on S, group
   operations omitted): `S_est(w) = w x R x P / sqrt(r)` where `R` is the mean
   number of relations of the verified solves of the same arm at the smaller
   k (k = 24; if none, lead A's 7 to 19, flagged) and `P` is lead B's modelled
   F4 multiply-adds per relation `PR(cov)` for that cell (k = 28, t = 10,
   G = 9: 2.96e9; its README). The trace saving is not applied to P, so the
   estimate is conservative toward the algebraic arm only in that respect;
   it is kept apart from measured rows and never ranked in the band.
3. Nothing else is extrapolated; no estimate substitutes for a measured row.

## Preregistered predictions and falsifiers

Notation: `ratio_X(w) = S_X(w) / S_proj-a(w)`, taken on the chunks where X
finished.

- **P1.** At every k where an algebraic arm runs (24, 28) and every
  w in {0, 0.1, 1}, every algebraic arm stays behind `proj-a`:
  `ratio > 1`, and `ecbench band` ranks `proj-a` ahead of it at all three
  weights. Falsified by any (arm, k, w) cell with `ratio <= 1` (the table says
  which). Expected size at w = 0.1: more than 100x (P1b; falsified below 100).
- **P2.** `rho-neg` stays ahead of every arm at every k and w (band verdict
  `rho-neg < arm`). Falsified by any arm below `rho-neg` at any (k, w).
- **P3 (the gap grows with k).**
  (a) generic: `S_proj-a / S_rho-neg` at w = 0 is larger at k = 28 than at
  k = 24 and larger at k = 32 than at k = 28 (the baseline's own values:
  10.6, 18.1, 24.7), allowing noise of one 6-target mean, i.e. P3a is
  falsified if a step goes the other way by more than 25 percent;
  (b) algebraic, same arm across k: the bridge arm `pkm-iso-t6-g4`
  `ratio(0.1)` at k = 24 exceeds lead A's 1.35e5 at k = 20 (a loose
  comparison, different targets and 2 against 6 targets);
  (c) algebraic t = 10 arm: `ratio(0.1)` at k = 28 exceeds that at k = 24.
- **What would show the gap shrinking** (pre-stated): (b) bridge
  `ratio(0.1)` at k = 24 below lead A's 1.35e5 / 1.5 = 9.0e4; (c) t = 10
  `ratio(0.1)` at k = 28 below the k = 24 value / 1.5, on arms that finished;
  or (a) a generic ratio step down of more than 25 percent. A change
  inside those bands is "flat within noise", not shrinking. Any single cell
  with `ratio <= 1` falsifies P1 outright.
- **P4 (feasibility).** The t = 10, G = 9, trace = 1 arm finishes at least
  one of its 6 solves at k = 24 within 3000 s, and none of its 2 solves at
  k = 28 (extrapolation rule 2 then applies). Falsified by zero finishes at
  k = 24, or any finish at k = 28.
- **P5 (the best setting helps the F4 term).** At k = 24 on the same
  targets, the t = 10 arm has fewer F4 multiply-adds per solve than the
  bridge arm (lead B's modelled 46x per relation at k = 20, carried
  to k = 24 as the direction only). Falsified if it has more. Judged on the
  chunks where both finished.
