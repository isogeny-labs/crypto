Mathematical assumptions and notation · Figure 1

A retraction onto the cyclic order-n component is an explicit hypothesis; such a retraction does not exist for every subgroup of an arbitrary finite abelian group. If #G=hn with gcd(h,n)=1, [h] followed by the inverse of h modulo n supplies one. For the ECCp-131 cover, f* composed with [2 inverse modulo n] and f_* supplies a retraction onto the embedded elliptic group. The matrix columns are logarithms of projected factor-base elements. A usable left-kernel vector must have nonzero pairing with b; a raw count of relations does not certify this.

Mathematical assumptions and notation · Figure 3

Semaev polynomials characterize geometric point-sum relations. Each variable of S_(m+1) has degree 2^(m−1). For x_i in the base field, coefficient expansion over a degree-d extension gives d equations; independence is an additional condition. The fixed-d Gaudry cost is a heuristic algorithmic regime, not an unconditional bound for every curve, target specialization, or factor base. For a,b,x_R and x_i all in the base field, the other coefficient equations vanish identically. This statement does not cover arbitrary nonrational affine x-lines.

Mathematical assumptions and notation · Figure 4

For C2 the other elliptic quotient is u=X^(−2), v=Y/X^3, with equation v²=b u³+a u²+1. The two quotient maps give a (2,2)-split Jacobian. The sextic has two rational infinity points, so the fiber divisor uses infinity_+ + infinity_- as reference. A general even-degree reduced-divisor convention needs care. For C3, the projective equation W^4=X^3 Z+aXZ^3+bZ^4 is smooth when the elliptic cubic is squarefree and the characteristic exceeds 3; its degree-two cover has an abelian-surface Prym. The norm–pullback identity proves injectivity on odd-order torsion, without implying that the full Jacobian is cyclic.

Mathematical assumptions and notation · Figure 5

For ECCp-131 the full quadratic group is odd and decomposes into the +1 and −1 eigenspaces, identified with the curve and its quadratic twist over Fp. For the cubic group, T3 is surjective onto E(Fp), since multiplication by 3 is invertible there; rho3 is its normalized retraction. The verified coprimality also separates the target prime from ker T3. Infinity is excluded from the literal x-coordinate factor base on the cubic side. Nonrational affine x-lines produce other factor bases; projecting their relations remains valid but does not prove a universal algorithmic lower bound.

The large-prime coefficient rows are (1,-1,0), (0,1,-1), and (-1,0,1), so their sum is zero. Mathematical assumptions and notation · Figure 9

For the published ECCp-131 curve, the quadratic character of x_P is +1 and that of x_Q is −1. The Q fiber is nevertheless an Fp-rational degree-two divisor because Frobenius swaps its geometric points. The plot uses a different, explicitly labeled toy curve E/F571: y²=x³+508x+137, #E=557. Its genus-two companion quotient has order 609, hence #J=339213. The signed factor-base point set F_B is stable under the deck and hyperelliptic involutions, and excludes X=0 and infinity. Let S_B consist of classes of A+A'−D_infinity supported on F_B with distinct X. There are B(B−1)/2 unordered point pairs, of which B/2 share an X; hence |S_B|=B²/2−B. They give distinct classes because nonspecial degree-two divisors on a genus-two curve are unique in their linear-equivalence class; distinct X excludes the hyperelliptic pencil. Exactly B/2 nonidentity elliptic targets have their full fiber in F_B. Thus the two displayed probabilities have different sampling spaces and are exact in this toy setup. The E targets and support-pair encodings are enumerated; the whole Jacobian is not. These counts are not an attack-cost estimate. The final projection identity is for a split fiber over P, with the specified infinity reference.

Mathematical assumptions and notation · Figure 10

The sliders are an illustrative phase-average model, not ECCp-131 measurements. Baseline A uses trial cost 1, verified-row probability 0.02, and effective information fraction 0.5, giving cost 100 per effective row. Alternative B initially has twice the verified-row yield but four times the trial cost, giving 200. Effective information is a modeling proxy encompassing rank/recombination; it is not automatically an independent Bernoulli success probability and may vary during collection. The sparse linear-algebra estimate describes a near-square filtered matrix and Wiedemann-style Krylov work, omitting algorithm-specific preconditioning, logarithmic factors, block strategies and dense-fill effects. Costs require a common calibrated work unit before comparison with rho or a different solver.
