# A Lean algebraic cutoff for the printed JV empty example

Lean proves that the three printed Joux–Vitse quadratics in the first
F101³ worked example have a proper homogeneous ideal containing every
monomial of total degree at least four. Setting the homogenizing variable
to one gives the unit ideal, with no geometric affine solution in any
field of characteristic101. This is a literal polynomial-ideal proof;
no generic regularity hypothesis is assumed. The whole gate passes39
declarations with zero failures and zero trusted-native exceptions.

## What the kernel checks

[LiteralQuadratics.lean](lean/LiteralQuadratics.lean) establishes universal
characteristic101 polynomial identities. Constant linear combinations
give the three normalized quadratics. Further explicit combinations give

\[
  c_y=yh^2+82h^3,\qquad c_x=xh^2+66h^3,
\]

and an exact certificate for h⁴ in the source ideal. These are identities
checked by the kernel, not a basis or ideal-membership oracle.
[pcc:unverified]

[ActualIdeal.lean](lean/ActualIdeal.lean) puts those identities in the
actual ideal generated by the three source polynomials. It proves the
dehomogenized ideal is the whole ring and rules out affine solutions over
every characteristic101 field. [pcc:unverified]

[CubicNormalForms.lean](lean/CubicNormalForms.lean) proves all nine cubic
normal forms in every characteristic101 commutative ring satisfying the
three equations. Each cubic is a specified scalar times h³. Multiplying
these checked relations and using h⁴=0 makes all15degree-four monomials
vanish. [pcc:unverified]

[MacaulayCertificate.lean](lean/MacaulayCertificate.lean) applies those
relations to the actual quotient F101[x,y,h]/I. Properness is proved using
evaluation at the origin before obtaining its characteristic101 structure.
Every degree-four monomial lies in I; an explicit exponent factorization
extends this to every higher degree. The closed main declaration is
`JVMacaulayAudit.literal_jv_macaulay_upper_certificate`. [pcc:unverified]

## Why the scope matters

This is an algebraic upper cutoff of four. It does not prove minimality,
the exact Hilbert function or quotient length8. It does not certify
msolve's internal execution degrees, or identify the three coordinate
equations with a formalized F101³ elliptic-curve descent. The separate
[native study](../groebner_system_audit_20261113/README.md) supplies bounded
source-field/input/GB checks as unsigned evidence. [pcc:unverified]

That native run returns a degree-four homogeneous GB and later processes
a degree-five selected-pair round with zero new polynomials/two zero
reductions. The new kernel certificate explains why that log alone is
not a disproof of the intended algebraic bound. General Joux–Vitse
Assumption2, generic FGHR regularity and larger m,n cases remain open.
