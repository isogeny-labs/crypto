## Statements, proofs, and correspondence

The historical sources are
[TransferBound.lean](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/blob/abee07ee7337d04a7e734333669ca1bacd6c763a/research/conductor_gap_cm_pairs_20261010/lean/TransferBound.lean)
and [SubfieldCollapse.lean](https://github.com/aburan28/crypto/blob/abee07ee7337d04a7e734333669ca1bacd6c763a/research/weak_subfield_hyperelliptic_decomposition_20261012/lean/SubfieldCollapse.lean).

For a finite subgroup S, additive homomorphisms f and back, and degree d,
assume `back(f(P)) = d P` for every P in S and `gcd(|S|,d)=1`.
If f(P)=f(Q), applying back gives dP=dQ. Multiplication by d is bijective
on S, so P=Q. Consequently `k f(G)=f(Q)` iff `k G=Q`, for every integer k.
The curve theorem specializes these exact hypotheses to actual elliptic-curve
point groups. It does not construct an isogeny, its reverse map, or the duality
identity, and does not establish a measured cost or a conductor-dependent gain.
Those bridges remain necessary for any concrete curve claim.

For an additive homomorphism f, its range is an additive subgroup. If every
factor-base element lies in that range, the universal property of subgroup
closure puts the entire generated subgroup in the range. A target outside
the range therefore cannot be generated by that base. This version uses
Mathlib groups and closure. Construction of a Jacobian base-change map and a
Weil cardinality bound remain outside this theorem.
