# Large-arity point decomposition for EC index calculus: literature survey

Scope: published trade-offs between decomposition arity m (number of factor-base points per relation) and factor-base dimension ℓ (or size |F|), with emphasis on m ≥ 5, binary curves E(F_{2^n}) with n prime, and small-degree extension fields. Everything below was read from the primary sources (PDFs fetched 2026-10-24; eprint.iacr.org is behind a bot wall, so IACR papers were read via the Wayback Machine or author mirrors). "Measured" = an experiment with reported timings; "claimed" = a heuristic/asymptotic estimate.

Notation used throughout: n = extension degree over the base field F_q (q = 2 for binary prime-degree curves); ℓ (= n′ = k = d in various papers) = F_q-dimension of the subspace V defining the factor base F = {P : x(P) ∈ V}, so |F| ≈ q^ℓ; m = number of factor-base points per relation; S_{m+1} = Semaev summation polynomial (degree 2^{m−1} in each variable); D_reg = max step degree in F4 ("solving degree"); D_ff = first fall degree.

Notes. (i) The degree of P_{ϕ,n} in each variable is still 2^{n−2} (FHJRV Prop. 5): the gain of torsion symmetrisation is sparsity in the invariant ring and an m-fold smaller factor base (LA ÷ m², relation probability ÷ m^{n−1}), not lower per-variable degree. (ii) FHJRV Prop. 4: the only subgroups usable this way are E[2], cyclic subgroups of order prime to char, or E[char]; FHJRV Prop. 3: a point of order m forces deg ϕ divisible by m, so 3-torsion needs degree-3 morphisms (never carried out; "higher order torsion … extended version" was promised, not found). (iii) FGHR JoC Remark 7 (pre-FHJRV): "the seventh summation polynomial … has never been computed". (iv) One paper (McGuire–Sheekey, [arXiv 1905.11755](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11755) §6.2) states S_m "have only been computed for m ≤ 9" with no reference; I could not find any S_9 computation, and McGuire–Mueller ([eprint 2017/1262](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/1262), Remark 6.8) say "the largest summation polynomial computed so far is S_8 (FHJ+14)". Treat S_9 as unverified. (v) FHJRV computed P_{ϕ,8} but ran decompositions only with P_{ϕ,6} (m = 5, F_{(2^31)^5} and F_{(2^31+413)^5}); no m = 6 or m = 7 decomposition experiment exists in the literature I found.

## (b) The m vs ℓ trade-off as published

**Semaev 2004** ([eprint 2004/031](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/031)) proposed V = {x < q^{1/n+δ}} (prime field) or polynomials of degree ≤ l/n+δ (F_{2^l}, Remark 2), i.e. ℓ ≈ n/m with m = n variables — but gave no algorithm for the PDP.

**Gaudry 2009 / Diem 2011** (q large, n small): m = n, ℓ = 1 (V = F_q), relation probability 1/n!, system of n equations in n unknowns of degree 2^{n−1}, ideal of degree 2^{n(n−1)} ⇒ FGLM dominates. Galbraith–Gaudry survey ([eprint 2015/1022](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/1022) §8): "When q is large and n is rather small, the best choice is k = n and ℓ = 1"; Õ(q²), Õ(q^{2−2/n}) with double large primes. Diem's rigorous subexponential result needs n ≈ sqrt(log q) with m = n.

**Joux–Vitse 2013** (m = n−1): success probability drops from 1/n! to 1/((n−1)! q) (JV §2.2, FGHR §5.2: "decreases the probability by a factor q/n"), but the system becomes overdetermined (n eqs, n−1 vars, degree 2^{n−2} vs 2^{n−1}), has generically 0 or 1 solution, so no FGLM. Their chart (JV §2.3): Gaudry–Diem: n! systems, n eq/n var, deg 2^{n−1}; hybrid (guess one variable): n!·q systems, n eq/n−1 var, deg 2^{n−1}; n−1 variant: (n−1)!·q systems, n eq/n−1 var, deg 2^{n−2}. Vitse's ECC 2010 slides ([PDF](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~viva/research/talks/ECC2010.pdf)) tabulate Gaudry–Diem n!·2^{3n(n−1)}·q^{2−2/n}, Nagao n!·2^{2ωn(n−1)}·q^{2−2/n}, Joux–Vitse n!·2^{ω(n−1)(n−2)}·e^{ωn}·q². JV's perspective for n > 6 is exactly the group's question: "Instead of just reducing the number of points m relatively to n, a more natural choice would be to also enlarge the factor base … subspace of dimension d > 1. The probability … becomes approximately q^{md−n}/m! and the linear algebra cost is Õ(m q^{2d}), so the best values for d and m involve a trade-off that depends heavily on the cost of the decomposition test. A major difficulty is that the equations … are no longer invariant under the full symmetric group S_{md} … but only under the smaller group S_m" — their Magma attempts via invariant-ring algebra "have been unsuccessful so far" (JV §6).

**FGHR 2014**: "for parameters for which it is possible to choose between the decomposition in n or n−1 points, the best solution is the first" (n ≤ 5); n = 6 forces n−1; "the only hope … would be to remove the bad dependence in q … intrinsic to the n−1 approach" (§5.3).

**Petit–Quisquater 2012**: with ℓ = n/m and the FFDA (D_reg ≈ D_ff ≤ m²+1), m should grow like n^{1/3} (ℓ = n^{2/3}); but for n ≤ 2 000, m ≤ 4 (Table 3); Shantz–Teske's reproduction gives the same table with small differences (n = 1 000: m = 3 vs 4; n = 50 000: m = 11 vs 12). ST quote PQ: "best running times are achieved with m ≤ 4 if n ≤ 2000, and with m ≤ 14 for n ≤ 100 000". Not confirmed: Kosters–Yeo, Huang–Kosters–Yeo and Kousidis–Wiemers show D_reg growing with n at fixed m (m = 2: 3 → 4 → 5 between n = 12 and n = 45; m = 3: D_reg = 7 = bound; m = 4: 13 = bound).

**Shantz–Teske 2013**: (i) at fixed m, decreasing ℓ below n/m ("Delta method", Δ = n − mℓ > 0) cuts Gröbner time by more than the 2^{m−1} needed to compensate (for m = 2 the optimum is Δ ≈ 2⌊(n−15)/6⌋), because the system becomes overdetermined; (ii) "using a value of m = 3 is much slower than using m = 2 … our experimental results suggest that using m = 2 will always remain the better option"; theoretically the switch to m = 3 is at n ≈ 290 (driven by linear algebra), while relation gathering is faster with m = 2 until n > 600; (iii) the hybrid method (fix k variables) gives less than Delta; block-hybrid is worse than fixing the first k variables; combining hybrid + Delta gives nothing extra.

**HPST 2013**: m = 3 fixed ("no hope … m = 2 because of the linear algebra; … unpractical for m = 4 even for very small n"); whole-ECDLP experiments found ℓ = 3 optimal for n ≤ 19 ("close to exhaustive search"). Their Table 5: increasing m raises the probability 2^{mℓ−n}/m! but increases both D and N; increasing ℓ raises the probability but increases N. The HPST symmetric/non-symmetric variable trick cuts D_reg from 6–7 to 3–4 at the price of ≈ (m²ℓ + mn)/2 variables (GG14 §4.1).

**Galbraith–Gebregiyorgis 2014**: with 4-torsion invariants, m = 4 and ℓ = 3, 4 become feasible with Gröbner (ℓ ≤ 7 with SAT); "The best we can seem to hope for in practice is m = 4 and ℓ ≤ 10 … number of trials … 2^n/2^{mℓ} ≥ 2^{n−40} ≥ 2^{n/2}" for n > 100, "so … our methods are worse than Pollard rho … even for static-DH". Symmetry breaking (P_i ∈ V + v_i) gains (m−1)! overall; D_reg often 3 instead of 4 at large n; "for large q and small n … not a useful idea". Gebregiyorgis' thesis ([PDF](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/Shishay.pdf), §3.7) adds a disjoint-block factor base F_i = {x ∈ span(θ^{(i−1)ℓ},…,θ^{iℓ−1})} so that the success probability is 2^{ℓm}/2^n ≈ 1 (no m!).

**Semaev 2015**: large m, small ℓ: k = ⌈n/m⌉ with m ≈ sqrt(2 ln 2 · n/ln n) (6–12 for n = 100–571), using chained S_3 systems with m−2 free intermediate x-coordinates. "The problem of generating [the Weil descent of S_{m+1}] and keeping it in computer memory … is difficult by itself for m ≥ 4"; the chained system has (m−1)n equations, ≈ (m−1)n variables, Boolean degree 3, D_ff = 4. He also notes that for t < m "the solving running time … drops dramatically and the probability of solving is relatively lower" (a smaller-arity, more-targets trade-off he does not pursue). Critiques: Kosters–Yeo (D_reg 5 at (45, 2, 2) and (25, 3, 3); the FFDA "does not 'see' the number of variables" and would "prove P = NP" via the splitting trick); HKY Crypto 2015 (chained-S_3 system with m = O(n) and fixed P_i = 2^i P would give a polynomial-time ECDLP under FFDA, "highly improbable"); Galbraith–Gaudry survey §9.2/§10.2 ("this conjecture is not yet demonstrated to our satisfaction"; "no consensus"; FFDA "too optimistic", last-fall-degree "more precise but much harder to estimate"); Shantz–Teske predate Semaev 2015 and only say "further research … is needed".

**Karabina 2015**: one auxiliary point for m = 4, 5 (fewer new variables than the full chain, higher D_ff 7 / 9); the trade-off "lowering the degree … and increasing the number of variables is worth"; m = 5 solved to n = 19 (D = 8); extrapolated 2^{n/5}·n^{27}·2^34 — "it is tempting to assume D_reg ≈ 9".

**HPST 2015** (generalized FFDA): balanced S_3 splits give D_reg = 4 for m = 3, 4 at all tested (n, ℓ) (up to n = 29, ℓ = 8 for m = 3; ℓ = 4 for m = 4 at 220 GB); for m = 5, 6 (ℓ = 2, 3) D_reg = 4.3–5.0, with run times of 13–34 h at n = 23–29. Gebregiyorgis' thesis summarises: "experiments made in [PTH15] handle up to m = 6 although the running time is huge".

**Trimoska–Ionica–Dequen 2020**: with SAT (WDSat) the PDP cost at fixed ℓ is almost independent of n and scales like 2^{mℓ}/m! (worst case): ℓ = 11, m = 3 takes ≈ 1 day per instance; even so "index calculus attacks are still not practical compared to the PCS generic method" (n = 59: PCS 0.8 h for the whole DLP vs > 86 h for 2^ℓ decompositions). Memory is no longer a constraint. Their stated open direction: SAT on the 2-/4-torsion-reduced systems of GG14 and m > 3 (thesis, not retrieved).

**Prime fields** (APS 2018, McGuire–Mueller 2017): with |F| = p^{1/m} and no Weil descent, m = 3 is optimal in every experiment; the McGuire–Mueller "Sum in F" enumeration (no Gröbner) is O(p) heuristically and "m = 3 is the best choice"; Yokoyama et al. 2020 prove (under statistical assumptions) a Gröbner lower bound ≥ m·d^{m−1} for the naive Semaev system, giving total > p^{m/(m−1)(1−ε)}, i.e. no m beats rho.

**Diem 2013** is the only rigorous large-m regime: m ≈ sqrt(log₂ q) points, each from a different summand U_i of a direct-sum decomposition of F_{q^n} (symmetry-broken factor base), ℓ = d ≈ n/m.

**Summary of published optima**: ℓ = 1, m = n (q large, n ≤ 5); m = n−1 for n = 5, 6 (JV, FGHR); m = 2 for n ≤ 48 and m = 3 up to n ≈ 50–90 with ℓ ≤ 8–11 (ST, HPST, GG, Trimoska); m = 4 only with ℓ ≤ 4 (Gröbner) or ℓ ≤ 7 (SAT); m = 5, 6 only at n ≤ 29, ℓ ≤ 3 (Karabina, HPST15), hours–days per decomposition; large-m claims (PQ m = n^{1/3}; Semaev m ≈ sqrt(n/ln n); Karabina m = 5) all rest on a bounded-D_reg assumption that the measured data (D_reg 5 at n = 45 for m = 2; D_reg 4.3–5 at m = 5, 6) do not support. Nobody has published a D_reg or time measurement at m ≥ 5 with ℓ ≥ 4.

## (c) Formulations for m ≥ 5 not obviously covered by the group's current experiments

1. **Balanced S_3 splitting with free intermediates (HPST15 variant).** HPST15 split S_5 as {S_3(x_1,x_2,w_1), S_3(w_1,X,w_2), S_3(w_2,x_3,x_4)} with the target X in the middle equation and report this is "better … than {S_3(x_1,x_2,w_1), S_3(w_1,x_3,w_2), S_3(w_2,x_4,X)} to obtain a better balance of variables". For m = 5, 6 they used the linear chain (Table 4). Kosters–Yeo's "splitting trick" and Semaev's system (4) are the linear chain. Test: at m = 5, 6 compare linear vs balanced binary tree (Karabina's §5 system: S_3(x_1,x_2,x_{12}), S_3(x_3,x_4,x_{34}), S_3(x_5,x_{50},x_R), S_3(x_{12},x_{34},x_{50})) at fixed (n, ℓ); record D_reg vs ℓ (HPST15 saw D_reg climb from 4 to 4.3–5 going from ℓ = 2 to 3 at m = 5, 6 — the only published hint of a D_reg(ℓ) dependence at m ≥ 5).
2. **Mixed S_3/S_4 splits with a single auxiliary point** (Karabina: S_4·S_4 for m = 5, D_ff ≤ 9, measured D = 7–8, only n extra Boolean variables; APS over F_p: S_3·S_4 for m = 5). Trade-off: fewer variables, higher degree than the pure S_3 chain. Not tested on binary curves beyond n = 19; a direct comparison with the group's chained S_3 at m = 5 is a one-afternoon experiment.
3. **Torsion-symmetrised P_{ϕ,6} (50 monomials, char 2) with HPST/GG-style mixed variables for prime n.** GG14 did (Z/4)^{m−1}⋊S_m invariants only to m = 4; FHJRV used P_{ϕ,6} only with ℓ = 1 over F_{2^31}. Nobody has Weil-descended P_{ϕ,6} (m = 5) or P_{ϕ,7} (m = 6, 2 247 terms) over F_2 with an ℓ-dimensional V. Test: GG14 Experiment 2 at m = 5 using P_{ϕ,6}; expected obstacle is the variable blow-up N ≈ (m²ℓ + mn)/2 noted by GG14 §4.1. Also untested: the Galbraith–Gaudry remark that "it is an open problem to exploit larger symmetry groups" inside the splitting formulation (§9.2), i.e. torsion-symmetrising each S_3 link of the chain.
4. **Symmetry-breaking disjoint factor bases for large m.** Diem 2013 (K = ⊕U_i, rigorous), GG14 Exp. 3 (affine translates V + v_i; D_reg often 3 instead of 4), Gebregiyorgis thesis Def. 3.7.1 (block subspaces, probability 2^{mℓ}/2^n with no m!), APS (partition of V into V_i), Matsuo/Nagao (GG survey §10.3). This removes the m! that dominates the group's cost model (m!·2^{n−(m−1)ℓ} targets per relation) at the price of m× more relations and m²× LA. Not measured anywhere for m ≥ 5.
5. **Factor bases defined by polynomials that split completely ("quasi-subfield" polynomials).** Huang–Kosters–Petit–Yeo–Yun (JMC 2020) is the EC transplant of the function-field-sieve idea: V = roots of X^{q^{n′}} − λ(X) dividing X^{q^n} − X, giving m equations S^{(k)} = φ^k(S_{m+1}) solved with Rojas' sparse resultants (no Gröbner); complexity m!·q^{n−n′m+n′}·Õ(m^{5.188}(3d)^{4.876 m²}) + m·q^{2n′}. Petit–Kosters–Messeng PKC 2016 ([PDF](https://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf)) note that the F_2-subspace factor base is implicitly L_V(x) = Π_{v∈V}(x−v) (linearized polynomial) and generalise to L = composition of small-degree maps over F_p; Euler–Petit ([arXiv 1909.11326](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11326)) show linearized polynomial families have β ≥ 3/4 while β ≤ 0.1 would be needed to beat generic algorithms; McGuire–Sheekey ([arXiv 1905.11755](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11755)) show linearized trinomials x^{q^d} − b x^q − a x splitting over F_{q^{(d−1)d+1}} are quasi-subfield but give cost > q^{n/2}. Bluher polynomials x^{q+1}+ax+b were not found applied to EC PDP. Conclusion in the literature: the "split completely" idea is known for EC and does not beat generic algorithms because the resultant/Gröbner cost grows like d^{O(m²)}. Searches for "linearized polynomial point decomposition", "subspace polynomial elliptic curve index calculus", "Bluher polynomial elliptic curve" returned nothing beyond these.
6. **No-Gröbner evaluation oracles over V.** McGuire–Mueller: fast evaluation of S_m over V^m (Eval S_m) or "Sum in F" (enumerate m−1-sums, test membership), heuristic O(p log² p) / O(p); their Heuristic Result 6.5 gives Gröbner on {S_m, f_i(X_i)} as O(p^{ω−ω/m}). These are the prime-field analogues of the group's table/MITM oracles and confirm the "product law" (cost independent of |F|).
7. **Under-determined / parametric Gröbner bases + sieving** (GG survey §9.3, JV Eurocrypt 2012): treat x_m as a parameter, compute one "generic" Gröbner basis, specialise it for many targets; "for the price of one Gröbner basis computation, we get many for free", but "in most cases, the generic Gröbner basis is too large", except with Nagao's quadratic systems (hyperelliptic). Untested for EC with m ≥ 5.
8. **Nagao/Riemann–Roch for EC**: n(n−1) quadratic equations in n(n−1) unknowns, ideal degree 2^{n(n−1)} (Vitse ECC 2010 table); the GG survey states "Nagao's approach is also valid for elliptic curves. However, until now, nobody managed to turn this into an algorithm that is faster than … summation polynomials". Consistent with the group's finding that it reduces to S_4 at prime n.
9. **Courtois 2016** ([eprint 2016/003](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/003)): for m = 2 with V = {deg < n/2} he claims a 2^{n/3}·poly, memory-free algorithm (guess a small multiple K·R with K ≤ 2^{n/6} and a common divisor M of degree n/6 of X, Y, then solve a linear system via the half-trace Tr_1); for m = 3 with deg < n/3 each, also 2^{n/3}. Unverified heuristics, but a non-generic oracle for the m = 2, ℓ = n/2 node that beats the 2^{n/2} MITM bound would feed any large-m tree.
10. **Trace-map filter** (Kosters–Yeo Prop. 4.2, Remark 4.8; HKY Prop. 5): E(F_{2^n}) → F_2, P ↦ Tr((x(P)+a_2)/a_1²) is a surjective homomorphism with kernel 2E; it yields a free linear Weil-descent equation (D_ff = 2 for S_3) and can be used to "only look for relations in the kernel" — a constant-factor filter that stacks with any arity.

## (d) Generic m-sum bounds (relation finding in a prime-order group without a quotient)

Setting: F ⊂ G with |F| = N = 2^ℓ, G cyclic of prime order; find P_1 + … + P_m = R. Only the group law and equality tests (plus a canonical labelling such as the x-coordinate for sorting) are available.

- **Meet-in-the-middle (the only method that applies generically).** Time N^{⌈m/2⌉}, memory N^{⌊m/2⌋} (sort the N^{⌊m/2⌋} partial sums, enumerate the other N^{⌈m/2⌉}): m = 4: T = M = N²; m = 5: T = N³, M = N²; m = 6: T = M = N³; m = 7: N⁴/N³; m = 8: N⁴/N⁴. Memory can be traded for time with van Oorschot–Wiener golden-collision search on f(i,j) = x(P_i + P_j) vs x(R − P_k − P_l): for m = 4 the domain has size N², so T ≈ (N²)^{3/2}/√M = N³/√M (vOW, J. Cryptology 12 (1999) 1–28). No paper states these numbers for EC relation finding at m = 4..8 explicitly; they follow from the definitions.
- **Wagner's K-tree** ([Crypto 2002](https://www.iacr.org/archive/crypto2002/24420288/24420288.pdf)): T = M = N^{1/(⌊log K⌋+1)} for K-XOR / K-sum in (Z/2^n) or Z/N with a quotient (filtering partial sums on low-order bits). Inapplicable in a prime-order EC group: there is no non-trivial homomorphism to a small group to filter partial sums (Galbraith–Gaudry survey §10.1 make the related point that pulling back x^q − x to E gives only the trivial relation P + (−P) = ∞).
- **Schroeppel–Shamir** (SIAM J. Comput. 10 (1981)): 4-sum in T = N², M = N; needs an order on the ambient set compatible with addition (priority-queue enumeration of A + B in sorted order) — not available on E.
- **Howgrave-Graham–Joux (Eurocrypt 2010) / Becker–Coron–Joux (Eurocrypt 2011)**: 2^{0.337n}, 2^{0.291n} for knapsacks via "representations" with modular constraints — need a quotient map; inapplicable generically.
- **Dissection** (Dinur–Dunkelman–Keller–Shamir, [eprint 2012/217](https://eprint.iacr.org/2012/217), JoC 2019): for bicomposite problems (e.g. r-fold encryption, knapsacks mod 2^n) first error-free attacks with T·M < 2^{3rn/4}, e.g. r = 7: T = 2^{4n}, M = 2^n; needs intermediate values that can be guessed/filtered (modular reduction) — not available in a generic group.
- **Time–memory trade-offs for GBP** (Nikolić–Sasaki Asiacrypt 2015: T²·M^{log K−1} = K·N; Dinur, [eprint 2018/575](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/575), DCC 2019: T^{⌈log K/2⌉+1}·M^{⌊log K/2⌋} = N for K ≥ 8; K = 6: T = N^{1/3}, M = N^{1/6}; K = 14: T = N^{1/4}, M = N^{1/8}) all live in the XOR/modular setting (they combine Schroeppel–Shamir variants with dissection) and do not transfer.
- **Lower bounds.** I found no published generic-group (Shoup/Maurer) lower bound for the k-sum/relation-finding problem; the only k-sum lower bounds found are Erickson's Ω((n−k)^{⌈k/2⌉}) in the linear decision tree model and Belovs–Špalek's quantum query bound Θ(n^{k/(k+1)}) ([arXiv 1206.6528](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6528)), neither in the generic-group model. Dinur–Keller–Klein ("Fine-grained cryptanalysis: tight conditional bounds for dense k-SUM and k-XOR") show the K-tree is essentially optimal for k = 3, 4, 5 in the dense regime, again with a quotient. Nagao's 2010 appendix ([eprint 2010/511](https://eprint.iacr.org/2010/511)) treats "decomposed", "almost decomposed" and "2-almost decomposed" elements in a general prime-order group but assumes an Õ(1) decomposition oracle.
- **Non-generic structured exception**: Courtois 2016 claims 2^{n/3} for m = 2, ℓ = n/2 using the F_{2^n} polynomial structure of V (see (c)9), which "in some sense violate[s] the generic group model"; unverified.

Consequence for the group's cost model: with the only generic oracle being MITM at 2^{⌈m/2⌉ℓ}, relations × targets × oracle = 2^ℓ · m!·2^{n−(m−1)ℓ} · 2^{⌈m/2⌉ℓ} = m!·2^{n−(m−2−⌈m/2⌉)ℓ}, i.e. m!·2^n for m = 4, 5, m!·2^{n−ℓ} for m = 6, 7 and m!·2^{n−2ℓ} for m = 8, 9 (own arithmetic, consistent with the "product law" for m ≤ 5); the memory N^{⌊m/2⌋} = 2^{⌊m/2⌋ℓ} and the m! are the binding constraints, not the per-oracle time.

## Mixed-arity / large-prime variants found (item 8)

- **Gaudry 2009 / Gaudry–Thomé–Thériault–Diem 2007** ([eprint 2004/153](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/153), Math. Comp. 76 (2007)): double large prime variation: factor base F_1 ⊂ F_0, relations allowed to contain ≤ 2 "large primes" from F_0 \ F_1; genus-g hyperelliptic Õ(q^{2−2/g}), EC over F_{q^n} Õ(q^{2−2/n}) (Gebregiyorgis thesis §2.3 describes the EC version). FGHR Table 5 use it to rebalance: cost log₂(q)(1 + r(n−1)/n)(n−2)!·q^{1+(n−2)(1−r)}·c(n,q,n) + n³ log²(q)·N^{2r}, r chosen to balance.
- **Joux–Vitse n−1** (m_1 = n−1 points of V_1 = F_q, m_2 = 0): the first "arity reduction" variant; their SDHP variant shrinks F to F′ = F/l and pays l^m in decomposition trials (JV §5), with the explicit m = 4 comparison c̃(4, Q^{1/5}) ≪ c(4, Q^{1/4}).
- **Joux–Vitse 2012 cover + decomposition** on E(F_{p^6}): two-level (GHS cover to genus 3 over F_{p^2}, then Nagao decomposition into 6 points, then sieving; "right-kernel" linear algebra).
- **Nagao 2010** appendix: rebalanced / one-external-element / two-external-elements methods in a general group with Õ(1) decomposition oracle ("almost decomposed" = one point outside the restricted base B ⊂ B_0).
- **Diem 2013**: m disjoint subspaces U_i (dimensions d−1 or d) — a multi-base, same-arity scheme.
- **Galbraith–Gebregiyorgis 2014 / Gebregiyorgis 2016 / APS 2018**: disjoint translates or blocks V + v_i, V_i.
- No EC-specific "special-q" or genuinely mixed-dimension (m_1 points of V_1 of dim ℓ_1 plus m_2 points of V_2 of dim ℓ_2 > ℓ_1) decomposition algorithm with experiments was found; the closest are the double-large-prime formulations above (which are mixed-dimension in spirit: ℓ_1 < ℓ_0) and JV's untested d > 1 proposal.
