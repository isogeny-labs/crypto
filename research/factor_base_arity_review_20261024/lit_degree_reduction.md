# Literature survey: reducing the solving degree / cost of Weil-descent summation-polynomial systems

Date: 2026-10-24. Scope: web-only survey (no code run). Notation: n = extension degree of F_{2^n} (prime unless stated), l = F_2-dimension of the factor-base subspace V, m = number of summands (so the (m+1)-th summation polynomial S_{m+1} is used), Δ = n − m·l.

Everything below is labelled **measured** (numbers reported in the paper from experiments), **proved**, or **claimed/heuristic**. Where I could only read an abstract or a secondary citation, I say so. Full texts of the following could *not* be retrieved (IACR ePrint PDFs and HAL are behind bot-walls from this machine; De Gruyter/Springer paywalled): FGHR JoC 2014 body (used FHJRV 2014 + Vitse's 2013 slides as proxies), Faugère–Svartz ISSAC 2013 body (abstract only), Trimoska–Ionica–Dequen AFRICACRYPT 2020 tables (abstract + CP 2020 tables), Shantz–Teske 2013 tables, Huang–Kosters–Yeo CRYPTO 2015 tables (used Kosters–Yeo's tables), Semaev–Tenti's exact overdetermination threshold.

A vocabulary mapping that matters for interpreting the literature against the group's measurements (details and citations in §3):

* The group's **plain XL / Macaulay refutation degree** = `solv.deg^s` (standard Macaulay solving degree, no mutants) in Gaggero–Gorla's notation.
* The group's **MutantXL degree-fall closure degree** = `sd` of Caminata–Gorla (Def. 1.1 of JSC 2023 explicitly re-multiplies degree-fallen rows), = `solv.deg^m`.
* Magma's F4 "maximal step degree" / "degree of regularity D_reg" reported in *all* the experimental ECDLP papers (Petit–Quisquater, Shantz–Teske, HPST, Galbraith–Gebregiyorgis, Semaev, Karabina, Kousidis–Wiemers) is an F4 step degree, i.e. a mutant-style quantity, **not** a plain-XL degree. No paper I found measures the plain-XL (`solv.deg^s`) refutation degree on these systems. The group's ⌈l/2⌉+4 and l+4 growth laws are therefore not contradicted by, nor comparable to, the literature's "D_reg = 4 flat" reports.

## (b) Techniques not in the group's covered list

(The covered list: FGHR S_m symmetrisation; 2-torsion symmetrisation (x ↦ 1/x on Koblitz; Edwards Y-line); 4-torsion/Klein; j=0 Z/3 grading; hybrid guessing; MutantXL closure; Crossbred; SAT/WDSat/CMS/Kissat/SMT; Riemann–Roch/Nagao; F4 with sparse structured elimination; F5; Frobenius-equivariant F4 (scoped); F4Remake (identified).)

1. **S_3-chain / auxiliary-point splitting with F_{2^n}-valued intermediates (Semaev 2015 system (4); Karabina 2015).** Replaces the single S_{m+1} by m−1 (or t−1 < m−1) cubic Boolean blocks S_3(x_i, u_{i−1}, u_i) with (m−2)n extra Boolean variables for the intermediate x-coordinates u_i (unconstrained in F_{2^n}). Why it might matter: it is the only encoding for which the literature reports a Magma step degree of **4 flat for m = 3..6** up to n = 21–26 (k = ⌈n/m⌉), and Semaev's own data show the t<m variant 200× faster than the full S_{m+1} at n=15, m=4. The group's XL-degree growth (⌈l/2⌉+4, l+4) is for the single-polynomial encoding; whether the chain encoding's *plain-XL* refutation degree also grows in l is unmeasured anywhere (Semaev/Karabina/Kosters–Yeo only report F4 step degrees, which correspond to the group's MutantXL closure). Experiment: build the chain system for the group's (l, m, n) grid, measure plain-XL and MutantXL refutation degrees vs l; compare Macaulay sizes at the refutation degree with the single-polynomial encoding (the chain has ~ (m−2)n more variables, which Semaev prices at n^{4ω}); test also with the trace relation added (Kosters–Yeo Remark 4.8) and with the intermediates restricted to a subspace (Karabina's S_4 variant).

2. **Symmetry-broken (coset) factor bases (Galbraith–Gebregiyorgis Exp. 3).** P_i ∈ V + v_i with disjoint cosets; relation probability ×m!, factor base ×m, net ×(m−1)!; invariant variables kept with fixed high bits. Measured d_reg = 3 instead of 4 for many (n ≥ 37, l = 5–7, m = 3) instances, with large speed-ups at large n but losses at some mid n. Why it might matter: it is the one published system change for which the F4 degree *dropped* at large n; it also changes the Δ regime. Experiment: at m = 3, 4 sweep l with coset factor bases; measure the plain-XL refutation degree and the MutantXL degree; test whether the degree-3 behaviour is a Δ effect (compare at equal Δ = n − m·l) or a genuine symmetry effect; combine with Trimoska's m!-ordering symmetry breaking in SAT.

3. **Δ-overdetermination sweep (Shantz–Teske "delta method", Joux–Vitse (n−1) trick, Semaev's "d_F4 > 4 when k > ⌈n/m⌉", Semaev–Tenti / Gaggero–Gorla thresholds).** All the "flat degree 4" literature data are at **large Δ** (GG: n = 37–97 with l ≤ 8 ⇒ Δ = 16–76; HPST: n up to 53, m·n′ ≤ 18), whereas the group's n ≤ 15 data have Δ ≈ 0. Semaev states that the degree exceeds 4 as soon as the system is under-determined. Experiment: fix (m, l), sweep n upward (Δ = 0 … 3l) and fit refutation degree vs Δ; compare with the generic prediction (Gaggero–Gorla Prop. 32: degree collapses to D once #equations > dim(R/(x_i²))_D, i.e. > C(N, D) for N = m·l Boolean variables) and with Semaev–Tenti's "sufficiently overdetermined" regime. If the degree is a function of Δ rather than of l, the ⌈l/2⌉+4 law is an artefact of Δ ≈ 0.

4. **Guess-count calibrated by the overdetermination theorems (principled hybrid).** The group found hybrid guessing "mostly degenerate" and that one guess restores degree 4 at l = 7. Semaev–Tenti/Gaggero–Gorla give the number of equations needed for degree D to be generic-minimal; for n cubic equations in N = m·l variables, guessing g variables gives 2^g systems in N − g variables, and the threshold n > C(N−g, D) predicts the g at which the plain-XL degree drops to D (and MutantXL to ≤ D+1 by Salizzoni). Experiment: measure refutation degree vs g at fixed (n, l, m) and compare with the threshold curve; this converts "degenerate hybrid" into a quantitative statement of how far the systems are from generic.

5. **Degree-of-regularity "smoothing" (Jaques–Ran–Samardjiska–Seitner 2026).** Use Macaulay submatrices (restricting multiplier monomials) so XL works at a fractional degree between D_ref − 1 and D_ref. Constant factor only, but at the group's refutation degrees (7–9) the C(N, D) jump between consecutive degrees is large. Experiment: at (n, l, m) where plain XL refutes at D_ref, test whether a submatrix with a subset of degree-(D_ref−3) multipliers already refutes; the paper's generalised semi-regularity assumption is checkable on these structured systems.

6. **Faugère–Svartz G-graded F4 as an alternative to invariant variables** — relevant only where the invariant ring is not polynomial (FHJRV's G_4 full-2-torsion case needs rational invariants w_0, w_1, odd characteristic only; over binary ordinary curves E[2] ≅ Z/2 so this case does not arise). For the Frobenius: the Weil-descent ideal of S_{m+1}(x_1..x_m, x_R) is Frobenius-stable only if V is Frobenius-stable **and** x_R is fixed by Frobenius, which fails for every random R; so no single system carries a Z/n action — consistent with the group's rank-only scoping finding nothing. Frobenius-stable V of dimension ≈ n/m exist only for primes n with small ord_n(2) (n = 31: dims 5k; n = 73: 9k; n = 127: 7k), and the useful consequence is the Galbraith–Granger–Merz–Petit "n relations per decomposition / n² linear-algebra" gain, not a degree change. No experiment on the degree is warranted; a relation-count experiment on a Koblitz curve over F_{2^31} with a Frobenius-stable V of dimension 10, m = 3, would quantify the constant.

7. **F4Remake — do not implement for Boolean systems.** Joux–Vitse's own analysis: success probability ≈ c(q)^{nstep} with c(2) ≈ 0.29; the trace of one F_2 system does not transfer to another. Measured gains (2–3.4× over F4) were over F_p with p ≥ 2^8. The idea survives only as "reuse the *shape* of the Macaulay/XL matrix (which rows/columns are present) across R", which is deterministic for the group's XL and presumably already done.

8. **Trace-kernel restriction and adding the trace relation explicitly (Kosters–Yeo Prop. 4.2/Remark 4.8).** The trace morphism E(F_{2^n}) → F_2 with kernel 2E(F_{2^n}) means decompositions must satisfy Σ Tr((x_i + a_2)/a_1²) = Tr((x_R + a_2)/a_1²); for S_3 this is in the ideal (hence the first fall), for m ≥ 3 it is a *consequence on solutions* that may not be in the degree-≤D ideal. The group already sees the fall at degree 3 ("Kosters–Yeo trace equation"); the cheap experiment is to add the linear trace equation (and its multiples) explicitly to the XL matrix at degree 1 and check whether the plain-XL refutation degree moves — i.e., whether part of the ⌈l/2⌉+4 growth is XL re-deriving a relation that is linear on solutions but of high degree in the ideal.

9. **Quasi-subfield factor bases (HKPYY 2020 / Euler–Petit 2021)** — published outcome negative ("no speedup"), but the construction changes the Weil-descent system (fewer variables for the same factor-base size when λ′ has small degree). Only worth a one-off check at an n where a quasi-subfield polynomial with deg λ′ ≤ 2 exists.

10. **Yokoyama et al.'s Euclidean-algorithm view of the PDP ideal.** For the naive prime-field method they prove the GB computation is an extension of the extended Euclidean algorithm and derive a *lower bound*. Whether the same structural view applies to the symmetrised Weil-descent ideal is open; a lower-bound result of this type is exactly what would settle "flat vs growing".

## (c) Theory on solving-degree growth for Weil-descent systems (as functions of n, l, m)

### Definitions and the identities that connect the group's measurements to the literature

* **First fall degree D_ff** (PQ 2012 / HPS 2014 definition): smallest degree with a non-trivial degree fall. **Last fall degree d_lf** (HKY15): smallest d such that every element of the ideal is reachable by degree-≤d combinations ("V_d closure"); order-independent. **Degree of regularity d_reg** (Bardet–Faugère–Salvy): smallest d with (F^top)_d = R_d. **Solving degree sd**: Caminata–Gorla JSC 2023 Def. 1.1 = Macaulay matrix at degree d with re-multiplication of degree-fallen rows until stable (this *is* MutantXL closure) = `solv.deg^m`; the no-mutant Macaulay/XL degree is `solv.deg^s` (Gaggero–Gorla notation). Trivially solv.deg^s ≥ solv.deg^m ≥ d_lf.
* **Proved relations** (degree-compatible orders): sd = max{d_lf, max degree of the reduced Gröbner basis} (Caminata–Gorla JSC 2023, Thm 3.1); sd ≥ d_lf (GMP22); sd ≤ d_reg + 1 (Salizzoni 2023, optimal); plain-XL degree ≤ 2·d_reg − 2 when d_reg ≥ max(q, deg F) (Semaev–Tenti 2021); d_reg ≤ reg(F^h) (Caminata–Gorla); sd ≤ Σ(d_i − 1) + 2 (Salizzoni Cor. 3.12); for overdetermined systems with max degree d: solv.deg ≤ (N+1)(d−1)+1 (Lazard/Caminata–Gorla).
* **Consequence for the group's systems (with field equations).** For a refutation (UNSAT) instance the reduced GB is {1}; for a SAT instance with a unique F_2-solution it is linear; in both cases max GB degree ≤ 1, so **MutantXL closure degree = sd = d_lf** (exactly), and plain-XL degree ∈ [d_lf, 2·d_reg − 2] with d_lf ≤ d_reg + 1.

### Upper bounds

1. **First fall degree**: D_ff ≤ m·t + 1 for a degree-(2^t − 1)-per-variable polynomial (PQ Prop. 1) ⇒ ≤ m² + 1 for S_{m+1}; improved to **D_ff ≤ m² − m + 1** for S_{m+1}, n′ ≥ m ≥ 3 (Kousidis–Wiemers Thm 3.2, proved; observed sharp: 7 for m=3, 13 for m=4 in Magma); D_ff = 2 for S_3 (Kosters–Yeo, proved via the trace). These are n- and l-independent. (The group's "D_ff = 3 everywhere" is a different, trace-based fall for the symmetrised/Koblitz systems; it is not in the literature for m ≥ 3 and would be worth writing up.)
2. **Last fall degree, rigorous and n-independent** (HKYY 2018 Thm 1.1): for F ⊂ F_{q^n}[X_0..X_{m−1}] zero-dimensional radical with ≤ s zeros (and a coordinate projecting injectively), d_lf(Weil descent + field eqs) ≤ max(τ(max(d_F, deg F, (m+1)s), q, m), m·τ(2s, q, 1), q) with τ(r,c,t) = ⌊2t(c−1)(log_c(r/2t)+1)⌋. HKYY explicitly say this does *not* apply to the single summation polynomial without field equations. **Derived here (not in any paper; check hypotheses before relying on it):** impose the factor-base constraint upstairs by the F_2-linearised polynomial L_V(x) of degree 2^l vanishing exactly on V, i.e. F = {S_{m+1}(x_1..x_m, x_R), L_V(x_1), …, L_V(x_m)} ⊂ F_{2^n}[x_1..x_m]. This F is zero-dimensional with s ≤ 2^{m·l} zeros, and its Weil descent is the group's system (the descent of L_V is linear and just parametrises x_i ∈ V). Plugging q = 2, s = 2^{ml} gives **d_lf ≲ 2m(m·l + log_2((m+1)/2m) + 1) ≈ 2m²·l + O(m)**, independent of n (the d_F and deg F terms enter only through a logarithm and are dominated by the s term). Hence, modulo the radicality/injective-projection hypotheses, the MutantXL closure (= d_lf) refutation degree is **at most linear in l, quadratic in m, and n-free**. For m = 3, l = 5 this is ≈ 90+, far above the measured 4–7, so the bound is loose, but it is the only rigorous statement of the *shape* of the growth and it is consistent with the group's measured linear-in-l plain-XL laws (slopes 1/2 and 1) via plain-XL ≤ 2·d_reg − 2 and d_lf ≤ d_reg + 1.
3. **HFE-type sharpening** (GMP22): for a single univariate polynomial of degree ≤ q^t, d_lf ≤ (q−1)(t+1)+1 — i.e. l + 2 over F_2 for degree 2^l, halving HKYY's constant; no multivariate analogue is published, so for the m-variable system the factor 2m in item 2 stands.
4. **Degree of regularity / generic behaviour**: for N = m·l Boolean variables and n equations of degree 3 with field equations, Gaggero–Gorla Prop. 32: if n > C(N, D) (dim of degree-D part of F_2[x]/(x_i²)) then generically d_reg = D, solv.deg^s ≤ 2D − 2, solv.deg^m ≤ D + 1. With n ≈ N (square systems) this threshold is never met for D ≥ 2, so generic theory predicts **growth** of the plain-XL degree with N unless the system is far from generic (which it is: measured degrees are far below semi-regular predictions — PQ 2012 already noted D ≈ mt+1 ≪ semi-regular).

### Lower bounds

* **None published for Weil-descent summation systems.** The only rigorous lower bound on GB cost in this area is Yokoyama–Yasuda–Takahashi–Kogure (JMC 2020) for Semaev's *naive* prime-field method (PDP ideal as an extended-Euclid computation ⇒ cannot beat generic algorithms).
* Indirect evidence against flatness: Kosters–Yeo's NP-completeness of deciding S_r = 0 (odd p; conditional in general); their observation that a flat degree for the S_3-chain would give a polynomial-time algorithm for that decision problem; HPST 2015 (ePrint 2015/358) "very unexpected consequences" if the first-fall assumption held for some of their systems.

### Does any published result predict a degree flat in l (or n)?

* **Heuristic predictions of flatness** (all contradicted or unsupported beyond small parameters): PQ 2012 Assumption 1/2 (D_reg = D_ff + o(D_ff) ≈ m² + 1, n- and l-free) ⇒ crossover with rho at n ≈ 2 000 (KW's sharper m² − m + 1 ⇒ n ≈ 1 250); Semaev 2015 Assumption 1 (d_F4 ≤ 4 for the S_3-chain, all n, m) ⇒ 2^{1.69√(n ln n)}; Karabina 2015 (D_reg ≈ 4 for the m = 5 auxiliary system, extrapolated from n ≤ 19). **Measured refutations**: Kosters–Yeo (S_3, l = ⌈n/2⌉): step degree 3 (l ≤ 8), 4 (9 ≤ l ≤ 18), 5 (l ≥ 19, up to l = 24 in Kousidis–Wiemers); Semaev's own remark that the degree exceeds 4 when under-determined. Whether this m = 2 growth is linear (slope ≈ 1/10) or logarithmic in l cannot be told from the data; the group's m = 3, 4 fits (slope 1/2, 1) rest on l ≤ 5 and likewise cannot distinguish linear from logarithmic growth.
* **Measured flatness at larger l** (Magma step degree = MutantXL-type quantity, large Δ): Galbraith–Gebregiyorgis: m = 3, l ≤ 8, n ≤ 97: d_reg ≤ 4 (often 3 with coset factor bases); m = 4, l ≤ 4: d_reg = 4; HPST: m = 3, n′ ≤ 6, n ≤ 53: D_reg 3–4. These agree with the group's "MutantXL refutes at 4 flat for m·l ≤ 20" and the rise past 4 at l = 7 (m = 3) is in the Δ ≈ 0 regime that none of these papers sampled.
* **Rigorous results**: none predicts flatness. The rigorous bounds that exist are n-independent but **grow linearly in l** (HKYY-derived, item 2) or are generic/semi-regular (growing in N). A proof of a flat-in-l last fall degree would, via Caminata–Gorla, give a flat MutantXL degree and hence a polynomial-in-l relation search — the Semaev/Karabina/PQ sub-exponential claims — which Kosters–Yeo argue is implausible. The published state of the art is therefore: **flat in n is plausible and partly proved (HKYY, for the mutant-type degree); flat in l is a heuristic with contrary data at m = 2 and no supporting theorem.**
