# Factor-base constructions for elliptic-curve index calculus (summation polynomials / PDP / Weil descent), 2004–2026

Literature survey, 2026-10-24. Web research only. Everything read on the web was treated as data.

Notation: n = extension degree, ℓ (or n′, l, k) = F_2- or F_q-dimension of the subspace defining the base, m = number of factor-base points per decomposition (so the (m+1)-th summation polynomial S_{m+1} is used), D_reg = largest step degree reported by Magma F4 unless stated otherwise. "Measured" = reported from an actual run; "claimed/extrapolated" = asymptotic or extrapolated.

Source-access caveat: eprint.iacr.org PDFs (and their mirrors at research.birmingham.ac.uk, core.ac.uk, HAL, iacr.org/archive) are behind an interactive Cloudflare/Anubis bot check, which I did not attempt to pass. For the papers whose only copy is there (Shantz–Teske 2013/596, Galbraith–Granger–Merz–Petit 2020/1315, McGuire–Mueller 2017/1262, Semaev–Tenti 2019/903, Huang–Petit–Shinohara–Takagi 2015/358, Courtois 2016/003 & 2016/704, Nagao 2010/511, 2013/549, 2015/984, Sarkar–Singh 2015/179) the entries below are built from the eprint abstract pages (readable), talk abstracts/slides, and the descriptions given in other papers (Karabina 2015, Gebregiyorgis thesis 2016, Tsakou–Ionica 2021, Trimoska thesis 2021, Hu 2024). Such entries are marked **[abstract-level]**. All other entries were read from full text (author/arXiv/repository copies).

## (b) Not in the group's covered list

1. **Position-dependent / disjoint factor bases (GG14 affine cosets F_i = V + v_i; Gebregiyorgis coordinate blocks; Nagao's "disjoint factor base"; Diem II's mixed-dimension pair (U, U′)).** The group's list has no construction in which the i-th summand is drawn from a *different* set. GG14's version (Table 3.3 of the thesis) is the only one with measurements: for m = 3, ℓ ∈ {5,6,7}, n = 37…97, the degree of regularity frequently stayed at 3 while the symmetric base gave 4, and the relation probability loses the 1/m! factor (net (m−1)! after paying m× more relations and m² in linear algebra). Diem II's variant is the theoretically cleanest: dimensions d and d−1 are mixed so that the F_q-condition count equals n exactly, which is what his e^{O(log(q^n)^{3/4})} bound needs. Nagao (2015/984) claims the same device makes the Semaev split system succeed with probability ≈ 1 at polynomial cost under the FFD assumption (claim only). Since the group already has the symmetrised S_3-chain machinery, trying V + v_i cosets or coordinate blocks is cheap and directly tests the "D_reg stays 3" observation.

2. **Random-point ("dynamic") factor base with a single relation (Amadori–Pintore–Sala 2018).** The factor base is not a structured subset at all: it is the set of ≈ p^{1/m} random points R_i = u_iP + v_iQ themselves, and one solves S_m(X_1..X_m) = 0 with each X_i constrained to the degree-s univariate Π(X_i − x(R_j)). One solution gives a relation among known combinations of P, Q, so no linear algebra is needed and only one Gröbner basis has to succeed. It is the only prime-field construction with full end-to-end DLP timings (p up to 22 bits, m = 3,4,5: 268–388 s). Its complexity model (one GB of a system of degree-s univariates plus S_m) is different from everything on the group's list; Kudo et al. (CANS 2018) report a hybrid-method speed-up and a complexity "limitation" of this very variant.

3. **Frobenius-invariant bases on subfield (Koblitz) curves (Galbraith–Granger–Merz–Petit, SAC 2020) and endomorphism-trace/norm bases (Tsakou–Ionica 2021).** The group covers Frobenius-invariant *kernels* only for composite n and Hamming-weight-in-normal-basis sets; GGMP instead exploit that on a Koblitz curve the Frobenius *endomorphism* acts on points, so a Frobenius-stable base needs only one system per orbit (reduction up to 1/n in the number of PDP instances, n² in linear algebra) and gives a second source of symmetry breaking. Tsakou–Ionica give a general recipe for any φ with φ^k = ±1: replace x by Tr_φ(x) or N_φ(x) and impose "μ ∈ F_q" through n−1 linear conditions in a normal basis, keeping S_{n+1,x} (the summation polynomial of μ itself has degree q^{n−1}). GGMP's full text (with the experimental comparison of several Frobenius-stable bases) was not readable; this is the single most useful blocked document.

4. **Trace-zero hyperplane-section base (Gorla–Massierer 2015).** For the DLP in T_n ≅ E(F_{q^n})/E(F_q) (n = 3, 5) the base is the set of F_q-points of the curve V_n ∩ {x_0 = … = x_{n−3} = 0} in the Weil restriction coordinates, i.e. a *geometric* rather than subfield/subspace condition, with |F| ≈ q and (n−1)-fold decompositions. They solved a full 36-bit T_3 DLP (957 s) and measured that a T_5 decomposition system costs 1.3 s. It is an extension-field base not on the group's list and the paper gives the only published D_reg-style bound ((2n−2)(n−1)2^{n−2} − 2n + 3) for this geometry.

5. **Binary-Edwards t = x + y with the order-4 translation and D_m-invariants (GG14).** The group's binary list has the 2-torsion frame u = 1/(x+1) (Weierstrass) and the FGHR Edwards fold for extension fields, but not the prime-degree binary Edwards construction in which V must satisfy v ∈ V ⇔ v+1 ∈ V so that the order-4 point acts by t ↦ t+1, and S_{m+1} is rewritten in e_1, s_2, …, s_m. This is what made m = 4 feasible at all in 2014 (D_reg 4 vs 5 and T_GB 11.6 s vs ≈1000 s at n = 53, ℓ = 3/4).

6. **Kosters–Yeo trace morphism E(k) → F_2.** Not a base by itself, but a structural fact the group may not be using: for ordinary binary curves there is a surjective homomorphism to F_2 whose Weil-descent image is linear, so every S_3-derived system contains a degree-1 combination (first fall degree 2) and the kernel is an index-2 subgroup that can be used as an extra membership condition or to halve the base. The same paper documents that the specific "deg < k" window of Semaev behaves better than a random subspace (n = 40 D_reg 4 vs. growth), which is a measured datum on base choice.

7. **Joux–Vitse proposal: m < n points with x in an F_q-subspace of dimension d > 1 (extension fields).** Only S_m-invariance survives (not S_{md}), and their Magma experiments with invariant rings failed; they suggest SAGBI-Gröbner bases. This is the extension-field analogue of the binary ℓ-window and is, to my knowledge, still untested in the literature.

8. **Sieving (item 10).** There is no published sieve for the summation-polynomial PDP on an elliptic curve itself. All sieves (Joux–Vitse 2012, Sarkar–Singh 2014/15, Vitse–Wallet 2015) act on Jacobians where the relation search is a 1-parameter family of functions f_λ and membership is a univariate splitting test; Sarkar–Singh 2015/179 only avoid the multivariate system for special hyperelliptic curves over F_{q^2}. If the group wants a sieve, the route in the literature is via a GHS/cover to a Jacobian over a quadratic extension, not via summation polynomials.
