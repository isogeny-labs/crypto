# Irreducible Frobenius factor: a natural-quotient bound

Status: **derived conditional theorem**. The argument uses the standard
Artin--Schreier character description of the Hess cover; an independent
function-field verification of that identification is pending. The
literature review in `PROTOCOL.md` found the underlying GHS/Hess families,
not this exact automorphism criterion. This is not a claim of publication
priority or a newly vulnerable family.

Let `n` be odd, `k=F_q` with `q` a power of two, `K=F_(q^n)`, and
`sigma(z)=z^q`. Let `p in F_2[T]` be an irreducible factor of
`(T^n+1)/(T+1)` of degree `s>=2`, and put `V_p=ker p(sigma)`.
Consider generalized GHS presentations with exact Frobenius orders

    Ord_sigma(gamma1)=p,
    Ord_sigma(gamma2)=(T+1)p,
    b=(gamma1*gamma2)^2.

The Hess genus formula gives a cover of genus `2^(s+1)-2`. Its natural
quotient by the constant-character deck involution has AS character
space generated by the orbit of
`((sigma+1)gamma1,(sigma+1)gamma2)`. Under the standard construction,
this quotient has genus `g=2^s-1`.

**Necessary condition.** If this natural quotient is geometrically
hyperelliptic, then `b in V_p`. Thus nonclassical coefficients outside
`V_p` are excluded from this natural hyperelliptic-quotient route.

**Proof.** The character space `U` is an irreducible rank-`s`
`F_2[sigma]` module because both transformed gammas have exact order p.
Both coordinate projections are injective, so every nonzero character
has equation `y^2+y=u/x+v*x` with simple poles at zero and infinity.
The conductor-discriminant formula yields

    2g(D)-2 = -2*2^s + 4*(2^s-1), hence g(D)=2^s-1.

Every nonidentity deck involution has quotient genus `2^(s-1)-1`,
which is positive for `s>=2`. The unique hyperelliptic involution,
if one exists, commutes with the deck group and therefore induces a
nonidentity involution of the x-line. It must swap zero and infinity;
in characteristic two it has form `x -> lambda/x` with `lambda in k*`.
Its action on `U` is `(u,v)->(lambda*v,u/lambda)`. This commutes with
sigma and has order two. Schur's lemma identifies the endomorphism ring
of the irreducible module with `F_(2^s)`, whose multiplicative group has
odd order. The action is therefore identity. Hence
`gamma2=lambda^(-1)*gamma1+d` with `d in k*`, so
`sqrt(b)=lambda^(-1)*gamma1^2+d*gamma1 in V_p` and `b in V_p`.

The descent assertions used here can be made explicit. Over an algebraic
closure, adjoining the AS equations for the sigma-conjugates of
`gamma1/x+a+gamma2*x` gives the standard Hess character module. Applying
`sigma+1` to its generators produces the graph U; its annihilator is a
one-dimensional deck subgroup invariant under sigma, so its quotient D
is defined over k. The parameter x belongs to the original rational
base `k(x)`. The hyperelliptic involution is unique for genus at least
two, hence Galois invariant and defined over k. Therefore its descended
Möbius map and lambda are k-rational. A characteristic-two Möbius
involution fixing both poles individually would be `x->mu*x` with
`mu^2=1`, hence the identity; the nonidentity map must swap them.

**Sharp coefficient-locus count.** Conversely, for each nonzero
`b in V_p`, the map `gamma -> gamma^2+gamma` is an F2-linear
automorphism of Vp: its only possible kernel elements are 0 and 1,
and `p(1)=1` excludes 1. Choose its unique preimage of `sqrt(b)`.
Then `gamma1=gamma`, `gamma2=gamma+1` gives a W1 presentation.
The inversion `x -> 1/x` fixes all AS character equations, and its
invariant field has seven (more generally `2^s-1`) characters with
only one simple pole. Its genus is zero by the same conductor formula.
Thus **there exists** a hyperelliptic natural-quotient presentation for
every nonzero `b in V_p`. This does not assert that every presentation
of the same b has a hyperelliptic quotient.

By the normal basis theorem, `dim_k V_p=s`, so the coefficient locus
has exactly `q^s-1` nonzero elements for this p, density
`(q^s-1)/(q^n-1)` among nonzero b. If `t_s` distinct irreducible
factors of degree s occur in `T^n+1`, their kernels intersect only at
zero and the union has exactly `t_s*(q^s-1)` coefficients. For n=7,
s=3 and `t_s=2`, this recovers `2(q^3-1)`.

These formulas count the natural hyperelliptic-quotient coefficient
locus, not the whole set of vulnerable curves. The transfer map on a
chosen source subgroup and the complete attack cost must be checked
separately. The finite F8 Lean lemma in `NaturalQuotient.lean` supports
only the n=7 endomorphism step; it does not formalize this general
theorem or the curve geometry.

## A bound applying to every transfer target

For any curve C/k of genus g, if a homomorphism from a prime-order
source subgroup of order r has nonzero image in `J_C(k)`, that image has
order r. The Weil bound on Frobenius eigenvalues gives

    r <= #J_C(k) <= (sqrt(q)+1)^(2g).

Thus every subgroup-preserving transfer target, regardless of its
construction, satisfies

    g >= ceil(log(r) / (2*log(sqrt(q)+1))).

This is a necessary genus bound, not a fast-DLP criterion. It can be
combined with the quotient coefficient bound above, but cannot replace
a map certificate or an end-to-end attack cost.
