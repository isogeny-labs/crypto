# Item 05 protocol: monomial, variable and block orders tuned to the descent structure

Frozen before any measured run. Source design: section "05" of
`research/blindspots_20261018/EXPERIMENTS.md` and its "Rules every item
follows". Written by the experiment-director lane, 2026-10-18.

Class: **stage diagnostic** (a solving degree, matrix shapes and a count of
matrix entries touched, for one oracle under different monomial orders). It
computes no `S`, no rho ratio and no GAE. It is not a cross-method comparison
(every arm is the same F4 with a different order), so the section 5a
three-point pricing band does not apply to its own rows; an arm that wins is
reported as a multiplicative factor on the oracle term of item 02's model,
and item 02's band (0, 0.1, 1 GAE per touch) prices it there. The gap to
matched rho is therefore not changed by this item and is reported as the
unchanged distance remaining.

## Question

Does a structure-aware order lower the solving degree `D` or the Macaulay
work of the descended chained Semaev `S_3` systems relative to grevlex?

Cell notation is `(n, l, m)`: field degree `n`, factor-base subspace
dimension `l`, `m` summands. Unknowns `N = m*l + (m-2)*n`: `m*l` leaf bits
(the bit coordinates of the `m` subspace elements `x_1 .. x_m`) followed by
`(m-2)*n` intermediate bits (the `m-2` chain points `u_i`). Equations are the
`n` bit coordinates of each of the `m-1` links `S_3(x_1,x_2,u_1)`,
`S_3(u_i,x_{i+2},u_{i+1})`, `S_3(u_{m-2},x_m,x_R)`; at `m = 2` the single link
is `S_3(x_1,x_2,x_R)` and `N = 2l`.

## What already exists, and what is reused (read before designing)

* **The incumbent's `D` on these systems is order-free, so it cannot move.**
  The committed solving-degree readings are MutantXL closure degrees
  (`research/dreg_closure_*_20261016/RESULTS.md`, 84 small draws in
  `07-blocked-f4-degree-cells/controls.tsv`) and msolve F4 readings
  (`research/ic_next10_20261014/06-equivariant-f4-m4/README.md`). The closure
  `V_D` is a subspace of polynomials of degree `<= D` closed under
  multiplication by variables; it does not depend on a monomial order at all.
  An order can therefore change `D` only in a Groebner engine whose pair
  selection follows the order. That is why this item needs its own engine
  (arm design below) and does not re-use the closure code.
* **msolve is grevlex-only in the committed readings.** Nothing in the
  repository measures `D`, matrix size or touches for a non-grevlex order on
  these systems. `research/chain_split_order_20260924/` varies the split
  variable of the DPLL decomposer (a different object: the branching rule of
  the inherited-F4 tree, 1.0x to 33x on its rungs), and
  `src/cryptanalysis/descent_treewidth.rs` computes the primal graph and an
  exact treewidth for `<= 18` vertices as a spectral-refutation follow-up
  (`research/notes/index-calculus/RESEARCH_FFD_WORKFLOW.md`); neither
  measures an elimination or monomial order inside a Groebner computation.
  No arm and no cell of this item is already measured; nothing is skipped.
* **Reused as the positive control, not re-measured:** the 84 committed
  unsatisfiable small draws of `07-blocked-f4-degree-cells/controls.tsv`
  (closure degrees; 32 of them with an msolve reading). The new engine under
  grevlex must reproduce their degrees (Controls).
* **Not reusable:** the draws of the six cells. They do not exist in the
  repository and are generated by the instrument (`gen`), committed in
  `draws.tsv`, and bound into every card by sha256.

## The instrument (one engine, the order is the only thing that varies)

`examples/blindspots_05_elimination_orders.rs`, native Rust, standard library
only, compiled by each card with `rustc --edition 2021 -C opt-level=3
-C target-cpu=native` (Deviation 2). It re-implements the field arithmetic, the
symbolic `S_3` chain system and the exact solution counters of the item 07
instrument (the same sparse irreducible, the same bit layout: leaf bits
`0 .. m*l-1`, then the intermediates) and adds a Boolean-ring F4.

* **Ring and monomials.** `B = F_2[x]/(x_i^2 + x_i)`; monomials are squarefree
  masks, so `lcm` is the union and a product collapses repeated variables.
* **Order.** A monomial order is a weighted degree (positive integer weight per
  variable), then grevlex under a variable ranking (rank 0 is the largest
  variable), optionally as a **product (block) order**: the leading block
  decides first. One integer key per monomial makes every comparison a single
  integer compare; the selftest checks that the identity order equals the
  committed DegRevLex convention (smaller mask larger among equal degree) on
  20,000 random pairs and that every order used is multiplicative with `1`
  minimal.
* **Pairs.** The ordinary `S`-pairs of two basis elements (multipliers
  `lcm \ LM`) and, for every `x` in `LM(g)`, the pair of `g` with the field
  equation `x^2 + x`, whose `S`-polynomial is `x*g` computed in `B`. The pair
  set is thinned by the Gebauer-Moeller product and chain criteria (the
  Becker-Weispfenning update); pairs with a field equation are never thinned.
* **Selection.** All pairs of least **degree in the order's own sense** are
  taken in one matrix (the normal strategy): the weighted degree of the `lcm`
  for a weighted order, the pair (weighted degree of block 1, of block 2) for a
  block order, the total degree for grevlex. The field-equation pair of `g` has
  the degree of `LM(g) * x` in the polynomial ring. Nothing else depends on the
  order: not the divisor choice (fewest terms, then earliest), not the
  elimination kernel, not the update.
* **Matrix.** Symbolic preprocessing adds one reducer `t*g` for every monomial
  of the matrix divisible by a leading monomial (the divisor with the fewest
  terms). Columns are the monomials in the order; reducers are sparse and kept
  unreduced; each `S`-row is accumulated densely, reduced left to right (every
  pivot hit is XOR-ed in, tail columns included), and a row that keeps a
  non-pivot column becomes a new pivot; new pivots are then back-substituted
  against each other so every new element is fully tail-reduced. New elements
  whose leading monomial is `1` end the run: `1 in I`.
* **Counted.** `touches` = matrix entries written while building rows (the `S`
  rows and the reducers) + entries toggled by every row operation of the
  elimination + entries toggled by the back-substitution. This is the repo's
  "monomial-column touch" at the granularity of one entry; it depends on the
  elimination kernel, so the kernel-free quantities are reported beside it:
  the number of steps, the rows and columns of every matrix (largest
  rows x columns, sum of columns) and the degrees.
* **Degrees.** `D_mat` = largest total degree of any monomial in any matrix
  (the quantity that sets the width of the Macaulay matrix, comparable to the
  closure degree); `D_sel` = largest total degree of a selected `lcm` in the
  polynomial ring (field-equation pairs count `deg LM + 1`). `D_mat` is the
  solving degree this item's rule uses; for grevlex it equals the committed
  closure degree on the control table below.

## Arms

All arms act on the same system, the same subspace base (one per cell, drawn
once) and the same targets; only the order differs.

| arm | order | definition |
|---|---|---|
| `grevlex` | incumbent | degree, then grevlex; variable order as built (leaf bits, then intermediate bits, variable 0 largest): the committed DegRevLex convention |
| `blkLI` | block, leaves first | block 1 = the `m*l` leaf bits, block 2 = the intermediate bits; each block grevlex in the built order |
| `blkIL` | block, intermediates first | the same two blocks, intermediates decide first |
| `wdeg` | weighted degree, Frobenius-conjugate weights | weight 1 on a leaf bit, weight 2 on an intermediate bit, then grevlex in the built order. The weight of a variable is the number of `S_3` links its field element enters: each chain point `u_i` enters two links (as the output of one and an argument of the next, through both Frobenius conjugates `u_i` and `u_i^2` that `S_3` is built from), each subspace element `x_j` enters one. This is the one weighting the chain structure fixes without a free parameter |
| `mindeg` | variable order (i) | grevlex under the ranking given by the greedy minimum-degree elimination order of the primal graph (ties by lowest index); the variable eliminated first is the largest |
| `tw` | variable order (ii) | the same with the greedy minimum-fill elimination order (ties by smaller degree, then lowest index) |
| `rand0 .. rand7` | negative control | grevlex under a uniformly random variable ranking, eight fixed seeds per cell, the same ranking for every target of the cell |

The primal graph is the one of `src/cryptanalysis/descent_treewidth.rs` (one
vertex per bit variable, a clique on the variables of each equation); the
module's exact treewidth is capped at 18 vertices and these systems have
29 to 50, so the order is a greedy elimination order (Deviation 4); the
selftest checks the greedy widths against the exact subset dynamic program on a
10-variable descended system.

Arms defined only for `m >= 3`: `blkLI`, `blkIL`, `wdeg` have no intermediates
at `m = 2` and are recorded `na` at (31, 15, 2) (Deviation 5).

Descriptive arms outside the rule: `mindeg-rev`, `tw-rev` (the same two
rankings reversed: the variable eliminated first is the smallest). The design
fixes no direction for a variable order, so the convention above is declared
before any measurement and the reverse is reported but cannot satisfy the
rule.

## Controls

* **Instrument soundness (selftest, exact).** On five toy cells
  ((m,n,l) = (3,5,2), (4,4,1), (2,7,3), (3,6,3), (4,5,2); 6 to 18 unknowns) with
  two unsatisfiable and two satisfiable targets each, every arm
  (`grevlex, blkLI, blkIL, wdeg, mindeg, tw, mindeg-rev, tw-rev, rand0..rand3`)
  is run to the end. An unsatisfiable system must end `1 in I`. A satisfiable
  system must end with a finished basis whose **number of standard monomials
  (squarefree monomials divisible by no leading monomial) equals the
  brute-force number of solutions**, which certifies it is a Groebner basis of
  `I + <x_i^2 + x_i>` for that order, and every basis element must vanish on
  every solution. The algebraic solution counter used to select unsatisfiable
  draws must equal brute force on every toy target. Any failure exits 2.
* **Positive control (the design's: the instrument reproduces a known
  reading).** On the 84 committed unsatisfiable draws of
  `07-blocked-f4-degree-cells/controls.tsv` with `N <= 26` unknowns (cells
  from (4,1,4) to (13,4,3)), grevlex must refute every one and `D_mat` must be
  reported beside the committed MutantXL closure degree (all 84 rows) and the
  committed msolve reading (the 32 rows that carry one); the committed
  solution counts must equal the rebuilt counts. The development run of this
  instrument matched the closure degree on 84 of 84 and msolve on 32 of 32;
  the frozen card re-runs it. Failure of the refutation or of a solution count
  exits 2; the table of agreements is printed and judged by the reader.
* **Sensitivity control (can the instrument see an order effect at the rule's
  size?).** Six planted systems, each `x` = 10 leaf-like unknowns, `y` = 8
  definition unknowns (`y_j + q_j(x)`, `q_j` random in `x`) and 14 random
  quadratic relations in `(x, y)`; block order `y >> x` substitutes the
  definitions at once, `x >> y` cannot. Both are run on each system and checked
  against brute force. The instrument passes when the larger touches ratio of
  the two block orders is at least 1.5 (the rule's threshold) on all six
  systems. The development run read 11.2 to 67.6 with `D_mat` 6 or 7 against 9
  or 10. Failure exits 2.
* **Negative control (the right null).** Eight random variable rankings per
  cell (`rand0 .. rand7`, grevlex under a uniformly random permutation, drawn
  once per cell and applied to every target of the cell). The random band of a
  cell is the range of their paired touches ratios against grevlex; it is the
  null against which a structure-aware arm must stand outside (the rule).
  Random rankings are the null for a *variable order*; for a *block* or
  *weighted* order the closest null is still the same band, which is the
  design's choice, and the rule's second condition (strictly above the band)
  is applied identically to every arm.

## Metric, pairing and the decision rule (frozen)

Per (cell, target, arm) the instrument records the status (`refuted`, or
`timelimit`, `memcap`, `colscap` when a cap stopped it), `D_mat`, `D_sel`, the
number of steps, the rows and columns of the largest matrix and their product
`rc_max`, the sum of columns, `touches` (and its build, elimination and
back-substitution parts), zero reductions, the size of the final basis, wall
time and peak resident memory. Wall time is descriptive.

All comparisons are **paired by target** against `grevlex` on the same system.
For an arm `A` and a cell, over the targets on which both finished:

* touches ratio `R_A` = geometric mean over targets of
  `touches(grevlex) / touches(A)` (larger is better for `A`);
* matrix ratio = geometric mean of `rc_max(grevlex) / rc_max(A)`;
* D-drop fraction = the fraction of targets on which
  `D_mat(A) <= D_mat(grevlex) - 1`.

The **random band** of the cell is the same two statistics for `rand0 .. rand7`
computed on the same targets as the arm being judged, so the arm is always
compared with the null on identical systems.

A cell is **countable** for the verdict when `grevlex` refuted at least 4
targets of it and all eight random arms finished the same targets. An arm is
**judged** in a countable cell when it was run on at least 2 of those targets
(Deviation 7: the expensive `blkLI` is run on 2 targets per cell) and finished
(`refuted`) on all the targets it was run on. An arm that hit a cap on a
target on which `grevlex` finished counts as "worse" in that cell.

* **ADVANCES (constant)**: an arm of the rule family (`blkLI`, `blkIL`,
  `wdeg`, `mindeg`, `tw`) has `R_A >= 1.5` **and** `R_A` above the largest
  random-band ratio, in at least **4 of the 6 cells**, and on those cells the
  matrix ratio is at least `1/1.5` (its largest matrix is not more than 1.5x
  larger than grevlex's). Fewer than 4 qualifying cells, or a larger matrix on
  them, is not ADVANCES.
* **ADVANCES (primitive)**: an arm lowers `D_mat` by at least 1 on at least 75%
  of the targets of a cell, strictly more often than any random draw does, in
  at least 4 of the 6 cells.
* **TRADES**: an arm meets the constant criterion on touches in at least 4
  cells but its largest matrix is more than 1.5x larger on them.
* **REGRESSES**: not ADVANCES or TRADES, and every arm of the family is at
  least 1.5x worse than grevlex (ratio `<= 1/1.5` or capped) in at least 4
  countable cells, so grevlex remains the best of the family.
* **MATCHES**: countable cells at least 4 and none of the above.
* **INCONCLUSIVE**: fewer than 4 countable cells, or a control failed.
* **BLOCKED**: a cell the instrument cannot run at the host's limits is named
  with the cap that stopped it and counts as not countable.

The level is stated with the verdict (exponent, constant or primitive). A win
is a ratio on one oracle's touches; its conversion into group operations is
item 02's `K` (unmeasured) and is not made here. The reverse arms
(`mindeg-rev`, `tw-rev`) and any arm outside the family are printed and are not
eligible. The same rule is applied by `b05 summarize` (mechanically, over the
recorded JSON lines); the summary reads the numbers, it does not interpret
them.

  card (build and clean-up included) they come to **6,630 s, 1.84

  cells can be *countable* (at least 4 grevlex-refuted targets, the 8 random
  arms on the same targets). `(17,4,4)` and `(31,15,2)` are recorded as
  measured, with their statuses and caps, and are **BLOCKED for the rule**
  by the budget and the instrument's speed: a countable `(17,4,4)` needs
  14 orders x 4 targets, each at least 150 s on development draws. So the
  "at least 4 of 6 cells" of ADVANCES can be met only if **all four** full
  cells qualify. This does not loosen the rule; it is the rule applied to the
  cells the budget allows. What would unblock the two cells: a faster
  elimination kernel (the current one toggles about 0.5 x 10^9 entries per
  second and the matrices at 50 unknowns need more than 10^10), or a
  signature criterion that avoids the pairs that reduce to zero (6,346 of the
  8,055 selected pairs on one development step of `c17-4-3`; item 06).

## Budget and execution

One thread per job (the instrument is single-threaded), every run wrapped in
`prlimit --as=4294967296` (4 GiB address space; the instrument stops itself
with status `memcap` at 88% of the limit and with `colscap` at 400,000
columns). At most 2 CPU-hours of measured runs: the worst case above is 6,630
s. No job runs longer than 370 s (405 s hard timeout), far under the 45-minute
job limit, and every card is under 9 minutes. Build products live in the shared `target/` under
`target/blindspots-05/card-NN/` (no per-item `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`; `rustc`, not
cargo, see Deviation 2) and each card removes its binary. Raw outputs and the
receipt go to `research/blindspots_20261018/05-elimination-orders/raw/`.

After the cards run, `b05 summarize --dir raw` applies the rule above to the
recorded lines and prints the table, every raw record and the verdict line;
the README, the SVG/PNG graph and the PDF (research-visuals skill) are written
by the assessing stage from those files.

## Deviations from the frozen design

Each tightens the design or makes it concrete; where one narrows a listed arm
or cell it says so.

1. **A new Groebner engine, not the committed solvers.** The design says "D,
   largest matrix". The committed readings (closure, msolve F4) are
   grevlex-only and the closure is order-free (above), so a new engine is the
   only way to vary the order. Its grevlex arm is the new incumbent for this
   comparison; it is validated against the committed degrees (84/84 closure,
   32/32 msolve) rather than assumed equal. Absolute touches are those of this
   engine's kernel and are not comparable with msolve's or the closure's word
   operations.
2. **Build tool.** `cargo build --release --example` is not used: the library
   does not compile at this branch base (item 02 and 07 protocols record 25
   errors in `ecbench` and `gaudry_quartic`). The instrument is std-only, so
   each card builds it with `rustc --edition 2021 -C opt-level=3
   -C target-cpu=native` to a path under `target/blindspots-05/`. It
   re-implements, instead of importing, the field arithmetic and system builder
   (checked against the committed solution counts and degrees, Controls).
3. **Pair selection for non-degree-compatible orders.** The design does not say
   how F4 selects pairs under a block order, which has no total degree. The
   selection degree of an order is its own degree vector (above); this is the
   normal strategy of that order. It is the same code path for every arm.
4. **Treewidth order.** `descent_treewidth.rs` computes an exact treewidth for
   at most 18 vertices; these systems have 29 to 50. The arm uses the same
   primal graph with a greedy minimum-fill elimination order (and minimum
   degree for `mindeg`), checked against the exact subset program on a
   10-variable descended system. The primal graphs are nearly complete (374 of
   406 possible edges at `c17-4-3`, greedy width 24 of 28), so `mindeg` and
   `tw` may coincide with each other and with `grevlex` on a cell; that is a
   property of the graph and is reported, not corrected.
5. **`(31, 15, 2)` has no intermediates.** The block orders and the weighted
   order are defined by the split into leaves and intermediates; at `m = 2`
   there is none, so those three arms are recorded `na` (a weighted order with
   all weights 1 is grevlex). Only the variable-order arms apply there.
6. **Variable-order direction.** The variable eliminated first is the largest
   (declared before the data); the reverse is descriptive only.
7. **Targets, arms and caps per cell** (card plan above): eight targets only on
   the four full cells; `blkLI` on two targets per cell with a 40 s cap
   (it cannot win: it was 25 to 100 times slower than grevlex at every
   development cell); `(17,4,4)` and `(31,15,2)` limited. Where the budget
   leaves a cell short of the rule's "countable" bar, that is said in the
   verdict, not hidden.
8. **Unsatisfiable targets only**, chosen by an exact count (above).
9. **Two guards added to the rule.** The arm must also keep its largest matrix
   within 1.5x of grevlex's (else TRADES, not ADVANCES), and the random band is
   recomputed on exactly the targets the judged arm ran. Both only make
   ADVANCES harder.
10. **Development runs preceded the freeze.** The instrument was developed and
    timed on `--seed 20261018` draws (cells `c17-4-3` targets 0-1, and target 0
    of each other cell). Those runs informed the caps and the arm sizing, and
    showed that `wdeg` was cheaper than grevlex in touches on `c17-4-3`,
    `c19-4-3`, `c13-3-4` and `c15-3-4` on every development target run (2.3x to
    41x), while at `c17-4-4` neither finished in 150 s. The frozen
    draws are different systems; the rule, the arms and the controls were fixed
    from the design text and not tuned to those readings, except that the
    weighting of `wdeg` was chosen by the link multiplicity before any run.

11. **Repair pass (2026-10-18): card defect, no instrument change.** The first run's
    cards 02 to 06 wrote `--arms` as a YAML flow list, which split on the commas, so
    `task_card.py` passed each arm name as a separate argv item and the instrument (which reads
    only the token after `--arms`) ran `grevlex` alone in those commands. The instrument is
    not at fault and is **unchanged** (sha256 `f21a7613...e82adf`, as bound in the first-run
    cards; `smoke-r2.sh` rebuilds it, runs every arm of the four full cells once on target 0
    with the arm list as one argument, and checks that grevlex touches equal the first run's).
    Nothing in this repair changes the question, arms, cells, metric, caps or decision rule.
    Revision cards `card-02-r2` .. `card-06g-r2` (card ids suffixed `-r2`) run exactly the arms
    that did not run, with the list quoted as one string and the same targets and per-job caps:
    the 14 (cells with reverse arms) or 12 (`c13-3-4`) non-grevlex, non-`blkLI` arms. They do not
    re-run `grevlex` (the first-run records are deterministic and are the pairing baseline) nor
