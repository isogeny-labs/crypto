# Semilinear quotient trace, including repeated roots
Let K=GF(2^n), H a positive-degree monic polynomial, A=K[X]/(H), sigma(a)=a^2. Because characteristic2 makes squaring a ring endomorphism, sigma is well-defined on A even when H has repeated roots. Define P_k=sigma^k(X), T_k=sum_(i=0..k-1)sigma^i(t). For a polynomial a, sigma^k(a)=sum_j a_j^(2^k) P_k^j in A. Coefficient twisting is essential unless coefficients are fixed by that Frobenius power. Coefficient exponents may be reduced modulo n because K is finite; exponents of X or arbitrary elements of A may not be reduced using field order when A is nonreduced.

P_(a+b)=sigma^a(P_b), T_(a+b)=T_a+sigma^a(T_b). Induct on k: base k1 has P1=X^2 and T1=t. At half=floor(k/2), compose its two blocks; for odd k append sigma^(2half)(t) and square P_(2half). The implementation returns the canonical degree<degH representative of T_k. It does not assert T_k is scalar or a rationality certificate by itself. Length0 returns0, length1 returns t modH; constants, zero and nilpotents remain covered. No inversion, factorization, division by a zero divisor or squarefree hypothesis occurs.

In the polynomial basis for K, Frobenius is GF2-linear. Columns are squares of basis vectors. Repeated self-composition builds maps for powers1,2,4,...; applying selected maps implements coefficient exponent2^k, using k modn. Preparation takes n field squarings plus O(n^2 log n) bit-XOR steps in a coarse bound. Applying a twist costs O(n log n) bit-XOR steps per coefficient. Naive degree-m modular Horner composition costs O(m^3) field work; O(log k) compositions compete with O(k m^2) sequential squaring. Storage for the map is O(n^2 log n) bits and recursion/quotient polynomials O(m log k) field elements. Every map is rebuilt within each measured jump call; no hidden amortization. Generic composition may lose for small k or larger m. Counts and instrumented component times are diagnostics, not an asymptotic or end-to-end speed certificate.

For characteristic p the proof is identical with sigma(a)=a^p and coefficient twisting a_j^(p^k), K=GF(p^n). F_p-linear coefficient maps replace GF2 maps. This is an odd-characteristic derivation, not an implemented or measured NIST solver improvement. For prime fields n1 the coefficient twist is trivial; this does not remove polynomial root extraction work.

This is classical Frobenius/modular-composition machinery. Its use here is a new versioned kernel experiment, not a claimed novel general rootfinding algorithm. A primary-paper novelty review remains separate and incomplete.
