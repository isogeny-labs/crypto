# Smoothness of random reduced divisors by genus

Binary: `src/bin/tower_smoothness.rs`. Raw output: `smoothness.json`, plot
`smoothness.svg`, plain table `table.txt` (stderr of the run).
Command: `target/release/tower_smoothness --json --svg smoothness.svg`
(defaults: 8 curves per cell, 20,000 samples per curve, fixed seed). [pcc:unverified]

## Method

- Cells: g in 1..4, p in {53, 101, 151, 211, 251}. Per cell 8 random
  squarefree curves `y^2 = f(x)` with `deg f = 2g+1` (random coefficients,
  non-zero leading coefficient, redrawn until `HCurve::new` accepts), 20,000
  samples each, so 160,000 samples per cell and 3.2M in all.
- Sampling: each sample takes a fresh `random_divisor` (a sum of g random
  points) and multiplies it with `mul_uncounted` by a random 48-bit scalar.
  `#J < 2^33` here, so the scalar is far above the group exponent and the
  result is close to uniform on the subgroup generated by that divisor. The
  subgroup is the whole Jacobian when `J` is cyclic generated by it, which was
  not checked. [pcc:unverified]
- Smooth: the criterion of `ecbench/hyper_ic.rs` (`Fp::split` returns `Some`):
  `u` splits into linear factors over `F_p`, repeated roots allowed, `deg u < g`
  allowed (deg 0 is smooth). The factor base is taken to be all `F_p` x
  coordinates, so "off-base" roots of the real IC (a partial factor base) count
  as smooth here. The check is a root-by-root synthetic division of `u`, not a
  call to the private `Fp::split`; the two were not cross-tested. [pcc:unverified]
- Intervals: Wilson 95% on the pooled binomial (all 160,000 samples of a
  cell). Samples within a curve are treated as independent. Because curves
  differ (see below) a second interval is given: the mean of the 8 per-curve
  fractions with a Student-t(7) 95% interval, which is the one to use for a
  statement about "a random curve".
- Prediction columns: `1/g!`, and the exact fraction of monic degree-g
  polynomials over `F_p` that split, `C(p+g-1, g)/p^g`.

## Comparison with 1/g!

- Averaged over the five primes (mean of curve-mean fractions), the smooth
  fraction is 1.000, 0.515, 0.169, 0.0450 for g = 1..4, so expected trials
  per relation are 1.00, 1.94, 5.91, 22.2. The `g!` prediction is 1, 2, 6, 24.
  [pcc:unverified]
- Consecutive-genus ratio of trials per relation: 1.94 (g 1 to 2), 3.04
  (g 2 to 3), 3.76 (g 3 to 4); prediction 2, 3, 4. Per prime, the g 2 to 3
  ratio is 3.13, 2.89, 2.90, 3.03, 3.28 for p = 53, 101, 151, 211, 251
  (conservative ratio-of-Wilson-bounds intervals of about +-0.05, which
  understate the curve-to-curve spread). [pcc:unverified]
- The measured fractions follow the exact split fraction `C(p+g-1,g)/p^g`
  more closely than `1/g!` at small p (it exceeds `1/g!` by about 2% at g=2,
  p=53, 6% at g=3, p=53, 12% at g=4, p=53). This is a finite-p effect and
  does not change the conclusion. [pcc:unverified]
- Deviation from 1/g!: the curve-to-curve spread is far larger than binomial
  noise. Per-curve fractions at g=2 range from 0.357 to 0.642 across cells,
  at g=4 from 0.018 to 0.094. Single-curve samples of 20,000 have a binomial
  half-width of about 0.007 at g=2, so the spread is a real property of the
  curve (it depends on the number of rational points and on `J`), not noise.
  The pooled Wilson intervals therefore only describe the sampled curves, not
  the population; they are reported but the curve-mean intervals are the
  honest ones. Against `1/g!`, 13 of the 15 curve-mean intervals at g=2..4
  cover the prediction; g=2, p=53 (0.506 to 0.607) and g=4, p=101 (0.043 to
  0.073) lie just above it. Both cover the exact split fraction.
  [pcc:unverified]

## Does 1/g! explain IC3/IC2 in TOWER.md

- TOWER.md reports IC3/IC2 of 2.33-3.40 over eight primes (mean of the eight
  ratios 2.99). The smoothness ratio measured here, 3.04 averaged over the
  five shared primes, and the 1/g! value of 3, both sit inside that range.
  So the mechanism accounts for the size of the rise from genus 2 to genus 3
  in aggregate. [pcc:unverified]
- It does not explain the per-prime pattern. At p=101 the IC ratio is 2.33
  against a measured smoothness ratio of 2.89; at p=53 it is 2.42 against 3.13;
  at p=251 it is 3.10 against 3.28. The IC cells have 8 seeds and no stated
  interval, so a per-prime match or mismatch cannot be judged from them.
  [pcc:unverified]
- In the real IC the cost is trials times one group operation, plus factor
  base construction, a few extra relations and a verification
  multiplication. At p=53, genus 2, IC is only 70 operations, so fixed
  overhead is a large share and drags the ratio down. That is consistent with
  the low ratios at the small primes, but this run did not measure it.
  [pcc:unverified]
- The number of relations needed also differs by genus (the factor base is the
  degree-1 places, about p of them for both, but the number of `F_p`-places in
  the real runs was not read here). The mechanism measured here is only the
  smoothness probability. [pcc:unverified]

## Limits

- The smoothness test here is a reimplementation of the criterion, not the
  IC's own code, and treats the factor base as all `F_p` abscissas.
- Samples are near-uniform on the subgroup generated by a random sum of g
  points, not shown to be uniform on `J`. The IC walk is a different sampling
  (`D1 + D2` plus factor base steps), whose smoothness rate was not measured.
- Only p < 256 and g <= 4. 8 random curves per cell is few given the large
  curve-to-curve spread; the t-interval on 7 degrees of freedom is wide.
- The curves are random squarefree, not the TOWER.md curves (the IC curves of
  the tower, built by chained transfer, were not used). Their smooth rate may
  differ.
- A smoothness probability is not an IC cost. No IC run was made here.
