# Amortized genus-2 index calculus against matched precomputed rho

All numbers below are measured counts from `amortized.json` (run 2 of `amortized_bench`, bench record `benchmarks/records/20261112T0402Z-chained-amortized-ic.json`) and carry [pcc:unverified]; the table rows are covered by this note. Protocol: [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md), frozen before measurement, with two dated amendments.

## Verdict against the frozen rule

**IC amortized does not beat matched precomputed rho.** [pcc:unverified] The frozen rule asks for a win at every prime of 1619 and above against the memory-matched and the precompute-matched Bernstein-Lange tables at all three section 5a prices. It holds at none of the 11 primes (211 to 205,661). [pcc:unverified] At every prime the best IC variant (`ic_walk_q90`) has an online mean per target within 0.0 to 1.2 group operations of the BL tables and on the wrong side of them in 10 of 11 rows; the one tie (p = 25,693) has a paired interval containing 0. [pcc:unverified]

**Against plain rho, amortized IC is a clear improvement** (online cost 2.0% to 3.1% of plain rho at every prime, interval of the paired difference far above 0, break-even N* finite at every price). [pcc:unverified] This is an improvement over both plain rho and the old target-in-every-relation IC (33,196 group ops per target at p = 1619 in `results_v2.json`, 15.7 now), but it is not an advantage over rho given Bernstein-Lange precomputation, which is the admissible comparison. [pcc:unverified]

Distance remaining, as ratio IC* online / BL memory-matched online (1.000 would be a tie): p=211: 1.068, p=419: 1.111, p=821: 1.087, p=1619: 1.051, p=3203: 1.034, p=6421: 1.024, p=12841: 1.021, p=25693: 0.999, p=51407: 1.016, p=102829: 1.042, p=205661: 1.013. [pcc:unverified]

## What was measured

Group: the order-r subgroup of J(C)(F_p) generated by the real pulled-back generator G = phi^*(G_E) of the verified transfer from the same bielliptic curve as `chained_transfer_bench` (the first four curves match `results_v2.json` in p, r and #E1). Targets: 128 per curve, T = phi^*([x]G_E) with planted x. Unit: Cantor additions and doublings; linear-algebra operations over Z/r counted separately and priced at 0, 0.1, 1 GAE. Every recovered log in every arm was checked by `certify_dlog` (a fresh double-and-add in `amortized.rs`) and equals the planted x: `all_certified_and_equal_planted` is true for every arm of every row and no row is marked invalid. [pcc:unverified]

Arms: plain rho (`rho_on_jac`); amortized IC in three variants (`ic_comb_q90`, `ic_walk_q90`, `ic_comb_q99`; weight-2 relations over the full degree-1 base, a union-find elimination for weight-2 rows, relations drawn from [alpha]G plus two random elements of the cofactor part, stop at 90% or 99% of the base determined); BL precomputed rho with the table sized by memory (`bl_b_memory`, entries = base size) or by budget (`bl_a*` matched to the headline IC one-time cost, `bl_s*` matched to IC* itself, Amendment 1). Online cost includes the group ops of the certificate for IC and BL (plain rho already includes its own check). [pcc:unverified]

## Why the online costs tie

For p of 1619 and above, IC* online is 0 to 1.2 ops above the BL tables. Both are dominated by the same two terms: forming the start T + [beta]G from the power table (about half of log2 r additions) and the [pcc:unverified] certificate (about 1.5 log2 r). A table that covers the group answers in one walk step; IC* answers in about 2.4 descent trials with one addition each (walk variant). The comparison is therefore between two methods that both reach "a few operations plus a certificate", and the one-time column decides it. [pcc:unverified]

## Limitations

- r is the largest prime factor of #E1 with r^2 not dividing it, with r >= 50, as in `chained_transfer_bench`. It varies from 71 to 34,211 and is far below p at p = 821, 25,693 and 102,829. When r is below the base size, a table of all r multiples of G is smaller than the IC state, which favours rho; the rows are not a clean scaling series in r. [pcc:unverified]
- The IC one-time phase could not use relations in G alone: they span only the r-element subgroup, so at most about r/2 smooth relations exist against about p/2 places. The pool is the whole group, [alpha]G plus two random elements of the cofactor part (a 48-element pool of [r]Y). Logs are those of the cofactor-projected places, not of the places themselves; unit tests check them against `[cof]` projections. A first version with a single cofactor generator failed because that generator had order 11 in a group of cofactor 605. [pcc:unverified]
- The elimination exploits weight-2 rows (union-find over Z/r). Dense Gaussian elimination as in `measure.rs` costs 14,633 to 603,721 mulmods at p = 211 to 1619 (`results_v2.json`), 26 to 148 times the IC* LA ops at the same p, so under that convention IC* would only get worse. [pcc:unverified]
- Hash-table lookups, union-find finds and hashing are unpriced in all arms. BL table memory is not priced either; the memory-matched arm limits entries to the base size. [pcc:unverified]
- IC* is chosen after the fact as the lowest mean among three variants, and BL k is chosen by tuning on 24 separate targets; both choices favour the arm that gets them. The two stop fractions (90%, 99%) were fixed in the protocol and not tuned. [pcc:unverified]
- One curve per prime and one seed per arm; the intervals are over targets (128), not over curves. [pcc:unverified]
- Run 2 took about 3,048 s in total: the last row (p = 205,661) alone exceeded the 300 s row cap, which by rule is the last row. Run 1 (frozen protocol, no `bl_s*` arms) is kept as `run1_*`; it gave the same verdict. [pcc:unverified]
- The transfer is reported only as wall time (cover, isogeny search and hops, 0.3 to 168 ms) and phi^* per target (about 1 to 2 us); neither enters any total. phi^* is a per-target evaluation in practice, so the "one-time" status of the transfer is the owner's accounting assumption, not something measured here. [pcc:unverified]
- Everything is `[pcc:unverified]`: no pcc claim has been signed for these figures.
