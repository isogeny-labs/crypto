# Degree-seven extension isogeny escape bound for the trace ±26 cover orbit

16 November 2026. Let `K=F243`, let `E/K` have Frobenius trace `t=±26`, and put `L=F_(243^7)`. A separable `L`-isogeny from `E_L` to a curve whose `j`-invariant is outside `K` has degree at least **21,156,227**. This is a bound on *isogeny transport to a primitive-j source*, not a bound on GHS covers or on all vulnerable curves. It applies to both five-model Frobenius orbits joined by the degree-two bridge in [the paired-orbit report](../ghs_twist_isogenous2_20261116/RESULT.md). [pcc:unverified]

The discriminant `26²−4·243=−296` is fundamental and has class number **10**, checked in the arithmetic receipt. The earlier [ten-model census](../ghs_odd5_carrier_20261115/TRACE26_CLASS_THEOREM.md) lists all ten `K`-isomorphism classes of trace `+26`; their quadratic twists list all ten trace `−26` classes. The paired-orbit construction gives a degree-seven cover for one five-element Frobenius orbit per sign and a degree-fourteen cover through a two-isogeny for the other orbit. Thus these **twenty exact `K`-isomorphism classes have an explicit cover route**, with degree at most fourteen, in this finite field. Cover reachability does not by itself give a measured attack win for every class or extension degree. The native s=7 same-target measurement currently exists for one negative representative and its exact two-isogenous partner via a transported suffix.

## Frobenius criterion

For a prime `ell≠3`, Frobenius `F` on `E[ell]` satisfies `F²−tF+243=0`. Define `U_0=0`, `U_1=1`, and `U_n=tU_(n−1)−243U_(n−2)`. Cayley–Hamilton gives `F^n=U_n F−243 U_(n−1) I`. An `ell`-isogeny defined over `L` but not `K` corresponds to an order-`ell` subgroup, hence a line of `E[ell]`, fixed by `F^7` but not by `F`.

For any two-dimensional vector space, a line fixed by `F^7` but not by `F`, with seven prime, forces `F^7` to be scalar. If `F^7` had a unique eigenline, commutation with `F` would preserve it. If it had two distinct eigenlines, `F` would preserve both or exchange them; exchange would make `F^7` exchange them too, impossible because seven is odd. Thus every line would be fixed by `F^7`. Since `F` itself is not scalar, its coefficient `U_7` in the Cayley–Hamilton identity must vanish modulo `ell`. Consequently **`ell | U_7` is necessary** for a new degree-`ell` kernel over `L`.

The exact recurrences give `U_7(−26)=U_7(26)=−21,156,227`. Its absolute value is prime. The saved [Sage receipt](check.json) checks both signs, primality, the mask-167 trace and modular-polynomial factor degrees for selected primes 7 through 61; none of those spot checks has a degree-seven factor. [Lean arithmetic](IsogenyEscapeArithmetic.lean) checks both recurrences and the square-root cutoff `4600²>21,156,227`; primality is checked by Sage, not Lean. The projective-Frobenius implication above is a paper proof, not a Lean theorem.

## From prime kernels to an isogeny degree bound

Suppose a separable `L`-isogeny has degree below 21,156,227. Strip its Galois-stable kernel one prime at a time. For `ell≠3`, if its `ell`-torsion contains a stable line, quotient by the corresponding `ell`-isogeny. Otherwise it contains all of `E[ell]`, and quotient by `[ell]`; this preserves the `K`-isomorphism class. At each intermediate `K`-curve in the same trace class, every prime-degree step with `ell<21,156,227` and `ell≠3` has a kernel already `K`-rational. For `ell=3`, ordinary elliptic curves have a unique geometric étale order-three subgroup, which is Galois-stable; separable three-isogeny steps are therefore also `K`-defined. Induction keeps every intermediate `j` in `K`, including the final one. Therefore a primitive-j codomain requires degree at least 21,156,227.

This bound explains why small-prime isogeny searches in the degree-seven extension stay in the proper subfield. It does **not** prove an isogeny of degree 21,156,227 produces a primitive-j codomain, nor that a primitive-j curve admits a fast GHS attack. The Frobenius equation, rational-kernel criterion, and prime-degree factorization are standard; see [Galbraith, constructing isogenies](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/30811E8101E76F7C2A22873EE7080237/S1461157000000097a.pdf/div-class-title-constructing-isogenies-between-elliptic-curves-over-finite-fields-div.pdf), especially its introduction and Proposition 4, and [Sutherland's isogeny-kernel lecture](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-783-elliptic-curves-fall-2025/mit18_783_f25_lec05.pdf). The exact prime threshold is a new computation for this recorded trace class.
