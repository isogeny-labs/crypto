# Exact fixed-target prime interval sum scan versus strong source scan

Contract: every ordered (x,y) in [0,M]^2 satisfying original short-Weierstrass S3(x,y,u)=0 over the declared odd prime, including repeated/torsion and twist polynomial branches. Bounds 2M<p and M^2<p, smooth curve, M<=4095. Exceeding an unproved bound is unsupported, not an empty exact set.

With S=x+y,T=xy, direct expansion gives S3=(T-uS-2u^2-A)^2-4f(u)(S+u). Each integer S in [0,2M] therefore has at most two field products T=uS+2u^2+A +/-2sqrt(f(u)(S+u)), also when f(u)=0. Unique integer lifts of S,T follow from the stated bounds. Products outside [0,M^2] are unsupported. The integer discriminant S^2-4T must be a nonnegative perfect square r^2, with S-r even and both (S-r)/2,(S+r)/2 in [0,M]. These conditions are necessary and sufficient for the monic integer factorization, and all orders are emitted. Root equality is deduplicated. All original roots lie on one enumerated sum and product, proving completeness. No curve rationality test discards a branch. No denominator is inverted.

Strong comparator: enumerate x only, then solve the exact original S3(x,u,Y) quadratic. Its discriminant is tested directly; x=u gives the linear/torsion exception. Batch all nonzero denominators before canonical y<=M filtering. This also costs O(M) field square-root tests, not O(M^2). The sum variant has 2M+1 iterations versus M+1 source iterations, so a 50x asymptotic advantage over this strong specialized method is NOT predicted. Its only proposed advantage is denominator-free recovery and an early integer support test. The two algorithms must be compared on every registered target, even if this refutes the sum variant.
