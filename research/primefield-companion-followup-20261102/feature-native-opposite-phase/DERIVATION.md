# Opposite-radius parity initialization

T-1878. Derived construction and producer checks; independent signed verdict pending. [pcc:unverified]

## Domain and source observations

Retain the caller-certified prime scalar order r, public-point sign source and exact-order zeta from the direct-phase predecessor. Require m >= 3 odd, 4m dividing r-1, and n=(r-1)/(4m) positive and even. Let h=(r-1)/2, A=m b^h, V=b^(-m) Z. At opposite radius -b, A is unchanged because h is even, whereas V becomes -V because m is odd. Since n is even, both observations use the same phase V^n=u. The two source orbits are disjoint: -1 is not in the odd-order group generated by zeta.

Normalize the two C0 observations to c+ and c-. Fold F0 into R_u modulo V^n-u as in the predecessor. Write R_u(V)=E_u(Y)+V O_u(Y), Y=V^2, with both E and O of degree below k=n/2. Set s=(c+ + c-)/2 and d=(c+ - c-)/2. The exact conditions are

    Y^k=u, E_u(Y)=s, V O_u(Y)=d, V^2=Y.

These are polynomial equalities in the scalar prime field. They do not require computing the target scalar or enumerating candidate roots.

## Half-degree initialization and orientation

First form g=gcd(Y^k-u, E_u-s), reusing sparse modular exponentiation when E_u-s is nonconstant. A zero equation expands the k-degree phase domain; a nonzero constant yields the unit polynomial.

If d=0, V is nonzero on the phase domain, so replace g by gcd(g,O_u). Both signs are admissible and the oriented support is exactly g(V^2).

If d is nonzero, reduce O modulo g and replace g by gcd(g,Y O^2-d^2). On every surviving Y, O is nonzero and the unique allowed orientation is V=d/O(Y). Instead of inverting O or forming a characteristic polynomial, lift g(V^2) and intersect it with V O(V^2)-d, reducing O modulo the surviving g first. The result is monic and has degree at most deg(g) <= k. This is an exact common-root construction, not a norm-only filter.

Y^k-u splits in F_r and all its roots are squares: (r-1)/k=8m, hence each root satisfies Y^((r-1)/2)=u^(4m)=1. The lift therefore loses no rational orientation. Domains are squarefree since r is odd and 0<k<r. Equivalently, the final support is the direct gcd of V^n-u, F0(V)-c+, and F0(-V)-c-.

## Persistent updates and recovery

The constructor initializes the four direct V supports from the paired C0 conditions, then applies the two C2 constraints with the existing scalar quotient evaluator. Its zero flag receives both original zero conditions; zero is never inserted into a nonzero phase. Later radii use the existing direct update at the fixed first anchor.

The new recovery adapter waits for the second opposite-radius observation before initializing, except for existing identity/endpoint exits. Unsupported parity or radius ordering falls back before source queries. At most four radii, 32 extra offsets, 128 calls and the existing materialization caps are retained. The public comparison uses the same separate [1,r-1,2,3] panel for both adapters. The original 4,563-case cohort and previous panels are unchanged.

## Work accounting

The first phase gcd now has domain degree k rather than n. This does not halve every operation: the four folded residues still require n slots each, n+1 coefficient deliveries and the inherited coefficient setup. The implementation copies the even residue into four temporary arrays when reusing the sparse gcd helper; those copies are charged. A future dedicated binomial-gcd API can remove this constant-factor duplication.

The norm step uses reduced odd polynomials, their square and a remainder; the oriented lift may temporarily have degree 2 deg(g), up to n. When d=0, the final support can still have degree n. Every source orbit, initial source grid, order check, observation transform, polynomial allocation/copy, quotient evaluation, inversion, final point verification and failed attempt remains charged. Mandatory second-orbit collection may cost more than an early one-radius recovery. No timing or total field-operation advantage is inferred from polynomial degree alone.
