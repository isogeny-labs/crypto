# Half-GCD updates for candidate root sets

2026-11-07, T-1800. Standard polynomial algorithm composed with the existing
prime-field source and fast moment-root recovery. Unsigned producer evidence.

## Change and scope

The previous RootSet updated its monic candidate polynomial G using ordinary
Euclidean gcd. This component dispatches that gcd to a capped driver over the
existing recursive half-GCD engine. The generator copies polynomial arithmetic
unchanged, relocates four test includes, and appends the new entry point.
It copies the fast moment converter and recovery implementation, replacing
only the polynomial-module location, test-module location and RootSet gcd call.
Source and output hashes record these transformations.

No source-character, seed, root-count, sign-window, query-limit or forward-check
rule changes. Prime-field coefficients and caller-certified primality remain
required. The gcd API itself has no resultant projection-order restriction.
The underlying word bound and u128 arithmetic are inherited from Poly::new.
Old implementations and their evidence remain intact.

## Gcd driver and invariant

Canonicalize A,B and put the larger degree first. Enforce the raw input-length
cap before copying, including trailing zero padding. Repeatedly:

1. If deg(A)>8 and ceil(deg(A)/2)<=deg(B)<deg(A), apply the existing half_gcd
   matrix to the full pair. Its checked output has second degree below the
   half boundary. Reject a nonzero output second polynomial that fails to
   reduce the previous second degree.
2. Otherwise perform one ordinary Euclidean division and rotate the pair.
3. Stop at B=0 and normalize A to monic. Define gcd(0,0)=0.

Each Euclidean step has matrix [[0,1],[1,-q]] with determinant -1; its entries
are polynomials and its inverse is also polynomial. Products of these matrices
preserve the ideal generated by the pair, hence their gcd up to a field unit.
The inherited half-GCD forms these products recursively from high coefficients
and applies the resulting matrix back to FULL inputs. Existing lift checks and
corrected final-remainder degrees are retained. Final monic normalization fixes
the unique gcd representative. Zero, constant, equal-degree and swapped inputs
are handled by the ordinary step or terminal normalization.

In the recursive branch, the second degree crosses the half boundary. A
subsequent ordinary rotation or another bounded reduction brings the first
polynomial to that scale. Thus the full driver inherits the standard
geometric-degree half-GCD complexity; it does not repeatedly invoke a large
matrix reduction on an unchanged pair.

## Root-update meaning

Let G=product_(x in C)(X-x) for a candidate set C in F_r. For nonzero sign s,

    G_new = monic_gcd(G, (X+a)^((r-1)/2) mod G - s).

For s=0 use monic_gcd(G,X+a). Because G is squarefree and split, the gcd keeps
exactly those candidates with the requested character value. Nonzero signs
exclude x=-a because its character value is zero. G=1 is the empty absorbing
state. The converter and actual candidate moments establish the initial
squarefree split polynomial; an arbitrary externally supplied polynomial is
not silently treated as a candidate set. Recovery returns a singleton only
when [candidate]P=Q, as before.

## Costs, including inversions

Write n=deg G and M(n) for polynomial multiplication. Standard half-GCD gives
O(M(n) log n) field operations, counting field inversions as field operations.
The native implementation realizes general inversions by exponentiation and
uses O(n) such scalar inversions conservatively across the reduction; charging
them at O(log r) modular multiplications gives the explicit conservative bound

    O(M(n) log n + n log r)

for the gcd. The modular-power portion of a sign update remains
O(M(n) log r), with monic-modulus divisions. Total source-oracle cost is
separate. Installed multiplication is Karatsuba, not an FFT backend, and no
FFT-specific constants from the literature are attributed to this code.
This replaces the ordinary Euclidean driver; no timing gain or tuned crossover
is asserted. The prior O(n M(n)) estimate was a conservative bound for that
implementation, not a lower bound or optimal Euclidean complexity.

Candidate polynomial storage remains linear in n. Retained transition records,
matrices and recursive temporaries are charged; O(n log n) words is a conservative
workspace envelope, not measured RSS. Element caps are not a memory-byte budget.
Reported batch/transition counts are diagnostic coverage, not total operation
counts. Larger-transcript compression and the approximately r/8 initial root
state are unchanged.
