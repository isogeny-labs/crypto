## Input and fixed global point field

Continue CT102–106 with E: y²+y=x³−x²−10x−20 and a nonzero global point T of exact order 11. CT102 gives L=Q(x(T)) of degree 60. CT105/106 establish that the fixed line field M has degree 12 and is generated by the single symmetric invariant

    s(T) = sum_{m=1}^5 x([m]T) ∈ M.

The five x-multiplication rational functions used to evaluate s are the exact division-polynomial functions from CT106; no input scalar appears. Let **q(Z)∈Q[Z]** denote the monic characteristic-zero minimal polynomial of s, which exists and has degree 12 (CT106). **This rung does not expand its rational coefficients.**

At every good prime p≠11, s is integral at p: nonidentity 11-torsion has finite reduction by good-reduction injectivity, so each x([m]T) is p-adically integral. Thus the coefficients of q are p-integral and q can be reduced at p=2 and p=59. The 11-division polynomial has invertible derivative at its roots for these primes.

## Source p=59: the monogenic invariant is locally maximal

Two independently written programs construct ψ11 using the classical integral recurrence and reconstruct s in the 60-dimensional quotient algebra F59[X]/ψ11, inverting every division-polynomial denominator and checking each inverse.

They calculate the unique first power relation in this quotient at degree **12**. In ascending coefficients:

    q mod 59 = [19,26,16,12,35,43,33,58,29,29,46,39,1].

The independent first twelve powers are linearly independent, and the displayed degree-12 relation vanishes. Because the global monic q has degree 12 and is 59-integral, this is **the actual reduction of q**, not a conjectured local polynomial.

The complete factorization over F59 is:

    (Z+27)(Z+36)
    (Z^5+14Z^4+11Z^3+35Z^2+22Z+41)
    (Z^5+21Z^4+45Z^3+57Z^2+2Z+16).

Both quintics are irreducible by explicit Rabin/Frobenius checks; q mod59 is squarefree. The two rational source-line invariant values are **32 and 23**. The source line containing (19,22) has s=32 by CT106. The squarefree q reduction proves, without computing q over Q, that **59 does not divide [O_M:Z[s]]**.

## Binary p=2: three primes collapse to a single quartic under s

In F2[X] the actual x-division polynomial splits as the product of three monic irreducible degree-20 factors:

    0x130027, 0x131203, 0x131211.

The implementations multiply the factors back to ψ11 mod2, verify their irreducibility using Frobenius and gcd, and independently evaluate s in each corresponding F_(2^20) quotient. **All three** resulting invariant values have exactly the same degree-four minimal polynomial

    h(Z)=Z^4+Z+1.

This is stronger than CT106's one fixed binary residue check. In the rank-60 x-field, the characteristic polynomial of multiplication by s has characteristic-two reduction

    h(Z)^(5+5+5)=h(Z)^15.

Since [L:M]=5, that characteristic polynomial over Q is q(Z)^5. Its reduction satisfies (q mod2)^5=h^15, hence by unique factorization

    **q(Z) mod2 = (Z^4+Z+1)^3.**

The reduction is **not squarefree**, even though 2 is unramified in M: E has good reduction at 2, prime-to-2 torsion is finite étale, and CT105's projective Frobenius has three degree-four orbits. Thus

    O_M/2O_M ≅ F16 × F16 × F16

while the image of Z[s] in this product has F2-dimension **four**, not twelve. More precisely, the subring generated by the residue of s is a *single* embedded F16, with possibly different Frobenius embeddings into the three components.

By passing to the quotient modulo two, the finite abelian group O_M/Z[s] has at least eight independent mod-two directions. Therefore the exact **index lower bound** is

    **v_2([O_M:Z[s]]) >= 12 − 4 = 8.**

In particular, a degree-12 power basis in s is **not 2-maximal**, and naive reduction of q modulo two cannot distinguish the **three** binary primes. This is an order-index/reduction-presentation obstruction, not ramification in the actual field M, not an ECDLP hardness theorem, and not a proof that more sophisticated 2-integral coordinates cannot distinguish the primes.

This lower bound does *not* prove v2(index)=8; that would require a maximal-order/discriminant computation. It does show that the next step must construct additional integral directions/idempotents rather than blindly reducing a characteristic-zero power-basis relation.
