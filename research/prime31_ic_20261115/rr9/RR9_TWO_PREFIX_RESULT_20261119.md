## Verified mathematical identity (subject to the stated nonexceptional hypotheses)

Use the unchanged ordinary nonsingular cofactor-one original Prime31 group:
\`p=2147483647\`, \`E:y²=x³+847514846x+2087444724\`, \`n=2147446831\`, \`G=(625037715,2100627251)\`.

Set \`F(X,Y)=(X-r)A(X)+(Y+s)V(X)\` for public \`R=(r,s)\`, with \`deg A<=3\`, \`V=X³+v2 X²+v1 X+v0\`. Let two **supplied signed** factor-base leaves be \`Q_i=(x_i,y_i)\` with \`x0 != x1\`, \`x_i != r\`. Put \`alpha_i=(y_i+s)/(x_i-r)\`, and choose five free variables \`z=(a2,a3,v0,v1,v2)\`. Then

\`C_i=-alpha_i*(x_i³+v2*x_i²+v1*x_i+v0)-a2*x_i²-a3*x_i³\`;
\`a1=(C1-C0)/(x1-x0)\`; \`a0=C0-a1*x0\`.

This is a **bijective affine parameterization** of normalized RR9 charts satisfying the two prescribed leaf equations \`F(Q0)=F(Q1)=0\`: equations uniquely solve for \`(a0,a1)\`; the five other coefficients are unrestricted. It eliminates two chart variables exactly, not heuristically.

For six remaining signed-weight slots, \`j=2..7\`, take the **public** eight unsigned x-coordinates \`x_(j,b)\`, \`b=0..7\`, and form

\`M_R,z(X) = (X²+rX+r²+a_curve)V(X)² - (X-r)A(X)² - 2s*A(X)*V(X)\`,
\`H_j(z) = product_{b=0}^7 M_R,z(x_(j,b))\`.

The six \`H_j\` are polynomials of total degree at most **16 in five unknowns**. The Rust candidate computes them and their exact 6×5 chain-rule Jacobian with prefix/suffix products. Public predicate evaluation uses no group-sum tables; the grader has known witnesses in a strictly separate path. A zero of the polynomial system still requires independent selection of actual roots/signs, exceptional-chart handling, original-curve sum replay and full solve costs.

## Exact prefix-guess arithmetic and limitations

B=8 leaves, two signs per leaf. Enumerating t given signed slot choices means \`(2B)^t=16^t\` branches, not merely 8^t. A dense degree-16 total monomial count in \`7-t\` unknowns is \`binom(16+7-t,7-t)\`. This is an **ambient polynomial support upper bound** and a rough F4-input size proxy, not a measured F4 matrix dimension, not a reliable runtime model, and not a lower bound on arithmetic complexity.

| Known signed leaves t | RR9 free variables | Residual equations | Candidate prefixes | Degree-16 monomial ceiling | Prefixes × ceiling |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 7 | 8 | 1 | 245,157 | 245,157 |
| 1 | 6 | 7 | 16 | 74,613 | 1,193,808 |
| **2** | **5** | **6** | **256** | **20,349** | **5,209,344** |
| 3 | 4 | 5 | 4,096 | 4,845 | 19,845,120 |
| 4 | 3 | 4 | 65,536 | 969 | 63,504,384 |

For t=2, **all-branch solving** must make the per-branch *complete* chart selection more than **256× cheaper** than t=0, after all prefix construction, candidate testing, root extraction and exceptional branches, merely to beat one t=0 solve. The crude \`prefixes×monomials\` proxy is already **~21.25× larger** at t=2 than t=0. A superlinear F4 cost in monomial count might still reverse this, so this is a **decision gate**, not a no-go theorem against hybridization. No wall-time, F4 solving degree, column count or memory for a public-only H-system was measured in this experiment.
