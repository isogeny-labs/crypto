## New exact mathematical explanation of the persistent 56→28 rank gap
Homogenize the eight affine RR9 coefficient quadrics `q_0,...,q_7` in **seven** chart unknowns using one fresh variable `h`, obtaining eight homogeneous quadrics in **eight** variables. Homogeneous degree `d` forms have dimension `N_d=binom(d+7,7)`. The Macaulay rows `m q_i`, with `deg m <= d-2` in the affine chart, homogenize to the **entire** degree-`d` ideal component generated by those eight forms. The observed quotient codimension is therefore the homogeneous degree-`d` Hilbert function.

For a *generic regular sequence* of eight homogeneous quadrics in eight variables, the Koszul resolution gives
```
H(t) = (1 - t^2)^8 / (1 - t)^8 = (1+t)^8.
```
In particular, the generic degree-five and degree-six Hilbert dimensions are `binom(8,5)=56` and `binom(8,6)=28`. The observed Macaulay ranks are **exactly** their generic maximal values:
```
d=5: 792 - 56 = 736   (previous #4548, positive and two negatives)
d=6: 1716 - 28 = 1688 (this experiment, positive and two negatives)
```
Why this is a valid *maximal-rank comparison*: the regular-sequence locus in the coefficient parameter space is nonempty (e.g. the eight quadrics `z_i^2`) and Zariski open; the degree-`d` rank is maximal on a nonempty open set. On the regular locus the Koszul resolution fixes these Hilbert dimensions. Since nonempty opens in the irreducible parameter space intersect, no special sequence can have larger degree-`d` rank. The measured equalities demonstrate **no additional low-degree rank deficiency** at either tested degree. They **do not** prove that the structured RR9 quadrics form a regular sequence, especially as the positive fixture has an actual affine solution. The rank agreement concerns the graded truncation alone.

For context, the generic complete-intersection prediction at `d=7,8,9` would leave `8,1,0` degree-`d` quotient dimensions, respectively, with dense matrix column counts `3432,6435,11440`; the row counts are `6336,13728,27456`. These numbers are **theoretical generic controls**, not measured RR9 ranks and NOT a universal lower bound on the first unit-certificate degree for the structured ideal. No degree-seven, -eight or -nine benchmark was run.

**Interpretation:** the previously unexplained invariant 56 and now 28 are consistent with the ordinary Koszul/Hilbert function of eight quadrics. The rank data do not support a hidden low-degree RR9 simplification. B8 F4/Macaulay escalation should therefore be gated by independent, *non-generic* algebraic structure rather than repeating larger dense matrices.

Even a *single* degree-six Macaulay attempt costs more wall time than that entire historical BSGS batch (not a valid matched solver comparison). No usable relation extractor or complete solver was obtained.

**Lead disposition:** Reject the dense public-RR9 B8 degree-six Macaulay candidate for further speedups; retain as a rigorous bounded null result and an algebraic Hilbert-function diagnostic. Priority for the next separately frozen study: test a genuine target-conditioned B31 public-only extractor exploiting factor-base membership and repeated-root branches without enumerating signed supports, using the existing Rust sparse F4/quotient engines.
