# LEAD-13 — Summation ideals (Faugère–Wallet 2018) and Nagao on Joux–Vitse covers: the even-characteristic solution-count reduction

**Verdict.** closed-by-theorem (for the repository's own cover path, which is
odd characteristic). The even-characteristic reduction is real but is a
constant-factor effect that no repository row or target can use (section 5).
**Confidence.** high for the repository's path, medium for the
even-characteristic numbers: the repository files were read directly, and the
mechanism is elementary, but the Faugère–Wallet full text was unreachable and
its numbers come from the abstract and the author's 2016 slides.
**Expected gain if it works.** None on any repository row. Odd characteristic
(`jv_cover.rs`, `p` up to 1,511, `ℓ = 2^{61.4}`): ideal degree `64 → 64`,
gain `1.0×`. Hypothetical even-characteristic Joux–Vitse-shaped cover
(`n = 2`, `g = 3`): degree `64 → 32` at best (`2×`), per-test time `1–6×`
(unverified desk estimate); no such instance exists in the repository, and
ECC2K-130 has no low-genus cover. The row it could not move is the cover row
at `S/rho ≈ 0.012–0.014` (`ℓ = 2^{61.4}`).
**Cost of the discriminating test.** T0 (reading frozen data): 0.1 person-day,
under 1 CPU-minute, negligible memory. T1 (optional even-characteristic
positive control, external CAS, not recommended): 3–5 person-days, at most
1 CPU-hour, 4 GB.

## 1. Mechanism (precise)

**Setting.** `H : y² + h₁(x) y = h₀(x)` of genus `g` over `F_{q^n}`,
`q = 2^k`, a divisor `R`, factor base `{(x, y) : x ∈ F_q}`, `m = ng`
summands. Here `m` is the number of Nagao summands, not an `S_m` index: `m = 6`
for the Joux–Vitse (JV) genus-3 cover over `F_{p²}` (`(n, g) = (2, 3)`) and for
Faugère–Wallet's (FW) genus-2 example over `F_{2^{93}}` (`(3, 2)`).

**Nagao's system** (read at source in FW's slides 25–26 and Joux–Vitse §3.1).
Take `f ∈ L(ng·O − D)`, coefficients `a` in `F_{q^n}`; the norm polynomial
`DP_R(x) = f f^ι / u = x^{ng} + Σ N_i(a) x^i` has all roots in `F_q` only if
every `N_i(a) ∈ F_q`. Writing `a` in an `F_q`-basis of `F_{q^n}` gives
`(n − 1)·ng = (n² − n)g` quadrics (the count in Nagao's abstract) in as many
unknowns over `F_q`, generically zero-dimensional of degree
`2^{n(n−1)g}`: 64 for `(2, 3)`, 4096 for `(3, 2)`. A residual decomposes with
probability `1/(ng)!`.

**Summation ideal** (FW slides 29–33). `I_{m,R} ⊂ F[x, a]` is generated by
`N_{m−i}(a) = (−1)^i e_i(x)` (the coefficients of `DP_R` against the elementary
symmetric functions of the roots). `V_{m,R} = V(I_{m,R})` is the variety of
`m`-tuples summing to `R`. The `m`-th summation ideal is
`I_{m,R} ∩ F[x]` (`m ≥ g + 1`); in the `e`-coordinates `deg V = 2^{m−g}`
(heuristic, proved for `g = 1`). The system over `F_q` is a subvariety of the
Weil restriction of `V`, so `deg W = (deg V)^n = 2^{n(n−1)g}` at `m = ng`:
**the same degree as Nagao, with fewer variables but an elimination basis to
compute** (slide 33: "the two modellings are equivalent").

**Even-characteristic mechanism** (slides 35–39). Put `h₁ = Σ_{i=t}^{d} α_i x^i`,
`L = d − t`. With `f = A u + B (y + v)` the norm is
`F = A² u + w B² + h₁ A B`, `w = (v² + h₁ v + h₀)/u` (my derivation, checked
below). Squaring is additive in characteristic 2 and every element of `F_q` is a
square, so a coefficient `N_i = Q(a)²` with `Q` linear is `∈ F_q` exactly when
`Q ∈ F_q`: the `n − 1` quadrics `N_{ij} = 0` become `n − 1` **linear**
equations. FW's theorem: exactly `g − L + 1` of the `N_i` are such squares, so

```
d_new = 2^{(n−1)((n−1)g + L − 1)},   d_old / d_new = 2^{(n−1)(g − L + 1)}
```

Best case `L = 0` (`h₁` a monomial) gives `2^{(n−1)(g+1)}`; worst case
`L = g` gives `2^{n−1}`. The condition is on the **shape of `h₁`, not on
rational 2-torsion**. `L = 0` forces all zeros of `h₁` (the Weierstrass points)
into `{0, ∞}`, i.e. 2-rank at most 1 (recalled standard fact: 2-rank = number of
distinct zeros of `h₁` on `P¹`, counting `∞`, minus 1). Their example
`y² + xy = x⁵ + a x³ + b x² + c` (`h₁ = x`) has `#J = 2·3·p`, cofactor 2,
consistent. In the weighted formulation the same squares give the `e_i`
weight 2 and a lower weighted degree.

**Reported numbers** (slides 36, 41). `n = 3`, `g = 2`, `F_{2^{45}}` solved over
`F_{2^{15}}`: `4096 → 64` solutions, 1500 s → 0.029 s in Magma 2.19 (slide:
"ratio ≈ 75000"; 1500/0.029 = 5.2·10⁴, unverified desk check). Realistic
instance over `F_{2^{93}}`, `n = 3`, `#J = 2·3·p`, `log p = 184`, generic bound
`≈ 2^{92}`: ideals of degree 64 over `F_{2^{31}}`, 3.2·10⁻³ s per decomposition
(DRL 3.87·10⁻⁴ s, sparse FGLM 5.93·10⁻⁴ s, univariate solving 2.22·10⁻³ s),
× 720 = 2.3 s per relation, `≈ 2^{31}` relations on 8000 cores `≈ 7` days
(`2^{31}·2.3/8000/86400 = 7.1`, unverified desk check). Relation matrix only.

**Odd characteristic.** Slide 43, "Limits": "No reduction in characteristic
`p > 2`", "Experimentally, Nagao > Summation in characteristic `p`", "Can't
exploit Jacobian automorphisms (2-torsion, …)". Perspective: "if `g = 1`,
degree reduction achieved with 2-torsion. ? Can we exploit automorphisms in the
Kummer variety?"

**Degree and regularity bound.** `N` quadrics in `N` unknowns that form a regular
sequence have `d_reg ≤ N + 1` (Macaulay), degree `2^N`. Measured in this
repository at `N = 6`: F4 solving degree 7, certification degree 10 without the
staircase stop (ledger section 3, P3, and section 10).

**Desk check (unverified).** A symbolic classification over `F₂` of which
coefficients of `A²u + wB² + h₁AB` are pure squares, which carry a linear
term and which carry cross terms (script in the scratchpad, not committed):
type II, `g = 2`, `n = 3`: `N₀`, `N₅` pure squares, `N₄` square plus linear;
`g = 3`, `n = 2`, `h₁ = x`: `N₀, N₄, N₅` pure, `N₃` square plus linear
(`g − L + 1 = 4` with the chain); `g = 3`, `n = 2`, `h₁` a full cubic
(`L = 3`): **no pure-square coefficient**, `N₅` is square plus linear. So the
"+1" in `g − L + 1` appears to need a pure-square coefficient to start a chain
that absorbs the linear term, and at `L = g` I find none (section 8).

## 4. Why it could lower the Gröbner cost or the degree of regularity

Only in even characteristic, and only through `L`. Desk table of
`d_new = 2^{(n−1)((n−1)g+L−1)}` (unverified desk check, from slides 36 and 39):

| `(n, g)` | `d_old` | `L = 0` | `L = 1` | `L = 2` | `L = g` |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `(2, 3)` (JV shape) | 64 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |
| `(3, 2)` (FW example) | 4096 | 64 | 256 | 1024 | 1024 |

Assumption chain for the JV shape. (i) GHS covers of an *ordinary* `E` have
`Jac_H` isogenous over `F_q` to a Weil restriction of `E`, hence 2-rank `g`
(recalled). (ii) Then `h₁` has `g + 1` distinct zeros on `P¹`; for the JV
char-2 cover `h₁ = N(x)` is an irreducible cubic with `N(0) ≠ 0` and `∞` the only
rational zero. (iii) A change of coordinates over `F_q` can move only rational
zeros to `{0, ∞}`, so `t = 0`, `d = g`, `L = g` for every `F_q`-model.
(iv) FW's formula gives `2^{n−1} = 2` at `L = g`; my symbolic recount finds no
reducible coefficient (`1×`). So the best a char-2 JV-shaped cover could hope for
is degree `64 → 32`. Per-test time: FW's own scaling (6 variables and 6 degree
doublings gave `5·10⁴`) suggests about `6×` per halving plus one variable
removed, so `1–6×` per test (unverified desk estimate), not the `16×` degree gain
of `L = 0`. The repository's test splits as F4 `1.09·10⁶` plus solver
`5.3·10⁵` (characteristic polynomial `∝ δ³`: `2.6·10⁵ → 3.3·10⁴` at `δ = 32`).

On the repository's best cover route the Nagao test is only the descent
(53–56 % of `S` at `p = 1511`), so a perfect per-test speedup bounds the
end-to-end gain by `1/(1 − 0.56) = 2.27×`, and `6×` gives `1.88×` (unverified
desk check). Both are moot: that route is odd characteristic.

## 5. Obstructions

1. **Characteristic.** The mechanism is Frobenius additivity. In odd
   characteristic `N_i` are generic quadrics (no linearisation), FW state "no
   reduction in characteristic `p > 2`", and the repository measured ideal degree
   64 (`2^{n(n−1)g}`, Bézout attained) from `p = 53` to `1,511`. The summation
   modelling has the same degree and was measured slower (0.34 s against 0.029 s
   in FW's own comparison, slide 40).
2. **No rational-2-torsion quotient at `g ≥ 2`.** The decomposition variety lives
   in `C^m` (`m = 6`) over `J`, and translation by a 2-torsion class `T` is an
   automorphism of `J` but not of `C`: it maps the fibre over `R` to the fibre
   over `R + T`, a different decomposition problem, not a quotient of one. At
   `g = 1`, `C = J`, so translation preserves the summand curve; that is the
   FGHR/FHJRV mechanism, already measured here at `k = 5` (`416×`, still
   `2^{233}`). On `H` the extra automorphisms (hyperelliptic `ι`, bi-elliptic
   `φ`) either are already quotiented (`ι`: the system is in `x`-coordinates
   and `F = f f^ι`) or do not preserve `{x ∈ F_p}` (`φ` is a Möbius map defined
   over `F_{q³}` with coefficients outside `F_p`). FW list this as an open
   limit, not a result. This is my structural argument, medium confidence.
3. **Wrong structure for the 2-torsion that exists.** FW's best case needs
   2-rank at most 1; the weak classes (full rational 2-torsion, order divisible
   by 4 in JV12; ordinary `E` in characteristic 2) are the opposite.
4. **The premise is false for both named paths.** The cubic-GHS genus-3 route
   (`q^{1.30}`) runs smoothness tests with large primes, `q` prime, no Gröbner;
   the JV route's sieve uses a lex basis computed once, and its per-relation
   Nagao test survives only in the descent.
5. **ECC2K-130 and prime `n`.** No cover (genus 1, `2^{130} − 1`, `2^{130}`), and
   at `n = 131` the factor-base field `F_q` is `F_2`, so the `F_q`-rationality
   linearisation has nothing to act on. The RR chart carries the same squares
   and is "`S′₄` with the linear part hidden".
6. **Generic bounds.** The Nagao test is not a group-operation oracle: it uses
   the subfield structure `F_p ⊂ F_q` (factor base `≈ p`, group `≈ p⁶`), so
   Shoup and Corrigan-Gibbs–Kogan do not bound it and the structured-GGM
   requirement is met by construction. That is why the cover route is already
   an exponent regime (`p^{12/7}` against rho's `p³`); this lead only touches
   its constant.

Distance to rho for the row in question: `S/rho ≈ 0.012–0.014` at `ℓ = 2^{61.4}`
on the weak class, isogeny walk unpriced (ledger section 13 prices it above rho
below `p ≈ 8,000`). Nothing here moves it.
