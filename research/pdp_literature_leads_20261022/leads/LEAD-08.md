# LEAD-08 — Learned orderings and branching heuristics for F4 and WDSat on Semaev and PKM systems

**Verdict.** constant-factor candidate, stage level only. The card's two headline mechanisms are closed: a variable order cannot change F4's solving degree on refuted or unique-solution targets (theorem, §5.1), and it cannot lower WDSat's node count (measured, §5.2). What survives is the word-XOR cost of F4 and msolve across degree-compatible permutations, where hand-derived orders already gave 2x to 33x at stage level and 1.0x end to end.
**Confidence.** medium: the theorem and the WDSat counts were read at source; the size of the residual cost effect is unmeasured, and WDSat was measured at one order only.
**Expected gain if it works.** F4 solving degree: 0 levels on UNSAT targets (theorem). WDSat conflicts, m = 3, symmetry on, UNSAT: at most 1.04x at ℓ = 6 falling to 1.001x at ℓ = 11 (unverified desk check, §5.2), argued ceiling 2x. F4 word XORs: 1.0–2x beyond the best existing hand rule (my guess, unverified), on the `pdp_split_dreg_20261011` rows `3:1:11:4` … `3:1:17:5`. End-to-end S: 1.00x, because no leaderboard row uses F4 or WDSat as its oracle. Distance to rho is unchanged: the cheapest F4 row (`3:1:11:4`, unsplit, g = 4) projects 22.8x strong rho and 391x signed-Frobenius rho, relation phase only.
**Cost of the discriminating test.** about 2 person-days core tier (two shims, one learner), about 10 CPU-hours mechanical tier, at most 16 GB (unmeasured, capped), no GPU.

## 1. Mechanism (precise)

**Binary systems.** E/F_{2^n}: y² + xy = x³ + x² + b (Koblitz K_a, b = 1 in the corpus), V = ⟨1, z, …, z^{ℓ−1}⟩. For m = 3 the PDP is S₄(x₁,x₂,x₃,x_R) = 0 with x_i ∈ V. Weil descent gives 3ℓ Boolean unknowns c_{i,j} and n Boolean equations, field equations v² = v built in. Cell (n,ℓ) = (13,4): 12 unknowns, 13 equations. The chain [2,1] (S₃(x₁,x₂,u), S₃(u,x₃,x_R)) has 3ℓ + n unknowns and 2n equations: 25 and 26 at (13,4). For m = 4, chain [2,1,1] at (11,3): 34 unknowns. WDSat's symmetrised S₄′ model at (19,6): 51 variables (18 coordinate bits plus 33 symmetrisation bits), 52 equations (my count from thesis §7.2; it matches the CP 2020 paper's "52 equations in 51 variables" if its cell was (19,6), which that paper does not state). WDSat branches only on the mℓ coordinate bits (thesis Prop. 7.2.1).

**PKM systems** (Petit–Kosters–Messeng, per `RESEARCH_PKM_TOWER_ORACLE.md`). E/F_p, p = 2013265921, Kummer tower: m blocks of t variables y_{i,j} with y_{i,j+1} = y_{i,j}², y_{i,t−1}² = 1 (degree 2), plus chain links through one intermediate variable u0; N = mt. m = 3, t = 3: 10 variables, 11 equations (committed `.ms` file). Blocks 0 and 1 are interchangeable (S₃ is symmetric).

**Degree bounds the formulations obey.** Caminata–Gorla (arXiv 2112.05579, Thm 3.1, Cor 3.3): for every degree-compatible order σ, sd_σ(F) = max{d_F, max GB deg_σ(F)} = max_i d_{g_i} over the reduced σ-Gröbner basis; rowsp(M_d) = V_{F,d} does not depend on σ; sd_σ ≥ d_F. Upper bounds sd ≤ reg(F^h) and sd ≤ 2 d_reg − 2 are quoted from the repository ledger (recalled). WDSat: at most 2^{mℓ}/m! conflicts (thesis eq. 7.14). Measured here (all order-free reads except the F4 column): F4 growth degree 7 (unsplit) and 4 (chain) on planted targets; XL refutation degree 5, 6, ≥ 7 at ℓ = 2, 3–4, 5 (m = 3); MutantXL closure 3–4; msolve on committed PKM systems D = 5 (m = 2, N = 12–18), 6 (m = 3, N = 9–12), 7 (m = 3, N ≥ 15, lower bound), 6–8 (m = 4).

**What a permutation of variables can and cannot touch.**
- (a) Invariant: the Macaulay rank profile, D*, d_F, first fall, closure degree. Permuting variables permutes rowsp(M_d) and leaves its dimension, and whether 1 lies in it, unchanged. This holds for any degree-compatible order.
- (b) Order-dependent only through the identity of the reduced GB. For a refuted target GB = {1}; for a unique-solution target GB = {v_i + a_i} in every order. So sd_σ is the same for every σ on both. Only targets with at least two solutions can differ (for example the 3! = 6 orderings of a planted triple in an unsymmetrised system).
- (c) Order-dependent: F4's pair selection, which pivots are displaced, matrix rows, columns and fill, hence word XORs, and any overshoot of F4's growth degree above sd_σ. This is a cost effect.

## 4. Why it could lower the Gröbner cost or the degree of regularity

It cannot lower the degree of regularity or, on UNSAT and unique-solution targets, the solving degree (§1b). It can only change cost at fixed degree. The repository's evidence for that mechanism: pivots of a reduced row sit on the large variables, so splitting on the smallest displaces fewest rows (HighestFree, 2x); fixing x_m first with linear elimination shrank trees 58,955 → 7,858 reductions (4.91x stage). Each was derived from visible structure (multilinear blocks, DegRevLex pivot bias), not learned.

Assumptions a learned order rests on: A1, F4 cost is dominated by matrix shape. A2, shape depends on permutation beyond the existing hand rules. A3, a score over structural variable features finds the good permutations without test-time search. A4, a stage gain reaches S. A4 fails by the oracle ladder: after the chain-order and support-local gains matrix-F4 is still about 3x enumeration and about 60x meet-in-the-middle per target at n = 15, m = 3, and the chain-order round moved S by 1.0x.

Because degree and cost are identical across targets within a cell, "learning" reduces to cell-level search over a fixed support, which is exactly Bunch et al.'s setting; their own Table 2 shows random search and annealing competitive. The honest comparator is equal-budget search, not the default order.

## 5. Obstructions

**5.1 Theorem.** On refuted targets sd_σ = D* for every degree-compatible σ; on unique-solution targets sd_σ is the same for every σ (§1b, Caminata–Gorla Cor. 3.3). Index-calculus relation search is dominated by refuted targets (thesis Table 7.7: 149,475 UNSAT against 16 SAT at ℓ = 4, n = 24). F4's growth degree is at least sd_σ (my argument: every basis element F4 produces by step d lies in rowsp(M_d)), so the only room is overshoot, measurable as F4(identity) − D*. Weight orders that are not degree-compatible fall outside the theorem and outside Macaulay-degree meaning (Caminata–Gorla Ex. 3.4); Bunch et al.'s action space is of that kind.

**5.2 WDSat, measured.** Thesis Table 7.6 (symmetry on, UNSAT, m = 3): conflicts 43,875 / 351,353 / 2,800,335 / 22,396,994 / 179,019,409 / 1,432,191,354 at ℓ = 6…11, against 2^{3ℓ}/6 within +0.8 % at ℓ = 6–7 and +0.3 % from ℓ = 8. Desk check (unverified): against the number of non-decreasing triples C(2^ℓ+2,3) the count is 0.959 (ℓ = 6), 0.982, 0.990, 0.995, 0.997, 0.999 (ℓ = 11); the shortfall, which is all the pruning above the last level, shrinks like 2^{−ℓ}. Table 7.5, no symmetry: 0.97–0.99 of 2^{3ℓ}. With Gaussian elimination on, conflicts drop only 0.2–0.6 % (Table 7.4). The monomial graph is complete, so for any order the residual system is guaranteed linear only after N − 1 assignments (a vertex cover of K_N); reasoning, not a theorem, says conflicts then start at depth N − 1 and the gain is at most 2x. The measurements are for one order. Under `-b` the legal orbit is the ℓ! relabellings of bit significance applied to all three vectors; anything else breaks Algorithm 7.1's correctness or forfeits the 3!. For SAT targets the expected nodes to first solution is half the tree for any order not informed by the answer.

**5.3 Generic bound.** WDSat sits a factor 2^ℓ/m above the one-word enumerator and matrix-F4 is slower still (survey, oracle ladder), so Corrigan-Gibbs–Kogan and Shoup apply to them as oracles from group operations and membership tests; an ordering cannot change that line. A learned value order or a model that outputs the GB of a unique-solution system is a decomposition predictor from x_R; it is verifiable by one group check, its null is chance, and a gain would need structure on at least a q^{−1/2} fraction of points (ePrint 2026/384, per the card).

**5.4 ML-literature fit.** Kera/HAT need shape position, which needs a large field; a Boolean ideal with more than two solutions is not in shape position. They train on random U₁PU₂G systems and fail on structured out-of-distribution inputs (Katsura); n ≤ 13, F7, unverified outputs. Bunch et al. fix a support set per policy, F_32003, at most 19 variables, proxy reward.

**5.5 Repository nulls.** `closure-degree-predictor` and `hardness` above.

## 6. Minimal discriminating test (frozen protocol)

The card's design (200 instances, 150/50 split at ℓ = 4) is replaced: degree and cost are cell-level constants, so the held-out unit is the cell, and a permutation null and an equal-budget control are added.

**Core-tier prerequisites (before any run).**
- P1: `examples/pdp_perm_f4.rs` in `crypto` (about 80 lines): build the system with `pdp_split::build_split_system`, permute monomial bit masks by a seeded π, run `pq_f4_f2::groebner_basis_f4`, print growth degree, steps, `word_xors`, `matrix_rows_max`, `matrix_cols_max`; on UNSAT draws also `koblitz_bench::closure_refutation_scan` (cap 70,000 columns) for D* and the closure degree.
- P2: `csd/order_probe.py` in ml-cryptanalysis: ANF relabeller, msolve header permuter, learner, analysis.
- P3: a frozen positive control: a small Boolean system on which the shim's growth degree differs between two orders, found by seeded search before the main run.
- Data go to `s3://crypto-autoresearcher/lab-results/lead08-order-probe` after `python -m csd.lab check-store s3://crypto-autoresearcher/lab` passes.

**Gate 0 (about 1 CPU-hour).** On 8 UNSAT and 4 planted draws per cell record D*, closure degree and F4 growth degree under identity. If F4(identity) = D* on every UNSAT draw, the degree arm is closed by theorem; continue with cost arms only.

**Arm A, WDSat (about 0.5 CPU-hour).** The 60 corpus inputs, pinned binary 61c6ff3 with `-b`, regression `run.py` unchanged. Relabel ANF ids j → π(j) uniformly on all three vectors: all 120 π at ℓ = 5; 60 seeded π plus reversal at ℓ = 6. Record conflicts and verdict (certified verdict from `frozen_targets.json`). Controls: identity must reproduce 1,280,281 exactly (A/A); non-uniform π (different per vector) must give a wrong verdict on at least one planted input at ℓ = 5 (detector sensitivity); no-`-b` arm with 60 random permutations of all 3ℓ ids at (15,5), normalised by 2^{3ℓ}. Prediction: UNSAT spread max/min ≤ 1.05 per input. **Kill (closed by measurement):** best-of-orbit ratio ≥ 0.95 on every UNSAT input. Alive only if some class shows ≤ 0.8 on at least 5 inputs; the harness's own bar (20 % fewer summed median conflicts, no case > 10 % worse) is the success test for a learned π. Per-input best-of-orbit on SAT inputs sees the answer and is not reportable.

**Arm B, Boolean F4 (about 6–8 CPU-hours).** Plans unsplit [3] and chain [2,1]; cells (n,ℓ): train (11,4), (13,4); test (17,4), (19,4), (13,5), (17,5), `a` ∈ {0,1} as in the committed grid. 8 targets per cell (4 planted, 4 random, seeded). Orders per target: identity, reverse, per-vector reverse, vector-interleaved, frequency-ascending, frequency-descending, 32 random permutations, learned. Record growth degree, word XORs, matrix rows and columns. A/A: identity reproduces `pdp_split_dreg` word XORs and degree exactly for the same cell and seed.

**Learner (frozen).** Score-based order: sort variables by w·f(v), f = (bit position j, vector index, monomial counts by degree 1–6, equation count). Fit w by a (1+λ) evolution strategy on log word XORs using 64 true evaluations per training cell and plan; seeds frozen. Controls: (i) transfer control, the best of 64 random permutations on the training cell mapped to the test cell by (i,j); (ii) test-time reference, best of 64 random permutations on the test cell itself; (iii) the best hand rule; (iv) label-permutation null, the same pipeline retrained on XOR labels shuffled within each training cell, 200 repeats.

**Arm C, msolve PKM (about 2 CPU-hours).** The 14 committed systems m2_t6–t9, m3_t3–t4, m4_t2 (two targets each), `run.sh` unchanged (`-t 1 -v 2`, 5 GB cap), the permutation applied to the header variable list only; identity reproduces `results.csv` (A/A); 16 random permutations plus reverse and block-interleaved; parse with `parse.sh` (D, rounds, max rows and columns). Skip m4_t3 and above (10–20 minutes per run at m4_t3, memory-capped beyond). Whether msolve takes its grevlex order from the header is recalled, not verified; the positive control is that max rows or columns change under some permutation.

**Prediction.** Growth degree and msolve D identical in at least 99 % of runs; word XORs and matrix shapes vary across permutations.

**Kill.** The lead is closed (stays a stage diagnostic with zero S effect) unless the learned order beats, on held-out cells, the median random order by at least 20 % in at least 4 of 6 cell-plan pairs, beats control (i) and the best hand rule, and falls below the 5th percentile of the shuffled-label null, with the ratio not shrinking from ℓ = 4 to ℓ = 5. If any permutation changes the growth degree in more than 1 % of runs, the degree claim reopens and the note must be escalated with the offending cell, to replicate at larger ℓ before any other statement.
