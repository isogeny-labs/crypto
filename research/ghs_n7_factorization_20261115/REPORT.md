# The seven-plus-one factorization in a published n=7 GHS family

15 November 2026. This report supplies a **rigorous specialization of an existing theorem**, two complete Frobenius-polynomial checks, and a measured complete attack with the resulting subgroup projection. It does not identify a new eligible family or a curve on which this measured attack beats rho. The protocols were [committed before the polynomial checks](PROTOCOL.md) and [before the optimized attack](ATTACK_PROTOCOL.md). [pcc:unverified]

## The exact family and its bound

Let `k=F_q`, `q=2^s`, `K=F_(q^7)`, and `σ(z)=z^q`. Write `p₁(T)=T³+T+1` and `p₂(T)=T³+T²+1`, and `V_i=ker p_i(σ)`. Then `T⁷+1=(T+1)p₁p₂`, `K=k⊕V₁⊕V₂` as F₂[σ]-modules, and each `V_i` contains `q³` elements. Fix `a∈k` with absolute F₂ trace one. Choose `c∈k×`, one arm `i∈{1,2}`, and `v∈V_i×`, and set `β=c+v`, `b=β²`. Consider

`E/K: y²+xy=x³+a x²+b`, and `E₀/k: y²+xy=x³+a x²+c²`.

The classical two-pole Hess construction applies to `f=1/x+a+βx`. Its Frobenius module has annihilator `(T+1)p_i` of degree four. [Hess's Theorem 12](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/315278965D8D277A85812D9498236A88/S146115700000108Xa.pdf/generalising-the-ghs-attack-on-the-elliptic-curve-discrete-logarithm-problem.pdf) gives genus `2⁴−2³−1+1=8`; `Tr_(K/k)(β)=c≠0` supplies the Frobenius extension condition in Theorem 11. Among the 15 nonzero Artin–Schreier characters, seven are rational and eight are elliptic. The eight elliptic characters form one fixed point and one orbit of length seven under σ: the fixed character is `p_i(σ)f=1/x+a+c x`, which defines `E₀`; any member of the seven-orbit defines the conjugate of `E`. [Hess's Theorems 15 and 26](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/315278965D8D277A85812D9498236A88/S146115700000108Xa.pdf/generalising-the-ghs-attack-on-the-elliptic-curve-discrete-logarithm-problem.pdf) therefore give, for the descended genus-eight curve `C/k`, the exact Euler factor

`L_C(T)=(1-uT+qT²)(1-tT⁷+q⁷T¹⁴)`, where `u=q+1−#E₀(k)` and `t=q⁷+1−#E(K)`.

Consequently `#J_C(k)=#E₀(k)·#E(K)`, and Tate's finite-field isogeny theorem identifies `J_C` up to k-isogeny with `E₀×Res_(K/k)E`. This is an isogeny decomposition, **not** an explicit map from `E` to a genus-seven Jacobian. By the disjoint direct-sum decomposition, `(c,v)` and the arm are unique for every b in this slice, so for fixed normalized trace-one a there are **exactly `2(q−1)(q³−1)` distinct source b values**, a fraction `2(q−1)(q³−1)/(q⁷−1)` of the nonzero coefficient values. This counts this fixed-one-pole genus-eight slice only. It neither exhausts Hess's generalized covers nor bounds every GHS-vulnerable curve. The formula is a deduction from published constructions, not a priority claim. [pcc:unverified]

For an odd prime `r` with `#E(K)=h r`, `r∤h·#E₀(k)`, and a nonzero image `G` of order r under the Hess transfer, the exact cofactor `m=h·#E₀(k)` kills every prime-to-r component of `J_C(k)`. Because `#J=m r` and `gcd(m,r)=1`, `<G>` is its entire r-primary part. Thus **every** factor-base class B has a unique selected-subgroup coordinate L satisfying `mB=(mL)G`. Hess's [Theorem 14](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/315278965D8D277A85812D9498236A88/S146115700000108Xa.pdf/generalising-the-ghs-attack-on-the-elliptic-curve-discrete-logarithm-problem.pdf) bounds the kernel by 2-power torsion for this full-field source, so odd-prime subgroups are preserved; the exact selected maps below were additionally evaluated and checked in Magma. The condition `r∤h·#E₀` matters: if r also divides the cofactor, this simple projection formula does not certify uniqueness. [pcc:unverified]

## Exact polynomial, map, and cost receipts

**q4 exact source.** `K=F2[z]/(z^14+z^5+1)`; coefficient masks `(a,b)=(5107,483)`; `#E=16634=2·8317`. The [Magma full-polynomial receipt](receipts/q4.json) gives `(1-3T+4T^2)(1+249T^7+4^7T^14)`, `#E0=2`, and `#J=33268`. The selected prime is `r=8317`, projection multiplier `m=4`. The [earlier complete map and attack](../ghs_n7_q4_ic_20261115/REPORT.md) cost 0.62–0.63 CPU seconds versus 0.00301–0.00302 for matched rho; it used Jacobian cardinality in setup.

**q16 exact source.** `K=F2[z]/(z^28+z^3+1)`; coefficient masks `(a,b)=(65712646,406166)`; `#E=268413186=6·44735531`. The [Magma full-polynomial receipt](receipts/q16.json) gives `(1+T+16T^2)(1-22271T^7+16^7T^14)`, `#E0=18`, and `#J=4831437348`. The selected prime is `r=44735531`, projection multiplier `m=108`. The explicit map verifies `G=h(P)≠0`, `rG=0`, and `h(1234567P)=1234567G`; all 143 factor-base projections passed. The [optimized complete attack](receipts/attack_optimized.json) cost **1.590 CPU seconds** versus **0.23470 seconds** per matched verified rho solve, a **6.77-fold** penalty. [pcc:unverified]

Sage independently checked that `β=√b` has trace c=1 and `β−c` lies in arm 2 for q4 and arm 1 for q16. The q16 optimized script [differs from the original complete run](attack_optimized.magma) only in deriving `#J` from `#E·#E₀` instead of invoking the Jacobian cardinality routine; the [full q16 polynomial receipt](receipts/q16.json) independently verifies that identity. It used the same source `P` (coordinate masks `179462209, 218074333`), target `Q=1234567P`, genus-eight curve and map, 208 reconstructed relations from 8650 attempts, full rank on 143 classes, 143 identities `108B_i=(108L_i)G`, and target descent after 35 offsets. The [independent audit](receipts/attack_optimized-audit.json) replays every modular relation, rank, coordinate, and target equation; Magma checked divisor reconstruction and group equalities. Setup was 0.200 CPU seconds and total GHS 1.590; the matched 100-solve rho batch was 23.470 CPU seconds. The original cardinality-based complete run took 5.200–5.210 seconds. All costs are measured on the same exact q16 subgroup and target, not extrapolated to larger curves. [pcc:unverified]

## Formal and novelty boundaries

[CubicOrbitLedger.lean](CubicOrbitLedger.lean) checks the F₂ cubic's seven-cycle and unique fixed point by finite decision, plus both exact integer cofactor identities. Lean 4.35.0 accepted the file; the orbit proofs use Lean's standard `propext` axiom and the integer identities have no axioms. The file does **not** formalize Hess's cover, L-polynomial theorem, Tate isogeny, or the cryptographic map. The geometric proof above is a cited paper-level specialization corroborated by two full-polynomial computations. [pcc:unverified]

Prior work: the original [GHS construction](https://www.iacr.org/archive/eurocrypt2002/23320028/smart.pdf), [Teske's n=7 selector](https://www.lix.polytechnique.fr/Labo/Francois.Morain/Crypto/IsogenyParty/teske.pdf), [Hess's generalized Artin–Schreier and L-polynomial theorems](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/315278965D8D277A85812D9498236A88/S146115700000108Xa.pdf/generalising-the-ghs-attack-on-the-elliptic-curve-discrete-logarithm-problem.pdf), and the committed [two-pole family bounds](../ghs_n7_family_bounds_20261115/REPORT.md). The repository KB's Hess hit was source ID `0a5c97e284d8c195960c985ca822bf02` (revision recorded in the session log); bounded exact-equation searches for the q16 masks/order did not find a match, which does not establish publication priority. The **measured contribution here** is a faster, theorem-backed projected-cofactor implementation on a known-family instance. A potentially new vulnerable family remains unestablished: the current exact q16 attack is 6.77 times slower than rho after the optimization, and no cover outside the published constructions has passed the same map, full-cost, and prior-work gates.
