## Setting

Upstream (math-st P5.2, A003–A005) studies a prime-field curve $E$ with a
small-discriminant CM endomorphism $\omega$ of degree $d\ge2$, acting on a
subgroup $\langle G\rangle$ of prime order $r$ by a scalar $\lambda$. Write
$H=\langle\lambda\rangle\le\mathbb F_r^*$, $m=|H|$ and $k=[\mathbb F_r^*:H]$.
The $\omega$-orbit of a point $P\ne O$ is $\{\lambda^iP\}$. Q025 asks whether
detecting that two walk states lie in one orbit (an "unoriented orbit
collision", with $\omega$ nonlinear in coordinates) can beat $\sqrt r$.
Upstream's own summary data has $k\in\{2,3\}$ on its fixtures [unverified
assertion].

## (A) Orbits of a non-automorphism are long [PROVED]

**Claim.** Assume $r$ does not divide $d$. Then
$r\le\deg(\omega^m-1)\le(d^{m/2}+1)^2$, hence
$m\ge 2\log(\sqrt r-1)/\log d$.

*Proof.* Since $r$ does not divide $d=\deg\omega$, $G\notin\ker\omega$, so $\lambda\ne0$ and
$m=\operatorname{ord}(\lambda)$ is defined. $(\omega^m-1)(G)=\lambda^mG-G=O$.
The endomorphism $\omega^m-1$ is nonzero: otherwise $\omega^m=1$ and
$d^m=\deg\omega^m=1$, contradicting $d\ge2$. A nonzero isogeny $\psi$ has
$\#\ker\psi=\deg_s\psi\mid\deg\psi$, and $G\in\ker(\omega^m-1)$ has order $r$,
so $r\mid\deg(\omega^m-1)$ and $r\le\deg(\omega^m-1)$. The degree is a
positive-definite quadratic form on $\operatorname{End}(E)$ (Silverman,
AEC III.6.3), so $\sqrt{\deg}$ is a norm and
$\sqrt{\deg(\omega^m-1)}\le\sqrt{\deg\omega^m}+\sqrt{\deg 1}=d^{m/2}+1$. ∎

So the orbits of $\omega$ have length $m\ge\log_d r-O(1)$. Short orbits occur
only for automorphisms ($d=1$, where $m\mid 6$). Orbit sizes are not the
obstacle. The obstacle is that no canonical representative of an orbit is
known to be cheap when $\omega$ is not an automorphism or the Frobenius.

## (B) Same orbit means same residue character of the logarithm [PROVED]

**Claim.** Let $\chi$ be a character of $\mathbb F_r^*$ with kernel exactly $H$
(it exists and has order $k$ because $\mathbb F_r^*$ is cyclic). For
$P=sG$ and $Q=tG$ with $s,t\ne0$: $P$ and $Q$ lie in one $\omega$-orbit if and
only if $\chi(s)=\chi(t)$.

*Proof.* Same orbit $\iff Q=\lambda^iP$ for some $i\iff t=\lambda^is\iff
t/s\in H=\ker\chi\iff\chi(t)=\chi(s)$. ∎

So an orbit-collision detector is exactly an oracle for
$(P,Q)\mapsto[\chi(\log_GP)=\chi(\log_GQ)]$. With $k\in\{2,3\}$ this is the
quadratic or cubic residue character of an unknown discrete logarithm. For a
random pair it is true with probability about $1/k$, so these "collisions" are
not rare events, and the event carries about $\log_2k$ bits of information
about the logarithms. A rho walk does not need that event. It would need
canonical orbit representatives, which (B) does not provide.

## (C) Generic lower bound [PROVED]

**Model.** Shoup's generic group model for $\mathbb Z/r$ with random
encodings. The algorithm receives $\sigma(1),\sigma(x)$ for uniform
$x\in\mathbb F_r^*$. It may query the group operation and an endomorphism
oracle $\sigma(y)\mapsto\sigma(\lambda y)$, with $\lambda$ public. It outputs
a guess for $\chi(x)$.

**Claim.** After $q$ queries, success probability is at most
$1/k+(q+2)^2/(r-1)$. Advantage $\varepsilon$ therefore needs
$q\ge\sqrt{\varepsilon(r-1)}-2$.

*Proof.* Every encoding the algorithm holds is $\sigma(f(x))$ for a known
polynomial $f=a+bX$ with $a,b\in\mathbb F_r$: group operations add such
polynomials, and the oracle multiplies one by $\lambda$, so all stay linear.
Run the usual simulation that answers with fresh encodings unless two
*formal* polynomials are equal. The real and simulated games differ only if
two distinct linear polynomials agree at $x$. Each such pair agrees at most
at one point, so this happens with probability at most
$\binom{q+2}{2}/(r-1)$ over $x$. In the simulated game the transcript is
independent of $x$. Since $\chi$ takes each of its $k$ values on exactly
$(r-1)/k$ elements, any guess is correct with probability exactly $1/k$. ∎

## Conclusion against the preregistered criteria

The preregistered kill condition was "(A)–(C) proved". It is met:
**Q025 is closed in the generic group model with an endomorphism oracle.**
Any gain over $\sqrt r$ from orbit-collision detection must come from
non-generic, coordinate-level information. Specifically, it needs a way to
compute the $k$-th power residue of $\log_GP$ from the coordinates of $P$.

**Scope.** This is a generic-model closure. It says nothing about a specific
curve's coordinates. The remaining non-generic question can be stated
precisely: is there a curve family where $\chi(\log_GP)$ is computable faster
than generic?

**Why a fast character oracle would matter (lead condition, unmet).** Given
oracle access to $\chi(\log(\cdot))$ at cost $c$ per call, the queries
$Q+tG\mapsto\chi(s+t)$ give chosen-input access to a power-residue PRF keyed
by $s$. Known key-recovery attacks on the Legendre/power-residue PRF cost
about $r/M^2$ with $M$ queries (Beullens–Beyne–Udovenko–Vitto 2020;
Kaluđerović–Kleinjung–Kostić 2020; check). Balancing $Mc$ against $r/M^2$,
this beats $\sqrt r$ only when $c\ll r^{1/6}$. No such oracle is known for any
curve family. Upstream's A005 likewise planned only synthetic constraints.

**Relation to upstream's proved items.** Upstream's "$m-1$ successor queries"
lower bound treats the orbit as an opaque cycle. That is a model choice: for
Koblitz curves the Frobenius orbit is canonicalized in $O(n)$ bit operations
by cyclic shifts of a normal-basis $x$-coordinate, as used in the ECC2K-130
attack. Within (A), the Frobenius on Koblitz curves ($d=2$, orbit length 131
on ECC2K-130) is the case where a canonical representative is cheap, which is
why it gives the known $\sqrt{131}$ factor.
