# Lifting attacks on prime-field ECDLP: what can be proved

**Standing.** `[pcc:unverified]` throughout. This is a paper proof written by the lifting worker on 2026-11-12. It is not machine-checked, except for the lemmas that cite a Lean declaration from the repo audit `crypto/research/jksst_xedni_audit_20261101/lean/`; those were kernel-checked by that audit and were not re-built here (no Lean toolchain on this host). Literature results are marked **[L]**. **[L-read]** means the repo corpus records a primary read (`docs/ecdlp-hardness-corpus/sources.tsv`). **[L-recalled]** means the statement is quoted from memory and the paper was not re-read.

## 0. Setting

- `p ≥ 5` is prime and `E/F_p` is an elliptic curve.
- `S ∈ E(F_p)` has prime order `q`, with `q ≠ p` and `q > 16`. The target is `T = kS`, with `k` unknown.

A **trial** of a lifting algorithm does the following:

- **(a) Masks.** It draws `s ∈ (Z/q)^r` uniformly at random, independent of the history and of `t`. It picks `t ∈ (Z/q)^r` by any rule; the rule may depend on the history and on the coordinates of `T`.
- **(b) Points.** It forms `P_i = s_i S − t_i T`.
- **(c) Lift.** By any procedure, it outputs an elliptic curve `E'/Q` in integral Weierstrass form with good reduction at `p`, an `F_p`-isomorphism `ψ: Ẽ' → E` sending the origin to `O_E`, and points `P'_i ∈ E'(Q)` with `ψ(red_p P'_i) = P_i`.
- **(d) Search.** It searches, by any method and at any cost, for `n ∈ Z^r \ {0}` with `Σ n_i P'_i = O`. It succeeds if `Σ n_i t_i ≢ 0 (mod q)`, and then outputs `k = (Σ n_i s_i)/(Σ n_i t_i)`.

The **size** `β` of a trial is the largest bit length among the Weierstrass coefficients and the numerators and denominators of `x(P'_i)`.

Classic xedni (Silverman 2000; JKSST 2000, §3.2) is an instance with `r ≤ 9` and `β = O(log p)`:

- The plane cubic through the lifted points is converted to a Weierstrass model.
- The auxiliary `M`-conditions (curves with few points mod small `l`) only restrict how the lift is chosen.

If a construction places the origin at a lifted point `P'_0` instead of a flex, apply everything below to the differences `P'_i − P'_0`. Their masks `s_i − s_0` are still uniform and independent.

## 2. Theorem 1 (finite; conditional only on a height-ratio certificate)

**Lemma 1 (a relation over Q reduces).** If `Σ n_i P'_i = O` in `E'(Q)`, then `Σ n_i P_i = O` in `E(F_p)`.

*Proof.* With good reduction, `red_p : E'(Q) → Ẽ'(F_p)` is a group homomorphism (Silverman AEC VII.2.1 [L]); compose with `ψ`. ∎

**Lemma 2 (each fixed relation is a 1/q event, whatever the lift).** Fix `n ∈ Z^r` with `0 < ‖n‖_∞ < q`. Conditionally on the history, on `t` and on `k`, `Pr[Σ n_i P_i = O] = 1/q`.

*Proof.*
1. `Σ n_i P_i = (Σ n_i s_i − kΣ n_i t_i) S`.
2. Some `n_i` is a unit mod `q`, so `s ↦ Σ n_i s_i` maps `(Z/q)^r` onto `Z/q`, and every fibre has the same size.
3. `s` is uniform and independent of `(t, k, history)`, so the value is uniform, and it equals the fixed residue `kΣ n_i t_i` with probability `1/q`. ∎

The lift never enters this event. [Lean: `JKSSTProbability.card_dotFiber`, `card_relationEvent`; the useful variant is `card_usefulEvent = (q−1)/q²`.]

**Lemma 3 (dependence forces a bounded relation).** Let `G = ⟨P'_1,…,P'_r⟩ ⊂ E'(Q)` with `rank G ≤ r−1`. Suppose:
- `H ≥ max ĥ(P'_i)`;
- `0 < λ ≤ ĥ(x)` for every non-torsion `x ∈ E'(Q)`;
- `T ≥ #E'(Q)_tors`.

Then there is a nonzero `n` with `Σ n_i P'_i = O` and `‖n‖_∞ ≤ C`, where `C` is either of:
- `C_lean = T·⌈1 + 2r√(H/λ)⌉^{r−1}`;
- `C_sharp = 2N`, where `N` is the least integer with `(2N+1)^r > T(1 + 2rN√(H/λ))^{r−1}`.

*Proof.*
1. Let `V = G ⊗ R`, normed by `√ĥ`; this is a Euclidean norm because ĥ is positive definite [L]. The map `π : G → V` has kernel `G_tors`, of size at most `T`.
2. For `c ∈ [−N,N]^r`, the point `x_c = Σ c_i P'_i` satisfies `√ĥ(x_c) ≤ rN√H`.
3. Distinct points of `π(G)` are at least `√λ` apart.
4. Balls of radius `√λ/2` around them are therefore disjoint. Those around the points `π(x_c)` lie inside a ball of radius `rN√H + √λ/2`.
5. Hence the number of distinct `x_c` is at most `T(1+2rN√(H/λ))^{dim V}`, with `dim V ≤ r−1`.
6. When `(2N+1)^r` exceeds this, two different `c`, `c'` give the same point, and `n = c − c'` is the required relation. ∎

[Lean: `JKSSTConditionalHeightCore.bounded_relation_of_certified_height_ratio`, which uses an uncentred box and the `C_lean` cutoff, and `JKSSTPacking.card_le_fiberBound_mul_nat_pow`.]

**Theorem 1.** Suppose every possible trial output satisfies `H/λ ≤ ρ` and `#E'(Q)_tors ≤ T`, and let `C = C(r,T,ρ) < q`. Then:

1. Every trial, however adaptive within (a)–(d), has
   `Pr[P'_1,…,P'_r dependent] ≤ ε := ((2C+1)^r − 1)/(2q)`,
   and success implies dependence.
2. `Pr[success within the first N trials] ≤ Nε`. Therefore `E[#trials] ≥ (⌊1/ε⌋+1)/2 ≥ 1/(2ε)`.

*Proof of (1).* Dependence implies (Lemma 3) a relation `n ∈ [−C,C]^r \ {0}` over Q. By Lemma 1 it holds in `E(F_p)`. The vectors `n` and `−n` define the same event. So dependence lies in the union of `((2C+1)^r−1)/2` events, each of probability `1/q` by Lemma 2.

*Proof of (2).*
1. Let `E_{j,n}` be the relation event for trial `j` and vector `n`.
2. By Lemma 2, applied to the fresh mask of trial `j` conditionally on everything before it, `Pr[E_{j,n}] = 1/q` exactly.
3. The event "success in some trial `j ≤ N`" is contained in `∪_{j≤N} ∪_n E_{j,n}`, so its probability is at most `Nε`.
4. `E[τ] = Σ_{N≥0} Pr[τ>N] ≥ Σ_{N=0}^{⌊1/ε⌋} (1−Nε) ≥ (⌊1/ε⌋+1)/2`. ∎

No conditional-hazard recurrence is used anywhere.

In the corpus's terms, Theorem 1(i) is the assumption `ASM-XEDNI-TRIAL-MARGINAL` ("each adaptive xedni trial succeeds with marginal probability O(1/p)"), now derived from a height-ratio certificate. Theorem 1(ii) is the marginal-to-waiting-time step that the corpus records as `THM-HAZARD-MARGINAL-SUFFICES` (Lean). Theorem 2 below supplies the certificate unconditionally, with a polylog in place of the constant.

JKSST Theorem 4.1 is the special case `ρ = 1/(c_Lang·C_1)`, which holds under H1 and H2.

## 3. Theorem 2 (unconditional, asymptotic: classic xedni over Q)

**Theorem 2.** In setting (a)–(d) over Q, suppose every trial has size at most `β`. Then:

```
H ≤ β ln2/2 + h(j)/12 + h(Δ)/12 + 1.07,      where h(j), h(Δ), D = log|Δ_min| ≤ 12 β ln2 + O(1)
λ ≥ λ_P(D) := ½ (ln 11)^6 / (10^15 · D^5 · ln²(18193 D²))
T ≤ 16
```

so `ρ = H/λ = O(β^6 log²β)`, `C = O_r(ρ^{(r−1)/2})`, and

```
ε ≤ O_r( β^{3r(r−1)} (log β)^{r(r−1)} ) / q .
```

Every polynomial-time lift construction has `β = (log p)^{O(1)}`. For such constructions `E[#trials] ≥ q / (log p)^{O(r²)} = q^{1−o(1)}`, which is asymptotically worse than rho's `Θ(√q)`, unconditionally.

*Proof.*
1. **Upper height.** For an integral model, `ĥ(P) − ½h(x(P)) ≤ (1/12)h(j) + (1/12)h(Δ) + 1.07` (Silverman 1990, *Math. Comp.* 55, Thm 1.1 [L-recalled]; any Zimmer-type bound `|ĥ − h/2| = O(h(E))` suffices). The coefficients and the `x(P'_i)` have at most `β` bits, and `Δ`, `c_4³` are weighted-homogeneous of degree 12 in the coefficients.
2. **Lower height.** Petsche 2006, Thm 2 [L-read]: every non-torsion `P` has `ĥ(P) ≥ log|Δ_min| / (10^15 σ^6 log²(104613 σ²))`, where `σ = log|Δ_min|/log N` is the Szpiro ratio.
   - Over Q the conductor satisfies `N ≥ 11`, so `σ ≤ D/ln 11`.
   - Petsche's constant decreases in `σ`, so it is smallest at `σ = D/ln 11`, which gives `λ_P(D)` without the ½.
   - `λ_P` decreases in `D`, so the bound holds with `D` replaced by `log|Δ|` of any integral model.
   - The factor ½ covers a possible normalisation mismatch between Petsche's ĥ and Silverman's.
3. **Torsion.** Mazur [L].
4. **Combine.** Theorem 1 with `C_sharp`, where `N ≈ T(2r√ρ)^{r−1}/2^r`, gives the stated bound. ∎

**What changed relative to JKSST.**
- Lang (H2) and the comparison (H1) are dropped. The price is a polylog numerator instead of a constant.
- The corpus's research target `RT-XT-SZPIRO-XEDNI-LIFTS` asks for a *uniform* Szpiro bound on selected lifts. That bound is unnecessary for the exponent, because Petsche's constant is polynomial in `σ` and `σ ≤ log|Δ|/log 11` holds for every curve over Q. A uniform bound would only shrink the numerator from a polylog to a constant.

## 4. Neighbouring lifts

**Proposition 3 (number fields and Q-curve lifts).** Replace Q by a number field `K` of degree `d`, with a prime `𝔭 | p`. The residue degree does not matter: Lemmas 1 and 2 only need the reduction map to be a homomorphism into a group containing `⟨S⟩`.

*Statement.* If the lifted curve has at least one prime of bad reduction (`N_{K/Q} f ≥ 2`), then:
- Petsche's Theorems 1 and 2 [L-read] bound both `λ^{-1}` and `#E'(K)_tors ≤ 134861·d·σ²·log(104613 dσ²)` polynomially in `(d, β)`.
- The proof of Theorem 2 then gives `ε ≤ (dβ)^{O(r²)}/q`, and the same exponent conclusion for `d, β ≤ (log p)^{O(1)}`.

*Q-curve and endomorphism relations.*
- **Reductions are known multiples.** Suppose the algorithm also uses group endomorphisms of `E'(K)` (for a Q-curve, `μ_σ∘σ` with `σ𝔭 = 𝔭`) whose reductions act on `⟨S⟩` as known scalars. This holds whenever `σ` fixes `𝔭`: the reduced map is an endomorphism of `E/F_p`, and its eigenvalue on `⟨S⟩` is the root of its characteristic polynomial that one scalar multiplication checks. Let `O` be the order these endomorphisms generate (assumed to be an order of rank `e` in a number field), and `χ : O → Z/q` its action on `⟨S⟩`.
  - Run the pigeonhole of Lemma 3 over coefficient vectors `a ∈ O^r` that lie in a box of a Z-basis of `O`.
  - Because `ĥ(φ P) = deg φ · ĥ(P)`, `ρ` grows by at most `max deg φ`, and the box exponent becomes `e·r`.
  - A nonzero `a_i` from the bounded box has norm `< q`, so `χ(a_i) ≠ 0`. Lemma 2 therefore applies to the scalar vector `χ(a)`, and tautological relations never enter.
  - Result: `ε ≤ ((2C'+1)^{e r} − 1)/q`, still `(log p)^{O(e² r²)}/q` for bounded `e` and `max deg φ`.
- **Reductions are not known multiples.** This happens when `σ` moves `𝔭`. Then relations bring in unknown logarithms and do not determine `k`.

*Not covered.*
- Lifts with **everywhere good reduction** over `K`. Then `log|NΔ| = 0` and Petsche's bound is empty. Over Q this case cannot occur.
- Degree `d ≥ p^{Ω(1)}`.

**Proposition 4 (Silverman–Suzuki / Miller lifted index calculus).** Fix `E'/K` reducing to `E`, of rank `s`, with minimal non-torsion height `λ` and torsion at most `T`. For uniform `R ∈ ⟨S⟩`,

```
Pr[R has a lift in E'(K) with ĥ ≤ X] ≤ T (1 + 2√(X/λ))^s / q .
```

*Proof.* Lemma 3's packing applied to the lattice `E'(K)/tors` of rank `s`. ∎

*Consequences.*
- To beat `√q` with lifts of height `X = (log p)^{O(1)}`, the curve needs `s · log(1+2√(X/λ)) ≥ ½ log q − log T`. With Petsche, `λ ≥ (log p)^{-O(1)}`, so this means `s = Ω(log p / log log p)`.
- Unconditional 2-descent rank bounds, `s = O(log|Δ|/log log|Δ|)`, do not forbid such ranks. So this route is excluded **only conditionally**: it needs a rank bound for curves that reduce to `E`. Bounded ranks (the Park–Poonen–Voight–Wood heuristic) would give one. The Huang–Kueh–Tan theorem (corpus `THM-XT-HKT-LIFT-RANK`, unread) points the same way.
- **Independently, the route is circular.** An oracle that lifts random `R = aS+bT` and returns coordinates in a basis of `E'(K)/tors` yields `k`: take `s+2` calls and solve the linear system mod `q` in the unknowns (logs of the reduced basis, `k`). So the lifting step is at least as hard as the ECDLP it is meant to solve.

**Proposition 5 (p-adic lifts).** Suppose `q ≠ p`. Every homomorphism from `⟨S⟩ ≅ Z/q` into a group with no `q`-torsion is zero. This covers:
- `Ê(pZ_p) ≅ pZ_p`, which is torsion-free for `p ≥ 3`;
- `E_1(Z/p^m)`, which is a `p`-group;
- any `Z_p`-module.

So canonical (Teichmüller) lifts and formal-group logarithms give no homomorphic transfer. Smart's attack needs `q = p`.

What remains is the defect of a non-homomorphic section. The repo measured it as generic [T, quoted]:
- H110: degree `r−2` on 28 of 28 rows;
- F2: full DFT support on 21 of 21 curves, and 0 of 4,200 null sequences below `N`.

There is no proof here.

## 5. What these statements do not cover

- **Concrete sizes.** The numerator in Theorem 2 is a huge polylog. §6 and `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/ad79db08-cd5f-5158-81c2-aa769b65fafd/scratchpad/finish/lifting/out/bounds.md` compute the crossover.
- **Super-polynomial lifts.** Trials whose output size is `β ≥ q^{Ω(1/r²)}` are not excluded.
- **Number fields.** Lifts with everywhere good reduction, and degree `d ≥ p^{Ω(1)}`, are not covered.
- **Silverman–Suzuki.** Excluded only conditionally, on a rank bound.
- **Correlated masks.** Algorithms whose `s`-masks are not fresh uniform, i.e. correlated with `t` or reused, are outside (a). Lemma 2 then needs another argument.
- **Literature status.** Three theorems are inputs, not re-proved: Petsche (read in the repo corpus), Silverman 1990 (recalled), and positive definiteness of ĥ (recalled). None is formalised.

## 6. Concrete sizes

Numbers are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/ad79db08-cd5f-5158-81c2-aa769b65fafd/scratchpad/finish/lifting/out/bounds.md`, generated by `code/bounds.py` and `code/crossover.py`. The inputs are the lift sizes measured in the toy (7-coefficient Weierstrass path, bits 8–16):
- `max ĥ(P'_i) ≈ 0.69b…2.75b` nats for `r = 2…6`;
- `log|Δ_W| ≈ 13.9b…74.9b` nats;
- `ρ_G ≈ 1.02–1.15` (cell medians).

All are linear least-squares fits, extrapolated.

- **Theorem 2 (unconditional) bites only above a threshold.** It excludes beating rho only from `b ≥ 265, 842, 1773, 3134, 5015` bits for `r = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6` (`T = 16`, sharp cutoff). At 256 bits it is vacuous for every `r ≥ 2`.
- **Theorem 1 (conditional on `ρ`) at 256 bits.**
  - With `ρ` equal to the measured `ρ_G` (an optimistic stand-in, since the true `λ ≤ λ_G`), the slack is `+122, +115, +98, +74, +42` bits for `r = 2…6`. It is `+0.2` bits for `r = 7`, and negative for `r = 8` (`−52`) and `r = 9` (`−114`).
  - With `4ρ_G`, `r = 6` keeps `+12` bits. With `10ρ_G`, `r = 6` drops to `−7.7` bits.

So even granting Lang-type constants, the JKSST "(2C_2)^r/p" bound does not exclude `r = 8, 9` at cryptographic sizes. The exclusion there rests only on the asymptotic Theorem 2 and on the toy evidence.
