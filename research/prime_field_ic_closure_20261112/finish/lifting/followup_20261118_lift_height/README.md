## 1. Paper results

### 1.1 Lemma 3H needs λ only on the subgroup generated by the points

In Lemma 3H the only use of λ is step 6, covol(L) ≥ (λ₁(L)²/γ_k)^{k/2}. Here L is spanned by the circuit's points, and L ⊆ ⟨P′₁..P′_r⟩/tors. So the hypothesis "λ ≤ ĥ(x) for all non-torsion x ∈ E′(Q)" can be weakened to:

> λ ≤ ĥ(x) for all non-torsion x ∈ ⟨P′₁..P′_r⟩, or even λ ≤ λ₁(L) for the circuit lattice L.

The full Mordell–Weil minimum λ_MW therefore never enters, and the calibration of λ_MW against λ_G (§4 of the 2026-11-15 round, and arm E here) is not part of the certificate. This is a paper argument with no machine check.

### 1.2 Explicit Lang-type bounds cannot supply the certificate (arm A)

- **Required constant.** The required λ at 256 bits is H(256)/ρ_max(r). H and log|Δ| come from linear fits over 12–32 bits, using the unscaled short model.
- **Why this comparison favours a proof.** log|Δ_model| ≥ log|Δ_min|, so the computed c_req is a lower bound on what is actually needed.

| construction, r | H(256) nats | log\|Δ\|(256) nats | λ required | c_req = λ_req/log\|Δ\| | gap vs Hindry–Silverman (σ=1) | gap vs Petsche Thm 2 (σ=1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O, 7 | 1,314 | 1.93·10⁵ | 40.3 | 2.1·10⁻⁴ | 3.4·10¹¹ | 2.8·10¹³ |
| O, 8 | 2,804 | 4.26·10⁵ | 259.6 | 6.1·10⁻⁴ | 9.8·10¹¹ | 8.1·10¹³ |
| tangent, 9 | 12,940 | 3.07·10⁶ | 2,620 | 8.5·10⁻⁴ | 1.4·10¹² | 1.1·10¹⁴ |

- **The constants.** σ ≥ 1 always holds, because Ogg gives v_p(N) ≤ v_p(Δ). Both constants decrease in σ, so σ = 1 is their best case.
  - Petsche 2006 Thm 2 (d = 1): c = 1/(10¹⁵ σ⁶ log²(104613 σ²)), from the corpus record of S-XT-PETSCHE-2006.
  - Hindry–Silverman: c = 2/((20σ)⁸·10^{4σ+1.1}), recalled and not re-read.
- **Lang's conjecture.** Even with an absolute constant, Lang would need c ≥ 8.5·10⁻⁴. On the toy subgroups the empirical ratio λ_G/log|Δ| has a minimum of 0.0061–0.0066 for the O-origin construction. For the tangent construction it is about 0.001, against a c_req of 1.3–8.5·10⁻⁴. That is consistent with a Lang constant of the conjectured kind, but it proves nothing.
- **No other route.** A local-height decomposition does not help: lower bounds on λ_v at the bad primes cost about (1/24)log|Δ|, which swamps log p.

## 2. Measurements

### 2.1 Constructions

**O-origin, r ≤ 8.** This is the 2026-11-15 `xedni_cubic.py` path. The 9-monomial cubic passes through the r lifts and through O = (0:1:0).

**Tangent-origin, r ≤ 9.** This construction is new (`code/lh_toy.py --origin tangent`).
1. **The cubic.** Take the 10-monomial cubic through the r lifts with u ≡ c·u_E (mod p). For r = 9 the integral kernel is 1-dimensional, and it reduces to E automatically whenever ΣP_i ≠ O.
2. **The origin.** O′ is the third intersection of the tangent line at lift 1. It is rational, and the check red(O′) = −2P₁ ran on every trial.
3. **To Weierstrass form.** A unimodular change of coordinates sends O′ to (0:1:0); a shear avoids Z′ = 0. The 2026-11-15 Washington map then gives the Weierstrass model.
4. **Group points and masks.** The group points are P_i − Q₀ with Q₀ = −2P₁. Their masks c_i + 2c₁ are uniform and independent.

A plane cubic through fixed lifts carries at most 9 points, so r = 9 independent group points requires an origin outside the lifted set. The tangent point is the cheapest rational choice.

### 2.2 Height estimator (Deviation 3) and its control

- **The problem.** The 5-doubling Néron–Tate Gram matrix of the 2026-11-15 code underestimates ρ: by 21% on the tangent construction, and by up to 14% on small control curves.
- **Pass 2 estimator.**
  - Heights come from the unscaled short model, keeping the points rational.
  - The Gram matrix is taken at 3, 4 and 5 doublings, and the reported matrix is the Richardson value G₅ + (G₅ − G₄)/3.
- **Control against PARI** (`out/validate_heights.jsonl`). The test set is 36 curves with factorable Δ (r = 3, 5, 7; e = 4, 8), compared with `ellheightmatrix/2`.

  | 5-doubling | 13.8% | — | 0.138 |
- **Consistency on the toys.** Across all pass-2 cells, the Richardson values from (3,4) and from (4,5) agree within 0.71%.
- **Pass 1.** It is kept in `out/pass1_n5_scaled/`. Its O-origin ratios agree with pass 2 to within 0.02 at 24–32 bits. Its tangent r = 9 ratios (3.29–3.33) are biased low.

**Why the tangent construction sits near 4.5.** If the lifted points behaved like an orthogonal frame v_i with equal heights h (the O-origin data approach this, with ρ → 1), then:
- the group points would be w₁ = 3v₁ and w_i = v_i + 2v₁;
- H = ĥ(w₁) = 9h;
- λ = min ĥ(v_i − v_j) = 2h;
- so ρ = 4.5.

The construction's ρ is structural, not noise. Lemma 3H's raw ρ for r = 9 therefore sits close to the threshold of 4.94 by construction.

### 2.4 Fixed-basis variant (not preregistered; exploratory)

**Why a fixed basis is allowed.** Lemma 2 and the union bound hold for any *fixed*, data-independent unimodular change of the group points. Masks transform unimodularly mod q, so they stay uniform and independent, and the event "dependent" is unchanged.

**The T3 basis.** T3 = {w₂+w₃−w₁, w₂−w₁, …, w_r−w₁}. In the frame model it has max height 3h against λ = 2h, so ρ = 1.5. The measurements in that basis:

| case | max ρ in T3 basis | 256-bit margin |
|---|---|---|
| r = 9, honest | 1.135–1.189 | about +74 bits |
| dependent tangent trials, ρ_L | ≤ 2.36 | ≥ +38.5 bits |

### 2.5 Arm D detail (6–8 bits, `out/depD/`)

**How ρ_L is computed.**
- The dependent trials are those whose exact relation over Q was verified by Jacobian arithmetic.
- ρ_L = H/λ₁(L), where L is the circuit lattice. It is computed from a unimodular completion of the primitive relation vector.
- The relation residual nᵀGn/H is at most 9.4·10⁻⁴.

**r = 9, tangent construction.**
- The dependent ρ_L values were 5.015, 3.577, 3.093 and 2.906 at 6 bits; 5.088 and 4.996 at 7 bits; and 5.024 at 8 bits.
- The independent maxima in those cells were 4.70, 4.77 and 4.73.

**Mechanism.** Dependence means L is finer than every proper sub-span, so λ₁(L) is pulled down. The upward shift is therefore expected, not an artefact.

**What it implies for r = 9.** The r = 9 exclusion relies on the random-map heuristic exactly where that heuristic is weakest. The T3 basis removes the problem in this sample.

## 4. Open or blocked

1. **No proof.** Any unconditional r ≥ 7 exclusion at 256 bits needs a covolume-ratio (height-ratio) bound for the construction's lattices, and the explicit Lang-type constants are 10¹¹–10¹⁴ too small. Such a bound would have to be specific to the construction, not a statement about all of E′(Q).
2. **The heuristic is empirical.** Turning the toy ratios into a probability bound still assumes that dependent outputs at 256 bits have ratios like those measured. Arm D shows a measurable upward shift under dependence at 6–8 bits, and it crosses the r = 9 threshold there.
3. **The bound depends on the construction.** Lemma 3H's bound depends on the construction and on the basis chosen. A construction whose origin is far from the lifts inflates H without changing dependence. A per-construction fixed basis (like T3) is needed, and choosing it is analyst work, not a theorem.
4. **Extrapolation range.** The toys run at 12–32 bits. All trends point down, but 256 bits is an extrapolation by a factor of 8.
5. **Not checked here.**
   - Lemma 3H and §1.1 have no Lean check.
   - The Hindry–Silverman constant is recalled.
   - The T3 analysis was not preregistered.
6. **Main not pulled.** `git pull origin main` filled the disk: it left a 2 GB temporary pack in `crypto/.git/objects/pack/`, which was removed after the pull failed. These runs used the local checkout, on branch `claude/prime-field-techniques-89ma6o`.
