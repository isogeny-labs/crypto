A finite rational two-torsion point on short Weierstrass y^2=F(x) has y=0, hence F(x)=0. Over a proved prime field, X^p-X has exactly all field roots. Compute h=(X^p-X) mod F, and emit U,V with U F+V h=1. Recomputing h by modular exponentiation and the Bezout identity certifies no finite rational two-torsion. This directly supplies the torsion-cut theorem criterion without a group-order implementation or point-count assumption. The standard generator is checked on the actual NIST curve. This is a theorem applicability certificate, not enumerated large-instance roots or a speed comparison.
