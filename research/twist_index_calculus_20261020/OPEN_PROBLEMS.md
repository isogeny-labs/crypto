# Open discriminating problems after the quartic twist24 panel

22 October 2026. These are implementation and evidence gaps, not proofs that an index-calculus improvement cannot exist. The [results report](QUARTIC_TWIST24_REPORT.md) and [frozen protocol](QUARTIC_TWIST_LINE_PROTOCOL.md) preserve the completed experiments.

## Polynomial relation-finder cost for the two invariant lines

**Observed:** The native four-factor meet-in-the-middle oracle independently replayed 69,045 residual queries and measured a larger hit yield for `x∈Fp ∪ t²Fp`. It uses an explicit signed-pair table whose cost grows with the physical base. The completed registered S5 pilot ran `gaudry_quartic::SymmetrisedS5` and `solve_s5_subspace` on a hit and miss at each of `p=37,73,97`; all six results matched the exact group oracle and each solve cost roughly `1.16–1.21×10¹² Fp M`. These were two queries per curve, not the full relation streams. A group oracle's hit rate is not a polynomial solver's work per relation.

**Blocker:** No polynomial `S5` formulation for the tagged line-0/line-2 union, full single-target S5 relation collection, or comparison to the same computation in msolve/Magma has been measured on this canonical panel. Without these, the effect of the 24 action on polynomial-system degree, solve count, duplicate roots and whole-method cost remains unknown.

**Exit criteria:** Derive `S5` for the exact `E/Fp⁴` model with factor variables restricted to line 0 or tagged line 0/2; prove the substitution corresponds to the group equation; cross-check every returned decomposition against the signed group oracle on a frozen full run and retain extraneous/censored roots. Run both systems on identical residuals, use the same one-target stopping rule and recorded source hashes, and compare the native solver with msolve on identical exported systems and host (Magma if available; otherwise name its absence). Record field operations, Gröbner reductions, memory, failures, and full cold `S` with the matched 24-fold rho. A positive result would establish a concrete polynomial relation-finding improvement on the tested panel only; larger-size scaling would still be open.

**Next bounded test:** On `p=37,73,97` and seeds `0,1,2`, replay the entire registered residual stream rather than the completed six-query sample, with an explicit per-instance time and memory budget. Expand to all seven curves only after the first three complete without a correctness mismatch. A solver timeout is retained as a censored result, not converted into a proof of infeasibility.

## Whole-method pricing and larger invariant bases

**Observed:** The mixed base raises same-target hit yield on all seven curves. Counted `Fp` multiplications favor it across all three seeds at `p=229,241`, but row operations increase everywhere. The two-dimensional invariant-space census tested every candidate on all eight curves and found `72–56,376` physical abscissae, including 72 at the one-dimensional empty-line control `p=13`. A bounded p=13 exact-oracle IC pilot recovered `[271]G` in seven queries with 4,041 decompositions and rank seven; an independent hash oracle replayed every query. On those same seven targets, the twist-quotient pair table preserved the complete decomposition sets using 426 pair representatives instead of 5,112 full entries, but its canonical lookup used 96.0 million versus 7.03 million query `Fp M`. At p=337 the full signed pair-table construction would require 3,178,197,000 entries before hash collisions, so larger bases have a separate resource problem. The main 42-run suite records setup/rho group additions but not per-phase IC-stream group additions, hash probes, table allocations or an admitted 0/0.1/1 GAE band. All wall times are shared-host L0.

**Blocker:** These missing counters prevent a complete cold one-target `S`, a ranked IC-versus-rho result or a leaderboard row under `docs/VALIDATION_GATE.md`. The 2D census and one tiny p=13 pilot cannot determine whether `x∈Fp+t²Fp` or a differently scaled invariant subspace gives a better total cost at larger `p`.

**Exit criteria:** Build an isolated single-arm producer that emits exclusive setup, pair-table, relation, matrix, target-check and rho counters including group operations, hash/table work and memory; charge the 0/0.1/1 GAE band on both IC and matched 24-fold rho. Run A/A and interleaved ABAB at least five rounds through `tools/isolated_bench.py` with one and many threads, independently replay every target, and compare a frozen baseline on the same curve and point. Promote a row only after a `VALIDATION.json` cites replay, validator and strongest rho reference. The two-dimensional exact liftable size is now known; measure actual table memory and set a cap before relation runs beyond p=13. A gain in one cost projection alone does not meet this exit criterion.

**Next relation-finder implementation:** A tagged orbit index could keep one pair tuple per orbit while indexing every transformed sum abscissa by a small `(orbit,action)` tag, avoiding per-lookup canonicalization. Freeze the exact same seven p=13 residuals and compare complete decomposition sets, setup/query operations, hash probes and allocated bytes against both measured tables. Its performance is an untested hypothesis; the direct quotient's 12-fold entry reduction alone does not establish a memory or time win.

## Concrete field proof in Lean

**Observed:** [QuarticTwistSymmetry.lean](QuarticTwistSymmetry.lean) proves the abstract fourth-to-eighth iterate, composed eigenvalue and row covariance implications. The concrete norm and line formulas are handwritten and checked by native enumeration on the registered curves.

**Blocker:** There is no Lean model of `Fp[t]/(t⁴−d)` with the explicit twist isomorphism, norm identity, `ψ⁴=[-1]`, exact stable-line fibers and orbit counts. The conditional lemmas are not presented as a formal proof of those concrete facts.

**Exit criteria:** Define the finite field and curve group in a pinned Lean project; prove irreducibility, the twist map, the norm identity and the stable-line action under the stated `p≡1 mod12` and odd-prime subgroup assumptions; derive the zero-line and 12/24-orbit conclusions; pass the proof gate with no research axioms or `sorry`, and retain an axiom audit receipt. This would formalize the geometry; it would not by itself measure an IC cost.
