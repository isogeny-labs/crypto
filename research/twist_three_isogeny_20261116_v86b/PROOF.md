# Twist-assisted3isogeny survey
For short curves at odd primes p!=3, twisting by nonsquare delta gives A_t=delta^2A,B_t=delta^3B and #E_t=2(p+1)-#E. We prove p and standard prime N via FLINT fmpz_is_prime, check G,N*G=O,nonsingularity and Hasse uniqueness; hence #E=N. Neither E nor its twist has rational2torsion because both orders are odd. A rational3torsion point on the twist exists iff3 divides its order, by Cauchy's theorem. A bounded point search must report unresolved if it fails, never false absence.

For exact-order3 point T=(r,s), kernel{O,T,-T}, Velu's short-curve target is A'=A-10(3r^2+A),B'=B-14(5r^3+3Ar+2B). Sum kernel coordinate differences to map every nonkernel point; map kernel toO. Every candidate satisfies source curve,3T=O and the3division polynomial. Target membership and homomorphism are checked independently on complete tiny groups and fixed actual-field samples; complete tiny inverse-fiber reconstruction retains all relation tuples. Actual NIST checks construct auxiliary curves, not all roots or a timing comparison.

An original prime-order E(K) has no3kernel. A3isogeny arising from the twist transports to a K-defined isogeny on E with nonrational kernel; its map on E(K) is injective. Thus a rational twist kernel does not supply free compression of original rational points. It can help genuine anti-coordinate/twist components only after exact torsion-cut/type routing or a declared factor-base redesign. Both characteristic3 and larger root families need separate mechanisms; no3isogeny code here is applied in characteristic3.
