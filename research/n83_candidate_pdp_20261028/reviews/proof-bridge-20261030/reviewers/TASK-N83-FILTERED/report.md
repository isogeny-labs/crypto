The mathematical dependency used here is the hypothesis that every homogeneous syzygy of weighted degree at most 16 on the complete original top-generator list is generated by homogeneous Koszul relations in the full polynomial ring. This review assumes that hypothesis; it neither establishes it nor substitutes a statement about a smaller quotient or a subset of generators. Once that hypothesis is supplied, the deductions below are complete mathematical arguments. No Lean proof of these arguments was present in this review packet or checked in this review.

Caminata and Gorla's [Solving degree, last fall degree, and related invariants](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.05579), Definition 1.5, defines V_d by linear closure and degree-bounded multiplication. Section 1 explains why repeated row reduction and multiplication can enlarge the initial Macaulay row space. Definitions 2.5–2.6 and Theorem 2.8 give the degree-fall convention used here. I use those definitions; the proofs that follow were worked through directly.

1. Bounded representation descent.

Let R=k[x_1,...,x_N], remove any zero input generator, let d_i=deg(f_i), and let p_i be the highest homogeneous component of f_i. Set

    W_d = {sum_i a_i f_i : deg(a_i)+d_i <= d for every nonzero a_i}.

This is the same vector space as the span of the bounded products. Write E=direct_sum_i R(-d_i), so that a vector b is homogeneous of weighted degree s precisely when b_i is homogeneous of degree s-d_i, with negative-degree components zero.

Take nonzero h in W_d, deg(h)=e, and a bounded representation a=(a_i). Let s=max_i(deg(a_i)+d_i). If s>e, write b_i=(a_i)_(s-d_i). Cancellation of the homogeneous degree-s component of h gives

    sum_i b_i p_i = 0.

By the hypothesis, write

    b = sum_(i<j) c_ij (p_j e_i - p_i e_j),
    deg(c_ij)=s-d_i-d_j.

Replace the top-form Koszul vectors by their affine analogues:

    k = sum_(i<j) c_ij (f_j e_i - f_i e_j).

Then sum_i k_i f_i=0 identically. The degree-(s-d_i) component of k_i is exactly b_i. Thus a'=a-k represents the same h, and every nonzero component now has deg(a'_i)+d_i<s. All lower affine terms really are lower in this weighted filtration; no term above s is introduced. Repetition terminates because s is a nonnegative integer and cannot pass below e while representing nonzero h. Consequently h has a representation of bound e, and

    W_d intersect R_(<=e) = W_e   for 0<=e<=d<=16.

The zero polynomial needs no leading form or degree-descent invocation: use its zero representation. Similarly, zero generators must be removed before defining d_i. The native helper's convention degree(empty)=0 is harmless for its storage header but is not a mathematical reason to give zero a highest homogeneous component.

This proves the reduction claimed in PRIME_FILTERED_FUNCTIONAL_DESIGN.md:49–61. The coefficient degree s-d_i-d_j is essential. Merely knowing that some quotient syzygies vanish, or that some leading products cancel, would not provide this homogeneous lift.

2. Closure under all allowed multiplication.

For nonzero h in W_d, the preceding proof supplies h=sum_i b_i f_i with deg(b_i)+d_i<=deg(h). If a is nonzero and deg(ah)<=d, the polynomial-ring domain property gives deg(ah)=deg(a)+deg(h). Therefore every product a b_i f_i has degree at most d, so ah is in W_d. Products with a=0 or h=0 are immediate.

W_d contains the admitted inputs of degree at most d and is a vector space. Its multiplication closure therefore gives V_d subset W_d by the minimal definition of V_d. Conversely, every bounded product a_i f_i lies in V_d by that definition, giving W_d subset V_d. Hence W_d=V_d for d<=16. This proof also covers any finite sequence of row reductions and allowed multiplications: none of those operations escapes the already closed W_d.

The use of the full polynomial ring matters. The degree-additivity step must not be applied inside the idempotent or square-zero quotient, which has zero divisors and degree drops. Those quotients below are tools for computing a representative or defining a functional; they do not replace R in this closure argument.

A relevant leading component of h in W_d belongs to the corresponding homogeneous component of (p_1,...,p_s). In particular, every degree-16 leading form of V_16 lies in the degree-16 top ideal.

The required missing-hypothesis control fails exactly where it should. In k[U], take f_1=U^2 and f_2=U^2-U. Then U=f_1-f_2 belongs to W_2 while W_1=0. The weighted-degree-2 top syzygy (1,-1) is not Koszul: the Koszul vector (U^2,-U^2) starts in weighted degree 4. To exhibit a genuine repeated-multiplication enlargement for the same generators, work in k[U,V]. Multiplying the newly obtained U by V gives UV in V_2, but W_2=span{U^2,U}, so UV is not in W_2. The functional extracting the coefficient of UV annihilates (U^2)_2 yet is nonzero on UV in V_2. Thus removing the syzygy hypothesis would make precisely the disputed inference false.

3. The functional kills the whole top ideal.

Use normalized selectors z_i=w_i b_i in the last block, all w_i nonzero, let X=sum_(i=0)^(2r) z_i, and retain the terminal intermediate U as an unrestricted indeterminate. Define

    A = k[U,z_0,...,z_(2r)]/(z_0^2,...,z_(2r)^2),
    H = product_(j=0)^(r-1) (z_(2j)-z_(2j+1)).

Let pi:R->A kill every variable except this last block and U. For homogeneous v of degree r+3, set

    lambda(v) = coefficient of U^2 z_0...z_(2r) in H*pi(v).

This is a linear functional on the full homogeneous polynomial space before quotienting.

Each selector-square top form maps to zero. Each nonterminal S3 top form also maps to zero. In particular, the penultimate equation can contain U as its third argument, but its first two arguments both map to zero. Its quartic top form

    (a-b)^2 c^2 - 2(a+b)ab c + a^2 b^2

therefore vanishes even when c maps to U. The terminal equation has top form U^2 X^2.

For every paired factor, (z_a-z_b)(z_a+z_b)=0 in A. Hence HX=H z_(2r), and HX^2=0. It follows that H*pi(p_i)=0 for every top generator. Since pi is a ring homomorphism, for every polynomial multiplier q,

    H*pi(q p_i) = pi(q) H*pi(p_i) = 0.

Taking the indicated coefficient proves annihilation of the entire degree-(r+3) component of the top ideal, including arbitrary multipliers involving U, retained selectors, or eliminated variables. This is stronger than testing finitely many multipliers or just evaluating the generators.

The terminal certificate's older source.projection field says that all variables outside the last selector block are sent to zero. That map also sets U=0 and can serve its older claim about the terminal syzygy coordinate H. It cannot be used as the present coefficient functional's projection. The design document correctly retains U, and the witness circuit explicitly marks U_is_unbounded_indeterminate=true. A downstream proof record must name these two distinct projections instead of treating the reused metadata string as the definition of lambda. This is a documentation boundary, not a counterexample to the intended functional.

4. The functional separates the actual witness, with all cancellations included.

Let N be canonical normal form modulo q_i=z_i^2-w_i z_i in the last block only, leaving U unrestricted. Put A_i=N(H X^i), i=0,1,2. Direct expansion of F=S3(U,X,t) gives F=B_0(U)+X B_1(U)+X^2 B_2(U), where

    B_0 = (4-12t) + (-4t-12)U + t^2 U^2,
    B_1 = (-4t-12) + (-2t^2-4)U - 2t U^2,
    B_2 = t^2 - 2t U + U^2.

These match prime_source_functional.rs:34–46 and the saved circuit. Thus

    g=N(HF)=A_0 B_0+A_1 B_1+A_2 B_2,
    [U^2]g=C=A_2-2t A_1+t^2 A_0.

Here is a direct derivation of the pairing formula. Write paired variables a_j=z_(2j), b_j=z_(2j+1), and c=z_(2r). Put P_j=a_j-b_j, Q_j=w_(2j)a_j-w_(2j+1)b_j, and H_j=product_(k!=j)P_k. The top relevant selector components are

    (A_1)_(r+1) = cH,
    (A_2)_(r+1) =
        w_(2r)cH + 2c sum_j Q_j H_j
        + sum_j (w_(2j)-w_(2j+1)) a_j b_j H_j.

There is no degree-(r+2) component of A_2, because HX^2 is zero modulo selector squares. In the square-zero algebra,

    P_j^2=-2a_j b_j,
    P_j Q_j=-(w_(2j)+w_(2j+1))a_j b_j,
    P_j a_j b_j=0.

Multiplying the displayed selector components by H and extracting the full selector product therefore yields

    lambda(g_(r+3)) = (-2)^r (sum_i w_i-2t).

The t^2 A_0 term has selector degree r and contributes nothing to this coefficient. The expression already includes cancellation between the A_2 and -2t A_1 terms; one must not infer nonzero g from just one of those terms. Lower powers of U cannot cancel its U^2 coefficient. Also, deg(A_0)=r and deg(A_1),deg(A_2)<=r+1, so deg(g)<=r+3. A nonzero displayed pairing consequently proves both g!=0 and deg(g)=r+3, rather than presupposing the degree.

5. The stored derivation really does place g in V_17.

This does not require the top-syzygy hypothesis. In normalized coordinates deg(H)=13 and deg(F)=4, so HF is an allowed bounded product of degree 17. Reduce its selector powers by q_i=z_i^2-w_i z_i. At each reduction of a monomial M containing z_i^2, subtract the appropriate multiple of (M/z_i^2)q_i. That multiple has degree deg(M)<=17; the newly introduced term has degree one less. Consequently division terminates with

    g=HF-sum_i a_i q_i,
    deg(a_i)+2<=17.

Every term on the right belongs to W_17, hence to V_17. This reconstructs the claimed bound from the actual equation and normal-form operation, rather than accepting the certificate field affine_derivation_degree_upper_bound=17 as proof by itself. The saved A factors and B circuit describe exactly that normal form; the native replay verifies their contents and assembles the full resulting g.

Combining the conditional equality V_16=W_16 with annihilation and the nonzero pairing gives g notin V_16. Since g has degree 16 and lies in V_17, its minimum filtered derivation degree is exactly 17. The descent/intersection identity excludes earlier positive filtered degree falls. Therefore the asserted first filtered fall at 17 and LFD>=17 follow under the stated hypothesis. No upper bound for later filtered falls is inferred.
