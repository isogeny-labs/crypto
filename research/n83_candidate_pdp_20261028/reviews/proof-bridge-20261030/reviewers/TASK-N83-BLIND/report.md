**1. A lower obstruction: no non-Koszul syzygy below s+4.**

The squares forming Q are a regular sequence. Their Koszul complex resolves R/Q. Tensoring this resolution with the free Koszul complex on (2) gives a graded quasi-isomorphism

\[
K(z_{i,j}^{\,2},H_0,\ldots,H_*)\longrightarrow K(H_0,\ldots,H_*;A).
\tag{3}
\]

First Koszul homology is precisely the module of syzygies modulo pairwise Koszul syzygies. It therefore suffices to prove vanishing of the degree-d first homology on the right of (3) for d<s+4.

Here is a derivation of the squarefree block decomposition needed for that proof. For one block,

\[
B_n=k[z_1,\ldots,z_n]/(z_1^2,\ldots,z_n^2),\qquad X=z_1+\cdots+z_n.
\]

As a graded module over k[X], it is a direct sum of strings

\[
\big(k[X]/X^{n-2r+1}\big)(-r),\qquad 0\leq r\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor.
\tag{4}
\]

The multiplicity of the string starting in degree r is
\(\binom nr-\binom n{r-1}\). The multiplicity formula follows by comparing the dimensions in degree r once the string lengths are known.

For completeness, the lengths in (4) can be proved by induction without invoking a characteristic-zero statement. Suppose an old string is generated by v, has length L, and starts in degree r. Tensor with k[y]/(y^2), and let E=X+y. The long string generated by v has length L+1, since

\[
E^jv=X^jv+jX^{j-1}yv,\qquad E^Lv=LX^{L-1}yv\ne0.
\]

For L>1, the vector

\[
h=Xv-(L-1)yv
\]

starts in degree r+1 and generates a string of length L-1. Indeed
\(E^{L-1}h=0\) and \(E^{L-2}h=X^{L-1}v-X^{L-2}yv\ne0\).
At every common degree, the coefficient matrix of these two strings in the basis \(X^jv,X^{j-1}yv\) has determinant -L. Thus the strings give a direct sum because L is nonzero in k. For L=1 there is only the long string. Starting with the one-dimensional block at n=0 proves (4); every integer inverted is at most n. In the present field all are invertible.

This is consistent with the large-characteristic squarefree Lefschetz theorem in Phuong and Tran, Theorem 2.7, which explicitly permits characteristic greater than n. The proof above specifies the decomposition and characteristic check actually used here. Stanley's original paper provides historical context for the Lefschetz approach, but no characteristic-zero theorem is silently transported to the supplied field. [Phuong–Tran](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.13548), [Stanley](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/42.pdf).

Tensor (4) over all m selector blocks. Each direct summand of A is, up to a total grading shift \(R_0=\sum_i r_i\), a module

\[
C=k[x_0,\ldots,x_{m-1},U_0,\ldots,U_{m-3}]/(x_i^{e_i}),
\qquad e_i=n-2r_i+1=2s+2-2r_i,
\tag{5}
\]

where multiplication by X_i is multiplication by x_i. Each e_i is at least 2.

Use lexicographic order

\[
x_0>\cdots>x_{m-1}>U_0>\cdots>U_{m-3}.
\]

The leading monomials of the quartics (2), considered before the truncations in (5), are

\[
g_0=x_0^2x_1^2,\qquad
g_j=U_{j-1}^{\,2}x_{j+1}^{\,2}\ (1\leq j\leq m-3),\qquad
g_*=U_{m-3}^{\,2}x_{m-1}^{\,2}.
\tag{6}
\]

They involve disjoint groups of variables. Filter the Koszul complex by this monomial order, shifting each exterior generator by the exponent vector of its leading monomial. At each fixed ordinary total degree the filtration is finite. Its associated graded complex is the Koszul complex of (6) acting on C. A leading monomial that is zero because of a truncation gives a zero associated graded map; this is included, rather than discarded, in the following homology calculation.

Because the variable groups in (6) are disjoint, the monomial Koszul complex is a tensor product over k of one-generator complexes. Its first homology is the direct sum of a first homology contribution from one factor, tensored with zeroth homology from the others. For a factor \(x_i^2U^2\), the minimum ordinary degree of a nonzero annihilating coefficient is e_i-2. For the factor \(x_0^2x_1^2\), it is \(\min(e_0-2,e_1-2)\). These assertions follow directly from the monomial bases of the truncated polynomial rings: multiplication can vanish only when a truncated x exponent reaches its bound; U has no truncation.

The syzygy's total degree includes the quartic's degree four and the shift R_0. Every possible first-homology contribution therefore has degree at least

\[
4+\sum_i r_i+(e_j-2)
\ \geq\ 4+r_j+e_j-2
=4+2s-r_j\ \geq\ s+4
\tag{7}
\]

for a suitable j. For g_0 choose the j giving the smaller bound. The other zeroth-homology factors have no negative degrees.

Consequently the associated graded complex has zero first homology below s+4. A leading-term cancellation argument for a finite filtered complex, equivalently its spectral sequence, then gives the same vanishing for the original complex: a cycle's leading part is a boundary; subtract a lifted boundary and repeat at strictly lower filtration until the cycle is a boundary. This argument is within a fixed finite-dimensional total-degree component. Summing over (5) and using (3) proves

\[
H_1(K(z^2,H))_d=0\qquad(d<s+4).
\tag{8}
\]

**2. An explicit witness at s+4.**

Use the final selector block only, omit its block index, pair the indices \((0,1),(2,3),\ldots,(2s-2,2s-1)\), and leave index \(\ell=2s\) unpaired. Put

\[
P_0=\prod_{a=0}^{s-1}(z_{2a}-z_{2a+1}),\quad
P_{0,a}=\prod_{b\ne a}(z_{2b}-z_{2b+1}),\quad X=\sum_{j=0}^{2s}z_j.
\]

The following is an identity in the polynomial ring, before taking any quotient:

\[
X^2P_0=z_\ell^2P_0+
\sum_{a=0}^{s-1}(X+z_\ell)(z_{2a}^2-z_{2a+1}^2)P_{0,a}.
\tag{9}
\]

It follows by first writing
\(XP_0=z_\ell P_0+\sum_a(z_{2a}^2-z_{2a+1}^2)P_{0,a}\), multiplying by X, and using the same identity in the term \(z_\ell XP_0\).

Multiply (9) by \(U_{m-3}^2\). This is a homogeneous syzygy of total degree s+4. Its coefficient on H_* is P_0, of degree s; its coefficients on selector squares have degree s+2, as required by the weighted degree convention.

It is non-Koszul. In a Koszul boundary, the coefficient on H_* belongs to the ideal generated by all the other highest-form generators. Set every other block and all U variables to zero, and then reduce the last block modulo its squares. Every other highest-form generator becomes zero, whereas P_0 is a nonzero sum of distinct squarefree monomials. Thus P_0 cannot be that boundary coefficient.

Combining (8) and (9), the exact onset is s+4=17. This derivation is uniform in m and in the stated weights. A convention that additionally quotients by p-th-power/Frobenius relations does not change degree 17 or any degree below it: the first possible affected polynomial degrees are at least p, and p is much larger than 17.

**3. Why the affine certificate is a strict filtered fall.**

First distinguish the closure in the question from one-shot rows. Define

\[
M_d=\left\{\sum_i a_i f_i:\deg(a_i)+\deg(f_i)\leq d\right\}.
\tag{10}
\]

This is the one-shot Macaulay rowspace, not the definition of V_d. Equation (8) implies that, for d<s+4,

\[
M_d\cap R_{\leq e}=M_e\qquad(e\leq d).
\tag{11}
\]

To prove (11), take a representation with bound D whose result has degree below D. Its degree-D coefficient parts form a syzygy on the highest forms. By (8) it is a sum of pairwise Koszul syzygies. Replace those homogeneous pairwise relations by the corresponding affine relations \(f_j e_i-f_i e_j\), which are exact syzygies. Subtracting them from the coefficient vector preserves the polynomial and reduces the representation bound by one. Repetition reaches the degree of the polynomial. This also treats combinations involving only selectors.

Equation (11) makes M_d closed under all the multiplication steps defining V_d: represent an included h at bound deg(h), then multiply that representation whenever deg(ah) is at most d. Hence

\[
V_d=M_d\qquad(d<s+4).
\tag{12}
\]

This is the reason a nonmembership certificate for M_16 is a nonmembership certificate for the iterated closure V_16.

Now center the last block. Let

\[
b_j=w_j/2,\qquad y_j=z_j-b_j,\qquad c=\sum_j b_j=\frac12\sum_jw_j.
\]

The affine selector equations become \(y_j^2-b_j^2=0\). Their quotient has the squarefree y monomials as a vector-space basis. Let NF denote the unique multilinear normal form in this block, let \(Y=\sum_jy_j\), so X=c+Y, and put

\[
P=\prod_{a=0}^{s-1}(y_{2a}-y_{2a+1}),\qquad
P_a=\prod_{b\ne a}(y_{2b}-y_{2b+1}),\qquad
\delta_a=b_{2a}^2-b_{2a+1}^2.
\tag{13}
\]

In this quotient,

\[
\operatorname{NF}(YP)=y_\ell P+\sum_a\delta_aP_a,
\tag{14}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{NF}(Y^2P)=b_\ell^2P
+2y_\ell\sum_a\delta_aP_a
+\sum_a\delta_a(y_{2a}+y_{2a+1})P_a
+2\sum_{a<b}\delta_a\delta_bP_{a,b},
\tag{15}
\]

where \(P_{a,b}\) omits both indicated factors. In particular (14) has degree s+1 and (15) has degree at most s.

For a strict nonmembership certificate define a linear operator on this squarefree vector space:

\[
\Pi=2^{-s}\prod_{a=0}^{s-1}(1-\tau_a),
\tag{16}
\]

where \(\tau_a\) swaps the two y labels of pair a in squarefree monomials. This is a linear operation on normal forms, not an asserted automorphism of the affine selector quotient; unequal b_j make that distinction essential. Its image is the two-dimensional space spanned by P and \(y_\ell P\). In particular, it kills every polynomial of last-block degree less than s.

Let E be squarefree multiplication by Y with squares set to zero, and let D be the operation removing a variable y_j with coefficient \(b_j^2\). Affine-quotient multiplication by Y is E+D. The operator E commutes with \(\Pi\), and D lowers last-block degree by one. Therefore, for every q of last-block degree at most s-1 and every polynomial a(X) of degree at most two in X,

\[
\Pi\operatorname{NF}(a(X)q)=0.
\tag{17}
\]

Indeed, the possible raising terms are q, Eq and \(E^2q\), whose projections vanish because E commutes with \(\Pi\) and \(\Pi q=0\). The remaining terms Dq, EDq, DEq and \(D^2q\) have degree at most s-1 and also project to zero. Coefficients in all other variables are allowed throughout.

The terminal affine equation, denoted g, is quadratic in X:

\[
g=S(U,X,t)=(U-t)^2X^2
-2\big((U+t)(Ut+2)+6\big)X
+(Ut-2)^2-12(U+t),
\quad U=U_{m-3}.
\tag{18}
\]

Every other chain equation is independent of the last block. In M_{s+3}, a multiplier on any quartic has total degree at most s-1. After selector reduction it still has last-block degree at most s-1. Thus (17) kills its terminal contribution, and the elementary degree property of \(\Pi\) kills every other chain contribution. Selector contributions themselves reduce to zero. Consequently

\[
\Pi\operatorname{NF}(h)=0\qquad(h\in M_{s+3}).
\tag{19}
\]

Define the explicit affine remainder

\[
F=\operatorname{NF}(P\,S(U,X,t)).
\tag{20}
\]

The multiplication before reduction has degree s+4. Each selector reduction subtracts a selector multiple of degree at most s+4. Hence \(F\in M_{s+4}\subseteq V_{s+4}\).

Applying (16) to (14) and (15) gives

\[
\Pi\operatorname{NF}(YP)=y_\ell P,\qquad
\Pi\operatorname{NF}(Y^2P)=b_\ell^2P.
\]

As a result,

\[
\Pi F=P\left[
S(U,c,t)+b_\ell^2(U-t)^2
+y_\ell\left(2c(U-t)^2-2((U+t)(Ut+2)+6)\right)
\right].
\tag{21}
\]

If t is not c, the coefficient of \(y_\ell P U^2\) in (21) is \(2(c-t)\ne0\). Equations (14)–(18) show that the degree of F is exactly s+3, namely 16.

If t=c, that degree-s+3 term cancels. However, the coefficient of \(P U^2\) in (21) is \(b_\ell^2\ne0\), because the unpaired weight is nonzero. Equations (14)–(18) now show that F has degree exactly s+2, namely 15.

In both cases \(\Pi F\ne0\). Equations (19) and (12) prove

\[
F\in V_{17}\setminus V_{16},\qquad \deg F\leq16.
\tag{22}
\]

Thus the construction is a strict filtered fall for every target in k. A non-Koszul highest-form syzygy by itself would not have proved this: the additional nonmembership certificate (19)–(21) is the affine bridge.

The only exceptional-target condition for the degree of this particular remainder is

\[
t=c=\frac{2^{27}-1}{2}\quad\text{for the original weights},
\qquad
t=c=\frac{2^{40}+2^{26}-1}{2}\quad\text{for the alternative weights},
\tag{23}
\]

with division by two taken in k. Leaving index 26 unpaired gives \(b_\ell=2^{25}\) or \(2^{39}\), respectively. Both are nonzero. There is no target exception to strictness. No condition about target membership in a selector domain, existence of a liftable point, or solvability of the chain was used or is needed for (22).

**4. Last fall degree and degree of regularity.**

Under the iterated V_d definition, (12) rules out strict falls before 17, and (22) supplies one at 17. Thus the first strict fall is 17 and the last fall degree is at least 17. Neither statement proves that there are no later strict falls. A numerical last-fall upper bound requires a separate bounded-degree spanning or stabilization argument; it does not follow from the first highest-form syzygy, from the degree of F, or from a one-shot rowspace calculation. No such numerical upper bound is established here.

There is also a concrete obstruction to an unqualified claim about degree of regularity. Let J be the highest-form ideal in R. All selector variables lie in its radical. After setting them to zero, (1)–(2) leave precisely the adjacent products

\[
U_0^2U_1^2,\ U_1^2U_2^2,\ldots,\ U_{m-4}^2U_{m-3}^2.
\]

Accordingly

\[
\sqrt J=(z_{i,j}:i,j)+(U_{j-1}U_j:1\leq j\leq m-3).
\tag{24}
\]

The second ideal is the squarefree edge ideal of a path on m-2 vertices. Its coordinate subspaces correspond to sets of nonadjacent vertices; the largest such set has size \(\lceil(m-2)/2\rceil\). Hence

\[
\dim R/J=\left\lceil\frac{m-2}{2}\right\rceil,
\tag{25}
\]

which is positive for every supplied m. Equivalently, one may keep a single U coordinate free while setting all other coordinates to zero to see immediately that every degree has a surviving homogeneous component.

Therefore a definition \(d_{\mathrm{reg}}=\min\{d:J_d=R_d\}\), or the corresponding first zero Hilbert-function cutoff, has no finite value for this highest-form ideal. A Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, regularity after additional equations or saturation, and a solving degree are distinct notions. Their numerical values are not computed by the present argument, and 17 must not be substituted for them merely because it is the syzygy/fall onset. Adding field equations for U would change the mathematical input; none was added here.
