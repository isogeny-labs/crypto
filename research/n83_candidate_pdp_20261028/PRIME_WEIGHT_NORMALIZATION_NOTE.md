# Search filter: nonzero selector weights preserve the highest-form ideal

For the exact prime chain used by this study, changing only the nonzero selector weights cannot change the graded isomorphism class of the highest-form quotient at fixed m and selector width. This follows directly from the validated source template; it gives a useful filter for the next elite-base constructions.

Let R=k[b_(i,j),U_a], with selectors b^2-b and X_i=sum_j w_(i,j)b_(i,j). Each weight is nonzero in k. The degree-preserving coordinate change z_(i,j)=w_(i,j)b_(i,j), leaving each U unchanged, carries the highest selector equation b_(i,j)^2 to a nonzero scalar multiple of z_(i,j)^2. It carries every X_i to the unweighted sum L_i=sum_j z_(i,j).

Consequently every such weighted highest-form ideal is carried to the same ideal generated by all z_(i,j)^2 and the canonical chain quartics Q(L0,L1,U0), Q(U0,L2,U1), ..., U_last^2 L_last^2. The field, m and width remain fixed. Target values and the lower curve terms have already disappeared on taking these highest forms.

The resulting graded quotient rings are isomorphic. Their Hilbert functions and Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity agree, and the generator-degree-preserving isomorphism also carries syzygies and their Koszul submodules to one another. No numerical CM regularity value is needed for this equality. This explains why original/baseline/spike40 reweightings share the reviewed first-fall onset.

The affine selector equations instead become z_j^2-w_j z_j, so this argument does not identify the affine filtered spaces across different weights or supply an exact LFD. The centered production panel checks concrete affine witnesses within those spaces.
