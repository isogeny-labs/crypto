# N83 fixed-base first-fall coverage

This directory indexes the currently saved candidate first-fall results against the frozen 624-system V2 panel. It keeps first-fall, last-fall, and degree-of-regularity claims in separate fields and preserves the provenance of every source summary.

The index covers 432 replayed systems across `spike27`, `spike28`, `spike30`–`spike33`, and `spike35`–`spike37`. The remaining 192 protocol rows are 144 systems on known completed bases (`spike29`, `spike38`, `spike39`) plus 48 systems for `spike34`, whose factor base has not been enumerated. Across the covered rows, every Koblitz and general-binary system has a degree-2 first-fall certificate; every prime system has lower bound 5 with no exact first-fall degree. These outcomes do not vary by candidate within a family, so they cannot supply a supervised target for finding a base with a lower regularity degree.

Every candidate first-fall row has unknown LFD. A separate 12-case m3 restricted propagation panel has inherited strict lower bound 4, but no unit or exhaustive affine upper certificate; it establishes no exact LFD. Neither panel measures affine PDP degree of regularity.

The expanded `coverage.json` also inventories the unlabelled source groups. `spike29` has 48 local PDP `.sing` systems in its indexed input set and 48 separately replayed top-form certificates. Its new [source-binding audit](../r29-attempt-02/source-binding-audit-20261103/README.md) verifies that all 48 source hashes match the frozen V2 family arms and saved replay rows. It does not create V2 first-fall certificates for those systems. The `spike38` and `spike39` complete-base arms each have a 48-system top-form preflight and a terminal failed attempt with no certificate summary; the `spike39` failure record identifies `aws s3 ls` as the failing command. `spike34` still has no enumerated base. None of those top-form records are affine Dreg, FFD or LFD labels, and failed attempts remain preserved.

The primary literature keeps these invariants separate: first-fall degree is only a heuristic proxy for solving behavior and can differ substantially from solving degree, LFD and regularity. Kousidis–Wiemers define their empirical regularity measurement as the highest step degree during Gröbner basis computation. A future affine-Dreg dataset must freeze the exact ideal, Gröbner engine, algorithm, term order, field representation, and timeout/censoring rules before measuring; current top-form quotient intervals cannot fill those labels. Sources: [Caminata–Gorla, *Solving degree, last fall degree, and related invariants*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05579), [Kousidis–Wiemers, *On the first fall degree of summation polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05594), and [Caminata–Gorla, *Solving multivariate polynomial systems and an invariant from commutative algebra*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06319).

Use `python3 build_coverage.py --check` to reproduce the counts and verify the source summaries and 12-case LFD panel against the stored aggregate. The spike35–spike37 archive remains in S3 and is identified in `coverage.json` and the linked custody record; it excludes the large system payloads, which remain at separately indexed S3 locations.
