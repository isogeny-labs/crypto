# N83 one-coordinate factor-base variant sweep

## Question

Does moving the high selector coordinate through positions 27 to 39 produce
N83 factor-base candidates whose PDP regularity evidence separates from the
existing low-27-bit baseline and `spike40` candidate? The full factor-base
enumeration is the first stage; it does not establish an FFD, LFD, degree of
regularity, or solver-cost result. A subsequent frozen PDP protocol must
measure every generated candidate for all three curve families, m=2..9, and
the retained planted and natural target classes.

## Candidate family and reuse contract

For each fixed bit r in 27..39 and every selector i in [0,2^27), define

`x_r(i) = (i & (2^26 - 1)) | ((i >> 26) << r)`.

This map is injective: the low 26 selector bits occupy coordinate positions
0..25, and selector bit 26 occupies position r. Each r defines a distinct
2^27-candidate x-domain on the unchanged N83 Koblitz, general-binary, and
prime curves. Enumerate every affine lift and both distinct signs, retain no
infinity point, and apply no subgroup or cofactor filter. Reuse each completed
family/r base unchanged for every m in 2..9. The existing baseline corresponds
to r=26 and existing `spike40` to r=40; neither is regenerated by this sweep.

The largest prime x in this sweep is below 2^40, so the existing u128
calculation of x^3+2x+3 remains within range. The native generator now stores r
in byte 10 of the N83FB02 shard header and in each shard/family/index manifest.
The record remains selector_index:u32-le followed by y:u128-le, and the
manifest reconstructs x from the recorded coordinate positions.

The exact work is 13 coordinate variants × 3 families × 128 complete shards,
or 4,992 shard computations. No variants, curve families, m values, target
classes, or shard ranges are sampled away. Partial shards, failures, and
timeouts remain retained under their attempt prefix. A retry uses a fresh
attempt directory and S3 prefix; it never overwrites a previous attempt.

## Data required for model training

The currently retained comparison has two distinct base domains per family:
the baseline and `spike40`. Their 48 matched top-form intervals overlap in all
48 cases, and their paired FFD/LFD summary has no degree-of-regularity values.
That does not provide a base-ranking training target. The earlier prime m=2
radix MLP used a different task and is not a factor-base model.

This sweep adds 13 independent coordinate-domain candidates. After it is
complete, generate and retain the full matched PDP systems and degree
certificates for each candidate. Do not convert intervals, lower bounds,
timeouts, first-fall degrees, last-fall degrees, or top-form vanishing degrees
into exact degree-of-regularity labels. A model may be trained only after the
candidate-level records expose a sufficient set of comparable exact labels or
explicit censoring intervals. Split validation by candidate bit/domain so
systems sharing a base cannot leak across train and test folds. Preserve
feature provenance to the file paths, manifests, and hashes from which each
feature was read.

## Controls and acceptance

The native development suite checks the map boundaries and injectivity for
every r=27..40, prime-field arithmetic bounds, every curve's equation, both
signs, omissions, shard joins, and the N83FB02 header. A production candidate
is complete only when all 384 shard receipts verify, all candidate x-values
have been checked for omitted lifts, each family manifest verifies, and the
top-level index binds the candidate bit and the same-base-for-m list.

The acceptance claim is exact enumeration and custody only. It does not rank
candidate bases. Later PDP analysis must report first-fall degree, last-fall
degree, top-form degree bounds, solving degree, and degree of regularity as
separate fields and preserve unknown/censored values.

## Prior work checked

- Kousidis–Wiemers, [*On the first fall degree of summation polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05594), proves a first-fall upper bound for a specified binary-field Weil-descent system and reports empirical regularity separately. Its assumptions do not establish a bound for these three N83 families; it informs the requirement to keep FFD and Dreg labels distinct.
- Trimoska–Ionica–Dequen, [*A SAT-Based Approach for Index Calculus on Binary Elliptic Curves*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7334981/), treats PDP on prime-degree binary extension fields and discusses factor-base dimension and PDP solving. It motivates retaining the full curve-family and factor-base identity in each case; its SAT timing results do not transfer to this Gröbner regularity measurement.
- `research/n83_candidate_pdp_20261028/DESIGN.md` records the prior `spike40` rationale. The coordinate-bit sweep is a controlled encoding-family extension, not a novelty claim.
- Existing `NEXT_EXACT.md`, `evidence/pdp/paired-summary.json`, and
  `evidence/top/verified-summary.json` keep FFD/LFD and top-form intervals
  separate and show that the current two-base comparison does not rank the
  bases by regularity.
