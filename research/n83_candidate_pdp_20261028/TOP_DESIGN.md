The complete 96-system panel uses both saved bases, three curve families,
m2..9 and planted/natural targets. No target, equation, factor-base point or
preprocessing rule changes. Match each input by source SHA-256 to the completed
paired FFD/V2 panel. Every target and planted tuple is identical between arms;
planted sampling is restricted to their common low26 support.

Literature checked before editing: Caminata and Gorla,
https://arxiv.org/html/2112.05579 (highest-form regularity and fall-degree
invariants), and Kosters and Yeo, https://arxiv.org/html/1503.08001 (S3 formulas,
binary first falls and limits of FFD heuristics). Reuse the original native
quotient verifier in ../n83_top_quotient_20261028/src/main.rs. This stage verifies
input-specific certificates, without a general theorem or elite-base claim.

Let H be generated by the highest homogeneous component of every original
saved polynomial, after cancellation within that polynomial. No affine row
reduction or derived equations are added. dreg is the first degree at which
the graded quotient vanishes. A surjection onto the square-zero polynomial
ring in k variables certifies dreg>=k+1. Every failed projection retains its
surviving monomials and contributes no lower bound. Positive dimension means
no finite graded vanishing degree here, not infinite CM regularity.

For binary/Koblitz cases test six predetermined coordinate projections:
first27 selectors, first26, all27m base selectors, low26 in every base block,
and ascending/descending greedy independent sets of squarefree top monomials.
The greedy rule admits a variable only if no complete squarefree top monomial
would survive; it does not claim an optimal independent set. All candidates
are checked against every original top generator. Preserve failures and choose
the largest verified lower bound deterministically.

Every original variable field equation supplies its square in H. Compute rank r
of only the original linear top generators over F2 and retain each echelon row
as an XOR of original equation IDs. In characteristic2 a linear coordinate
change preserves the ideal of all variable squares. Thus the quotient by
squares and these r linear forms is a square-zero ring on nvars-r variables;
R/H is its graded quotient, proving dreg<=nvars-r+1. This strengthens the prior
nvars+1 upper bound without changing H. Exact values are claimed only if a
verified lower and upper bound coincide. No interval ordering establishes a
base improvement when the intervals overlap.

For prime systems, verify each literal x-block definition using the recorded
coordinate positions (baseline0..26 or spike400..25,40), S3 equation, field
equation and ring. Both maps have first weight1, so the existing nonzero
x_first^2*y_first^2 witness still has coefficient1. For m2 the square-zero
projection retaining27 first-block selectors and one second-block selector
gives lower29, and all54 selector squares give upper55. For m>=3 retaining
only u0 maps every highest S3 term to zero; the absence of its field equation
in this saved presentation gives a surjection onto Fp[u0]. The source checker
must verify the actual weight map and equations before admitting this proof.

## Prime top-quotient invariance across candidate bits

The same diagonal proof applies to every candidate coordinate bit r=27..40.
For each 27-selector block substitute baseline v_26 by 2^(r-26) times the
candidate v_26 and leave all other variables fixed. The baseline selector
form then maps exactly to the r-weighted candidate form. Every original
highest Semaev form maps to its candidate counterpart, and each selector-square
generator maps to a nonzero scalar multiple because 2 is invertible modulo the
prime-field modulus. Thus the prime-family original top ideals have isomorphic
graded quotients for every r in the frozen sweep. `src/bin/top.rs` checks this
map for m=2,3 fixtures across r=27..40. This removes prime-family top-quotient
regularity as a varying response for the proposed candidate model; it says
nothing about affine solving degree or the binary/Koblitz systems.
