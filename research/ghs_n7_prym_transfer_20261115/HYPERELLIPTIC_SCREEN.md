# Natural genus-seven quotient: hyperellipticity screen

Status: **derived conditional criterion**, awaiting an independent
function-field automorphism check. It applies to the natural
genus-seven quotient of the standard n7 W1 genus-fourteen cover;
it does not classify every possible cover or correspondence.

Let the W1 Artin--Schreier coefficients be `gamma1,gamma2`, with
`Ord_sigma(gamma1)=p` and `Ord_sigma(gamma2)=(T+1)p`, where `p` is one
of the irreducible cubics dividing `T^7+1`. Put
`delta1=(sigma+1)gamma1` and `delta2=(sigma+1)gamma2`.
The natural quotient `D` has a `(Z/2)^3` deck group over the
`x`-line, with seven nonzero character classes represented by

    a/x + b*x,  (a,b) in U\{0},

where `U` is the irreducible three-dimensional F2[σ] graph
generated by `(delta1,delta2)`. Both coordinate projections of
`U` are injective, so every nonzero character has simple poles
at 0 and infinity and is elliptic.

Suppose `D` is hyperelliptic. Its unique hyperelliptic involution
is central and therefore commutes with the deck group. It descends
to an involution of the x-line preserving `{0,infinity}`. A
nontrivial characteristic-two Möbius involution fixing both points
does not exist. If the induced map were the identity, the
involution would lie in the deck group, but each deck-involution
quotient has genus three: its rank-two AS character group has
three nonzero two-pole elliptic characters. Thus the induced map
must swap the poles, so `x↦lambda/x` for some `lambda∈k*`.

On simple-pole coefficients this swap acts by
`(a,b)↦(lambda*b,a/lambda)`. It preserves `U` and commutes with
σ. Its restriction is an involution in
`End_(F2[σ])(U)≅F8`; in characteristic two the only element
whose square is one is one. Hence the swap fixes every element
of `U`, giving `delta2=lambda^(−1)*delta1`. Therefore
`gamma2=lambda^(−1)*gamma1+d` for some `d∈k*`. By the
kernel-closure proof in `THEOREM.md`, this forces `b_E∈V_p`;
the source is already in the classical genus-seven GHS slice.

The proposed converse follows from the explicit inversion
involution in the D4 model. If checked, this would show that
the natural quotient `D` is hyperelliptic **exactly on the
classical slice**. It is a method-class obstruction: a
nonclassical W1 candidate needs a different quotient or a
non-hyperelliptic Jacobian strategy.

**Verification gap:** independently derive the deck character
graph and genus-three deck quotients from the Hess function
field, check the `k`-descent of `x` and `lambda`, and test the
converse on one coefficient in each cubic arm. Until then this
is a screened lead, not a checked theorem. Relevant published
construction and quotient caveats are [Menezes--Teske §5.2](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2004/corr2004-25.pdf)
and [Hess §8.3](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/315278965D8D277A85812D9498236A88/S146115700000108Xa.pdf/generalising-the-ghs-attack-on-the-elliptic-curve-discrete-logarithm-problem.pdf).
