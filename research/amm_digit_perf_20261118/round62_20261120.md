# Round 62: unit-coefficient products and monic-binomial squares

**Date:** November 20, 2026. **Patch:** [PR #4706](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/pull/4706). **Native Rust CI:** [passed dedicated round-62 job](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/actions/runs/44728070390/job/136260586674).

## Changes

All edits are confined to \`src/cryptanalysis/prime_root_bounded.rs\`. The three producer and three independent-verifier paths are:

1. Scalar multiplication by \(-1\): replace modular multiplication with canonical field negation.
2. Multiplication by monic \(x+1\) or \(x-1\): construct coefficients by one sum/difference each, instead of scaling every coefficient.
3. Squaring monic sparse \(x^d+c\), \(2\le d\le8\): form \(x^{2d}+2cx^d+c^2\) directly, with a zero/±1 constant shortcut.

Generic cases remain on their previous kernels. The standalone exact-source A/B harness [\`round62_unit_products_isolated.py\`](round62_unit_products_isolated.py) removes only the six \`R62\` blocks to reconstruct the immediate pre-round-62 baseline, retaining all previous rounds. Its output and gate field-work counts were checked for **7,168** before/after cases and **32** complete event-stream and certificate-replay pairs. A separate wide-\`u128\` polynomial oracle is included as the production Rust unit test \`round62_unit_coefficients_and_binomial_squares_match_wide_oracle\`.

## Actual compiled Rust isolated kernels

Optimized Rust, **13 alternating A/B timing pairs**, median nanoseconds, \(p=1,000,000,007\):

| Operation | Before (ns) | After (ns) | Speedup |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Producer scalar -1 | 326.94 | 302.17 | 1.082× |
| Verifier scalar -1 | 551.04 | 534.44 | 1.031× |
| Producer multiply by x+1 | 531.50 | 278.33 | **1.910×** |
| Verifier multiply by x+1 | 781.11 | 546.14 | **1.430×** |
| Producer multiply by x-1 | 531.39 | 311.88 | **1.704×** |
| Verifier multiply by x-1 | 787.92 | 577.09 | **1.365×** |
| Producer binomial square | 294.89 | 110.48 | **2.669×** |
| Verifier binomial square | 996.01 | 112.71 | **8.837×** |

For \(p=2^{61}-1\), multiply by \(x+1\) improved **1.915×** (producer) and **1.432×** (verifier), binomial squares improved **2.695×** and **8.817×**, respectively. Other unit operations also improved.

**Negative controls:** Generic scalar/linear multiplication and dense square saw approximately 0.977–1.002×. There is a 1–2.3% branch/dispatch regression on some noneligible kernels. Do not generalize the special-case results to all polynomial arithmetic.

## Local reproduction and \`perf\`

Local AMD EPYC 9V74, pinned CPU, GCC and Clang \`-O3 -march=native\`; 13 alternating A/B medians. C++ *mirrors* (not compiled production Rust) gave 1.03–2.00× across targeted paths in the final validated run. The C++ runs each passed **1,280** baseline/optimized/independent-wide-schoolbook comparisons, and GCC UBSan completed.

The local \`perf\` executable was absent; direct \`perf_event_open\` attempts for software task-clock, hardware cycles, and instructions all failed with \`EACCES\` (\`perf_event_paranoid=2\`). Therefore **all numbers are pinned wall-clock measurements, not PMU-counter measurements**. Hardware cycles/instructions are unmeasured. A suitable PMU-enabled host is needed for hardware profiling.

## Follow-up

First target real relation-generator workload counters (hit rate of unit-linear and binomial-squaring fast paths) and relations/second. If representative dense workload regressions persist, move fast-path dispatch behind a cheap shape predicate or revert the rarely exercised branch. Avoid interpreting 8.8× verifier-binomial speedup as a solver-wide speedup.
