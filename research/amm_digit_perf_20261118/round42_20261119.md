# Round 42: Mersenne addition chains for complete bounded root extraction

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Code:** `src/cryptanalysis/prime_root_bounded.rs`, PR #4706.

## Optimization and correctness argument

The most expensive remaining 61-bit bounded-root operations are modular polynomial powers
`x^p mod f` (Frobenius linear-part extraction) and
`(x+delta)^((p-1)/2) mod f` (Cantor-Zassenhaus-style splitting).
For the supported prime `p=2^61-1`, the two exponents are `2^61-1` and
`2^60-1`, i.e. runs of all-one bits.

Let `U_k = b^(2^k-1) mod f`. Then

- `U_(2m) = (U_m)^(2^m) * U_m mod f`
- `U_(2m+1) = (U_(2m))^2 * b mod f`

The degree-8 case retains exactly `k-1` modular polynomial squarings but
cuts full polynomial products from **60 to 8** (k=60) or **61 to 9**
(k=61). Squaring uses the existing symmetric implementation.

Two independently implemented production paths were changed:

1. Producer `power`: recurse on half-length bit counts, power by repeated
   squaring, and recombine using the identity above. Standard right-to-left
   exponentiation remains unchanged for all other exponents and primes.
2. Replay `modular_power`: construct the bit length iteratively, while
   retaining the verifier's **independent diagonal squaring and descending
   monic-cancellation kernels**. The event/certificate format remains unchanged.

Both paths retain admission charging, deadline checks, and checked field
arithmetic; the reduced operation count also reduces recorded resource use.
No claim is made about constant-time behavior (the input and exponent are public).
