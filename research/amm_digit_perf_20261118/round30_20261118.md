# Round 30: fixed-degree division and sparse verifier square (2026-11-18)

## Source changes
- Nonmonic linear divisor: one synthetic running remainder, no intermediate quotient-polynomial trims.
- Monic quadratic, binomial and sparse-trinomial divisor: update at most two coefficients per descending quotient digit and skip zero quotient coefficients.
- Dense/nonmonic long division: eliminate repeated trailing-zero trim scans and multiplications by zero divisor coefficients via fixed-degree descending loop.
- Sparse symmetric square with >=5 nonzero terms and at least two zero coefficients, without affecting the existing dense/smaller sparse specializations.

Local equivalence passed 3,168 exact before/after C++ checks on p in {487, 1,000,000,007, 2^61-1}, and GCC undefined-behavior sanitizer passed. Standalone squaring measurements refer to the *generic symmetric fallback* only; all prior shorter specializations are preserved in the Rust version.

**perf limitation:** the local \`perf\` binary is absent. Direct \`perf_event_open\` returns EACCES for both software task-clock and hardware CPU cycles. Results above are pinned steady-clock times, not perf hardware counter measurements.

An isolated CI step compiles the actual changed Rust functions, checks frozen baseline equivalence including small and large primes, and runs 13 paired actual-Rust timing trials. Results should gate the decision to retain or revert fast paths; do not claim a production or whole-rootfinder speedup until this step succeeds.
