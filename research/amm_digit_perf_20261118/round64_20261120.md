# Round 64: linear-factor GCD and nonmonic linear squaring (November 20, 2026)

## Implementation

Four bounded, public-input prime-field rootfinding paths in `src/cryptanalysis/prime_root_bounded.rs`:

1. Producer GCD recognizes a monic linear second operand `x+c` and evaluates the dividend modulo it; returns `x+c` iff evaluation is zero, otherwise `1`. Removes quotient construction and the extra Euclidean iteration.
2. Independent certificate verifier GCD applies the same mathematical decision through its separate `remainder` routine.
3. Producer nonmonic-linear square directly calculates `(ax+b)^2 = a²x²+2abx+b²` instead of generic symmetric convolution.
4. Independent verifier nonmonic-linear square doubles `b` before the cross-product. The monic, constant and sparse shortcuts remain ahead of this specialization.

The coefficients are public, no constant-time claim is made, and the input/degree checks and per-field deadline metering remain in place.

## Local performance screen and rejected candidate

Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C; GCC 14.2 and Clang 17; -O3; CPU-pinned; 13 alternating A/B trials; 96 deterministic inputs and 220 repetitions; median steady-clock ns/call. These C++ arithmetic reproductions are **not compiled Rust**.

Representative GCC / Clang speedups:

- GCD with a monic linear factor, polynomial degree four, p=1e9+7: **2.753× / 1.430×**.
- GCD with a monic linear factor, degree eight, p=1e9+7: **1.801× / 1.254×**.
- Nonmonic linear square, p=1e9+7: **1.443× / 1.234×**.
- Nonmonic linear square, p=2^61−1: **1.296× / 2.320×**.

A fifth candidate (nonmonic linear multiplication) was **rejected**: compiler and degree sensitivity caused regressions down to 0.967× (GCC) and 0.907× (Clang). The local source and CSVs are in the round-64 attached benchmark archive. GCC UBSan passed.
