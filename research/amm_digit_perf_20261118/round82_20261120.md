# Round 82: complete-split root-certificate verification (2026-11-20 PT)

Baseline: Round81 commit `9addd2a4f4b2401b21455bc9e97e03e56aa0a857`; candidate: [PR #4872](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/pull/4872).

## Source changes

1. **Proven full-split fast path in the independent verifier.** Reconstruct the product of supplied distinct linear factors and verify that `f = leading(f) * product`. By Fermat's little theorem, `x^p - x` is divisible by this square-free polynomial. The verifier then checks `xp_mod_f == X mod f` and `linear_part == product` without replaying polynomial powering and GCD for the final completeness certificate. In degree one, `X mod f` is the listed root; for higher degrees it is `[0,1]`. For partial splits, repeated factors, or inconsistent factorization, the existing full independent powering/GCD validation is retained. No producer changes or trace schema changes.
2. **No transient output-vector cloning** when comparing independently recomputed division and GCD-step quotients/remainders, or the split quotient. This removes allocation/copy work while preserving the independent arithmetic checks.
3. **Restrict monic linear-product specialization** to products whose other operand has at least five coefficients, avoiding the short-polynomial dispatch overhead implicated in Round81 full-workload regressions.
4. **Regression tests** for complete linear and quartic splits, including nonmonic leading coefficients and tampered `xp_mod_f` / `linear_part`. Historical producer/replayer tests also passed in the isolated Rust compilation.

Proof obligation for fast path: the roots are independently checked to be canonical, strictly ordered, distinct, and their product agrees coefficient-by-coefficient with `f / lead(f)`. Therefore the polynomial divides `X^p-X` over `F_p`, and `gcd(f, X^p-X)=monic(f)`. Neither event-derived roots nor supplied residuals are trusted without checking.

## Local reproductions and Linux perf

On a CPU-pinned Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C using GCC 14.2 and Clang 17 (`-O3 -march=native`), independent C++ reproductions of the final completeness check verified 768 valid full-split cases (64 per degree/prime, degree 1/2/4/8, primes 65,521, 1e9+7, 2^61-1) and rejected 1,536 tampered claims. GCC UBSan passed. Each compiler used 13 alternating paired clock trials. The *isolated* full-split certificate-check shortcut was **18.6x–225.9x faster with GCC**, and **22.3x–115.7x faster with Clang**. This is intentionally not interpreted as a whole-solver gain. A separate clone-versus-borrowed-vector comparison microbenchmark also demonstrated substantially cheaper borrowed comparisons (approximately 6.8x–10.5x locally), but the isolated comparator gain does not establish a similar rootfinding speedup.

Tried `perf stat`: no local `perf` executable. Attempted `apt-get install linux-perf`: package unavailable. Compiled a direct `perf_event_open` syscall probe: task-clock, cycles and instructions all returned `EACCES` (kernel `perf_event_paranoid=2`). Therefore **no hardware performance counters were collected**. Local numbers are CPU-pinned clock measurements, not `perf` profiling. Raw CSVs and standalone C++ repros accompany the Round82 downloadable artifact in the ChatGPT run.

## Decision

The exact Rust and targeted correctness benchmark passed. Leave [PR #4872](https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/pull/4872) **draft and unmerged** while its Round81 parent and broader rootfind validation remain unresolved; repeat A/B and resolve the parent regressions before merging. These trace/certificate verification paths are not on the main prime index-calculus relation-generation hot path, so **relations/sec remains unmeasured**.
