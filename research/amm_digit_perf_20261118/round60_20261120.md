# Round 60: unit-linear cancellation in bounded prime-field root finding (2026-11-20)

## Scope and algebra

In `src/cryptanalysis/prime_root_bounded.rs`, expand the tested unit-binomial
`x^d+c` cancellation specialization to **degree one** (`d=1`) for
`c=+1` and `c=-1` in `Fp`. Both `x+1` and `x-1` reduce a high
coefficient `a_i` to the next-lower coefficient via one modular subtraction
or addition, since `x=-c (mod x+c)`. This avoids modular multiplication by
`c`, repeated polynomial scans and repeated trimming.

The change applies independently to four production functions:

- Producer: `division` computes quotient and remainder by descending synthetic cancellation.
- Producer: `reduce_monic` folds higher coefficients into the constant remainder.
- Certificate replay: `remainder` uses its independent descending traversal.
- Certificate replay: `cancel_monic` folds modulo the monic linear divisor.

The previous fast paths for exact `x`, monomials and degree >=2 unit
binomials remain. **Non-unit linear divisors are unchanged**. All gates keep
their existing per-operation deadline and work accounting.

## Interpretation and remaining work

The 1.42x–1.52x native Rust results measure degree-one unit-divisor
kernels only. They **do not imply a complete root-finder improvement**,
nor do they establish any index-calculus relation-throughput benefit.
Unit-linear divisors can be rare for arbitrary dense polynomials, so the
next high-value experiment is a representative dense-root extraction
profile with per-path counts, then actual relations/sec.
