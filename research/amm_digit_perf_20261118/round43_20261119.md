# Round 43: hoisted row doubling in dense polynomial squares

**Date:** 2026-11-19. **Source:** `src/cryptanalysis/prime_root_bounded.rs`, PR #4706.

## Production changes

Two separately implemented root-finding code paths now cache a doubled field coefficient once per row rather than computing `2*(a_i*a_j)` afresh for every off-diagonal term:

1. **Producer** `square`: for dense polynomials with at least five coefficients, compute `twice_ai = 2*a_i mod p` once and reuse it to form `twice_ai*a_j mod p` for `j>i`. Sparse and smaller inputs retain their prior implementation.
2. **Independent certificate replay** `square_diagonals`: precompute `2*a_i mod p` and iterate over *output diagonals*, rather than sharing the producer's row-major square kernel. Dense-only degree guard preserves the prior sparse and low-degree paths.

The algebraic identity `(2a_i)a_j == 2(a_ia_j) mod p` is exact in every supported prime field. For a fully dense eight-coefficient input, the transformation replaces 28 per-pair modular additions with 7 row doublings, saving 21 charged field additions per squaring. Both paths retain explicit admission, deadlines, fixed storage, and independent replay arithmetic.

## Local CPU-pinned C++ reproductions and rejected candidates

A standalone C++ reimplementation of the degree-3/6/8 modular exponentiation kernels was compiled with GCC 14.2 and Clang 17 at `-O3 -march=native` on Intel Xeon Platinum 8573C. CPU 0 affinity was requested, and every result is the median of 13 alternating A/B pairs over 32 seeded polynomials. The independent baseline, verifier, and optimized candidate agreed for all 192 polynomial/exponent pairs per compiler, with UBSan passing.

For dense degree-8 exponents, GCC reported **1.309x / 1.389x** (60/61 bits); Clang reported **0.980x / 0.978x**. These are *standalone C++ reproduction* timings, not production Rust. Compiler-dependent regressions are why actual Rust benchmarks control retention.

Two **rejected** local candidates were also screened:
- A direct monic-linear multiplication inside the all-one exponent addition chain reduced polynomial multiplication counts, but the degree-8 C++ results were inconsistent (GCC 0.966–0.977x, Clang 0.991–1.020x).
- Synthetic inversion of `x+delta` followed by `b^(2^k) * b^(-1)` removed several full polynomial products. Degree-8 local results were only 1.028–1.029x GCC and 1.011–1.074x Clang; the extra inverse and nonunit fallback introduce additional complexity, so this was not retained.

**Local perf limitation:** `perf` CLI is unavailable; `perf_event_open(PERF_TYPE_SOFTWARE, PERF_COUNT_SW_TASK_CLOCK)` returned **EACCES**; `kernel.perf_event_paranoid=2`. All local figures are CPU-pinned *wall-clock*, not Linux hardware PMU measurements. No performance-counter results are claimed.
