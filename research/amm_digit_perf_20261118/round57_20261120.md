# Root finding optimization, round 57 (November 20, 2026 UTC)

## Change

Two separately implemented, metered fast paths now evaluate
\((x+\delta)^p \bmod x^d\) in the producer root finder and independent
certificate replay. By Frobenius in characteristic \(p\), the answer is
\(\delta\) when \(p\ge d\), or \(\delta+x^p\) when \(p<d\).
This is exact even for small primes and when the original modulus is a
nonmonic multiple of \(x^d\). The event stream is preserved.

Files changed: src/cryptanalysis/prime_root_bounded.rs,
research/amm_digit_perf_20261118/round57_monomial_frobenius_isolated.py,
.github/workflows/radical-root-arithmetic.yml.

## Local CPU reproductions and perf restriction

Local platform: AMD EPYC 9V74, CPU 0 pinned, GCC 14.2 and Clang 17
-O2, 13 alternating wall-clock trials. This is a C++ arithmetic mirror,
not compiled production Rust. On p=1e9+7, producer Frobenius ran 12.41x
faster in GCC and 34.25x in Clang. For p=2^61-1, the same local mirror
improved 27.45x and 91.47x. The verifier affine Frobenius mirror
improved 51.20x/142.53x at 1e9+7 and 137.40x/428.78x at 2^61-1.
The local mirror passed 560 exact polynomial arithmetic comparisons.

We also benchmarked producer and verifier sparse polynomial squaring;
both regressed on the mixed sparse/dense workload (0.83x to 0.97x), so
neither candidate was committed.

The local perf command is absent, and a direct perf_event_open probe
returned EACCES for task-clock, cycles, and instructions. The kernel perf
setting is read-only, so these are pinned wall-clock measurements,
**not** perf cycles/instructions/IPC measurements.

## Limits

These gains are for monomial moduli (repeated-root cases), not generic
dense or squarefree polynomial rootfinding. There is no demonstrated
impact on index-calculus relations/sec or ECDLP throughput.
