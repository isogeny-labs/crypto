# Round 58: fast Frobenius modulo nonzero binomials (2026-11-20)

## Production changes

PR: https://github.com/isogeny-labs/crypto/pull/4706

In `src/cryptanalysis/prime_root_bounded.rs`, the producer's `power` and the independent certificate verifier's `modular_power` now handle `(x+delta)^p mod (x^d+c)`, for `d>=3`, `c!=0`, and zero middle coefficients. The existing R57 `x^d` specialization remains unchanged.

Mathematical proof: In the quotient ring, `x^d=-c`; write `p=q*d+r` with `0<=r<d`, so `x^p=(-c)^q*x^r`. By Frobenius over Fp, `(x+delta)^p=x^p+delta`. This also handles `r=0` and `p<d`. Normalization of nonmonic input occurs before the shortcut.

Only a scalar field exponentiation is needed instead of repeated polynomial squaring, convolution, and reduction. Producer uses right-to-left scalar powering; replay independently uses a left-to-right implementation.

## Local profiling (not Rust)

Pinned AMD EPYC 9V74, GCC 14.2 and Clang 17, `-O3 -march=native`, 13 alternating trials, median of 110 invocations. A standalone C++ arithmetic mirror reproduces binary polynomial exponentiation before and scalar Frobenius after.

At p=1e9+7, GCC d=4: 5,797.7 ns -> 154.7 ns (**37.48×**) and d=8: 19,349.8 ns -> 146.9 ns (**131.76×**). Clang d=8: 17,804.2 ns -> 144.5 ns (**123.22×**). C++ results are *not* used to claim production-Rust performance. 3,318 local arithmetic equivalence tests and GCC UBSan completed successfully.

`perf` is **not available** in the local sandbox. Direct `perf_event_open` for software task-clock and hardware cycles/instructions returned `EACCES` (`kernel.perf_event_paranoid=2`); consequently local timings use pinned `steady_clock`, not performance counters.

## Limits and next measurements

The improvement applies to sparse `x^d+c` moduli, not generic dense polynomials. The benchmark does not establish a speedup in ECC index-calculus relations/sec, GPU throughput, or constant-time execution. Next measurement should exercise the representative relation-finding pipeline to quantify how often binomial fast paths occur and the overall effect.
