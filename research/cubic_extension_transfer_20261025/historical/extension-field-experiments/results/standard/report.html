A successful transfer is not a measured DLP speedup. Toy cover experiments use the same generic BSGS algorithm and the same subgroup order on both sides. Transfer timings include the measured point-transfer and generator-search work; separate construction and validation work is included in total runtime.

What each experiment establishes

  • Tian kernel: published prime-order cubic curve, rational maximal isotropic 3-torsion kernel, Frobenius stability, coordinate-factor rejection, and full source ECDLP recovery.
  • Tian rational map: evaluate all six published functions, trace the divisor classes, match both published images, and verify the subgroup order and homomorphism relations. This evaluates one published isogeny, not a constructor for arbitrary new curves.
  • Tian Jacobian: replay the actual non-hyperelliptic genus-three divisor classes. The target BSGS searches a 4096-element scalar interval; it is not comparable to the full-order source search and is excluded from the timing-ratio plot.
  • Constructed covers: explicit genus-two and genus-three pullback-and-trace maps, including genuine curves over F_(p⁴) and F_(p⁶) mapping to Jacobians over proper intermediate fields. This is not a reproduction of the full Joux–Vitse record computation.
  • Trace zero: exact orders, sampled prime subgroups, Frobenius action, small-field kernel enumeration, and scalar recovery. Relation experiments use exhaustive signed decompositions and linear algebra, not the paper’s Gröbner-basis implementation.
  • Intermediate-field controls: trace transitivity, a surviving base-field subgroup, and a new extension subgroup annihilated by ordinary trace.
  • Binary GHS: original characteristic-two Artin–Schreier rank and candidate-genus screening. It does not construct a divisor map or time an attack.
