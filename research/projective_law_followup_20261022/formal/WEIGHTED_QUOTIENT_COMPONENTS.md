# Weighted quotient grading and degree-zero chart ring

This packet records a pinned Lean proof for the weight `(1,3,1)` sextic over an arbitrary field `K` and arbitrary coefficients `a3,a2,a1,a0`. It changes no collector run, rho control, or cost ratio. The [PDF](WEIGHTED_QUOTIENT_COMPONENTS.pdf), [dependency graph](weighted-quotient-components.svg), and [standardized CSV](weighted-quotient-components.csv) expose the checked and open boundaries for independent review.

`WeightedRingComponent.lean` proves that weighted-degree projection descends to the sextic quotient as additive maps. The projections are orthogonal and idempotent, reconstruct every quotient element from finitely many components, separate quotient elements, and give direct finite sums. Products of homogeneous quotient lifts have the summed degree. `WeightedRingGraded.lean` packages these facts into degree submodules, an internal direct sum, and an actual `GradedRing` instance.

`WeightedChartKernelRec.lean` proves a general saturation theorem: in a nonnegative graded ring, if homogeneous `h` belongs to `(z−1)` for homogeneous degree-one `z`, then `z^k h=0` for some `k`. `WeightedChartRoute.lean` constructs a map from homogeneous localization away from `z` to any ring where `z` maps to `1`, proves its finite-component surjectivity when the original ring map is surjective, and proves injectivity under the saturation hypothesis. `WeightedChartIso.lean` applies those lemmas to the sextic quotient and proves a ring equivalence between its degree-zero homogeneous localization away from `Z` and the earlier affine quotient `K[x,y]/(y²−f(x))`. `WeightedProjSchemeChart.lean` applies Mathlib's basic-open isomorphism to prove the scheme isomorphism `D₊(Z) ≅ Spec(K[x,y]/(y²−f(x)))`. `WeightedProjCover.lean` proves `D₊(X) ∪ D₊(Z) = Proj` and that both opens are affine; it uses the sextic relation to place `D₊(Y)` inside `D₊(Z)`.

This identifies the `Z≠0` scheme chart and establishes a finite affine cover. Identifying `D₊(X)` with the explicit infinity-chart ring and local parameter, the subsequent gluing/completion comparison, `Pic⁰` class equation, and Rust-to-Lean execution bridge are separate and remain open. The finite-zero multiplicity argument in [PROOF_AUDIT.md](../PROOF_AUDIT.md) is written mathematics, not a Lean divisor theorem.
