# Weighted quotient grading and degree-zero chart ring

This packet records a pinned Lean proof for the weight `(1,3,1)` sextic over an arbitrary field `K` and arbitrary coefficients `a3,a2,a1,a0`. It changes no collector run, rho control, or cost ratio. The [PDF](WEIGHTED_QUOTIENT_COMPONENTS.pdf), [dependency graph](weighted-quotient-components.svg), and [standardized CSV](weighted-quotient-components.csv) expose the checked and open boundaries for independent review.

`WeightedRingComponent.lean` proves that weighted-degree projection descends to the sextic quotient as additive maps. The projections are orthogonal and idempotent, reconstruct every quotient element from finitely many components, separate quotient elements, and give direct finite sums. Products of homogeneous quotient lifts have the summed degree. `WeightedRingGraded.lean` packages these facts into degree submodules, an internal direct sum, and an actual `GradedRing` instance.

`WeightedChartKernelRec.lean` proves a general saturation theorem: in a nonnegative graded ring, if homogeneous `h` belongs to `(z−1)` for homogeneous degree-one `z`, then `z^k h=0` for some `k`. `WeightedChartRoute.lean` constructs a map from homogeneous localization away from `z` to any ring where `z` maps to `1`, proves its finite-component surjectivity when the original ring map is surjective, and proves injectivity under the saturation hypothesis. `WeightedChartIso.lean` applies those lemmas to the sextic quotient and proves a ring equivalence between its degree-zero homogeneous localization away from `Z` and the earlier affine quotient `K[x,y]/(y²−f(x))`. `WeightedProjSchemeChart.lean` applies Mathlib's basic-open isomorphism to prove the scheme isomorphism `D₊(Z) ≅ Spec(K[x,y]/(y²−f(x)))`. `WeightedProjCover.lean` proves `D₊(X) ∪ D₊(Z) = Proj` and that both opens are affine; it uses the sextic relation to place `D₊(Y)` inside `D₊(Z)`.

`WeightedChartDehomog.lean` proves a generic equivalence between degree-zero localization away from a degree-one element and quotient by that element minus one. `WeightedInfinityOrdinary.lean` specializes `X=1` and proves the resulting quotient is equivalent to the previously defined explicit infinity chart `K[z,w]/(w²−z(1+a3z²+a2z³+a1z⁴+a0z⁵))`. `WeightedProjInfinityChart.lean` proves the resulting scheme isomorphism `D₊(X) ≅ Spec` of that chart ring. Independently, `WeightedProjProper.lean` proves finite type over the degree-zero subring; `WeightedProjBase.lean` identifies that subring with `K` and proves the weighted `Proj` proper over `Spec K`.

`WeightedProjSmoothPoints.lean` checks two bounded Jacobian facts: over a perfect field of characteristic other than two, a squarefree quintic gives a nonzero partial derivative at every affine `K`-point; every point of the infinity boundary `z=0` has nonzero `z`-partial without extra assumptions. `WeightedJacobianIdeal.lean` proves a stronger ring-global affine certificate: for a perfect field of characteristic other than two and squarefree quintic, the ideal generated by `2y` and `f′(x)` in the affine coordinate ring is the unit ideal. This does not itself assert a scheme-level smoothness instance. `WeightedOverlapScratch.lean` maps the chart generators to the common homogeneous localization away from `XZ`, checks that both chart equivalences send their coordinate fractions to the expected generators, and proves `x·z=1` and `w·x³=y` in the common ring. These identities match the existing transition formulas on generators. `WeightedOverlapFull.lean` constructs both localized projective chart rings, identifies each with its separately defined overlap ring and with the common homogeneous localization away from `XZ`, and composes these equivalences into a canonical overlap-ring transition. Equality with the separately defined transition map remains open.

This identifies both scheme charts, establishes their finite affine cover, proves properness over `K`, and checks the pointwise and ring-global affine Jacobian facts, overlap-coordinate identities, and a canonical overlap-ring equivalence. Scheme-level smoothness, equality of the overlap morphisms with the separately built glued curve, local-stalk transport, the `Pic⁰` class equation, and the Rust-to-Lean execution bridge remain open. The finite-zero multiplicity argument in [PROOF_AUDIT.md](../PROOF_AUDIT.md) is written mathematics, not a Lean divisor theorem.
