# Degree-five Jacobian carrier theorem and its exact scope

15 November 2026. All finite enumeration and algebraic claims in this note are [pcc:unverified] until the repository's independent PCC gate signs them. The scripts and immutable receipts below allow the arithmetic to be checked without relying on this prose.

## Statement

Put `k=F3`, `K=F3[a]/(a^5+2a+1)`, and `S={-26,-22,-14,-10,10,14,22,26}`. For each `t∈S`, there is an ordinary elliptic curve `E_t/K` and a genus-five hyperelliptic curve `Y_t/k` such that

`L_(Y_t/k)(T)=1-tT^5+3^5T^10=L_(Res_(K/k) E_t)(T)`.

Consequently `J(Y_t)` and `Res_(K/k) E_t` are isogenous over `k`. The degree-ten polynomial `X^10-tX^5+3^5` is irreducible over `Q`, so both abelian varieties are `k`-simple of dimension five. Any nonzero algebraic transfer from this restriction into a Jacobian requires genus at least five. These `Y_t` attain that dimension floor **as isogeny-class carriers**. The theorem does not specify an evaluable isogeny or its action on a chosen subgroup.

The full ordinary `K`-isomorphism-class census has `2(3^5−1)=484` classes. Exactly **100** have trace in `S`: counts `10,10,20,10,10,20,10,10` in ascending trace order. Every such class is in one of the eight isogeny classes above, and every source order is `244−t≡2 (mod 4)`. The 2013 Iijima--Momose--Chao direct degree-five genus-five `(2,...,2)` case has four rational branch roots and hence full rational 2-torsion, so its order is divisible by four. Thus the 100 classes are outside that **specific direct-cover family and its same-field isogeny closure**. This is an exact count of carrier-eligible ordinary source classes over `K`, not a count of cryptographically vulnerable classes and not a classification of all GHS routes.

The earlier exhaustive degree-five direct-cover census counted **122** ordinary `K`-classes with order `2 mod 4` and derived a direct `(2,...,2)` cover genus floor of **33** for that torsion type. The 100 classes here are a subset of those 122, so the isogeny-class carrier genus five is strictly below the established direct-cover floor on 100 exact classes. This comparison is between two constructions, not a contradiction or a faster DLP claim. [pcc:unverified]

For every `s≥1` coprime to five, the base changes to `k_s=F_(3^s)` and `K_s=F_(3^(5s))` retain a simple degree-five restriction and a genus-five Jacobian carrier over `k_s`. The source curve has coefficients in the proper subfield `K` when `s>1`; this matters for attack-cost comparisons.

### Exact field-extension and lower-genus gate

More strongly, let `Y/F3` be **any** curve in one of the eight Jacobian isogeny classes above, and put `A=J(Y)`. For every `m≥1`, set `d=gcd(5,m)`. Then `A` over `F_(3^m)` is isogenous to the `d`-th power of a simple abelian variety of dimension `5/d`. In particular:

- If `5∤m`, `A` remains simple of dimension five. There is **no nonconstant map defined over `F_(3^m)` from `Y` to any curve of positive genus below five**, including an elliptic curve. This applies to hyperelliptic and nonhyperelliptic genus-five carriers alike; maps to genus-zero curves are outside this statement.
- If `5|m`, `A` is isogenous to the fifth power of an elliptic curve over `F_(3^m)`. An elliptic quotient, and hence a nonconstant curve map to an elliptic curve, exists abstractly over that field. This assertion does not give an evaluable quotient map or its degree. For the hyperelliptic carriers in the finite screen, every such elliptic map has geometric degree at least four by the separate cover theorem below.

The proof is a root-power calculation. Write `P_t(X)=X^10−tX^5+3^5` and let `α,β` be the two roots of `Z²−tZ+3^5`. Since `3∤t`, one of `α,β` is a 3-adic unit and the other has 3-adic valuation five. The ten roots of `P_t` are the fifth roots of `α` and of `β`; irreducibility of `P_t` is certified below. Taking `m`-th powers identifies exactly `d` roots within each fifth-root cluster and never identifies roots across clusters, whose 3-adic valuations differ. The original Galois group acts transitively on the ten roots, hence also on their distinct `m`-th powers. Their irreducible polynomial has degree `10/d` and the base-changed Frobenius polynomial is its `d`-th power. The finite-field isogeny classification gives the stated simple factor. A nonconstant map to a curve of positive genus below `5/d` would induce a nonzero Jacobian homomorphism, which is impossible when the target Jacobian is too small to contain that simple factor. When `5|m`, the two distinct powered roots give the elliptic factor; the rational point at infinity on every listed `Y_t` supplies an Abel--Jacobi map through an elliptic quotient. This is an application of [Diem's Weil-restriction analysis](https://www.esaga.uni-due.de/f/ulrich.goertz/iem-archiv/dissertation_diem.pdf) and the [Waterhouse--Milne finite-field isogeny theorem](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1971a.pdf), not a new attack construction. [pcc:unverified]

### Prime-subgroup transfer exists abstractly in seven classes

There is a stronger existence certificate for seven selected prime subgroups. The discriminant is

`disc(P_t)=5^10·3^20·(t²−972)^5`.

For a prime `r` coprime to this discriminant, `Z_r[X]/(P_t)` is a finite etale `Z_r`-algebra. The `r`-adic Tate modules of `Res E_t` and `J(Y_t)` are rank-one free modules over it, because each has characteristic polynomial `P_t` and the polynomial is irreducible over `Q`. They are therefore Frobenius-equivariantly isomorphic. [Tate's integral homomorphism theorem](https://pazuki.perso.math.cnrs.fr/index_fichiers/Tate66.pdf) lifts an isomorphism modulo `r` to an `F3`-isogeny `Res E_t → J(Y_t)` whose degree is coprime to `r`; the finite-etale lattice criterion is also explicit in [Poonen--Rybakov, Lemma 2.2(iii)](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/tate_modules.pdf). Such an isogeny is injective on every order-`r` subgroup. The following exact arithmetic is checked in `Odd5Ledger.lean`:

| trace `t` | source order `244−t` | selected prime `r` | `gcd(r,disc(P_t))` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| -22 | 266 | 19 | 1 |
| -14 | 258 | 43 | 1 |
| -10 | 254 | 127 | 1 |
| +10 | 234 | 13 | 1 |
| +14 | 230 | 23 | 1 |
| +22 | 222 | 37 | 1 |
| +26 | 218 | 109 | 1 |

For `t=-26`, the odd prime divisors of the source order 270 are 3 and 5, both dividing the discriminant; this criterion is inconclusive there. The seven isogenies above are **existence results**. They do not specify computable formulas, map degree, image coordinates, or target recovery, so they are not executable subgroup transfers or measured vulnerabilities. [pcc:unverified]

### A finite degree window for the first elliptic cover

[Howe, Theorem 4.17 and Remark 4.18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.04221) give an upper bound for a map from a curve to an ordinary elliptic factor of its Jacobian. Over `K=F243`, the real Weil polynomial of each `Y_t` is `(X−t)^5`. Also `t²−4·243` is one of `−872,−776,−488,−296`; each is a fundamental imaginary quadratic discriminant, so the endomorphism ring of every `E_t/K` in that isogeny class is `Z[π]`, as Howe's theorem requires. With `h0=1`, `b=1`, `n=5` and `γ_10^10<5669`, it gives a nonconstant `K`-map `Y_t→E_t` of integer degree no larger than the following bound. The geometric lower bound applies to every hyperelliptic carrier in this eight-trace slice:

| `|t|` | `|t²−972|` | proven elliptic-cover degree window over `K` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 872 | 4–35 |
| 14 | 776 | 4–33 |
| 22 | 488 | 4–26 |
| 26 | 296 | 4–20 |

The upper bounds follow from the strict integer checks `5669·D^5 < 4^5·(U+1)^10` for each row, where `D=|t²−972|` and `U` is the listed maximum. This reduces explicit cover search to finite degree windows; it does not construct the cover, provide its evaluable action on a chosen source subgroup, or price a transfer attack. The upper bound is a published theorem applied here to these exact curves, not a new cover theorem. [pcc:unverified]

The degree ceiling gives a further **subgroup-preserving cover-existence** result in four traces. For `t=-14,-10,+22,+26`, choose `r=43,127,37,109`, respectively. Each `r` exceeds the corresponding ceiling `U=33,35,26,20`. Let `φ:Y_t,K→E_t` be the map supplied by Howe. The standard push-pull identity `φ_*φ^*=[deg φ]` on `E_t` implies that `φ^*:E_t[r]→J(Y_t,K)[r]` is injective because `0<deg φ<r`. Thus a nonzero subgroup-preserving **curve-induced** transfer exists for each of these four exact traces, including the `t=+26` catalog-uncertain class. This is stronger than the abstract isogeny existence argument above, but Howe's theorem still does not output the rational functions for `φ` or its pullback, so it is not an evaluable attack map. For the other four traces, this degree argument alone gives no injectivity guarantee. [pcc:unverified]

## Proof and computational certificates

1. The finite screen enumerates all `3^11=177147` monic degree-eleven polynomials over `F3`. The odd degree gives one rational point at infinity. It checks squarefreeness and the exact character-sum point counts `N_m=3^m+1` for `m=1,2,3,4`, and `N_5=244−5t`. Newton identities set the first five coefficients of the zeta numerator to `0,0,0,0,−t`; the genus-five functional equation forces the remaining five, yielding `1−tT^5+3^5T^10`. `audit_sage.py` independently replayed every polynomial and all 288 retained rows with different finite-field arithmetic (`audit.json`). `floor_cert.json` gives Rabin irreducibility certificates for each `X^10−tX^5+243` by reduction modulo 19 or 59. Tate's finite-field isogeny theorem supplies the abstract isogeny. Irreducibility makes the restriction simple; a nonzero image in `J(C)` then forces `dim J(C)≥5`.
2. In characteristic three, an ordinary elliptic curve has nonzero `x²` coefficient after completing the square and translating `x` to remove the linear term. Scaling `x=u²x'`, `y=u³y'` puts that coefficient in one of the two square classes `1,2`; the remaining constant is nonzero. The residual normal-form automorphisms do not identify different `(a2,b)` pairs, yielding exactly 484 classes. `class_count.py` enumerated every pair with Sage elliptic cardinality. Frozen `replay_class_count.py` independently counted all 484 orders by 117612 quadratic-residue evaluations and matched every row, trace histogram and selected row (`class_replay.json`). The eight selected source orders were also directly recounted in the original census.
3. The direct-cover exclusion uses only the published family's quartic branch-point form: four roots in `K` give all three nonzero rational 2-torsion points, so `4 | #E(K)`. Our eight group orders are 2 modulo four. Same-field isogenies preserve group order. This excludes that published **direct** family, without excluding every possible cover or correspondence.
4. Let `π` be a root of `U²−tU+3^5` in its ordinary imaginary quadratic field `F`. The degree-ten irreducibility at `s=1` implies `π` is not a fifth power in `F`. If `gcd(s,5)=1` and `π^s=z^5`, choose integers `u,v` with `us+5v=1`; then `π=(z^uπ^v)^5`, contradiction. Ordinarity implies `π/πbar` is not a root of unity, so `Q(π^s)=F`; the prime-degree binomial criterion makes `X^5−π^s` irreducible over `F`, hence `X^10−t_sX^5+3^(5s)` irreducible over `Q`, where `t_s=π^s+πbar^s`. Finite-field linear disjointness for `gcd(s,5)=1` identifies the base change of `Res_(K/k) E` with `Res_(K_s/k_s) E_s`. Base changing the Tate isogeny gives the genus-five carrier. `base_change_check.json` tests all 80 pairs of the eight traces with `s=1..10`; it is a check, not the all-s proof.

`Odd5Ledger.lean` checks only the arithmetic equalities (484 classes, sum 100, eight orders modulo four, representative trace, the cubic-search candidate counts, 531441 even-degree masks and 168 branch transformations) with no axioms. It does **not** formalize the point-count computation, Tate theorem, binomial criterion, base-change argument, or geometric cover theorem.

## Geometric lower bound for curve-to-elliptic covers

For **every genus-five hyperelliptic curve `Y/F3`** whose Jacobian has Weil numerator `1−tT^5+243T^10` for one of the eight traces `t∈S`, every nonconstant morphism from `Y` to an elliptic curve over the algebraic closure has degree **at least four**. This geometric cover bound survives every finite base change of such a curve. It does not bound the degree of an abstract Jacobian isogeny or cover nonhyperelliptic genus-five carriers. The complete reduction to the 84 monic degree-eleven models is proved below. [pcc:unverified]

The degree-two exclusion is geometric. Combine the hyperelliptic degree-two map to `P¹` and a hypothetical degree-two map to an elliptic curve. They have no common degree-two quotient, since that would force a nonconstant map from `P¹` to the elliptic curve. Castelnuovo--Severi then gives `g(Y)≤2·0+2·1+(2−1)(2−1)=3`, contradicting `g(Y)=5`. A degree-one map is also impossible. This argument works in characteristic three because both degree-two maps are separable. See also the [published bielliptic criterion, Lemma 4.1](https://mat.uab.cat/~francesc/xzeroquotientgeneral.pdf). [pcc:unverified]

For degree three, translate the elliptic target so the rational Weierstrass point at infinity maps to the origin. The morphism then intertwines the hyperelliptic involution and elliptic negation. All twelve geometric Weierstrass points map to the four elliptic 2-torsion points; a degree-three map permits at most three distinct points in each fiber, so every such fiber consists of three distinct Weierstrass points. The induced rational function on the x-lines has a simple pole at infinity and two finite poles at roots of `f_t`. After normalizing its numerator and denominator, a map implies

`f_t = B(A³+c2 A²B+c1 AB²+c0 B³)`,

where `B` is a monic squarefree degree-two divisor of `f_t` over the field of the map, `A` is monic cubic and coprime to `B`, and the cubic `u³+c2u²+c1u+c0` is smooth. Conversely that identity constructs a degree-three morphism via `(x,y) -> (A(x)/B(x), y/B(x)²)` to the indicated elliptic curve. All 84 `f_t` factor over `F3` as a linear factor times an irreducible degree-ten factor. Over `K=F243` that degree-ten factor splits into five irreducible quadratics, giving five possible `B` and `5·243=1215` exact monic cubic candidates per model. Over `L=F_(3^10)` all eleven finite branch roots split, giving `binom(11,2)=55` possible `B`. For fixed `B`, `A³≡f_t/B (mod B)` fixes `A mod B` uniquely by inverse Frobenius; every monic cubic lift is `A=A0+B(x+v)` for one parameter `v`. [pcc:unverified]

The frozen Sage 10.10.rc0 source `cubic_screen.sage` tested all 1,215 lifts for each of the eight witnesses and found zero smooth elliptic targets (`cubic_screen.json`). Public Magma 2.30-1 independently replayed both catalog-uncertain traces `t=±26`, each with 1,215 trials and zero maps (`cubic_replay2.json`, successful and untruncated). The first Magma attempt failed on an intrinsic name and is retained separately as `cubic_replay.json`; [the Magma polynomial handbook](https://magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/magma/handbook/text/233) identifies the corrected `Modexp` intrinsic. ### Closure across all hyperelliptic models over `F3`

The complementary frozen monic degree-twelve screen examined all `3^12=531441` coefficient masks for `Y:y²=f(x)` over `F3`. For even monic degree twelve, both points at infinity are rational, so the exact character-sum count is `#Y(F_(3^m))=3^m+2+sum_x chi(f(x))`. Requiring the same five point counts and the eight traces retained **84** smooth models. `screen_even.json` records all stages and rows; an independent vectorized Sage replay in `audit_even.json` matched all `531441` inputs, every stage count and all 84 retained rows. All 84 polynomials factor over `F3` as two distinct linear factors times one irreducible degree-ten factor. [pcc:unverified]

For each of the **168 rational branch roots** in those 84 even-degree models, the audit constructs an explicit `F3`-isomorphism to a model in the prior odd-degree screen. If `r` is a root and `c=f'(r)∈F3×`, set `x=r+c/u` and `y'=u^6 y`. Then `G(u)=u^12 f(r+c/u)` is squarefree, monic of degree eleven; its exact mask and trace occur in the earlier 84 rows. The audit checks every polynomial identity and retains all 168 `(even mask,root,c,odd mask)` records. Thus the monic even-degree slice adds **no new `F3`-isomorphism class** in these eight Weil-polynomial classes. [pcc:unverified]

Every genus-five hyperelliptic curve over `F3` has a degree-two map to a genus-zero quotient, and every smooth genus-zero curve over a finite field has an `F3`-point. Hence it has some squarefree equation `y²=f(x)` of degree eleven or twelve, up to a constant quadratic twist. For odd degree eleven, scaling `x` by `2` makes a nonzero leading coefficient monic. For even degree twelve with nonsquare leading coefficient, divide the right side by that coefficient to get a monic model and a quadratic twist; its Weil numerator changes from `L(T)` to `L(−T)`, so `t` changes to `−t` and stays in the symmetric set `S`. The monic even-degree model has a rational branch root by the exhaustive screen, which remains a branch root after twisting; moving it to infinity produces an odd-degree model, whose leading coefficient can again be normalized. Consequently **every hyperelliptic `Y/F3` in these eight isogeny classes is `F3`-isomorphic to one of the 84 odd-degree equations**. The 84 equations are model records and may include mutual isomorphisms; they are not asserted to be 84 distinct isomorphism classes. Combining this reduction with the independently replayed geometric cubic exclusion proves the opening cover-degree bound for the whole hyperelliptic category over `F3`. [pcc:unverified]

## Prior work and novelty boundary

- [Iijima--Momose--Chao 2013, Sections 2 and 7](https://eprint.iacr.org/2013/487.pdf) classifies direct `(2,...,2)` covers under an isogeny condition and lists the degree-five minimal-genus elliptic case `(1,4,5)`. The [2015 follow-up](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/805.pdf), whose indexed abstract was checked on 15 November 2026 (the PDF endpoint returned HTTP 403), states a complete classification of elliptic curves with `(2,...,2)` covers for prime extension degrees `2,3,5,7`. These direct-cover classifications do not classify all Jacobians isogenous to a Weil restriction; they do rule out calling the direct-cover eligibility problem itself new.
- The prior repository [degree-five direct-cover census](../../ghs_cover_census_n5_20261013/README.md) exhaustively checked all 488 elliptic isomorphism classes over `F243`, including 122 of order `2 mod 4`, and derived genus floor 33 for their direct `(2,...,2)` covers. It did not construct a genus-five Jacobian in those restriction isogeny classes. Its existence and bounds materially narrow the novelty statement here.
- The complete local LMFDB `av_fq` isogeny-class snapshot was extracted on 19 October 2026 from `public.av_fq_isog` (2,945,722 rows). It already lists **all eight** Weil-polynomial classes. `has_jacobian=1` for traces `-22,-14,-10,+10,+14,+22` (labels `5.3.a_a_a_a_w`, `5.3.a_a_a_a_o`, `5.3.a_a_a_a_k`, `5.3.a_a_a_a_ak`, `5.3.a_a_a_a_ao`, `5.3.a_a_a_a_aw` respectively). It records **uncertainty** (`has_jacobian=0`) for `t=-26`, label `5.3.a_a_a_a_ba`, and `t=+26`, label `5.3.a_a_a_a_aba`. The [LMFDB field inventory](https://github.com/LMFDB/lmfdb-inventory/blob/master/db-abvar.md) explicitly defines 0 as uncertain, 1 as known yes and -1 as known no. The two explicit curves above resolve those two uncertain snapshot flags mathematically. A connected read-only LMFDB mirror query on 15 November 2026 returned the same zero flags for both exact labels (`lmfdb_connected_mirror.json`), though its refresh timestamp is unavailable; the live site timed out. **Six of eight are catalog-known Jacobian classes; two are potentially new Jacobian identifications relative to the dated snapshot and connected mirror.** Publication priority remains unconfirmed. [pcc:unverified]
- [Tian--Li--Wang--Yu 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-018-0457-6) gives degree-three hyperelliptic covers in odd characteristic for elliptic curves with cofactor two. The indexed abstract and Tian's later introduction were checked; the publisher full text was not available in this pass. Therefore cofactor two by itself is not a novelty discriminator. [Tian 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.07173), whose full arXiv paper was checked, constructs an explicit rational `(ell,ell,ell)` isogeny from a cubic Weil restriction to a genus-three Jacobian, recovers image divisors, and describes a DLP transfer without a curve cover. Thus an isogeny-class carrier alone is not a new attack method, and a cubic example is not an unexplored family.
- [Fan--Fan--Song--Wang 2022](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9833393), full open-access article checked, constructs degree-two and degree-three genus-two hyperelliptic covers of prime-order elliptic curves over quadratic extensions and discusses transfer and decomposition. Its field degree and genus differ from this degree-five carrier, but prime source order and a hyperelliptic target are already published attack features; the paper's suggested isogeny walk is also prior art.
- [Kudo--Harashita 2022, Theorem 1.1 and Procedure 4.1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.06805) enumerated genus-five hyperelliptic curves over `F3` to determine those with maximal `F9` point count **20** and computed Weil polynomials for the selected maxima. Our 84 models have `#Y(F9)=10`, so the paper's published maximal-point tables do not list this eight-trace slice. Its broader enumeration methodology is prior work, and its unpublished or linked working files have not been exhaustively checked for these exact equations. This narrows, but does not settle, priority. [pcc:unverified]
- The internal KB lead `LEAD-TRF-weil-restriction-jacobian-class` (`source:d83cb619b3afdb7b9d999946161057c7`, `crypto-autoresearcher:knowledge/leads/ecdlp/transfer/LEAD-TRF-weil-restriction-jacobian-class.yaml`) already proposed searching abstract Jacobian carriers. ## Falsifier for practical vulnerability

### A primitive-j source in the degree-eleven base change
