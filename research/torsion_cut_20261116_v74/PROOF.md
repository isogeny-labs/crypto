# Torsion-cut routing and full-chain rationalization

On a nonsingular elliptic curve in the declared binary or odd Weierstrass model, a finite ground-field x lifts to P over the quadratic extension with pi(P)=P or -P. Rational two-torsion is neutral; every other x has a unique rational/anti-rational type. A finite S3 root is equivalent to suitable signed point lifts summing to O. Applying pi shows that its non-two-torsion vertices all have the same type. A mixed triple can therefore change type only through rational two-torsion. This is a type-routing invariant, not sufficient polynomial satisfiability.

For a finite summation chain, fix internal torsion coordinates explicitly and connect all remaining non-torsion vertices cooccurring in an S3 equation. Every connected component is monochromatic. A component attached to a rational non-torsion target must be rational. Unanchored components retain both types. All torsion values and finite intermediate restrictions remain; point signs are reconstructed on the chain tree. A label assignment alone never certifies roots or UNSAT.

If E(K)[2]={O}, there are no finite torsion cuts. Induction from the target along S3 equations forces every original source and intermediate to have rational lifts. This holds for arbitrary source domains, repeated coordinates and chain length. A finite rational-chain root set then equals rational group reconstruction after retaining every point sign and excluding infinite intermediate sums. The NIST group certificates verify this criterion independently by proved p,n, nonidentity generator order n, and Hasse uniqueness, not by assuming the field has no torsion.

Stronger binary five-chain theorem: assume G1/G2/G3 from C73, and Tr(A)=0. Anti-source counts 1/3/4/5 are excluded as in C73 without distinctness. For anti count2, their sum is O or T. G3 excludes distinct reciprocal x for sum T; equal x then means order four at x=B^(1/4). Its Artin-Schreier trace is Tr(A), so it is rational under the added guard. The anti pair must therefore cancel to O. The three rational sources sum to Q; no proper nonempty subset of them can sum to O or T, by G1/G2. Any partial sum containing one anti source and at least one rational source has non-ground x. If the two anti sources occur first, their partial sum is infinite. Every ordering is thus excluded by finite ground intermediate requirements. Hence all finite original five-chain roots have rational lifts, including repeated sources.

The actual C73 instance has A=0,B=1,odd degree83 and certified G1/G2/G3, so this theorem applies, subject to the exact stated chain model. It is an equivalence reduction, not a completed five-sum search. Rational group joins still have potentially quadratic storage and cubic work; field size alone does not establish tractability or a speedup. Odd extensions with rational two-torsion require the general torsion-cut planner, not the NIST shortcut.
