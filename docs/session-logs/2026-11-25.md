## 2026-11-25 12:33 PST — Doubling-norm exactness criterion

### What was done

Proved that scalar 2 generates the quotient by the first
remaining P-192 subgroup, using its checked order in the
scalar quotient. Since 2 is a scalar square and the y product
already transforms correctly inside the subgroup, full sign
exactness is equivalent to invariance under doubling at every
nonzero marking. Derived the doubling sextic and an x-only
resultant norm for that ratio. Added a pure-Python finite replay
on two order-97 curves over F_79: the positive curve has zero
doubling/exactness mismatches; the negative curve has 32 of
each. The replay checks the sextic ratio and product norm
at all 48 signed classes on both curves. [pcc:unverified]

### Decisions made

Use the doubling norm as a precise symbolic exactness or
falsification target. A dense subgroup polynomial at the
P-192 size is far beyond the memory gate, so the identity
alone does not supply an efficient evaluator. [pcc:unverified]
