## 2026-11-15T00:10:00Z — Q&A: why DLP solvers are faster on genus ≥ 2 Jacobians

### What was done (commands, files, features)

- Answered a conceptual question in chat; no code, data or experiment touched.
- Summarised: Mumford decomposition gives a natural smoothness notion with
  constant probability 1/g! and a q-sized factor base against a q^g group;
  Gaudry / Thériault / GTTD Õ(q^{2-2/g}) beats rho q^{g/2} for g ≥ 3 [pcc:unverified] and ties
  at g = 2; Diem's line-based relations give Õ(q) on non-hyperelliptic genus 3
  (plane quartics), Diem–Kochinke Õ(q^{2-2/(g-1)}) for g ≥ 4; Smith's trigonal
  isogeny transfers ~18.6% of hyperelliptic genus-3 instances to that case;
  large genus goes L(1/2) then L(1/3) (Enge–Gaudry, EGT, Diem), which is what
  makes Weil descent (GHS) dangerous for E(F_{q^n}).
- Sources checked: answered from standard literature; KB not queried
  (conceptual question, no lead or novelty claim made).

### Decisions made (and why)

- Flagged for the program: a prime-field ECDLP transferred into genus 2 gains
  no exponent (Õ(q) vs rho Õ(q)); only genus ≥ 3, ideally non-hyperelliptic,
  changes the asymptotics, and the known routes need extension-field or special
  curve structure. Assertion from literature, [pcc:unverified].
