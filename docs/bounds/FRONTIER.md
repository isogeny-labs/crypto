# Measured bounds and the frontier

Generated by `ecbench frontier build` from the bound records in `docs/bounds/records`; do not edit. Frontier `ECFR1he8c5a2de771e` (procedure 2), built from 54 bound(s), dominance on `ops`, `memory`. The protocol is [`README.md`](README.md).

A row is **on the frontier** when no other method's bounds of its domain are, together, clearly better on some axis and no worse on any, **read on the curves they share** (two bounds that share no curve are not comparable and neither dominates). Clearly better means disjoint 95 % intervals on a shared curve; a missing interval separates nothing; ties stand. A method with several bounds is ranked on its worst: it dominates only if every one of its bounds that shares a curve does. Where either bound has unpriced work, dominance must also hold at 0, 0.1 and 1 GAE per unpriced operation (AGENTS.md §5a), and the price at which the order flips is shown. Only bounds fitted on four or more curves of a whole session are ranked; the rest are listed beneath each table with the reason. A leader is named only when its interval separates from every other method's on shared curves; otherwise the tie set is listed. `ops` is the mean ratio of `S = gae / √r` to the generic floor `√(π / 2A)`; `memory` is table entries per `√r`; `uncharged` is counted-but-unpriced work per `√r`. `ops tail` is the largest run over the median run, and the largest run's share of the total: a mean carried by its tail is marked **large**. `α` is the fitted exponent of `gae = C · r^α`, with an interval only from four sizes. Toy stays toy: nothing here speaks about cryptographic sizes.
