# The monic-square graph quotient basis and cubic preclosure

Consider the monic-square graph presentation on the four-point parameter
section $t^2=t,\ u^2=u$. The section is also used in the
[projection proof](m31_native/projection_20261124/PROOF.md).

## Multiplication rules

Let $k$ be a field, $q\in\{1,2\}$, and $C=\{1,t,u,g\}$. For each
$0\le i<q$, take $\Delta_i\in\operatorname{span}_k(C)$. Let $B$ be
the base graph ideal generated by

$$
t^2-t,\quad u^2-u,\quad g-tu,\quad z_i^2-\Delta_i\quad (0\le i<q).
$$

For $q=2$ include $h-z_0z_1$ in $B$. Write $A$ for the quotient of
the ambient polynomial ring by $B$, with $h$ present only for $q=2$.
Use $W=\{1,z_0\}$ for $q=1$ and $W=\{1,z_0,z_1,h\}$ for $q=2$.

The products $C\cdot W$ have degree at most two. The parameter relations
reduce every product of elements of $C$ to $\operatorname{span}_k(C)$. For example,
$tg=ug=g^2=g$ in $A$. Use $z_i^2=\Delta_i$ for the squares, and for
$q=2$ also use

$$
z_0z_1=h,\quad z_0h=\Delta_0z_1,\quad
z_1h=\Delta_1z_0,\quad h^2=\Delta_0\Delta_1.
$$

These rules make $\operatorname{span}_k(C\cdot W)$ closed under
multiplication modulo $B$. It contains the classes of $1$ and all
ambient variables, so it spans the quotient in every degree.

Substituting $g=tu$ and, for $q=2$, $h=z_0z_1$ identifies $A$ with the original
monic-square quotient over $k[t,u]/(t^2-t,u^2-u)$. The parameter
quotient has basis $1,t,u,tu$, and each monic-square extension is free
of rank two over the previous ring by division by its monic quadratic.
Thus $\dim_k A=4\cdot 2^q$, which is eight for $q=1$ and sixteen for $q=2$.
This equals the number of products in $C\cdot W$.
They are a vector-space basis of $A$, and each class in $A$ has a
unique representative in $\operatorname{span}_k(C\cdot W)$.

The standard monomials of $B$ can depend on the monomial order. For
example, take $q=2,\ \Delta_0=g,\ \Delta_1=1$. The relation
$z_0h-gz_1\in B$ has two quadratic terms. In graded lex with $g$ first,
the leading monomial is $gz_1$, an element of $C\cdot W$. In graded
lex with $z_0\succ z_1\succ h\succ g\succ t\succ u$, the multiplication
relations have every quadratic product within $\{t,u,g\}$ or within
$\{z_0,z_1,h\}$ as a leading monomial. Any monomial divisible by one of
these products is in the initial ideal. The remaining candidates are
the 16 elements of $C\cdot W$. Since the quotient has dimension 16,
these are all standard for that order. The standard-monomial count follows from
the [Gröbner remainder theorem](https://macaulay2.com/doc/Macaulay2/share/doc/Macaulay2/Macaulay2Doc/html/_normal_spforms.html).

## Preclosure

Fix any degree-compatible monomial order. For each cubic monomial
$\mu$, the multiplication rules give a representative $r_\mu$ in
$\operatorname{span}_k(C\cdot W)$ with every term of degree at most
two. Hence $\mu-r_\mu$ is a nonzero element of $B$ with leading
monomial $\mu$, and $\mu\in\operatorname{in}(B)$. Every monomial of
degree at least three is a multiple of a cubic monomial, so
$\operatorname{in}(B)$ contains them all.

For the full ideal $I=B+(T)$, where $T$ is the added equation,
$\operatorname{in}(I)\supseteq\operatorname{in}(B)$. Every monomial of
degree above three has a proper divisor in $\operatorname{in}(I)$.
Thus every minimal monomial generator of $\operatorname{in}(I)$ has
degree at most three. These minimal generators are the leading
monomials of the reduced Gröbner basis of $I$. Degree compatibility
bounds the degree of each basis element by that of its leading
monomial. The resulting degree bound of three holds for every
degree-compatible order. [pcc:unverified]

## Representatives in $V_3$

In the $V_3$ step, the resulting representative of a polynomial is
unique in the base quotient as an element of
$\operatorname{span}_k(C\cdot W)$, by the basis property above.
